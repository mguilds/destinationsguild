When to Go to Brazil

The Best Time to Visit Brazil

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: May and September to October are the sweet spots, with April and August close behind. You get warm rather than punishing days, far less rain in the southeast, thinner crowds and prices well below the summer peak. Remember that the seasons are the reverse of ours — December to February is high summer — and that Brazil spans everything from the equatorial Amazon to the temperate south, so the perfect month shifts with the region. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to plan around Carnival, and the best timing for Rio, the Amazon, the Pantanal, Iguaçu, the Northeast and the south.

Brazil is not a country so much as a continent with one language. It stretches from four degrees north of the equator to thirty-three degrees south — the distance from Havana to Boston, and then some — so at any moment some part of it is having its best month. The trick is to match your dates to your route. A trip built around Rio de Janeiro and the beaches wants different timing from one built around jaguars in the Pantanal or the flooded forest of the Amazon, and the country’s two great parties, Carnival in February and Réveillon on New Year’s Eve, will either make your holiday or double its price, depending on what you are after. Here is how to choose.

The Seasons at a Glance

South of the equator everything is upside down — here is what each season actually feels like, with weather, crowds, price and who it suits.

Summer — December to February

$$$$ PeakHot & humidCarnival & RéveillonBook far ahead

The Brazilian holiday season, and the most expensive time of the year. Rio de Janeiro and the southeast sit in the low-to-mid thirties Celsius with high humidity and dramatic late-afternoon thunderstorms that clear as fast as they arrive; the beaches are packed with Brazilians on their long school break. This is when the country is at its most exuberant — Réveillon on Copacabana on the last night of December, Carnival in February — and at its least comfortable for walking a city all day. The Amazon and Pantanal are in their rainy months. Wonderful if you want the party and the sea; hard work if you want to sightsee.

Autumn — March to May

$$ ValueCooling and dryingQuiet sightsGreat value

The most underrated season in Brazil. Once Carnival has passed, the crowds vanish, prices drop steeply and the heat softens week by week; by May Rio is warm, dry and glorious, the Pantanal is beginning to dry out and the whole southeast is at its most agreeable for walking. The exception is the Northeast coast around Salvador and Recife, which has its rainy months from about April to July. If you want the classic Brazil at its best for the least money, this is the window to watch.

Winter — June to August

$$$ MixedDry & clearWildlife seasonJuly is busy

Brazilian winter is nothing like ours. Rio sits around 25 degrees Celsius by day with low humidity, blue skies and cool evenings — genuinely the most comfortable weather of the year for sightseeing, if a little cool for swimming. Inland, this is the dry season that makes the Pantanal the best wildlife destination in South America, and the Amazon rivers fall to reveal their white sand beaches. The catch is July, the Brazilian school holiday, when domestic travel surges and beach and mountain towns fill up. In the far south it is properly chilly, with frost in the hills.

Spring — September to November

$$ ShoulderWarm & brightBest all-roundJacaranda in bloom

Our pick for a first visit. The southeast warms into the high twenties without the summer humidity, the rains have not yet returned in earnest, São Paulo’s streets turn purple with jacaranda, and the crowds and prices are still low. September and October are excellent almost everywhere: the Pantanal is still dry, the Amazon is at low water, Iguaçu has good flow without the summer heat, and the Northeast beaches are drying out beautifully. By late November the southeast is turning humid again as summer approaches.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$$$$ PeakHottestSchool holidays

Hot, humid and wet in the southeast, with Rio often above 35 degrees Celsius and heavy afternoon storms, and the beaches at their busiest as Brazilian families take their long holiday. The Amazon and Pantanal are in full rains. Prices are at or near their annual high. Come for the sea, the caipirinhas and the atmosphere, not for long days of city sightseeing.

February

$$$$ CarnivalHot & wetBook far ahead

Still hot and stormy, and dominated by Carnival, which usually falls this month and turns Rio, Salvador, Recife and Olinda upside down for the best part of a week. Hotel prices multiply and minimum stays apply. Outside the Carnival week the month behaves much like January. Decide early whether you want to be in the middle of it or well clear of it.

March

$$ Value returnsRain easingCrowds gone

The moment the party ends, Brazil becomes very good value. It is still warm and showery in the southeast, and the Amazon is at high water, but the beaches empty, hotels discount and the sights are quiet. A shrewd month for a Rio-and-Iguaçu trip if you do not mind the odd downpour, and the falls are impressively full.

April

$$ ValuePleasant southeastNortheast rains begin

A lovely, quiet month in Rio and São Paulo: warm days, cooler nights, the humidity dropping away. The Pantanal begins to dry and wildlife starts to concentrate. The one caution is the Northeast coast, where Salvador and Recife enter their wettest stretch. Watch out for the Easter long weekend, when Brazilians travel and coastal prices jump.

May

$ Best valueWarm & drySuperb all round

One of the two best months of the year. The southeast is warm, dry, clear and almost empty of visitors, prices are at their lowest, and the days are perfect for Sugarloaf, Corcovado and long lunches. The Pantanal is entering its excellent season and Iguaçu still carries plenty of water. If you can travel whenever you like, put May at the top of the list.

June

$$ ValueDry & clearFesta Junina

Winter begins: crisp, sunny days in Rio around 25 degrees Celsius, cool evenings, very little rain. Inland the dry season opens the Pantanal properly. This is also Festa Junina month, the country’s warm-hearted second festival season of bonfires, forró music and country food around the saints’ days of 13, 24 and 29 June, at its most spectacular in Caruaru and the Northeast interior, while the Parintins folklore festival fills a small Amazon town in late June.

July

$$$ Domestic peakDry & comfortableWildlife prime

The weather is excellent — dry, mild and clear across the southeast, and prime jaguar season in the Pantanal — but this is the Brazilian winter school holiday, so beach towns, mountain resorts and domestic flights fill up and prices rise. Book early if you travel now. The Northeast is coming to the end of its wet months and the Amazon waters are falling.

August

$$ ValueDriestJaguars & river beaches

The holidays end and prices fall while the fine dry weather continues: one of the most reliable months of the year. The Pantanal is at its wildlife peak, the Amazon is dropping toward low water and its sand beaches are emerging, and the Northeast is drying out fast. Rio can be a little cool for swimming but is faultless for walking and views.

September

$$ ShoulderWarming upSweet spot

Spring arrives and everything gets easier: warm afternoons, dry air, low crowds and good prices. Excellent for a combined trip — Rio, Iguaçu and either the Amazon or the Pantanal all work well in the same fortnight. Watch for the Independence holiday on 7 September, which makes for one busy long weekend.

October

$$ ShoulderWarm & brightFestivals

Warm, bright and still comparatively quiet, with the first showers returning to the southeast late in the month and the jacaranda in flower in São Paulo. Two of the great regional festivals fall now: the vast Círio de Nazaré religious procession in Belém, on the second Sunday, and Blumenau’s Oktoberfest in the German-settled south. Very good value for the weather you get.

November

$$ ValueWarm, showeryLast of the quiet

Warm and increasingly humid in the southeast as the rains build, and the last stretch of low prices before the summer surge. The Amazon rains return and the Pantanal begins to flood again. Still a perfectly good month for Rio and the beaches, and the mountain town of Gramado lights up for its long Christmas season toward the end of it.

December

$$$$ Rising fastHot & stormyRéveillon

High summer begins. The first half of the month can still be reasonably priced; from about the twentieth everything climbs steeply toward Réveillon, when around two million people dress in white and gather on Copacabana beach for the midnight fireworks — one of the great sights of the travelling year, and one that needs booking many months ahead. Expect heat, humidity and short violent storms.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, Brazil’s pattern is unusually clear, because it follows the Brazilian holiday calendar rather than ours.

The shoulder months — with May and late August the best of all

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsFine weather too

The lowest fares from North America and the lowest hotel rates in Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo come in the two shoulder windows: March after Carnival through to early June, and late August to the end of November. The happy accident is that these are also among the most pleasant months to be in the southeast, so unlike most countries Brazil’s cheap season is not its unpleasant one. May and September in particular give you excellent weather at low-season prices.

The peaks to avoid if money matters are mid-December to Carnival, the July school holidays, and the Easter and 7 September long weekends. Within any month, midweek flights are cheaper than weekend ones, and São Paulo hotels — a business city — discount sharply from Friday to Sunday, which is the reverse of the beach towns. Domestic flights are the other lever: Brazil is enormous, internal fares rise steeply close to departure, and booking them a few months out often saves more than the hotel choice does.

Carnival, Weather & the Crowds

Brazil’s busy periods are entirely predictable, which makes them easy to plan for — or around.

Carnival: the date moves, so check before you book

Feb or early Mar2027: 5–9 FebruaryMinimum staysBook 6–12 months out

Carnival is tied to Easter and therefore moves each year, landing in February or the first days of March. In 2027 it runs from Friday 5 February to Tuesday 9 February, with Rio’s main Sambadrome parades on the Sunday and Monday nights and Ash Wednesday on 10 February. Rio’s version is the famous one, but Salvador’s street carnival is bigger and more participatory, and Olinda and Recife are the most traditional. Whichever you choose, hotel prices multiply, four- and five-night minimum stays are normal, and the best rooms are gone six to twelve months ahead.

If you would rather not be in the middle of it, you do not need to avoid February altogether — the country returns to normal within a day or two of Ash Wednesday, and rates fall sharply from the following weekend. The other date to note is Réveillon, New Year’s Eve, when Copacabana hosts around two million people in white for the fireworks and hotels along the beach sell out with long minimum stays.

The weather to plan around

Summer stormsNortheast rains Apr–JulAmazon wet Dec–MayJuly domestic peak

Nothing in Brazil should stop a trip, but four patterns are worth knowing. Summer afternoons in the southeast bring short, heavy thunderstorms — plan sightseeing for the morning and keep Corcovado flexible, since low cloud can hide the view for days. The Northeast coast, counter-intuitively, is wettest from about April to July, so Salvador is better in September to March. The Amazon is wet from December to May, though rain there tends to come in short tropical bursts rather than all day. And the July school holiday is a crowd event rather than a weather one, filling beaches and domestic flights.

For thinner crowds anywhere, travel midweek and start early: Sugarloaf, Corcovado and the Iguaçu walkways are all quietest in the first hour after opening. And if you are combining regions, remember that a single fortnight can easily cover both the dry Pantanal and a comfortable Rio if you go in September — see our guides to where to stay in Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo.

Best Time by Region

Brazil is vast and varied — the ideal month shifts considerably depending on where you are headed.

Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo & the southeast

Best: Apr–Jun, Sep–OctBeach heat: Dec–FebAvoid: Jan humidity

The classic cities are all about walking, viewpoints and long lunches, so the mild, dry shoulders win outright: April to June and September to October give you warm days in the mid to high twenties Celsius, clear air for the Christ the Redeemer view and half the crowds. December to February is beach weather and the great festivals, but also heat, humidity and afternoon storms. Winter nights are cool enough for a light jacket. The Amazon — high water and low water

High water: Dec–MayLow water: Jul–NovHot all year

The Amazon has no cold season, only a wet one and a dry one, and each offers a different trip. In the high-water months of December to May the rivers rise many metres and small boats glide directly into the flooded forest, which is the more magical way to see it. In the low-water months of July to November the waters fall to expose white river beaches and forest floors, giving better walking, fishing and beach picnics. Expect high twenties to low thirties Celsius, high humidity and rain in any month. Manaus is the usual gateway.

The Pantanal — the wildlife window

Best: Jun–OctJaguars: Jul–OctFlooded: Dec–Mar

The world’s largest wetland is the finest wildlife destination in South America, and its calendar is simple: as the floodwaters recede through the dry season from about June to October, the animals concentrate around the shrinking pools and rivers and sightings become reliable rather than lucky. July to October is the prime window for jaguars along the Cuiabá river, along with caiman, capybara, giant otters and hundreds of bird species. From December to March the wetland floods, the greenery is spectacular and the wildlife disperses.

Iguaçu Falls

Best: Aug–Nov, Mar–MayFullest: Dec–MarHot summers

The falls are magnificent in any month, and the choice is between water volume and comfort. The summer months of December to March bring the heaviest flow, and also heat in the low thirties Celsius, humidity and the biggest crowds. August to November and March to May give you comfortable walking weather, good flow and shorter queues on the walkways — our preference. Allow a full day for the Brazilian side and, if you can, a second for the Argentine side.

Salvador & the Northeast beaches

Best: Sep–MarRainy: Apr–JulWarm all year

The tropical Northeast runs on a different clock from the rest of the country: it is warm all year, in the high twenties and low thirties Celsius, and its wet months fall roughly from April to July — exactly when the southeast is at its most pleasant. For Salvador, Recife, Natal and the beaches of Bahia, aim for September to March, with the sea warm and the skies bright. February brings Salvador’s enormous street carnival and June the Festa Junina celebrations of the interior.

The south — Florianópolis, Curitiba & the hill towns

Beaches: Dec–MarCool winters: Jun–AugWine & Christmas lights

Subtropical and genuinely seasonal, the southern states are the closest thing Brazil has to a temperate climate. The island beaches of Florianópolis are at their best from December to March, when the water is warm and the whole coast is on holiday. Winter, from June to August, is cool and sometimes frosty in the hills — which is precisely the appeal of the German- and Italian-settled towns of Gramado and Bento Gonçalves, with their wine country, log fires and long Christmas light season from November into January.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to Brazil.

What is the best time of year to visit Brazil?

For a first trip taking in Rio de Janeiro, Iguacu Falls and one wild place, the strongest months are May, September and October, with April and August close behind. These are the shoulder seasons: warm rather than sweltering, comparatively dry in the southeast, free of the Christmas and Carnival crowds, and noticeably cheaper. December to March is high summer, which means beach weather and festivals but also heat, humidity, afternoon storms and the highest prices of the year. Remember that Brazil is a continent rather than a country, so the ideal month shifts with the region.

When is Carnival in Brazil, and should I plan around it?

Carnival moves with Easter and falls in February or early March. In 2027 it runs from Friday 5 February to Tuesday 9 February, with the main Sambadrome parades in Rio on the Sunday and Monday and Ash Wednesday on 10 February. If you want to be there, book flights and hotels six to twelve months ahead and expect multiplied prices and minimum stays of four or five nights. If you would rather avoid the crush, simply travel outside that week; the country returns to normal within a day or two, and prices fall sharply from the following weekend.

What is the cheapest time to visit Brazil?

The cheapest windows are the shoulder months either side of the Brazilian holidays: March after Carnival through to early June, and again from late August to the end of November. Airfares from North America and hotel rates in Rio de Janeiro and Sao Paulo are at their lowest then, and the sights are quiet. Avoid the peaks if price matters: mid-December to Carnival, the July school holidays and the Easter and Independence long weekends. Midweek flights are usually cheaper than weekend ones.

When is the best time to visit the Amazon and the Pantanal?

The Pantanal is at its best in the dry season, roughly June to October, when the floodwater recedes and animals gather at the remaining pools; July to October is the prime window for jaguars along the rivers. The Amazon is rewarding all year but changes character: the high-water months of December to May let boats glide into the flooded forest, while the low-water months of July to November expose river beaches and forest trails and bring better hiking and fishing. Both regions are hot and humid throughout, and rain is possible in any month.

Are Brazil seasons really the opposite of ours?

South of the equator, yes. Summer runs from December to February, autumn from March to May, winter from June to August and spring from September to November, so a Brazilian winter holiday is our summer and Christmas falls on the beach. The reversal is strongest in the south and the southeast, where Rio de Janeiro, Sao Paulo and the southern states have a real if mild winter. In the tropical north and northeast the year is better described as wet and dry than as hot and cold, with temperatures near the equator staying in the high twenties and low thirties Celsius all year.
