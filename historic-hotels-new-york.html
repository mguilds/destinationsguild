Historic Hotels of New York

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

17 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 17 in New York, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1766

Beekman Arms and Delamater Inn

Rhinebeck, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2006

William Traphagen opened a tavern at the Rhinebeck crossroads in 1704, and in 1766 it was enlarged into the building now called the Beekman Arms, after the Beekman family of early Hudson Valley landowners. As Bogardus Tavern it fed and lodged George Washington, Alexander Hamilton and Benedict Arnold during the Revolution, and the quarrel behind the Hamilton and Burr duel is said to have started here. Franklin Roosevelt spoke from its front porch in all four of his campaigns. The Gothic Delamater Inn, by Alexander Jackson Davis, dates from 1844.

Est. 1869

Mohonk Mountain House

New Paltz, New York · Historic Hotels member since 1991

Alfred Smiley saw Lake Mohonk on a picnic in 1869, and his twin brother Albert bought the land, with its ten-room inn and tavern, from John F. Stokes. The brothers grew it into a Victorian castle built in stages between 1879 and 1910, and the Smiley family still runs it. The family hosted conferences here on American Indian affairs and on international arbitration, and guests included John Burroughs, Andrew Carnegie and Presidents Theodore Roosevelt, Taft, Hayes and Arthur. It became a National Historic Landmark on 9 December 1986.

Est. 1883

The Sagamore Resort

Bolton Landing, New York · Historic Hotels member since 1991

Myron O. Brown, who ran the Mohican House in Bolton Landing, found backing from four Philadelphia millionaires who summered on Millionaires' Row, and together they bought Green Island in Lake George for $30,000. With John Boulton Simpson as president of their company, they hired the Philadelphia architects Wilson Brothers, and the Queen Anne-style resort opened in July 1883. Its name, meaning respected chief, points to Chingachgook in James Fenimore Cooper's The Last of the Mohicans. The hotel was restored in 1983.

Close by

  • Bolton Historical Museum, in an 1890 church
  • Fort William Henry, at the lake's south end
  • Cat and Thomas Mountains Preserve

Est. 1898

Martinique New York on Broadway, Curio Collection by Hilton

New York, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2010

William R. H. Martin, owner of the Rogers Peet men's clothing store, began buying lots off Broadway at 32nd and 33rd Streets in 1892 and hired Henry Hardenbergh, who took the chateaus of the Loire Valley as his model. Martin named the hotel Martinique after himself and at first let its rooms to long-term guests. Wings built between 1901 and 1910 added the mosaic floors and marble spiral staircase. From 1916, Rodman Wanamaker hosted gatherings here that led to the founding of the PGA of America.

Est. 1901

The Wall Street Hotel

New York, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2023

Brokers opened the Tontine Coffee House on this spot in the 1790s as their meeting place, and trading went on here until the Merchants' Exchange moved elsewhere on Wall Street. In 1901 developer William K. Aston hired Clinton and Russell to replace the later building with a rusticated Beaux-Arts skyscraper, the Tontine Building, finished in 1902 for insurance and finance firms. Allan Gerdau of the trading house Otto Gerdau & Co. bought it in 1937, ran his company from it and opened the Tontine Emporium inside in 1965.

Est. 1902

The Algonquin Hotel Times Square, Autograph Collection

New York, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2023

The Puritan Realty Company hired architect Goldwin Starrett, who raised a 12-story building of limestone and red brick on West 44th Street in 1902. Manager Frank Case renamed it the Algonquin and let women stay without an escort, a first among the city's leading hotels. From 1919 the Round Table of Dorothy Parker, George S. Kaufman and their circle lunched here every day, and Harold Ross launched The New Yorker after a poker win at one of those lunches. Tallulah Bankhead moved in at sixteen.

Est. 1907

The Plaza - A Fairmont Managed Hotel

New York, New York · Historic Hotels member since 1991

The first Plaza, completed by McKim, Mead and White for the New York Life Insurance Company, proved too small, so new owners Fred Sterry, Harry S. Black and Bernhard Beinecke pulled most of it down. Henry J. Hardenbergh designed its successor in the spirit of a French chateau, ordering 1,650 crystal chandeliers, and it opened on 1 October 1907 at a cost of $12.5 million. Alfred Vanderbilt drove up by carriage that morning to be the first guest. It was named a National Historic Landmark in 1986.

Est. 1909

The Otesaga Hotel

Cooperstown, New York · Historic Hotels member since 1994

Brothers Stephen Carlton Clark and Edward Severin Clark, heirs of Singer Corporation cofounder Edward Clark, bought about 700 feet of Otsego Lake shoreline and began building in 1909. Architect Percy Griffin gave the hotel a Federal-style front with eight 30-foot columns and an octagonal dome, and Devereux Emmet laid out the Leatherstocking Golf Course beside it. Names inside honor James Fenimore Cooper, who called the lake Glimmerglass. In 1927 the family took in the neighboring Cooper Inn to add more rooms.

Est. 1915

Bear Mountain Inn

Bear Mountain, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2016

The Palisades Interstate Park Commission opened Bear Mountain Inn on 26 June 1915 with an invitation-only party hosted by Governor Charles S. Whitman. Commissioner George W. Perkins wanted a lodge to rival those at Yellowstone and the Grand Canyon, and the firm Tooker and Marsh built it in nine months from chestnut timber, with foundations of stone taken from old colonial boundary walls. Its open stone arches were glassed in by 1923, and Presidents Franklin Roosevelt, Harry Truman and Dwight Eisenhower all came here.

Est. 1919

OHEKA CASTLE

Huntington, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2004

Banker Otto Hermann Kahn of Kuhn, Loeb & Co. lost his New Jersey house, Cedar Court, to fire, so for his Long Island estate he had Delano & Aldrich build entirely in steel and concrete. He bought some 440 acres near Cold Spring Harbor in 1915, and the house, named OHEKA from the letters of his name, became the second largest private home in America. Later it housed city sanitation workers, a Merchant Marine radio school and a military academy, and Orson Welles drew on it for Citizen Kane's Xanadu.

Est. 1924

Marriott Syracuse Downtown

Syracuse, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2016

The Hotel Syracuse was built from 1922 to 1924 as a community hotel owned by local shareholders, designed by George B. Post and Sons, and opened in August 1924 with three towers and more than 600 rooms. In 1948 Syracuse University professor Carl Roters painted a 40-foot mural above the front desk showing 20 events from the city's first century, among them the 1851 rescue of William "Jerry" Henry from the city jail. Mirrored panels covered it in the early 1980s, and the restoration brought it back to light.

Est. 1926

InterContinental New York Barclay

New York, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2025

The New York Central Railroad leased this Lexington Avenue lot near Grand Central, and a syndicate headed by architect Eliot Cross and William Seward Webb Jr., with Vanderbilt money behind it, put up a 14-story hotel designed by Cross and Cross. The Barclay opened in 1926. Composer Amy Beach and actor Charles Ray were among its first residents, and Marlon Brando, Bette Davis and Gloria Swanson came later. Ernest Hemingway finished For Whom the Bell Tolls here in 1940, and diplomat Perle Mesta made it her home.

Est. 1926

Omni Berkshire Place

New York, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2010

Warren & Wetmore, the architects of Grand Central Terminal, built the Berkshire in 1926 for their Terminal City plan for hotels and homes around the new station. Its unusually large rooms drew artists and socialites who made it their permanent address, and Broadway star Ethel Merman lived here for years. In 1942 Richard Rodgers and Oscar Hammerstein wrote Oklahoma! in suite 2100, which now carries their names. The Dunfey group, owners of Omni Hotels, bought it in 1978, and it was fully restored in 1995.

Est. 1926

The Queensbury Hotel

Glens Falls, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2017

Glens Falls leaders began planning a hotel in 1916 that would double as the city's community center. In spring 1924, 100 of them gathered at the offices of Glens Falls Insurance Co. and formed a hotel corporation, and a stock drive matched by Finch, Pruyn and Company raised $440,000 in eight days. The Queensbury opened in 1926. Benny Goodman and Guy Lombardo played its ballroom, Ronald Reagan visited, and Robert F. Kennedy kept a promise to host a luncheon here the day after his 1964 election.

Est. 1927

The Chestnut Inn

Deposit, New York · Historic Hotels member since 2023

In the 1920s Samuel Retzer, who had sold his share of an express company, bought the site of the Knapp family's old Monoquaga cottage on Oquaga Lake. Work began in 1927 on a lodge finished almost entirely in American chestnut, with a 100-foot porch and a great stone fireplace, and the Hotel Monoquaga opened in 1928. In the late 1960s IBM engineers working out the Apollo 11 lunar module's orbit plan retreated here. It closed in 1989, and Bill and David Maines bought it in the early 1990s.

Est. 1931

JW Marriott Essex House New York

New York, New York · Historic Hotels member since 1994

Work on the Essex House began the day after Black Tuesday in 1929, and the crash slowed it so badly that the doors on Central Park South did not open until late 1931. Its red neon sign went up a year later and has since appeared in countless films. The first owners went bankrupt, and the federal Reconstruction Finance Corporation held the building for 15 years. During the Watergate investigation, Nixon's former Attorney General John Mitchell stayed here, a moment recalled in the film All the President's Men.

Est. 1997

Hotel Saranac, Curio Collection by Hilton

Saranac Lake, New York · Historic Hotels member since 1998

Architect William Scopes came to Saranac Lake as a patient of the Adirondack Cottage Sanitorium, recovered in its climate, and stayed. In 1925 he and businessman Morton Marshall formed a hotel company and sold stocks and bonds to pay for it, and their Hotel Saranac was finished in 1927, its second-floor Great Hall modeled on the Grand Salon of Florence's 14th-century Davanzati Palace. It was booked months ahead of the 1932 Lake Placid Winter Olympics. From 1962 Paul Smith's College owned it and taught hotel management inside.
