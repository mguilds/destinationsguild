Saint Lucia, month by month

Best Time to Visit Saint Lucia

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Saint Lucia has two seasons and they are easy to tell apart. From about the middle of December to May it is dry, bright and breezy, and expensive. From June to November it is hotter, wetter, greener and considerably cheaper, and it is also the Atlantic hurricane season. Neither half is a bad time to come — this is a tropical island, not a resort with a closing date — but the trip you get is genuinely different, and so is the bill. Below is the year month by month, with what the weather actually does, what is on, and where the price breaks fall.

The short answer

Three ways to choose, depending on what you are optimising for.

Best weather: February and March

The driest, least humid, breeziest weeks of the year, with reliable sun and calm water on the west coast. Also the busiest and the most expensive, with the popular restaurants needing a booking.

Best value with good weather: early December, and late April into May

Dry-season conditions either side of the peak. Early December comes before the Christmas rates start; late April and May bring warm sea, long dry spells and the Jazz and Arts Festival.

Cheapest: September and early October

The wettest, stormiest stretch of the year, when prices are at their lowest and some places close. Worth it if your dates are flexible and a wet week would not spoil the trip.

Saint Lucia month by month

Temperatures are the usual daytime range in Fahrenheit near the coast; the interior is cooler and a great deal wetter at any time of year.

January — Dry, bright and busy

Highs in the mid-eighties, nights in the low seventies, a steady trade wind and some of the clearest skies of the year. One of the three driest months. Rates are high through the whole month and the island marks Nobel Laureate Week in honour of its two laureates. A very good month to come, if you are not counting the cost.

February — The driest month, and the dearest

The classic Caribbean February: hot sun, low humidity, little rain and a breeze that makes the heat easy. Independence Day falls on the twenty-second. Hotels are at peak rates and the popular restaurants in Rodney Bay need booking. If weather is all that matters to you, this is the month.

March — Dry, hot, still busy

Much like February with a degree more warmth. Rain is rare, the sea is calm on the west coast and visibility for snorkelling and diving is at its best. Peak prices continue, with a lull between the winter season and Easter in some years.

April — The best value in the dry season

Still dry and reliably sunny, with the sea warming and the crowds beginning to thin after Easter. The second half of the month is one of the two windows we would pick: dry-season weather at prices that have come off the February peak.

May — Warm, green at the edges, and the Jazz Festival

The first real showers appear, usually brief and in the afternoon. It is warmer and more humid than March but still largely dry, and the Saint Lucia Jazz and Arts Festival fills the island with music and fills the northern hotels with it. Good weather, better prices, best atmosphere.

June — The green season opens

Heat and humidity build and the showers become regular, though they pass quickly. Hurricane season begins on the first of the month, with very little historical risk this early. Rates drop sharply after the first week or two, which makes June one of the better bargains of the year.

July — Hot, wet in bursts, and Carnival

The wettest month so far, with short heavy downpours, and the hottest water of the year beginning. Carnival takes over the island in July with two days of parades and weeks of fetes before them. Prices are low by winter standards; the north is anything but quiet.

August — Hot, humid, and the first real storm risk

Afternoon rain most days, high humidity and the start of the serious part of hurricane season. The La Rose flower festival falls at the end of the month. The sea is at its warmest and the island at its greenest. Cheap, lively, and a month for flexible bookings and good insurance.

September — The wettest and quietest month

The peak of hurricane season and the heaviest rainfall of the year. Some restaurants and tour operators close for a few weeks. In exchange you get the lowest prices on the island and almost no crowds. Come only if you can shrug off a wet week and can move your dates.

October — Still wet, still stormy, and the best party of the autumn

Heavy rain continues and storm risk remains until the end of the month. The compensation is Jounen Kweyol, Creole Day, at the end of October, when villages across the island cook, dance and speak Kweyol all day - the most genuinely local event of the year. The La Marguerite flower festival falls in the middle of the month.

November — The turn of the season

Hurricane season formally ends on the thirtieth and the rain eases through the month. By the last week the dry season is arriving, prices have not yet climbed and the island is still green. The second half of November is quietly one of the smartest times to come.

December — Dry, festive and rising fast

The first half is excellent: dry, breezy, uncrowded and still at shoulder prices, and the Festival of Lights in the middle of the month lights up Castries. From about the twentieth, rates jump to their highest of the year, the Atlantic Rally for Cruisers brings a fleet of yachts into Rodney Bay and the island fills for Christmas and New Year.

Rain, wind and hurricanes

The honest version, because this is the part that decides whether a trip is a bargain or a gamble.

Saint Lucia's rain is tropical rain: it arrives in heavy bursts, often in the afternoon, and is usually over within the hour. A rainy month is not a month of grey skies. What changes through the year is how often those bursts come — a few times a week in the dry season, most days in September and October. The mountains make their own weather, so the rainforest in the middle of the island is wet when the coast is dry, and the windward east coast catches more than the sheltered west.

The trade wind blows from the east almost all year and is the reason the heat is bearable. It is strongest from December to April, which is also when the Atlantic side is at its roughest and the kitesurfers gather at the southern end of the island.

Hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, with the real risk concentrated between August and October. Saint Lucia is in the Windward Islands and in the belt, and it has taken serious damage before: Hurricane Tomas in 2010 wrecked roads, water supplies and much of the banana crop. In most years nothing of the sort happens, and the island is practised at the season. The sensible precautions are the ordinary ones: travel insurance that covers weather disruption, a booking with some flexibility in it, and a look at the forecast in the week before you fly. Do not let the season put you off June or late November, which are both low-risk and well priced.

What is on through the year

Dates for the bigger fixtures move from year to year — check locally before building a trip around one.

Nobel Laureate Week — January

Lectures, readings and school events in honour of Sir Arthur Lewis and Derek Walcott, the two Nobel laureates born on the island. A quiet, serious week and a very Saint Lucian piece of pride.

Independence Day — 22 February

Parades, church services and a national holiday marking independence in 1979. Expect some services and shops to close for the day.

Saint Lucia Jazz and Arts Festival — May

The island's best-known festival, with concerts across several days and venues in the north and at Pigeon Island. It fills the northern hotels, so book ahead if you want to be near it, or stay south if you would rather not be.

Carnival — July

Two days of street parades with weeks of fetes, calypso and soca competitions leading up to them. The biggest event of the year, in the cheapest half of it.

La Rose and La Marguerite — August and October

Two old flower festivals, the Rose at the end of August and the Marguerite in the middle of October, kept up by rival societies in villages around the island with music, costume and feasting.

Jounen Kweyol — late October

Creole Day: the one day of the year the whole island speaks, cooks and dresses Kweyol, hosted by a different set of villages each time. The most genuinely local day in the calendar and well worth planning a trip around.

Festival of Lights — December

Lanterns and lights through Castries in the middle of December, marking the national day. A gentle, pretty evening and the start of the Christmas season.

The yacht season — December onwards

The Atlantic Rally for Cruisers crosses from the Canaries and finishes at Rodney Bay in December, after which the marina and its restaurants are at their liveliest. Good fun to watch; a busy time to want a table.

Prices, crowds and what is open

The price swing across the year on this island is large, and it is not subtle.

High season runs from about the middle of December to the middle of April, and the fortnight from roughly the twentieth of December is the dearest of all. February and March sit just below it. From late April rates begin to slide, and by June they have dropped a long way — the same room in September can cost a fraction of its Christmas price. Rates start climbing again through late November.

Crowds follow the same curve, with one local wrinkle: Soufriere and the Sulphur Springs are busiest in the middle of the day whenever a large ship is in Castries, in any season. Going early or late to the southern attractions makes more difference to how they feel than the month does.

In the quietest weeks of late summer and early autumn some smaller restaurants, dive shops and tour operators close for a few weeks' maintenance. The large resorts stay open. If you are travelling in September, check that the particular thing you are going for is running before you commit.

Travelling in your later years

When to come, if heat and steps are a consideration.

The gentlest months for anyone who finds heat and humidity hard going are January through March, when the air is drier and the trade wind is at its strongest. July to September is the opposite: hot, heavy air and little relief in the afternoon. If you are coming in those months, plan the walking and the excursions for the morning and treat the middle of the day as something to sit out, which is what the island itself does.

The other seasonal point is the transfer. Hewanorra, the long-haul airport, is about ninety minutes from the northern hotels on a winding road, and that drive is harder in the wet months. A first night in the south, or a regional connection into the small airport at Castries, takes the sting out of arrival at any time of year. Questions about when to visit Saint Lucia

What is the best month to visit Saint Lucia?

If the weather alone decided it, February or March: those are the driest, least humid, breeziest weeks of the year. They are also the most expensive and the busiest, so the answer most people actually want is one of the shoulders. Early December gives you dry-season weather before the Christmas rates begin. Late April and May give you warm sea, long dry spells and the Jazz and Arts Festival, at noticeably lower prices than February. Those two windows are what we book ourselves.

What are the rainy months in Saint Lucia?

Roughly June to November, with September, October and November the wettest of them. The rain is mostly short and heavy rather than all day, and it often falls in the afternoon and clears, so a wet month is not a washout. The interior rainforest is far wetter than the coast all year round, which is why a drive inland can run into cloud and rain while the beach you left is still in sunshine.

When is hurricane season in Saint Lucia?

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, and the risk is concentrated in the second half of it, from August to October. Saint Lucia is in the Windward Islands and in the hurricane belt, and it has been hit before, notably by Hurricane Tomas in 2010. Most seasons pass with nothing worse than heavy rain. If you travel in those months, take insurance that covers weather disruption and choose a booking you can move.

How warm is the sea in Saint Lucia?

Warm all year and never cold: roughly the high seventies Fahrenheit in the coolest months of February and March, and the mid-eighties by late summer and autumn. Nobody needs a wetsuit here at any time of year. The calm, clear swimming water is on the Caribbean west coast; the Atlantic east coast has heavy surf and strong currents and is not for swimming whatever the temperature says.

When is Saint Lucia Carnival?

July. The two days of street parades and the build-up of fetes and calypso competitions before them are the island's biggest party of the year, and they fall in the green season when rooms are cheaper, which is a rare combination. If you want the island quiet, avoid Carnival week; if you want it at full volume, that is the week to come. Book early, because the northern hotels fill.

Is the green season worth the saving?

For many people, yes. Hotel rates from June to November run well below the winter price, the island is greener, the sea is warmer and there are far fewer cruise passengers in Soufriere in the middle of the day. Set against that: more rain, more humidity, hurricane risk, and some smaller restaurants and tour operators close for a few weeks in late summer. If your dates are flexible and your booking is, it is good value.

Which month is quietest?

September, and to a lesser extent early October. It is the wettest and stormiest stretch of the year, so visitor numbers are at their lowest, prices at their softest and some places shut. If you do not mind the chance of a wet week and want the island nearly to yourself, that is when to find it. For quiet with better odds on the weather, early December is the gentler choice.
