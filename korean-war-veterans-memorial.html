Before you go

The "Forgotten War," remembered in steel, granite and names.

From 1950 to 1953, American forces fought alongside the United Nations to defend South Korea. About a million and a half Americans came home from that war, and for decades many of them felt it had been forgotten. The Korean War Veterans Memorial, dedicated on July 27, 1995, the anniversary of the armistice, set out to change that.

The memorial sits on the south side of the Reflecting Pool, a short walk from the Lincoln Memorial. It is best seen slowly, and many visitors find it most powerful in rain, snow or early-morning mist, when the soldiers seem to be walking out of the weather.

What to see at the Korean War Veterans Memorial

The heart of the memorial

The 19 soldiers

Nineteen stainless steel figures of a squad on patrol, in ponchos, crossing a field of juniper. Reflected in the granite wall beside them they become 38, for the 38th parallel.

Black granite

The Wall of Faces

A long, polished granite wall etched with thousands of faces drawn from photographs of the people who served, on land, at sea and in the air.

New since 2022

The Wall of Remembrance

A granite wall carrying the names of the Americans and the Korean soldiers serving with them who died in the war.

A place to pause

The Pool of Remembrance

A quiet circular pool ringed by trees, with the numbers of those killed, wounded, missing and captured carved nearby.

The words

"Freedom Is Not Free"

Carved in silver letters on the granite near the pool. Look also for the dedication honoring those "who answered the call to defend a country they never knew and a people they never met."

Undercroft tickets

The ticket kiosk

Same-day tickets for the new museum beneath the Lincoln Memorial are handed out here each morning. See our Lincoln Memorial guide.

The Wall of Remembrance

The names of the fallen, added in 2022.

For more than 25 years the memorial carried no names. That changed on July 27, 2022, when the Wall of Remembrance was dedicated. It carries the names of more than 36,000 Americans who died in the Korean War, and of more than 7,000 soldiers of the Korean Augmentation to the U.S. Army, known as KATUSAs, who served and died alongside them.

The wall forms a ring around the Pool of Remembrance. If you are looking for a name, ask a park ranger or volunteer to help you find it.

How to visit respectfully

Stay on the paths

Please do not walk into the juniper field among the soldiers or climb on the statues.

Keep it quiet

Veterans and families come here to remember. Speak softly and give them room.

Photography

Photos are welcome. Ask before photographing veterans or grieving families.

Thank a veteran

Korean War veterans are now in their nineties. If you meet one here, it is worth a moment of your time.

Korean War Veterans Memorial FAQ

Is the Korean War Veterans Memorial free?

Yes. The Korean War Veterans Memorial is free to visit and open 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

How many soldiers are at the Korean War Veterans Memorial?

There are 19 stainless steel soldiers. Reflected in the granite wall beside them, they appear as 38, a reference to the 38th parallel.

Are there names on the Korean War Veterans Memorial?

Yes. Since July 27, 2022, the Wall of Remembrance has carried the names of more than 36,000 Americans and more than 7,000 Korean soldiers of the KATUSA program who died in the war.

When was the Korean War Veterans Memorial dedicated?

It was dedicated on July 27, 1995, the anniversary of the armistice that ended the fighting in 1953.

Where is the Korean War Veterans Memorial?

On the National Mall, on the south side of the Reflecting Pool, a short walk from the Lincoln Memorial. The nearest Metro station is Foggy Bottom-GWU.
