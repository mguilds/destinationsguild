Where to Stay in Buenos Aires

Where to Stay in Buenos Aires — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Buenos Aires is a city of neighbourhoods, and choosing the right one matters more here than choosing the right hotel. Recoleta is the grand one — wide, flat, tree-lined avenues, the famous cemetery, and most of the city’s great old hotels. Palermo is leafier and younger, full of restaurants and small design hotels, and spread over a big area, so check how far your hotel really is from the parks. Puerto Madero, the redeveloped docks, has the newest buildings and by far the easiest walking in the city: wide, level, traffic-free promenades along the water. The Microcentro puts you among the historic sights but empties out at night. Below are the hotels we would send our own readers to, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

A word for travellers taking it gently. Buenos Aires is mostly flat, which is a gift, but its pavements are famously uneven and its drivers are quick. Puerto Madero and Avenida Alvear in Recoleta are the two smoothest, calmest walks in the city. Book a radio taxi through your hotel or use a ride-hailing app rather than flagging a cab in the street, and keep your phone out of sight while you walk.

Buenos Aires Hotels — Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of Buenos Aires should I stay in?

Recoleta for most first-time visitors: flat, safe-feeling, elegant, and walking distance from the cemetery, the museums and the Alvear shops. Palermo if restaurants matter most to you, though it is a very large district — check on a map how far your hotel really is from Palermo Soho. Puerto Madero if easy walking is the priority: the old dock promenades are wide, level and free of traffic, which is not something you can say of much of Buenos Aires. The Microcentro puts you among the historic buildings but goes quiet and can feel deserted after office hours.

Which Buenos Aires hotels have a lift?

Of the hotels on this page, Trip.com confirms lifts at the Hilton Buenos Aires, the Ibis Congreso, the Fierro Hotel and the Sofitel Recoleta — though the Sofitel’s own guest reviews note the lifts are slow at busy times. For the others we have not claimed a lift, because Trip.com’s listing does not state one, and absence from a listing is not proof either way. If stairs are a real difficulty, ask the hotel directly when you book.

Are there adults-only hotels in Buenos Aires?

Not that we can verify. Every property we checked on Trip.com either welcomes children of all ages or accepts them in some rooms, so we have not listed an adults-only section for Buenos Aires — we would rather tell you the category is empty than fill it with something that does not fit.

Do I need a car in Buenos Aires?

No, and we would advise against one. Traffic is fast and parking is difficult. The city is flat and walkable within each neighbourhood, the Subte is cheap, and taxis are plentiful. Book a radio taxi through your hotel or use a ride-hailing app rather than flagging one in the street.
