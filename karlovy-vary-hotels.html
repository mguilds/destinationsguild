Where to Stay in Karlovy Vary

Where to Stay in Karlovy Vary — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Karlovy Vary — Carlsbad to the nineteenth century, and to most of the plaques on its buildings — is a spa town built along a narrow river valley, and its whole shape follows from that. The colonnades, the springs, the shops and the cafes sit in a flat ribbon along the Teplá, which makes the town itself one of the easiest and most pleasant walks in the Czech Republic. The grand hotels climb the wooded slopes on either side, which is a different matter. One thing worth understanding before you book: many hotels here are medical spa hotels in the Central European sense — doctors, treatment plans, drinking cures and set mealtimes — rather than the leisure spas an American traveller may be picturing. Both kinds are listed below, and we have said which is which. Every hotel here was checked against Trip.com before it was written in.

Choosing Where to Stay

The valley floor or the slopes above it. This is the only decision that really matters here, and it is a decision about walking.

The colonnade valley, the slopes, or the new town?

OrientationFlat valleySteep slopesFuniculars

The spa quarter along the Teplá — the stretch from the Mill Colonnade up past the Hot Spring Colonnade — is the town as people picture it, and it is genuinely flat, which makes Karlovy Vary one of the kindest destinations in this book for unhurried walking. Staying here puts the springs, the colonnades, the cafes and the Beethoven-and-Goethe plaques outside your door. The wooded slopes above hold several of the grandest hotels and the finest views, and they are reached by steep roads, steps or funicular; a hotel that looks a hundred metres from the colonnade on a map may be a hundred metres up. Always ask. The new town, downstream around the bus and train stations, is where ordinary Karlovy Vary lives — cheaper, plainer, perfectly convenient, and about fifteen flat minutes’ walk or a short bus ride from the spa quarter.

Spa & Medical Spa — and what that actually means

This is the category that defines the town, and the one most likely to surprise a visitor who has not been to a Central European spa before.

Read this before you book a “spa hotel” here

Honest noteMedical vs leisureSet mealtimesAsk first

A Czech lázňský hotel is often a medical institution with a hotel attached. That can mean an initial consultation with a doctor, a prescribed programme of baths, wraps, inhalations and drinking cures, dining rooms with fixed hours and sometimes a prescribed diet, and a clientele staying two or three weeks on a course of treatment. Many visitors find it wonderful and go back every year. Others arrive expecting scented candles and a swim-up bar and are baffled. Neither expectation is wrong — but they are not the same holiday. Before booking anything described as a medical or spa hotel, ask two plain questions: is a doctor’s consultation required, and are meals at set times in a single dining room?

Family-friendly

An honest word: this is not really a children’s town, and pretending otherwise would not help you.

Karlovy Vary with children — the honest position

Honest noteAdult townThermal poolDay trips

Karlovy Vary is built around slow walking, taking the waters and sitting down, and a great many of its hotels are medical spas whose other guests are on treatment courses and value quiet. That is a poor fit for lively children, and several spa hotels set minimum ages or simply discourage them. It is not impossible — the Thermal’s outdoor pool is a genuine hit with families, the funiculars are fun, and the wooded walks above the town are lovely — but if you are travelling with children we would honestly suggest Prague or Brno as a base and Karlovy Vary as a day out. Where you do book, ask directly about the hotel’s policy on children before paying.

Hotel Thermal — the practical family option

$$ ValueEdge of spa quarterOutdoor thermal poolFamily rooms

If you are staying the night with a family, this is the one. It is a large hotel rather than a treatment institution, it has proper lifts and family-sized rooms, and it has the outdoor thermal pool on the terrace, which is the single thing in Karlovy Vary children reliably enjoy.

Adults-only

Uniquely in this book, the honest answer here is that a good deal of the town functions this way in practice.

Adults in practice, if rarely by policy

Honest noteQuiet by designMinimum ages varyAsk directly

We could not verify a formally advertised adults-only hotel in Karlovy Vary on Trip.com, so we are not going to label one. But it is worth saying plainly that the town’s spa hotels are quiet, adult places by design and by clientele — several operate minimum age limits, and the general atmosphere in a treatment-led house is closer to a sanatorium than a resort. If a genuinely peaceful, grown-up stay is what you are after, almost any of the medical spa hotels above will provide it. Ask the property directly about age policies, which vary and are not always published.

Karlovy Vary Hotel FAQ

Is Karlovy Vary flat, or is it hilly like the rest of Czechia?

Both, and the distinction matters. The spa quarter along the river — where the colonnades, springs, shops and cafes are — is flat and easy, and is one of the most comfortable town walks in the country. The slopes on either side, where several grand hotels sit, are genuinely steep. Choose a valley-floor hotel if walking is a consideration, and ask specifically whether the hotel is on the flat.

How do I get there from Prague?

By express coach, not by train. The direct bus takes a little over two hours and is comfortable and frequent; the railway line wanders and takes considerably longer. This is one of only two places in this book where we would steer you away from Czech trains.

What is a “medical spa hotel” and will I like it?

It is a hotel with a doctor and a treatment programme attached — baths, wraps, inhalations, drinking cures — often with set mealtimes and guests staying two or three weeks on a course. Many people love it. If you are expecting a leisure resort spa, it will surprise you. Ask before booking: is a doctor’s consultation required, and are meals at fixed times?

Do I really have to drink the spring water?

Not at all, but you should try it. Buy one of the porcelain cups with the flat spout from any shop on the colonnade, and taste your way along the springs; they vary from pleasantly mineral to distinctly medicinal, and they are served at different temperatures. Then buy the spa wafers, which are the reward.

How many nights do I need?

Two is plenty to walk the colonnades, ride a funicular, take a hillside walk and eat well. A treatment course at a medical spa is a different proposition entirely and typically runs a week or more.

Is it very expensive?

The grand hotels are the most expensive rooms in the Czech Republic outside central Prague. But the town also has genuinely good mid-range and value options, and eating, drinking and the springs themselves cost very little. It is possible to have an excellent stay here without paying grand-hotel prices.

When is the town at its best?

May to early October for walking and the hillside paths, with September particularly good. Early July brings the international film festival, when the town fills, rates rise and rooms genuinely run out — check the dates before booking if you are not coming for it. Winter is quiet, cheap and rather atmospheric in the steam off the springs.
