Cairns’ Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Cairns — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Cairns is a small tropical city that exists to put you on the Great Barrier Reef, and almost every bed worth having is within a few blocks of the water. The Esplanade is the front row: a long waterfront promenade with the free public swimming lagoon at its southern end and most of the better hotels looking over it. The city centre behind it, around Abbott and Lake Streets, is a five-minute walk and a little cheaper. The Marina and the Reef Fleet Terminal, where the reef boats leave early in the morning, sit at the southern end of the Esplanade — which is the single best reason to sleep in this part of town. One honest warning that decides the whole trip for some visitors: Cairns has no swimming beach of its own. The city fronts mudflats and mangroves, and tropical north Queensland has a marine stinger season from roughly November to May. That is precisely why the Esplanade lagoon and the hotel pools matter here more than anywhere else in Australia. Luxury

Cairns has two genuinely good large hotels, and both are on the water. Choose by whether you want the lagoon and the promenade, or the marina and the boats.

Crystalbrook Riley

$$$$ LuxuryEsplanade131-141 EsplanadeLargest resort pool in the city

The best hotel in Cairns, on the Esplanade with the longest and most generous pool and pool deck in the city, a rooftop bar and large, cool, properly finished rooms. The public lagoon is a short walk south, the promenade starts at the door, and the Reef Fleet Terminal is ten minutes on foot for early reef departures. Ask for a sea-facing room high up: the view across Trinity Bay to the hills is the thing you are paying for, and the inland rooms are the same rate.

The most convenient hotel in Australia for a reef trip, standing directly on the Marlin Marina with the pontoons and the Reef Fleet Terminal a two-minute walk from the lobby — which matters when your boat leaves at half past seven. Boutique

The two most interesting smaller rooms in Cairns are both a block back from the water, which is where the city itself actually is.

Crystalbrook Bailey

$$$ UpscaleCity centre163 Abbott StreetArt-filled, rooftop pool

The liveliest of the three Crystalbrook hotels, on Abbott Street a block behind the Esplanade, hung with contemporary art and built around a rooftop pool and a good ground-floor restaurant. Rooms are smaller than at the Riley but better value, and being one street back costs you the sea view and saves you real money. The night markets, the Rusty’s weekend market and the city’s restaurants are all within a few minutes’ walk.

Budget & Value

Cairns is a backpacker town as well as a resort town, so the value is real — but the cheapest rooms are inland and you will want a pool in this climate.

Hilton Cairns

$$ Mid-rangeEsplanade south34 EsplanadePool, near the marina

A long-established hotel at the southern end of the Esplanade, between the lagoon and the marina, with a pool, a lawn and rooms that look out over Trinity Inlet from the upper floors. It is plainer than the Crystalbrook pair and usually costs noticeably less, while keeping the one thing that matters in Cairns — a short flat walk to both the public lagoon and the reef boats. A sensible base for a week of day trips.

Family-friendly

In tropical north Queensland the pool is not a luxury, it is the plan for the middle of the day. Families should buy pool space rather than a view.

Cairns Colonial Club Resort

$$ Mid-rangeManunda18-26 Cannon StreetThree lagoon pools, gardens

A low-rise garden resort about three kilometres inland at Manunda, built around three lagoon-style pools in four hectares of tropical planting, with family and apartment-style rooms and a shuttle into the city. It is the best value in Cairns for a family that wants to swim all afternoon, and the trade-off is honest: you are a short drive or shuttle ride from the Esplanade rather than on it. Ask for a room near the main pool if you have small children.

Adults-only

Cairns has resort properties further north, at Palm Cove and Port Douglas, that market themselves to couples, but no significant hotel in the city itself enforces a strict adults-only age policy — and we will not invent one. In town, a high sea-facing room at the Riley or the Shangri-La is the quiet, grown-up choice. Ask for a floor away from the pool deck, which in this climate is busy from breakfast onwards.

Spa & pool

The best swim in Cairns costs nothing: the Esplanade Lagoon is a large lifeguarded saltwater swimming pool on the waterfront, free to use, netted from the sea and open year round, and it is the reason the city has a social life at all in the stinger season. For hotel water, the long pool deck at Crystalbrook Riley is the best in Cairns, the Shangri-La’s lagoon pool is the quietest, and the Colonial Club’s three pools are the most fun for children. Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask us most when choosing where to stay in Cairns.

What is the best area to stay in Cairns?

The Esplanade, and the southern end of it if you are going out to the reef. That puts you within a flat walk of the free public lagoon, the promenade, the restaurants and the Reef Fleet Terminal where the boats leave early. The city centre one block back — Abbott, Lake and Wharf Streets — costs less and loses only the sea view. Stay inland, as at Manunda, only if you are buying pool space for a family.

Does Cairns have a beach?

Not a swimming one, and this is the single most important thing to know before you book. The city fronts mangroves and tidal mudflats, and the sea along this coast has a marine stinger season from roughly November to May. That is why the council built the free Esplanade Lagoon, and why hotel pools matter so much here. For real beaches, go north to Palm Cove, Trinity Beach or Four Mile Beach at Port Douglas, where the patrolled swimming enclosures are netted in season.

How much do Cairns hotels cost?

Expect roughly 110 to 190 Australian dollars a night for clean value rooms and garden resorts, about 190 to 330 for comfortable four-star rooms with a pool, and from around 400 upward for the best sea-facing rooms. The seasons are the opposite of what northern visitors expect: the dry, mild, popular months of June to October are the expensive ones, and the hot, wet weeks of January to March are the cheapest.

When do reef boats leave, and does my hotel matter?

Most full-day reef trips leave the Reef Fleet Terminal between about half past seven and half past eight in the morning and want you checked in well before that. It genuinely matters where you sleep: from the Shangri-La or the Hilton it is a short flat walk, from the northern Esplanade a ten-minute one, and from an inland resort you are depending on a shuttle or a taxi at seven in the morning. Confirm the departure time with the operator when you book.

Which Cairns hotels take dogs?

We have not confirmed a pet policy in writing for any Cairns hotel, so we are not naming one. Tropical resort properties are less likely to accept dogs than city hotels. Shortlist from the city list on this page and telephone before you pay.

Do I need a car in Cairns?

Not for the city, the reef or Kuranda — the boats, the Skyrail and the Scenic Railway all start in or near the centre, and the tour buses collect from the hotels. Hire a car for the drive north to Port Douglas, Mossman Gorge and the Daintree, or for the Atherton Tablelands, and pick it up on the morning you need it rather than paying to park it all week.
