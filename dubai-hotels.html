Dubai’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Dubai — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Dubai runs for about ninety kilometres along one road, and the district you choose decides what kind of holiday you have. Do you want the Burj Khalifa and the fountain outside the window and the biggest mall in the world downstairs, a beach and a balcony at the Marina or on Jumeirah, a resort island you need never leave, or the old trading city around the Creek where rooms cost a third as much? We have grouped the hotels we would actually recommend by district and by character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you only have to choose your dates.

Downtown Dubai & Business Bay

The postcard district. You are at the foot of the Burj Khalifa, beside the Dubai Mall and the fountain, and on the metro. It is the easiest base for a first visit and the best for an evening on foot — which matters here, because almost nowhere else in the city is walkable. The trade is that the nearest swimmable beach is a fifteen-minute drive, and the area is a building site in parts.

Armani Hotel Dubai, Burj Khalifa

$$$$ LuxuryInside the Burj KhalifaDesigned by Giorgio ArmaniDirect mall access

The hotel occupies the lower floors of the Burj Khalifa itself, which is a thing no other hotel on earth can offer, and Giorgio Armani designed every square metre of it — muted, low-lit, deliberately understated against the extravagance outside. Rooms have the stone, the silk and the hidden storage you would expect, and there is a private lift to the observation deck queue and a covered walk into the Dubai Mall. Best for a traveller who wants the address and the design rather than a pool-and-beach holiday.

Dubai Marina & Jumeirah Beach Residence

Where the beach holiday happens. A forest of towers around a man-made marina, with the JBR beach and The Walk — an open-air strip of restaurants and shops — along the front. It is the most genuinely walkable part of Dubai, the tram and metro both serve it, and you can swim before breakfast. It is also dense, loud at the weekend and about thirty minutes from the old city.

Grosvenor House, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Dubai

$$$$ LuxuryMarina viewsBeach club accessStrong restaurants

Two tall towers over the marina, long established and still one of the most reliable upper-end addresses in the district. Rooms are large and look either down the marina or out to the Gulf, guests use a nearby beach club, and the restaurant and bar line-up is better than most hotels of its size. Best for a traveller who wants marina life and a serious dinner without being on the beach itself.

Jumeirah, Umm Suqeim & the Palm

The resort coast, and the most expensive beds in the city. These are self-contained places with their own beaches, waterparks and restaurant collections, built on the assumption that you will not leave much — and if a holiday rather than a city break is the plan, that assumption is correct. Everything is a taxi ride away, including the metro, and the Palm in particular is a long drive in and out at the wrong time of day.

Jumeirah Al Naseem

$$$$ LuxuryBurj Al Arab viewsTurtle rehabilitation projectWaterpark access

The newest and the most relaxed of the Jumeirah beach hotels, lower and lighter than its neighbours, looking straight along the sand at the Burj Al Arab. Guests get access to the Wild Wadi waterpark and the wider resort’s restaurants, and the hotel hosts a long-running sea-turtle rehabilitation project whose lagoon you can visit. Best for the classic Dubai beach stay without the formality of the older properties.

Deira, Bur Dubai & Al Barsha — the old city and the value

The Dubai that existed before the towers, and the cheapest rooms in it. Deira and Bur Dubai face each other across the Creek, with the gold and spice souks, the wind-tower lanes of Al Fahidi and the abra boats that still cross for small change. Rooms here cost a fraction of the beach rate and the metro links you to everything — but there is no beach, and the area is working and crowded rather than polished. Al Barsha, inland near the Mall of the Emirates, is the other value answer.

Ibis Dubai Al Barsha

$ BudgetMetro and Mall of the EmiratesReliable, plain roomsNo pool

A plain, well-run budget hotel on Sheikh Zayed Road at Al Barsha, a short walk from the Mall of the Emirates, the indoor ski slope and the metro. There is no pool and no view; what there is, is a clean, quiet, air-conditioned room at a price that leaves money for the rest of the trip. Best for travellers who treat the hotel as somewhere to sleep.

Where to stay in Dubai — questions

Which area should a first-time visitor pick?

Downtown if you want to see the city, the Marina or JBR if you want to swim and walk to dinner. Those two answers cover most first trips. Downtown puts the Burj Khalifa, the Dubai Mall, the fountain and the metro on your doorstep and the old city a short ride away, but the beach is a drive. The Marina and JBR give you sand, a tram, and an open-air strip of restaurants, at the cost of being half an hour from the historic quarters. Choose the Palm or the Jumeirah resorts only if you intend to have a resort holiday rather than a city one.

Is it worth staying on the Palm Jumeirah?

It is if the hotel is the holiday. The Palm resorts have the best beaches, the waterparks and the broadest restaurant choice, and for a family with a week to spend that is a complete trip. It is not if you want to see Dubai. The Palm is a single road on and off a man-made island, there is no metro on it beyond the monorail, and a trip to the old city is forty minutes each way in traffic. A common and sensible compromise is a few nights Downtown followed by a few on the beach.

How much cheaper is the old city?

Substantially — it is normal for a decent mid-range room in Deira or Bur Dubai to cost a third or less of a comparable beach-district room in the same week, and the gap widens in the December-to-February peak. You give up the beach and a certain amount of polish, and you gain the souks, the Creek, the abra crossings and the most interesting street food in the city. The metro makes the rest of Dubai accessible, so the practical cost is the time, not the access.

Do I need a car?

No, and in Downtown or the Marina a car is a mild liability given the parking. Taxis are metered, numerous and inexpensive by European or American standards, ride-hailing works everywhere, and the driverless metro runs the length of Sheikh Zayed Road connecting the airport, the old city, Downtown and the Marina. Hire a car for a day if you want Hatta, the east coast or Abu Dhabi under your own steam, and return it rather than paying a week of hotel parking.

When is the cheapest time to book a Dubai hotel?

July and August, by a wide margin, which is also when the heat makes outdoor sightseeing unrealistic. The best compromise is late April, May or late September, when rates are well below the winter peak and the early mornings and evenings are still usable. The most expensive weeks of the year are Christmas and New Year. Our best time to visit page sets the whole year out month by month.

Is there a beach I can use if my hotel is not on one?

Yes. Dubai has several free public beaches, including the stretch at JBR and the long beach at Umm Suqeim within sight of the Burj Al Arab, with showers, lifeguards during the day and cafés behind. Many of the beach hotels and beach clubs also sell day passes that include a sunbed and pool access, which is a common and reasonable way to have a beach day from a Downtown or old-city hotel.
