Wadi Musa — the Town at the Gate of Petra

Where to Stay for Petra — Wadi Musa

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Wadi Musa is the town that exists because Petra is next to it, and choosing a hotel here is unlike choosing one anywhere else in Jordan. The town runs up a long hill away from the Petra gate, which sits at the very bottom. That single fact decides most of it: a bed near the gate means you walk in at opening time and come back for a rest at lunch, and a bed up in the town means a steep climb at the end of a day that has already been eight or nine miles on foot. Everything below follows from the gradient.

By the gate — the lower town, and what we would book

The cluster of hotels within a few hundred metres of the Petra visitor centre, at the bottom of the hill. They are not the cheapest and they are not all the prettiest, but they buy you the one thing that matters here: you can be at the gate when it opens, before the coaches and before the heat, and you can walk back for an hour in the afternoon instead of writing the day off. If you are visiting Petra over two days, which we think you should, this is worth paying for. It is also much the best answer for anyone who is not confident on a long climb.

Up the hill — the town centre and the ridge

Most of Wadi Musa’s rooms, and most of its value, are up the slope above the gate. You get more space for the money, a proper choice of restaurants, and from the higher ground some genuinely fine views over the valley. The cost is the walk: it is roughly a kilometre and a half from the top of the town down to the gate, and all of that is uphill coming back. Most hotels run a shuttle down in the morning; far fewer run one back at dusk, so ask specifically about the return before you book rather than after.

Towards Little Petra and Taybeh — quiet, with a car

Outside the town itself there are smaller places in the villages towards Little Petra to the north and Taybeh to the south, along with some desert-style lodges. They are quieter, often more characterful, and they look out over open country rather than a hillside of concrete. They also need a car or a taxi for everything, including the Petra gate, so they suit a second visit or a slower trip rather than a one-night stop.

What to know before you book

The distance to the gate is the decision

It is the single most useful question to ask a Wadi Musa hotel and the one most booking pages answer vaguely. Ask how many metres to the visitor centre, and whether there is a shuttle in the evening as well as the morning.

Petra is a long day on foot

The Siq alone is well over a kilometre before the Treasury, and the site runs for miles beyond it. The Monastery is a long climb of rock-cut steps. Comfortable shoes are not optional, and a hat and water are not either.

Buy the two-day ticket

Petra rewards two days far more than one, and the two-day ticket costs comparatively little more than the single. If you are using the official Jordan Pass, entry is already included — see our Jordan guide for how that works.

Petra by Night

The candlelit evening walk down the Siq runs on set evenings each week rather than nightly, and it is a separate ticket from the day entry. Check the current evenings locally when you arrive rather than relying on any printed schedule.

Winter can be cold and wet

Wadi Musa sits high enough to get genuinely cold, rainy winters, and snow has closed Petra for a day or two in past years. Hotel heating varies a great deal. Ask, and keep a spare day in the plan if you travel in January or February.

Eating

Most visitors eat in their hotels, and the town’s independent restaurants cluster in the upper town rather than near the gate. If you are staying at the bottom for the walking, check what your hotel does for dinner before you arrive.

Wadi Musa — questions

How many days do I need at Petra?

Two. One day shows you the Siq, the Treasury and the main street, and most people who do only that say afterwards they wished they had stayed longer. A second day gives you the Monastery, the High Place of Sacrifice and the quieter side valleys without a forced march.

Should I stay near the gate or up in the town?

Near the gate if you can, especially for a two-day visit or if a long uphill walk at the end of the day would be hard. Up in the town if value and choice of restaurants matter more, and in that case ask specifically whether the hotel shuttle runs in the evening as well as the morning.

Is there any way to avoid the walking at Petra?

Partly. Horses, horse-drawn carts, donkeys and camels are all offered inside the site, and a cart along the Siq is the usual way to shorten the approach. They do not reach everywhere, and the Monastery steps are walked or ridden by donkey only. Choosing a hotel near the gate removes the extra climb at either end of the day, which is the part most people underestimate.

Can I visit Petra as a day trip from Amman?

You can, and we would rather you did not. It is about three hours each way, so a day trip gives you perhaps four hours on a site that deserves two days, and you would arrive in the middle of the hottest, busiest part of it. Sleep in Wadi Musa.

Is Wadi Musa worth any time of its own?

Not really, and that is no criticism. It is a working hillside town that serves the site, with a spring traditionally associated with Moses striking the rock. Give your time to Petra and to Little Petra just north of it.
