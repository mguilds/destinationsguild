Where to Stay in Galle

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Galle is a Dutch walled town on a headland on the south coast, and the only place in Sri Lanka where the main attraction is somewhere you can sleep. Inside the ramparts the Fort is a grid of merchant houses, churches, a lighthouse and a mosque, lived in rather than merely preserved, and over the last twenty years a great many of those houses have become small hotels. Outside the walls are the station, the bus stand and an ordinary busy Sri Lankan town, and ten minutes east the beaches start. The choice is essentially this: the Fort, or the sea.

Inside Galle Fort — the one to pick for a night or two

World HeritageBoutique housesDearest

The Fort is a UNESCO World Heritage site and the most atmospheric place to stay on the island: coral-stone ramparts you can walk the whole way round at sunset, a seventeenth-century Dutch grid of lime-washed houses with pillared verandas, the Dutch Reformed church, the lighthouse on the point, and cricket on the green outside the walls. Most of the accommodation is in converted merchant houses with courtyards and a handful of rooms each, and it is the dearest in the district by some way. There is no real beach inside the walls, so swimming is a short ride away, and the lanes fill with day-trippers from about ten until five — which is exactly why staying the night is worth it, because the Fort is at its best when they have gone.

Unawatuna, Dalawella and Thalpe — the nearest swimming

Best beachesBudget to mid10-20 min east

Five kilometres east, Unawatuna is the long-established travellers’ beach: a curving bay, reef-sheltered water calmer than most of this coast, and a dense strip of guest houses, cafes and dive shops behind it. It is busier and more built-up than it was, and the beach has been narrowed by erosion and sea walls in places, so it repays a careful look at where exactly a room sits. A little further on, Dalawella and Thalpe are quieter, smarter and more villa-ish, with sandy coves between rock outcrops. All of it is ten to twenty minutes from the Fort by tuk-tuk, which makes this the sensible base for a family or a longer stay.

Galle town, outside the walls — cheapest and most practical

CheapestStation and busesLocal

The modern town spreads north and east of the Fort around the harbour, the cricket stadium, the bus stand and the railway station, and this is where the cheapest and plainest rooms are. It is not pretty and it is not a beach, but it is a five to fifteen minute walk from the Fort gate, it is where the ordinary shops and the local eating are, and it is the obvious choice if you are arriving late by train or leaving early by bus and want to spend as little as possible.

West to Hikkaduwa, east to Mirissa — the longer beach strips

SurfWhales in season30-60 min

If the beach is the point rather than the Fort, the coast either side has more of it. North-west, Hikkaduwa is the old surf and party town with a reef lagoon and turtles, about forty minutes away. East, through Koggala and Ahangama, a string of surf bays runs on to Weligama and Mirissa, an hour or so off, which is the departure point for blue and sperm whale trips between about November and April and the easiest beach town on this coast in which to spend a week. Both are far enough that you would choose one and visit Galle, rather than the other way round.

Getting around

Galle is the easiest place on the island to reach without a car. The coastal railway from Colombo Fort takes between two and three hours depending on the service, costs very little, and runs along the shoreline for much of the way — sit on the right coming south. The Southern Expressway does the same trip by road in about two hours and is the fast option for a car or an airport transfer.

Inside the walls the Fort is entirely walkable and better for it; the grid is about four hundred metres across and the rampart circuit a pleasant half-hour. Outside, tuk-tuks are the way along the coast — agree the fare or use an app — and the local buses and the train serve Unawatuna, Hikkaduwa and the eastern bays frequently and for almost nothing.

A word on the sea, because it matters more here than the transport. This is a surf coast with reefs, rips and sea walls, and conditions change a great deal between December and July. Swim where other people are swimming, ask at your hotel which end of the bay is safe that week, and treat an empty beach with suspicion rather than delight.

When to come

Galle is on the south coast, firmly in the path of the southwest monsoon, and its year divides cleanly. December to April is the dry season and the reason people come: hot, bright, calm seas, swimmable beaches and the whale boats running out of Mirissa. It is also peak season, and rooms inside the Fort over Christmas and the New Year are both scarce and dear.

May to September is the southwest monsoon. Mornings are often fine and the rain tends to come in heavy afternoon bursts, so it is not a washout, but the sea turns rough, some beaches lose their swimming entirely and the whale trips stop. The compensation is that prices fall a long way and the Fort is yours. October and November are the unsettled inter-monsoon weeks, with thunderstorms and the least reliable weather of the year.

If you want one recommendation: late January to March is the best stretch on this coast. Our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Sri Lanka goes through the year on both coasts.

Getting in

Most visitors need an Electronic Travel Authorisation, the ETA, arranged before you fly, and airlines check it. Sri Lanka has revised its entry rules, its fees and the list of nationalities more than once in recent years, including spells when some passports were admitted free of charge, so we deliberately do not print a figure or a list here — a number that is out of date is worse than no number. Read it off the Department of Immigration and Emigration’s own portal at immigration.gov.lk at the moment you book, and apply there rather than through one of the look-alike sites that charge a mark-up for filling in the same form. Your passport should have at least six months left on it and the officer may ask to see an onward ticket.

Two practical notes for the first hour. Draw rupees or change a little money at the airport, because tuk-tuks, markets and small restaurants want cash and small notes are useful. And remember the airport is not in Colombo: Bandaranaike International is at Katunayake, about 35 kilometres north of the capital and some ten minutes from Negombo, which is why so many itineraries begin and end there. Galle hotels FAQ

Should I stay inside Galle Fort or on a beach?

Inside the Fort for one or two nights, and on a beach for anything longer. The Fort is the sight, and it is at its best early in the morning and in the evening when the day-trippers have left, which is an argument for sleeping there. But there is no swimming inside the walls and the rooms cost more, so a common and sensible plan is two nights in the Fort followed by several at Unawatuna, Thalpe or Mirissa.

How do I get to Galle from Colombo or the airport?

The train from Colombo Fort down the coast takes two to three hours, costs very little and is a pleasure in itself. By road the Southern Expressway takes about two hours from Colombo and two and a half to three from the airport, which is north of the city. A pre-booked car is the simplest option with luggage; the train is the better experience.

Is Galle Fort just for tourists now?

No, and that is what makes it unusual. The Fort is still a working neighbourhood of several thousand people, with a mosque, churches, schools, government offices and ordinary houses alongside the hotels and the shops. The middle of the day on the main lanes is firmly geared to visitors; walk the quieter streets in the morning and it is plainly somebody's home town.

Can I swim in Galle?

Not usefully inside the Fort, where the shore is rocks and ramparts. The nearest reliable swimming is Unawatuna, ten to fifteen minutes east, which is reef-sheltered and calmer than most of the coast. From May to September the southwest monsoon makes much of this coastline rough and some of it unsafe, so ask locally rather than judging by appearance.

How long should I stay?

Two nights in the Fort is the honest answer for the town itself: an evening and an early morning on the ramparts, a wander of the grid, the museums and a good dinner. Add as many more as you like on the beaches either side, and if you are here between November and April keep a morning free for the whales from Mirissa.
