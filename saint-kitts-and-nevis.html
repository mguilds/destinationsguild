Saint Kitts and Nevis Travel Guide

By the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Two islands, two miles apart, that share a passport and very little else. St Kitts is the working one: a capital with a cruise berth on its doorstep, a resort strip and a casino at Frigate Bay, a fortress the size of a small town on a hill, and the last sugar railway in the West Indies still running. Nevis is a single green volcano across the channel, where the hotels are old plantation houses under enormous trees and the loudest thing most afternoons is the sea. The whole country is smaller than the Isle of Wight. This is the Guild’s plain guide to both halves — where to sleep, how to cross between them, when to come and what you now need before you fly.

Why visit Saint Kitts and Nevis

A fortress that UNESCO calls one of the best in the Americas

Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park went on the World Heritage list in 1999. UNESCO’s own data sheet for the property gives the inscribed area as 15.37 hectares and the criteria as an outstanding British fortress and an exceptional, well-preserved example of seventeenth and eighteenth century British military architecture. The fortress’s own site says building began in 1690 and went on, in fits and starts, for just over a century, and that it opens daily from half past nine in the morning until half past five. UNESCO does not soften what it was: it was built by slave labour, to exact standards, and the site says so.

The last railway in the West Indies

The St Kitts Scenic Railway runs on track laid between 1912 and 1926 to carry cane from the estates down to the sugar factory at Basseterre. The operator’s own site describes the tour as eighteen miles by rail and twelve miles by bus, a complete thirty-mile circle of the island, roughly two hours on the train and three quarters of an hour on the coach, and it sometimes runs the other way round as bus-then-train. The sugar industry that paid for the line closed in 2005. The railway outlived it.

Two islands you can see across

From the southern tip of St Kitts you can see Nevis across a two-mile channel, and the crossing takes less time than the drive to most airports. That proximity is the trip. You can have the casino, the cruise ships and the beach bars on one island and the quiet on the other, and move between them in an afternoon without packing for a second country.

The plantation inns

Nevis in particular turned its old sugar estates into small hotels rather than knocking them down — stone mills, long verandahs, gardens that have had two hundred years to grow. Several of them have well under twenty rooms. It is a specific kind of holiday and the island knows exactly what it is doing: nobody is pretending these are beach resorts, and the beach is a short drive away when you want it.

Saint Kitts and Nevis at a glance

Money

The Eastern Caribbean dollar, fixed to the US dollar at EC$2.70 to US$1.00 since 7 July 1976 — the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank’s own figure. US dollars are taken almost everywhere; change usually comes back in EC.

Language

English, with a local Creole in everyday speech. No language barrier for English speakers anywhere on either island.

Getting around

Drive on the left, with a temporary local licence the hire firm arranges — EC$62.50 for three months or EC$125 for a year, per the UK Foreign Office. Both islands are small; Nevis is a single ring road.

Flying in

Robert L. Bradshaw International (SKB) serves St Kitts; the port authority gives its runway as 2,321 metres and 317,618 passengers in 2024. Vance W. Amory International (NEV) serves Nevis with a single 1,220-metre runway and regional flights from Antigua, St Maarten and Puerto Rico.

Weather

Warm all year. Drier from about January to April, wetter from July through December, hurricane season June to November. We have not found a monthly temperature table for these islands from a source we can name, so our best-time page describes the months rather than printing numbers.

How long to stay

A week for both islands, and give Nevis at least three nights of it. Four nights is enough for St Kitts alone. A cruise call gives you a single day, which is enough for the fortress or the railway, not both.

The two islands — which part is which

Basseterre and the cruise berth

The capital sits on the south-west coast of St Kitts with the cruise facility, Port Zante, built out into the water on its edge. It is a working Caribbean town rather than a resort: the Circus with its clock, the old Georgian streets, the market, government offices. Most visitors see it either from a ship for a day or on the way through to somewhere else, which is a pity, and our Basseterre page makes the case for a night there.

Frigate Bay and the Strip

Ten minutes south-east of the capital, Frigate Bay is where most of the island’s resort rooms are. It is an isthmus, so it has two sides: the calm Caribbean side with the big beach resort and the casino, and the wilder Atlantic side where the beach bars of the Strip sit on the sand. It is the easiest base on St Kitts for a first visit.

The South East Peninsula

Beyond Frigate Bay the island narrows into a long peninsula of salt ponds and empty bays, with the best swimming water on St Kitts and the view across to Nevis at the end of it. There is very little here — a handful of beach restaurants and one large luxury resort at Christophe Harbour — and that is the appeal.

Nevis

A near-perfect cone of a volcano with a road around the bottom of it and a small capital, Charlestown, on the western side facing St Kitts. Pinney’s Beach runs north from the town. The plantation inns are scattered up the slopes among the trees. You can drive the whole island in under an hour and there is no traffic light on it.

The north of St Kitts

The windward and northern coasts carry the fortress at Brimstone Hill, the old estate villages along the main road, black-sand beaches that are better for walking than swimming, and the rainforest on the slopes of Mount Liamuiga. The railway runs through all of it.

Things to do

Brimstone Hill Fortress

The World Heritage fortress above the west coast, open daily 9.30am to 5.30pm according to its own site. Steep in places and worth the climb for the view to Statia and Saba.

The Scenic Railway

Eighteen miles of cane railway and twelve by bus, a full circle of St Kitts in about three hours. Book it rather than turning up: the schedule follows the ships.

Cross to Nevis for the day

Twenty-five to forty-five minutes on the passenger ferry from Basseterre Pier to Charlestown Pier. Even as a day trip it changes the shape of a week.

The South East Peninsula beaches

Cockleshell, Majors and the bays beyond Frigate Bay, with the Nevis cone across the water. The swimming is the best on St Kitts.

Diving off Nevis

Oualie Beach Resort’s own site says its dive operation has been running since 1985 and is the island’s first and only full scuba outfit, a PADI five-star centre.

Golf on Nevis

The Four Seasons’ own fact sheet gives its Robert Trent Jones II course as eighteen holes, par 71, 6,766 yards, open since February 1991, running up the skirts of the volcano.

When to go

The dry half of the year runs from about January into April and that is the busy, expensive, reliable season — the months the cruise ships come in numbers and the plantation inns fill. July to December is wetter and greener, with the hurricane risk the UK Foreign Office dates from June to November, and it is also when the two big festivals happen. The shoulder weeks either side of the dry season are the Guild’s usual answer: late April and early May, or the first half of December before Sugar Mas builds.

Arriving by ship

Port Zante is the cruise facility at Basseterre, and the St Christopher Air and Sea Ports Authority — the port authority itself — publishes the useful numbers on its own site. The facility is dedicated exclusively to cruise ships, has two piers, and can take up to four cruise ships at once, including Icon-class vessels; pier one now offers shore power and pier two is being fitted for it. It handled 624,494 passengers in 2024. After dredging, the authority’s information service said in 2021 that every pier can berth an Oasis-class ship on any side.

A new terminal is coming. The government’s information service announced a groundbreaking at Port Zante in July 2026 for a purpose-built terminal to support home-porting, with new passenger processing, security and baggage screening and immigration technology. No cost, size or completion date has been published; the only date given is that home-porting preparations remain on schedule for November 2027. Expect construction around the berth in the meantime.

What a day ashore looks like: the berth is on the edge of the capital rather than out of town, so you walk off into Basseterre instead of queueing for a shuttle. Port Zante’s own site describes the centre of town as a five-minute walk and the main streets as within five hundred metres of the port entrance, with duty-free retail, restaurants and bars and a taxi and tour zone directly outside the terminal. We could not confirm that walking time on any government or port authority page, so take it as the port’s own description rather than ours.

One day ashore is one big thing, not two. Brimstone Hill is up the west coast and takes the best part of a morning with the drive. The railway is about three hours and runs its schedule around the ships. The ferry to Nevis is possible but tight and the timetable is not published online, so it is not something to attempt against an all-aboard time. Travelling in your later years

Both islands are easier than most of the eastern Caribbean on this count, with one real exception. Frigate Bay is flat, the big resort there has level ground between the rooms, the pools and the sand, and Basseterre’s main streets are walkable. The ferry between the islands is a step down into a boat from a pier rather than a gangway, and that is worth asking about at the ticket office before you buy.

Brimstone Hill is the exception: it is a fortress on a steep hill and the upper citadel involves real climbing on uneven stone. There is a road most of the way up, so the view is reachable even if the top is not. The scenic railway is the opposite — you sit down for two hours and the island comes past you — and it is the single best thing on St Kitts for anyone who does not want to walk far.

The plantation inns on Nevis are old buildings on sloping ground, often with rooms in separate cottages up a garden path and no lift anywhere. They are charming and they are not step-free. Ring the property and ask exactly how far the room is from the car and how many steps are involved; every one of them will tell you straight. Health care is limited on both islands and serious cases are flown out, so travel insurance that will move you is not optional here.

Getting in

The important change first: everyone visiting Saint Kitts and Nevis now needs an Electronic Travel Authorisation obtained before travel. The UK Foreign Office states it plainly, and so does the US State Department, which adds that without it you may be refused boarding or entry. The government’s own information service says the system launched on 26 May 2025, with a biometric corridor at Robert L. Bradshaw International Airport from 2 June, and names the official portal at knatravelform.kn along with an official mobile app. It has also publicly warned against unofficial sites selling the same thing, which is why that is the only address we name.

Beyond the eTA: the UK Foreign Office says British passport holders need no visa for stays of up to six months, and that your passport must be valid for at least six months after the date you arrive. The US State Department gives American visitors ninety days without a tourist visa, one blank passport page per stamp, and no required vaccinations. A yellow fever certificate is required if you are arriving from a country listed as a transmission risk. Extensions are applied and paid for through the Immigration Department, and overstaying or working without a permit is illegal.

Arriving by sea on your own boat is a separate process: the Foreign Office says to contact the port authority at least twenty-four hours before arrival, complete the immigration form, and contact SCASPA for St Kitts or NASPA for Nevis. Departure tax is usually included in the price of commercial flights, with one exception the Foreign Office names — LIAT flights leaving Robert L. Bradshaw, where you pay it at the Airport Finance Office in cash or by card. Customs rules are strict; declare anything prohibited or dutiable.

Rules differ by nationality and they change without notice. Check what your own government currently says before you book, and apply for the eTA through the official portal rather than through whichever site a search engine puts first.

Fishing here

Saint Kitts and Nevis is a blue-water destination rather than a flats one. The shelf falls away quickly off both islands, which puts wahoo, kingfish, mahi and marlin within a short run of the dock, and the channel between the two islands fishes well on a running tide. What it does not have is the broad hard-bottomed bonefish flats of the Bahamas, so bring the heavy rods rather than the eight-weight. Questions travellers ask about Saint Kitts and Nevis

Do I need a visa for Saint Kitts and Nevis?

Probably not, but you almost certainly need an eTA. The UK Foreign Office advice we read for this page says British passport holders can visit without a visa for up to six months, and the US State Department says American visitors need no tourist visa for stays of ninety days or less. Both say the same thing about the electronic travel authorisation: everyone visiting must obtain one before travelling. Your passport should have at least six months left after the day you arrive. Rules differ by nationality and they change, so check your own government’s current advice before you book.

What is the eTA, and where do I apply for it?

It is an Electronic Travel Authorisation, introduced by the government in May 2025 and applied for online before you travel. It is a pre-arrival permission, not a visa. The government’s own information service names the portal at knatravelform.kn and an official mobile app, and has separately warned travellers against unofficial look-alike sites that charge for the same thing. A fee applies. We are not printing a figure here: the only number we could find was in an eighteen-month-old press release alongside a launch discount that has since expired, and a stale price is worse than none. Check the official portal.

Which island should I stay on?

Both, if you have a week. They are twenty minutes apart by boat and they are not the same holiday. St Kitts is the bigger and busier one: the capital, the cruise berth, the casino and resort strip at Frigate Bay, the scenic railway and the fortress. Nevis is the quiet one, a single volcano in the sea with plantation inns under the trees and almost no crowd anywhere. A common split is three or four nights on St Kitts and the rest on Nevis, and many people wish afterwards they had given Nevis the longer half.

How do you get between St Kitts and Nevis?

By ferry, and it is easy. The Nevis Tourism Authority’s own pages give the passenger crossing between Basseterre Pier on St Kitts and Charlestown Pier on Nevis as twenty-five to forty-five minutes. There are also two car ferries, Sea Bridge and iConnect, and private water taxis that run between Reggae Beach or the Park Hyatt on St Kitts and Oualie Bay on Nevis. No timetable is published on the tourism authority’s site, which points travellers instead at local apps and listings, so confirm sailings locally rather than planning a tight connection around them.

Is Saint Kitts and Nevis a good cruise stop?

It is one of the better ones in the eastern Caribbean, mostly because of where the berth is. Port Zante sits on the edge of Basseterre rather than out of town, so you step off into the capital instead of onto a shuttle bus. The port authority says the facility has two piers and can take four cruise ships at once, including Icon-class vessels, and that it handled 624,494 passengers in 2024. Our page on arriving by ship, below, has what a day ashore actually looks like.

What is the money, and will US dollars work?

The currency is the Eastern Caribbean dollar. The Eastern Caribbean Central Bank, which issues it, states that it has been fixed to the US dollar at EC$2.70 to US$1.00 since 7 July 1976 — so this is a genuine peg rather than a floating rate that happens to be stable. Hotels, car hire and tours will quote and take US dollars. Small restaurants, buses and market stalls want local cash, and you will usually get change in EC dollars whatever you pay with.

Can I drive, and which side of the road?

You drive on the left, which the Nevis Tourism Authority states plainly on its own getting-around page. You also need a local permit: a temporary Saint Kitts and Nevis licence, which the hire company normally arranges for you. The UK Foreign Office gives the cost as EC$62.50 for three months or EC$125 for a year. Both islands are small enough that a car is a convenience rather than a necessity, and on Nevis in particular the main road is a single ring you cannot really get lost on.

When is the hurricane season?

June to November, which is what the UK Foreign Office states. That does not mean six months of storms; it means six months in which a storm is possible, with the risk concentrated in the second half. It overlaps almost exactly with the cheaper, quieter, greener half of the year, so the sensible approach is not to avoid those months outright but to book flexible rates and buy travel insurance that covers weather.
