Ambergris Caye

Where to Stay on Ambergris Caye

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Ambergris Caye is the largest of Belize’s islands and takes more visitors than the rest of the country put together. San Pedro, its one town, sits at the southern end with the airstrip running through it and the reef a few hundred metres offshore — close enough that you can hear it break on a quiet night. Everything here is organised around that reef. The island itself is long, narrow and flat, the roads are sand, the cars are golf carts, and the single question that shapes a stay is how far from town you are willing to be.

Where to stay on Ambergris Caye, area by area

The island runs roughly twenty-five miles north from the town of San Pedro, and where you sleep on it decides what your day looks like far more than the hotel does. The question is not really which hotel — it is how far from town you want to be, and whether you are content to cross the bridge.

San Pedro town

The town itself is three sandy-concrete streets running parallel to the sea, with the airstrip at one end and the bridge at the other. Staying here means you walk to dinner, walk to the bank, walk to the dive shop and walk home, and you never pay for a taxi or wait for a cart. It also means golf-cart traffic outside the window, music from the bars on Barrier Reef Drive until late at the weekend, and buildings close enough together that few rooms have much of a view. For a first visit, for anyone travelling alone, and for anyone who likes to be able to change their mind about dinner at seven o’clock, town is the right answer.

South of town

The road south runs several miles past a more or less continuous line of resorts, condominium blocks and restaurants, thinning as it goes. This is where most of the larger properties with pools and sea frontage are, and the further south you go the quieter and greener it becomes. A mile or two out you are five minutes by cart from town — close enough to go in for dinner, far enough to sleep. Four or five miles out, the trip into town becomes a decision rather than an impulse, and you will want your own cart or a hotel that runs a shuttle. The south also has the advantage of being the end of the island closest to Hol Chan.

North of the bridge

The toll bridge over the river cut at the top of town is the dividing line on Ambergris Caye, and crossing it changes the holiday. North is quieter, emptier and more beautiful, with long stretches of beach between properties and genuine darkness at night. It is also committed: the road deteriorates as it goes, carts take a long time, water taxis run to a timetable, and the further north you are the more you will eat at your own hotel because there is nothing else within reach. For a honeymoon, a week of reading or a family that wants space, the north is the best thing on the island. For a short stay, or if you want choice at dinner, it is a mistake.

What the beach is actually like

This is the thing Ambergris Caye is most often misunderstood about, and it is worth being plain. The shallows along most of the island are sea grass rather than clean white sand, which is exactly why the water is clear and the fish are there, and it is protected. Many hotels therefore swim from a pier rather than off the beach, and the ones that advertise swimmable frontage have usually built a cleared area or a dock to a deeper spot. The sand is real and pleasant to sit on; the wade-in-from-the-shore picture is not generally the reality. Ask any hotel directly how you get into the water.

Getting around Ambergris Caye

Getting here

Two ways. The flight from Belize City takes about a quarter of an hour in a small aircraft, lands at the airstrip in the middle of town, and is the easy option; both domestic carriers fly it many times a day from the international airport and the municipal strip. The water taxi takes an hour and a half or so from Belize City, costs far less, and is a pleasant trip in calm weather and a wet, bouncy one when it is not. With luggage, with children or with anyone who is unsteady, fly.

Golf carts

The golf cart is the island car. You can hire one by the day or the week, and almost every visitor staying outside town ends up doing so. They are slow, open-sided and easy to get in and out of, but the roads are rough sand and concrete and the ride is bumpy, particularly north of the bridge. A driving licence is required. If you are in town, you will probably not need one at all.

On foot

San Pedro town is entirely walkable end to end in twenty minutes, and most visitors who stay in town never use anything else. The streets are flat but the surface is uneven in places and there are few kerbs.

Taxis and water taxis

Shared cart taxis work the town and the near south. North of the bridge, a scheduled water taxi runs up and down the coast calling at the resort docks, which for the far north is often faster and more comfortable than the road.

When to come, and what it costs

Ambergris Caye follows the Belizean year: dry and expensive from about December to April, green and cheaper from June to November, with September and October the wettest and the quietest, when some businesses close for maintenance. Christmas, New Year and Easter are the dearest and busiest stretches by a wide margin, and the better hotels need booking many months ahead for them.

Two island-specific things to weigh. Northers — winter cold fronts — can bring a day or two of wind and chop in January and February that cancels boat trips, even in the best season. And sargassum, when it arrives, generally falls between about March and August and affects the windward shore most. The fuller picture is in Best Time to Visit Belize.

Getting in: what changed, and what has not

Most visitors from the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, the European Union and much of the Commonwealth may enter Belize as tourists without arranging a visa in advance, and are admitted for a limited stay that can usually be extended at an immigration office for a fee. A passport with sufficient remaining validity, onward or return travel, and an address for the first night are the ordinary requirements.

Beyond that we do not print the numbers, and nor should anyone else. Entry rules, the length of stay granted, and the departure and border fees are exactly the details that change without notice. Check the current position with the Belize Immigration and Nationality Department and with your own government’s travel advice shortly before you fly.

Ambergris Caye — questions

Should I stay in San Pedro town or outside it?

In town if this is a first visit, a short visit, or you want to walk to dinner and have choices; the trade is noise and little sea view. South of town if you want a pool, sea frontage and a quieter night but still want to reach restaurants in five or ten minutes by cart. North of the bridge if you want space, silence and beach, and are content to eat mostly at your hotel and to treat going into town as an outing. The bridge, not the hotel, is the real decision.

Do I need to hire a golf cart?

Not if you are staying in town, where everything is within a twenty-minute walk. Almost certainly yes if you are staying any distance south, and yes if you are north of the bridge, though north of the bridge the scheduled water taxi along the coast is often the better way to move. Carts are hired by the day or the week and you will need a driving licence.

Is there a proper swimming beach?

Not in the way the word is usually meant. The shallows along most of the island are protected sea grass, and most hotels put you into the water from a pier or a cleared swimming area rather than letting you wade in from the sand. The sand itself is genuine and pleasant to sit on. This is not a flaw in any particular hotel; it is the island, and it is the same thing that makes the snorkelling so good. Ask your hotel how you get into the water before you book.

How do I get to Hol Chan and Shark Ray Alley?

By boat with a licensed operator, from San Pedro or from one of the resort docks. The marine reserve is at the south end of the island, so a south-end hotel is slightly closer, though the difference is minutes. It is a half-day trip, it works for confident swimmers who have never dived, and the earlier boats are generally the quieter ones. Entry to the reserve carries a park fee, usually collected by the operator.

Can I get to Caye Caulker for the day?

Easily. The water taxi between the two islands takes around half an hour and runs several times a day, so a day trip in either direction is straightforward. A good many visitors split their stay instead, which costs nothing extra and gives you both versions of the northern cayes.

Is there a hospital?

San Pedro has a polyclinic and private medical clinics, and there are pharmacies in town. It is much the best-equipped of the Belizean cayes for this. Anything serious is flown to Belize City or out of the country, which is the reason to hold travel insurance that explicitly covers medical evacuation.
