Lake Fishing in the Carolinas — Six Great Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Santee Cooper, Lake Murray, Lake Norman, Fontana, Lake Hartwell and Jordan Lake: what swims in each, the rules from the North Carolina Wildlife Resources Commission and the South Carolina Department of Natural Resources, the licence a visitor needs, where to put a boat in, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the NC Wildlife Resources Commission and SC DNR, 14 November 2026

The two Carolinas share a long border and some of the best warm-water lake fishing in the East. In the lowcountry of South Carolina, Lake Marion and Lake Moultrie — together called Santee Cooper — are famous for giant blue catfish and for striped bass that live their whole lives in fresh water. Lake Murray, beside Columbia, is a striper lake too. Up in the North Carolina Piedmont, Lake Norman is the state’s biggest man-made lake and Jordan Lake is the Triangle’s crappie water. Far to the west, Fontana Lake lies deep and clear under the Great Smoky Mountains. And on the Georgia line, Lake Hartwell is one of the South’s great striper and bass lakes.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, bass and crappie anglers towing a boat, catfish and striper fishermen who want a guide, and anyone choosing which lake town to stay in. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 14 November 2026 from the agency that sets it — mainly the North Carolina Wildlife Resources Commission (NCWRC) and the South Carolina Department of Natural Resources (SCDNR) — and is dated. Two states means two licences: the lake you choose decides which one you buy. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The South: long hot summers and mild winters. Spring and autumn are the times to come; in summer, fish at first light.

Santee Cooper — Lake Marion and Lake Moultrie

Two lakes joined by a canal on the Santee River, between Columbia and Charleston. Santee Cooper, the state utility that maintains them, gives Lake Marion as 110,000 acres and Lake Moultrie as 60,000 acres. Marion is the largest lake in South Carolina.

Lake Marion and Lake Moultrie, from Santee to Moncks Corner

What swims there. Blue, channel and flathead catfish — the blues grow very large here — plus striped bass that live and spawn in fresh water, largemouth bass, crappie and bream. Lake Marion’s upper end is flooded swamp and cypress; Moultrie is rounder and more open.

How people fish it. Drifting or anchoring with cut bait for catfish, often with a guide; trolling or live-baiting for stripers in season; casting for bass in the cypress and the creeks; crappie on brush and bridge pilings.

Keeping a fish. Striped bass: closed 16 June to 30 September. From 1 October to 15 June, three a day, and they must be between 23 and 25 inches long, except that one may be longer than 26 inches. Blue catfish: 25 a day, and no more than two over 32 inches. Largemouth bass: 14-inch minimum here. Crappie: 20 a day at 8 inches.

Confirmed with the SC Department of Natural Resources, 14 November 2026 — 2026–27 freshwater limits: Santee River system (including Lakes Marion and Moultrie) striped bass open 1 Oct–15 June, limit 3, 23–25 inches except one over 26 inches; closed 16 June–30 Sept. Nongame rules: blue catfish 25 a day on Marion, Moultrie and the upper Santee, no more than 2 over 32 inches. Largemouth 14-inch minimum on Marion and Moultrie. Seasonal closure: on Lake Marion, all boats and fishing are banned from 1 November to 28 February in Cantey Bay, Black Bottom and Savannah Branch. On Lake Moultrie, the Hatchery WMA is closed to fishing on Saturdays until noon.

Boat ramps and hire. There are public landings all round both lakes, and Santee State Park sits on Lake Marion near the town of Santee. We did not check a full ramp list today — Santee Cooper publishes its lake-access maps. The guide services below are on the water all year.

Nearest towns: Santee, right off I-95 on Lake Marion; Summerton and Manning, on the north shore; Moncks Corner, for Lake Moultrie and the canals.

Lake Murray — stripers beside the capital

The Saluda River, dammed west of Columbia and run by Dominion Energy for its Saluda hydro project. SC State Parks calls it the “50,000-acre Lake Murray”.

Lake Murray, from Chapin to Lexington and Irmo

What swims there. Striped bass are the headline, with largemouth bass, crappie, catfish and bream.

How people fish it. Stripers with live bait or by watching for schools chasing bait on top, usually from a boat and often with a guide; bass on docks, points and grass; crappie on brush piles and bridges.

Keeping a fish. Striped and hybrid bass, together: from 1 October to 31 May, five a day, each at least 21 inches. From 1 June to 30 September, still five a day, but any length. Largemouth bass: 14-inch minimum here.

Confirmed with the SC Department of Natural Resources, 14 November 2026 — 2026–27 freshwater limits, Lake Murray: striped/hybrid bass 21-inch minimum, 5 a day, 1 Oct–31 May; any length, 5 a day, 1 June–30 Sept. Largemouth 14-inch minimum on Lake Murray.

Boat ramps and hire. Dreher Island State Park, three islands on the lake near Chapin, has three boat ramps; admission is $6 for adults, $3.75 for South Carolina seniors and $3.50 for ages 6 to 15 (SC State Parks). Dominion Energy’s Irmo Side boat-launch area charges $5 a day or $50 a season, paid online — no cash (Dominion Energy). Lighthouse Marina, Lake Murray Boat Rentals and the striper guides are below.

Water level. Lighthouse Marina’s website mentions a drawdown of Lake Murray announced by Dominion Energy. We did not confirm its dates; check Dominion Energy’s lake pages before you tow a boat.

Nearest towns: Lexington, on the south shore; Chapin, by the upper lake and Dreher Island; Irmo, near the dam; Columbia, the capital, for a city base.

Lake Norman — North Carolina’s inland sea

The Catawba River, dammed at Cowans Ford by Duke Energy, which began the dam in 1959. NC State Parks calls it the largest man-made lake in North Carolina, with 520 miles of shoreline; the NC Department of Natural and Cultural Resources gives “more than 32,000 acres”.

Lake Norman, from Mooresville to Cornelius and Huntersville

What swims there. Largemouth and spotted bass, striped bass and hybrids, catfish (including big blues), crappie and white perch — the species the local guides below fish for.

How people fish it. Bass on docks and points; stripers and hybrids from a boat with live bait or by following the birds; catfish from a drifting pontoon; crappie under the bridges and on brush.

Keeping a fish. Crappie: 20 a day, 8-inch minimum. Striped bass and hybrids together: 4 a day, 20-inch minimum. Largemouth and smallmouth bass: 5 a day, 14-inch minimum, except that 2 of the 5 may be under 14 inches. Spotted bass: no size or creel limit. Blue catfish: only one over 32 inches a day.

Confirmed with the NC Wildlife Resources Commission, 14 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations Digest: Lake Norman is listed among waters with crappie 8-inch minimum, 20 a day; inland striped and hybrid bass 20-inch minimum, 4 a day (Lake Norman has no exception — confirmed in NCWRC’s 2026 Lake Norman striped bass report); black bass statewide rule; blue catfish only one greater than 32 inches on Lake Norman.

Boat ramps and hire. Lake Norman State Park has a boat launch with 24-hour access through its St. Johns Road entrance, and day use is free (NC State Parks). There are more public access areas round the lake; we did not find an official list of them today. Lake Norman Boat Rentals and two fishing charters are below.

Nearest towns: Mooresville, on the east shore; Cornelius, at the south end; Huntersville, near Cowans Ford Dam.

Fontana Lake — deep water under the Smokies

The Little Tennessee River, held back by the Tennessee Valley Authority’s 480-foot Fontana Dam. TVA gives 10,230 acres and 238 miles of shoreline; Great Smoky Mountains National Park forms the whole north shore, and the Appalachian Trail crosses the dam.

Fontana Lake, from Bryson City to Fontana Dam

What swims there. Walleye, which NCWRC stocks every year; smallmouth bass, which the National Park Service says gather at the inlets of Eagle, Hazel and Noland creeks; plus largemouth bass and, on a fly in the cold months, brown and rainbow trout.

How people fish it. Mostly from a boat — the shore is steep and much of it is national park with no road. Trolling or jigging for walleye; casting for smallmouth on rocky points and at the creek mouths; fly-fishing with a guide in late autumn and winter.

Keeping a fish. Fontana follows North Carolina’s statewide rules: walleye 8 a day, no minimum length; black bass 5 a day at 14 inches, with 2 allowed under 14. If you walk up a park stream from the lake, the National Park Service’s rules apply instead: one rod, artificial flies or lures with a single hook, no bait; trout and smallmouth at least 7 inches, five a day combined. A North Carolina or Tennessee licence is valid in the park, no trout stamp is needed, and licences are not sold in the park.

Confirmed with the NC Wildlife Resources Commission, 14 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations Digest: walleye statewide no minimum, 8 a day (Fontana not among the exceptions); black bass statewide 14 inches, 5 a day, 2 may be under 14. NCWRC: walleye stocked in Fontana yearly. National Park Service (Great Smoky Mountains fishing page): licence, gear, size and creel rules quoted above. TVA: 10,230 acres, 238 miles of shoreline.

Boat ramps and hire. Fontana Village Resort & Marina, at the dam end, rents pontoons (fishing is allowed on its deluxe pontoon) and kayaks; Almond Boat & RV Park, near Bryson City, rents pontoons and boats and sells bait and fishing licences. Both are below. Winter hours are short — call first.

Nearest towns: Bryson City, the gateway town on the east end; Fontana Dam, the resort village by the dam.

Lake Hartwell — stripers on the Georgia line

The Savannah, Tugaloo and Seneca rivers, dammed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers between 1955 and 1963. The Corps gives 55,950 acres of water and 962 miles of shoreline — part in South Carolina, part in Georgia.

Lake Hartwell, from Anderson and Clemson to Hartwell, Georgia

What swims there. Striped bass and hybrids, largemouth and spotted bass, crappie and catfish.

How people fish it. Stripers and hybrids with live bait, trolled lures or a fly, year-round, much of it with guides; bass from a bass boat on points, docks and timber; crappie in the coves in spring.

Keeping a fish — and why many people don’t. Striped and hybrid bass: 10 a day, only 3 over 26 inches. Black bass: 10 a day combined, largemouth at least 12 inches. But read South Carolina’s fish advisory first: because of PCBs, do not eat any fish from Twelve Mile Creek or the Seneca River arm, and do not eat any striped or hybrid bass from the rest of the lake. Channel catfish, largemouth and spotted bass: no more than one meal a month. Hartwell is a lake for catch-and-release.

Confirmed with the SC Department of Natural Resources, 14 November 2026 — 2026–27 freshwater limits: Lakes Hartwell and Thurmond striped/hybrid bass 10 a day, only 3 over 26 inches; Hartwell tailwater 2 a day, only 1 over 34 inches; black bass 10 combined, largemouth 12-inch minimum. Reciprocal licence agreement with Georgia (SCDNR regulations; Georgia’s version also read). SC Department of Environmental Services, Lake Hartwell fish consumption advisory, read the same day.

Licence. A valid South Carolina or Georgia fishing licence covers the whole lake under the two states’ reciprocal agreement — but not the tributary streams that flow into it. Georgia’s laws apply in Georgia’s waters.

Boat ramps and hire. The Corps runs many parks and ramps round the lake. Portman Marina, near Anderson, has a large launch area and a fuel dock; Big Water Marina, at Starr, rents pontoons. Both are below, with three guide services.

Nearest towns: Anderson, the main South Carolina town; Clemson, on the Seneca River arm; Hartwell, Georgia, by the dam on the Georgia side.

Jordan Lake — the Triangle’s crappie water

B. Everett Jordan Lake, on the Haw and New Hope rivers between Raleigh, Durham and Chapel Hill, run by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers with the State of North Carolina. The Corps gives 13,940 acres and 200 miles of shoreline at normal level.

Jordan Lake, from Apex to Pittsboro

What swims there. Black crappie, largemouth bass and white perch — the fish the local guide below targets.

How people fish it. Crappie from a boat on brush and under the bridges, or from the bank in the recreation areas; bass along the wooded shore and creek arms.

Keeping a fish. Crappie: 20 a day, 8-inch minimum. Striped bass and hybrids together: 4 a day, 20-inch minimum. Largemouth bass: 5 a day at 14 inches, 2 may be under 14. NC Health and Human Services lists no advisory for Jordan Lake itself, but its statewide mercury advice applies: women aged 15 to 44, pregnant or nursing women and children under 15 should not eat largemouth bass from any North Carolina water, and everyone else should keep to one meal a week.

Confirmed with the NC Wildlife Resources Commission, 14 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations Digest: B. Everett Jordan Reservoir crappie 8-inch minimum, 20 a day; inland striped/hybrid bass 20 inches, 4 a day; black bass statewide rule. NC State Parks: ramps and fees as below. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (Wilmington District): 13,940 acres at normal pool.

Boat ramps and hire. Jordan Lake State Recreation Area has ramps at New Hope Overlook (open all year), Ebenezer Church (24-hour ramp), Seaforth and Vista Point (open 2 March to 30 November in 2026). Entry is $10 a vehicle ($5 for seniors 62 and over and for military and veterans) on weekends and holidays in April, May and September and every day from Memorial Day to Labor Day; the rest of the year it is free (NC State Parks). Crosswinds Boating Center rents boats, but says all rentals are closed until spring 2027.

Nearest towns: Apex, on the east side; Pittsboro, on the west; Cary, for a bigger choice of hotels.

The licences and the limits, plainly

Two states, two agencies, two licences — and on both sides, children under 16 fish free.

North Carolina fishing licences, 2026–27 prices

Who needs one. Everyone 16 and older. Youth under 16 need no fishing licence. On 4 July each year, Free Fishing Day, no licence is needed in any public water.

For a visitor. Nonresident annual inland fishing licence $56; nonresident 10-day inland licence $29. The inland licence includes public mountain trout waters.

For a North Carolinian. State inland fishing $32 a year; 10-day $12; unified inland and coastal $51.

Where to buy. Online at gooutdoorsnorthcarolina.com, by phone on 833-950-0575, or from a wildlife service agent. A $5 transaction fee is added to each order.

Confirmed with the NC Wildlife Resources Commission, 14 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations Digest, licence pages: resident inland $32, 10-day $12, unified $51; nonresident inland $56, 10-day $29; youth under 16 exempt; inland licence includes public mountain trout waters; $5 transaction fee; phone 833-950-0575; Free Fishing Day 4 July. Prices change. Buy from NCWRC or a licensed agent.

South Carolina fishing licences, 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027

Who needs one. Everyone 16 and older; under 16, no licence.

For a visitor. Nonresident freshwater licence $35 a year or $11 for 14 days (there is no 7-day freshwater licence); 3-year $105.

For a South Carolinian. Freshwater $10 a year, $5 for 14 days, $30 for three years. Residents 64 and over can buy a senior licence for $9.

Where to buy. Online at gooutdoorssouthcarolina.com, in the Go Outdoors SC app, from a licence vendor or at an SCDNR office.

Confirmed with the SC Department of Natural Resources, 14 November 2026 — SCDNR resident and nonresident licence-price pages and the 2026–27 regulations: the prices above; “No hunting or fishing license or permit required until person reaches age 16.” Prices change. Buy from SCDNR or a licensed vendor.

Boats and life jackets

North Carolina: anyone born on or after 1 January 1988 must have passed an approved boating course to run a boat with a motor of 10 horsepower or more, and children under 13 must wear a life jacket on a recreational boat that is under way (NCWRC). South Carolina: anyone born after 1 July 2007 needs an approved boating course to run a boat of 10 horsepower or more (renters may use a 30-day rental certificate), and anyone under 12 must wear a life jacket on a boat under 16 feet (SCDNR).

Eating what you keep

Both states publish fish advice by lake. Lake Hartwell has the strictest (above). Lake Norman: hybrid striped bass, no more than two meals a week, because of PCBs (NC DHHS). Fontana: pregnant women, women who may become pregnant and children under 15 should not eat walleye; everyone else, one meal a week, because of mercury (NC DHHS). Santee Cooper: South Carolina’s advisory table lists bowfin and Lake Marion largemouth bass at one meal a week, and most other species with no limit — but the table we read carries no date, so check it before a fish fry. We found no Lake Murray entry in that table.

Questions

Do I need a fishing licence in North Carolina and South Carolina, and what does it cost a visitor?

Yes, in each state, from age 16. Anglers under 16 need no licence in either state. In North Carolina a nonresident inland fishing licence is $56 a year or $29 for ten days (2026-27 NC Wildlife Resources Commission regulations), plus a $5 transaction fee per order. In South Carolina a nonresident freshwater licence is $35 a year or $11 for 14 days (SC Department of Natural Resources, in effect 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027). Residents pay less. Buy at gooutdoorsnorthcarolina.com or gooutdoorssouthcarolina.com.

Which licence do I need for Lake Hartwell?

Lake Hartwell sits on the South Carolina-Georgia line, and the two states have a reciprocal agreement: a valid South Carolina or Georgia fishing licence lets you fish the whole lake. The agreement does not cover the tributary streams that run into it, so once you go up a creek you need the licence of the state you are standing in, and Georgia's own laws apply in Georgia's waters.

When can I keep striped bass on Santee Cooper?

On Lake Marion and Lake Moultrie, striped bass may be kept only from 1 October to 15 June; the season is closed from 16 June to 30 September. The daily limit is three fish, which must be between 23 and 25 inches long, except that one of them may be longer than 26 inches. That is the Santee River system rule in the SC Department of Natural Resources' 2026-27 regulations.

What are the crappie limits on Lake Norman and Jordan Lake?

Twenty crappie a day, each at least 8 inches long. The NC Wildlife Resources Commission lists both B. Everett Jordan Reservoir and Lake Norman among the waters with that rule. On many other North Carolina waters there is no crappie size or creel limit at all, so check the list for each lake.

Is it safe to eat the fish from Lake Hartwell?

Not all of them. South Carolina's fish consumption advisory for Lake Hartwell, which is about PCBs, says do not eat any fish of any kind from Twelve Mile Creek and the Seneca River arm, and do not eat any striped or hybrid bass from the rest of the lake. Channel catfish, largemouth and spotted bass are limited to one meal a month. Hartwell is a lake to fish catch-and-release, or to read the advisory before you keep anything.

What we could not check today, and will not pretend to have

The part of a fishing page most sites leave out.

Six honest gaps

Lake levels and ramps. Santee Cooper, Dominion Energy, Duke Energy, TVA and the Corps of Engineers run these lakes, and levels change with the rain and with drawdowns. A marina on Lake Murray mentions a Dominion Energy drawdown we could not date. Check the lake’s operator before you tow a boat.

Ramp lists. We checked state-park ramps on Murray, Norman and Jordan, but not every public landing on Santee Cooper, Lake Norman, Fontana or Hartwell.

South Carolina free fishing days. We found no official SCDNR page with 2026 or 2027 dates, so we give none. North Carolina’s Free Fishing Day is 4 July every year.

Some catfish limits. We did not find South Carolina’s channel and flathead catfish limits on the pages we read; check SCDNR before you keep them.

The Santee Cooper fish advisory. South Carolina’s advisory table carries no “current as of” date. Read the newest one from the SC Department of Environmental Services.

Fishing guides. Every lake has more guides than we list, but we name only those whose own website we could open today.
