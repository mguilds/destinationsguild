Negombo

Where to Stay in Negombo

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Negombo is the town next to the airport, and that is the honest reason nearly every visitor stays here: it is ten to twenty-five minutes from the terminal, against an hour or more into Colombo, which makes it the sensible first and last night of a Sri Lankan trip. It is not the best beach on the island and nobody should plan a holiday around it. What it is, is a real working fishing town with a long brown-sand beach, a lagoon full of outrigger boats, an old Dutch canal and four hundred years of Catholic history — and a gentle place to arrive jet-lagged or to spend a last afternoon.

The beach strip at Ethukala and Lewis Place — where almost everyone stays

Most hotelsWalkable dinner15-25 min from the airport

Nearly all of Negombo’s visitor accommodation is on the two or three kilometres of beach road running north from the town through Lewis Place and up into Ethukala. It is a straightforward strip: hotels and guest houses on the sea side, restaurants, tour desks and small shops on the other, and a wide beach of grey-brown sand behind them. You can walk out to dinner, which is the main argument for it on a first night, and you can be at the airport in fifteen to twenty-five minutes. The northern Ethukala end is generally quieter and newer; the Lewis Place end is cheaper, busier and closer to town.

Negombo town and the lagoon — the real place, not a beach base

Fish marketCheapestLocal

A couple of kilometres south, the town itself is where Negombo is actually interesting. The lagoon mouth holds the fishing fleet and the lellama fish market, which at dawn is one of the best hours in the district; the old Dutch canal runs north behind the beach; and the town is unusually Catholic even for this coast, with St Mary’s and a dozen other churches and a Good Friday worth travelling for. Rooms here are the cheapest in Negombo and entirely ordinary. Stay here if you want the town; stay on the strip if you want to walk to the sea.

Waikkal and the quiet north — river mouth, fewer people

QuietestSmarter20-30 min north

Fifteen or twenty kilometres up the coast, around Waikkal and the Maha Oya river mouth, is a quieter and generally smarter run of hotels set in gardens between the river and the sea. It is still close enough to the airport to be practical, half an hour or so, and it is a considerably more restful place to lose a day to jet lag than the beach road. The trade-off is that there is nothing to walk to: you eat where you stay, and a trip into Negombo or out to the fish market is a drive.

Katunayake, beside the airport — for a four in the morning flight only

Transit onlyMinutes from the terminalNothing to walk to

There are transit hotels within a few minutes of the terminal at Katunayake, and they have exactly one purpose: a very early departure or a very late arrival where even twenty minutes of driving is unwelcome. Some offer day rooms and short stays. Do not book one for any other reason — there is no beach, no town and nowhere to eat beyond the hotel itself. For almost everybody the beach strip is a better first night for the same money and a quarter of an hour more in the car.

Getting around

The one number that matters: Bandaranaike International Airport is about ten kilometres from the Negombo beach strip, which is fifteen to twenty-five minutes by road and rarely more. The fixed-price taxi counters in arrivals are straightforward and a hotel pick-up is easy to arrange in advance; either is a good idea on a first arrival at two in the morning.

In town, tuk-tuks do everything, and the ride between the strip and the fish market costs very little — agree it first or use an app. Bicycles are a genuinely good option here because the coast is flat and the canal road is quiet, and most hotels have them.

Colombo is 35 to 40 kilometres south: an hour by car in light traffic and up to two in the morning peak. There is a railway line, and the train is cheap and slow at around an hour and a half to two hours. This is also the usual place to meet a hired car and driver on day one, since nearly every itinerary heads north-east to the Cultural Triangle or south past Colombo to the coast from here.

When to come

Negombo is on the west coast and shares Colombo’s year. December to April is the dry season: hot, bright and the best time for the beach, with calmer water and reliable sunshine. February and March are the pick.

May to September is the southwest monsoon. The pattern is bright mornings and heavy afternoon rain rather than constant drizzle, but the sea turns rough and the beach narrows, and swimming can be poor. October and November are the inter-monsoon weeks and the most thundery and unsettled of the year.

None of this should change your plans very much, because the reason to be in Negombo is the flight rather than the weather. It matters more for where you go next: our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Sri Lanka sets out which coast is dry when, and in the monsoon months the answer is usually the east.

Getting in

Most visitors need an Electronic Travel Authorisation, the ETA, arranged before you fly, and airlines check it. Sri Lanka has revised its entry rules, its fees and the list of nationalities more than once in recent years, including spells when some passports were admitted free of charge, so we deliberately do not print a figure or a list here — a number that is out of date is worse than no number. Read it off the Department of Immigration and Emigration’s own portal at immigration.gov.lk at the moment you book, and apply there rather than through one of the look-alike sites that charge a mark-up for filling in the same form. Your passport should have at least six months left on it and the officer may ask to see an onward ticket.

Two practical notes for the first hour. Draw rupees or change a little money at the airport, because tuk-tuks, markets and small restaurants want cash and small notes are useful. And remember the airport is not in Colombo: Bandaranaike International is at Katunayake, about 35 kilometres north of the capital and some ten minutes from Negombo, which is why so many itineraries begin and end there. Negombo hotels FAQ

Is Negombo a good place to start a trip?

Yes, and that is its real job. It is fifteen to twenty-five minutes from the airport against an hour or more into Colombo, there is a walkable row of restaurants on the beach road, and it is an undemanding place to sleep off a long flight before meeting a driver the next morning. Most itineraries use it for the first night, the last night, or both.

How far is the airport?

About ten kilometres from the beach strip, a fifteen to twenty-five minute drive that is seldom affected by traffic. The fixed-price taxi desks inside arrivals are simple to use, and a pre-arranged hotel transfer is worth it if you are landing in the small hours, which many long-haul flights do.

Is the beach any good?

It is adequate rather than good, and you should not come to Sri Lanka for it. The sand is wide but grey-brown, the sea is open and can be rough, and parts of the strip have erosion and sea walls. From December to April it is perfectly pleasant for a swim and a sunset; in the monsoon months it is not the point. The south and east coasts are where the beaches are.

What is actually worth doing here?

The fish market at the lagoon mouth at first light, which is a genuine spectacle and free; the fishing fleet coming in; a bicycle along the old Dutch canal; and the churches, if the Catholic south-west interests you. A lagoon boat trip and a day's sea fishing are both easy to arrange. It is half a day of real interest rather than two.

Should I stay here or in Colombo?

Negombo for the airport, Colombo for the city. If your flight is the fixed point, arriving late, leaving early, or meeting a driver at the start of a tour, stay in Negombo and save yourself two hours in the car. If you want the museums, the markets and the best eating on the island, spend the night in Colombo and accept the drive.
