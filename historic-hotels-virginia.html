Historic Hotels of Virginia

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

18 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 18 in Virginia, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1766

The Omni Homestead Resort & Spa

Hot Springs, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 1989

Captain Bullitt received a land grant here in 1764 for his service in the French and Indian War and raised a log hotel beside the mineral springs. Thomas Jefferson came for the waters in 1818. Dr. Thomas Goode bought the springs in 1832 and introduced European spa treatments, and later M.E. Ingalls, backed by investors including J.P. Morgan, rebuilt the resort after a disaster in 1901. The red brick Tower by architect Charles D. Wetmore went up in 1929. Henry Ford, John D. Rockefeller and Edward VIII stayed.

Est. 1778

Inn at Willow Grove

Orange, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2017

Land surveyor Joseph Clark built this farmhouse in Orange County in 1778; he later surveyed Montpelier for the widowed Dolley Madison before its sale. Around the middle of the 1800s the Clarks added brick walls, Jeffersonian columns and porches, using craftsmen who had worked on Jefferson's University of Virginia. Civil War trenches were dug nearby, and a cannonball was once found lodged in the eaves. David and Charlene Scibal bought it in 2008 and restored it, keeping the old schoolhouse, smokehouse, weaving house and spring house.

Close by

  • Montpelier, home of James and Dolley Madison
  • Exchange Hotel Civil War Medical Museum
  • The Rapidan River in Shenandoah National Park

Est. 1828

The Blackburn Inn and Conference Center

Staunton, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2018

In 1828 the state opened Western State Hospital on 80 acres in Staunton. Its superintendent, Dr. Stribling, believed in caring for patients' emotional health, and in the 1830s he brought in architect Thomas R. Blackburn, who had learned his trade from Thomas Jefferson, to enlarge it with new wings, gardens and a cupola, finished in 1836. A century later the hospital became a prison, which closed in the early 2000s. Developers bought the empty buildings in 2006 and opened them as an inn named for the architect in 2018.

Close by

  • Woodrow Wilson Presidential Library and Museum
  • The Frontier Culture Museum
  • Blackfriars Playhouse of the American Shakespeare Center

Est. 1834

Boar’s Head Resort and Birdwood Mansion

Charlottesville, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2001

A colonial tavern called Terrell's Ordinary once served pioneers heading for the Blue Ridge here. William Garth bought the Birdwood estate in 1819 and built its central manor in the manner of Jefferson's builders, and in 1865 General George Armstrong Custer halted Union scouts before they wrecked the whole place. In 1959 John Rogan and John Rhea began an inn next door, saving timbers from Birdwood's 1830s gristmill for its restaurant. Boar's Head Inn opened in 1964; the University of Virginia's real estate foundation bought it in the 1980s.

  • The University of Virginia's Academical Village
  • The Fralin Museum of Art

Est. 1885

Hilton Richmond Downtown

Richmond, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2024

In 1885 Linton Miller, Webster Rhoads and Simon Gerhart, all from Pennsylvania, started a dry-goods business in Richmond and soon moved it to Broad Street. As Miller & Rhoads it grew into a department store filling a whole city block, and in 1933 Starrett & VanVleck gave it the Art Deco front still seen today. Shoppers came from across the Mid-Atlantic, and its top-floor Tea Room served a Miller & Rhoads chocolate cake. The chain went bankrupt in 1990; the flagship reopened as this hotel in 2006.

Close by

  • Maymont, a Gilded Age mansion and gardens
  • Lewis Ginter Botanical Garden
  • The American Civil War Museum

Est. 1895

The Jefferson Hotel

Richmond, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 1989

Lewis Ginter spent close to $2 million on three years of building, and The Jefferson opened on Halloween of 1895. A disaster in 1901 damaged three-fifths of it, and a citizens' group led by Lieutenant Governor Joseph Willard reopened it in 1907 with its grand staircase opened up. Live alligators lived in the Palm Court fountains until 1948. Closed by 1980, it reopened in 1986 after a $34 million restoration. Thirteen presidents have stayed, along with F. Scott Fitzgerald, Charles Lindbergh and Sarah Bernhardt.

Close by

  • Virginia Museum of Fine Arts and its Fabergé collection
  • Lewis Ginter Botanical Garden, named for the founder
  • The Byrd Theater in Carytown

Est. 1899

Airlie

Warrenton, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2015

Harry Connelly Groom, who had served in the U.S. Customs Office under Grover Cleveland, finished a Georgian Revival manor north of Warrenton in 1899 and named it Airlie House after a Scottish castle. In 1959 Dr. Murdoch Head bought the estate and reopened it in 1961 as the Airlie Center, a neutral meeting ground Life Magazine called an "Island of Thought." Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. attended meetings here, and Senator Gaylord Nelson's 1969 conference at Airlie launched the idea of Earth Day.

Close by

  • Manassas National Battlefield Park
  • The Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center
  • Old Town Alexandria's red brick streets

Est. 1912

Keswick Hall

Keswick, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2015

Robert and Florence Crawford asked architect Eugene Bradbury for a house like an Italian palace, and Villa Crawford, a two-story stucco mansion under a slate roof, was finished in 1912. After World War II it became the Keswick Country Club, with a golf course by Fred Findlay that hosted the Virginia State Open in 1969 and 1970. The club faded in the 1980s. Sir Bernard Ashley, widower of designer Laura Ashley, revived it as Keswick Hall, and Margaret Thatcher was among its guests.

Est. 1913

The Commonwealth

Richmond, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2023

German immigrant Louis Rueger opened a saloon facing the Virginia State Capitol in 1846. During the Civil War the Confederate Navy took it for its Commodore's headquarters, and the building burned when Richmond was evacuated in 1865. Rueger rebuilt it as the Lafayette Saloon and his family added a small hotel. In 1912 his grandson William Rueger and architect Charles M. Robinson began a Classical Revival hotel that opened in 1913 as the Hotel Rueger. Its restaurant still carries the family's name.

Close by

  • The Poe Museum and the Old Stone House
  • Whitewater rafting on the James River

Est. 1913

The Virginian Lynchburg, Curio Collection by Hilton

Lynchburg, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2018

For the Lynchburg Hotel Company, Philadelphia architect Verus T. Ritter of Ritter & Shay raised a five-story Classical Revival hotel at Church and Eighth streets, with a 30-foot barrel-vaulted lobby and columns carved with the letter V. The Virginian opened in 1913, and Eleanor Roosevelt and Ronald Reagan later stayed. Hard times in the 1960s brought a sale; it became the Stewart Arms and then a dormitory for the young Liberty Baptist College. Developers George Stanley and Blair Godsey restored it as a hotel from 2015.

Close by

  • Percival's Island Natural Area on the James River
  • Point of Honor, an 1815 Federal-style house
  • Anne Spencer House & Garden Museum

Est. 1916

Hotel Petersburg, Tapestry Collection by Hilton

Petersburg, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2024

As Petersburg rebuilt after the Civil War, local citizens decided the city needed a fine hotel beside the U.S. Customs House, where the Shirley Hotel had stood. Architect Charles M. Robinson began work in 1915 with the Fulton Brick Company, using Flemish-bond brick, Ionic columns, glazed terra cotta and a grand marble staircase. It became the heart of local society, near enough to the courthouse to draw lawyers to its dining rooms. The hotel closed in 1969; Nat Cuthbert bought it in 2017 and spent seven years restoring it.

Close by

  • Petersburg National Battlefield
  • Blandford Church and its Tiffany windows
  • Centre Hill Mansion Museum, built 1823

Est. 1924

Hotel 24 South

Staunton, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2006

Staunton, the birthplace of Woodrow Wilson, was largely spared in the Civil War, which is why so much of its downtown survives. This red brick hotel on South Market Street opened in 1924, designed by architect H.L. Stevens at a cost of $440,000, and its Colonnade Ballroom and rooftop sign made it the town's social center. Up on the mezzanine sits its 1924 Wurlitzer organ, thought to be the last working example anywhere. After years as a home for the elderly and homeless, it reopened as a hotel.

Close by

  • Woodrow Wilson Presidential Library and Museum
  • The Frontier Culture Museum
  • Skyline Drive and Shenandoah National Park

Bolling Wilson Hotel

Wytheville, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2025

The railroad had made Wytheville a busy crossroads, so the local Rotary Club hired the Virginia firm Frye and Stone to design a hotel with Corinthian columns and classical trim. It opened in 1927 as the George Wythe Hotel, named for the Declaration signer, charging $1.50 a night for a single. Competition later closed it. Bill and Farron Smith bought it in 2010, restored it and renamed it for Wytheville's own Edith Bolling Wilson, the First Lady who had visited the old hotel herself.

Est. 1931

The Mimslyn Inn

Luray, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2008

Henry and Elizabeth Mims already ran two hotels in Luray when they set out, with Henry's brothers, to build a grander one on land once part of the Aventine Hall estate. A steam shovel was brought in from Richmond to dig the foundations, and after fifteen months the Mimslyn opened in May 1931 with a party for several hundred people. Its winding staircase was designed by J.R. Mims Sr., who burned the plans so it could not be copied. The Asam family carries on the inn today.

Close by

  • Luray Caverns, discovered by candlelight in 1878
  • Skyline Drive in Shenandoah National Park
  • Canoeing on the Shenandoah River

Mountain Lake Lodge 

Pembroke, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2025

Explorer Christopher Gist described this mountain lake in 1751, and in the 1820s lawyer Henley Chapman bought the land as a stagecoach stop, later turning it into an inn. William Lewis Moody Jr. had loved the place as a boy; he bought it in 1930 and rebuilt the main Chestnut Lodge in the Arts and Crafts style. His daughter, Mary Moody Northern, ran it for three decades, and director Emile Ardolino filmed Dirty Dancing here, with the lodge playing Kellerman's resort for Patrick Swayze and Jennifer Grey.

Close by

  • Cascade Falls in the national forest
  • Fishing float trips on the New River
  • Mountain Lake, one of Virginia's two natural lakes

Est. 1937

Williamsburg Inn

Williamsburg, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 1994

Encouraged by the Reverend Dr. W.A.R. Goodwin, John D. Rockefeller Jr. spent millions restoring Williamsburg, then needed somewhere for its visitors to stay. He rejected a big convention hotel and had his architects design an inn in the town's own 18th-century manner on Francis Street; it opened in 1937 with air-conditioning in every room. In 1943 General George C. Marshall brought Britain's military chiefs here, Eisenhower entertained Winston Churchill in 1946, and Queen Elizabeth II stayed for Virginia's 350th anniversary and returned in 2007.

  • Jamestown Settlement

Est. 1939

Williamsburg Lodge, Autograph Collection, and Colonial Houses

Williamsburg, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2000

The Williamsburg Inn's 61 rooms could not hold all of Colonial Williamsburg's visitors, so John D. Rockefeller Jr. had architect Gilbert Stanley Underwood begin a lodge in 1937 in the same colonial spirit. His team also turned 27 mid-18th-century buildings into guest houses, among them the Orlando Jones House, home of Martha Washington's grandfather, and the Nicholas-Tyler Office, where John Tyler is said to have learned he was president. Both opened just before America entered World War II, and Cary Grant stayed while filming The Howards of Virginia.

Est. 1959

Colony House Motor Lodge

Roanoke, Virginia · Historic Hotels member since 2026

As passenger trains faded in the 1950s, Richard Trent and Horace Fralin built a motor lodge on U.S. Route 220 at the edge of Roanoke, opening it in 1959. The Salem firm Kinsey and Motley gave it the futuristic Googie look, with folded-plate roofs and wide glass walls, and Trent's construction company supplied the block and brick. Johnny Unitas and Herman's Hermits were early guests, and its Town and Ranch restaurant earned it the nickname "guestroom of South Roanoke." Its original neon VACANCY sign is still there.

Close by

  • Mill Mountain and the Roanoke Star overlook
  • Virginia Museum of Transportation and its Class J 611
  • The Grandin Theatre, a 1931 movie house
