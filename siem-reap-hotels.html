Where to Stay in Siem Reap

Where to Stay in Siem Reap — Best Areas for Angkor

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Siem Reap is a small town with a very large reason to visit it, and almost everything about choosing a room here comes down to two questions: how early you want to leave for the temples, and how much noise you can sleep through. The Old Market quarter puts you in the middle of everything, including Pub Street, which runs loud until well after midnight. Wat Bo and the east bank of the river are five quiet minutes away and are where most Guild travellers end up. The road to Angkor holds the large resorts and shaves fifteen minutes off a sunrise start. One thing that catches people out: since 2023 the town is served by Siem Reap–Angkor International Airport, roughly 40 kilometres east, so the old ten-minute hop from the runway to your hotel is now closer to an hour.

Choosing Your Area in Siem Reap

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

Old Market & Pub Street

The compact centre, and the one area you can do entirely on foot — the night market, the river, the restaurants and every tour desk in town. The trade is noise: Pub Street and the lanes around it run loud until one or two in the morning, and a street-facing room will hear all of it. If you want this address, ask for a room at the back or on a high floor, and check that there is a lift before you accept a fourth-floor walk-up in a converted shophouse.

Wat Bo & the east bank

Cross the Siem Reap River and the volume drops by half. Wat Bo Road and the lanes behind it hold most of the town’s small, well-run boutique hotels, set in gardens with pools, five to ten minutes’ walk from the Old Market and the same distance from a quiet dinner. For most visitors — and for anyone who wants to be asleep by ten and at Angkor by five — this is the sensible base in Siem Reap.

The road to Angkor & Charles de Gaulle

The avenue running north toward the temples carries the big international resorts, with the space, grounds and service that implies, and it is the closest you can sleep to the Angkor ticket gate without leaving town. Pavements are wide and level, which matters here. The trade is that you will take a tuk-tuk or a car to dinner rather than walk to it.

Sala Kamreuk & the south

Quieter again, and better value: guesthouses and small pool villas in residential lanes south and west of the centre, ten to fifteen minutes by tuk-tuk from the Old Market. A good choice for a longer stay, a family that wants a kitchen and a pool, or anyone who would rather spend the difference on a private guide at the temples.

Out in the countryside

A handful of small lodges sit among the rice fields on the fringes of town, where the evening sound is frogs rather than traffic. They are lovely, and they are a commitment: everything is a drive, and a late return from the temples means arranging it in advance. Worth it at the end of a trip rather than the start.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Siem Reap trip goes.

Buy the Angkor pass from the official ticket centre

One-, three- and seven-day passes for the Angkor Archaeological Park are sold only through Angkor Enterprise, at its ticket centre on the road out of town or through its own official website. Your photograph is taken and printed on the pass, so it cannot be shared or resold, and anyone offering you a pass in the street or in a bar is selling you nothing. Most hotels will send a driver to the ticket centre on the way to a sunrise start. Fees change from time to time — confirm the current ones with Angkor Enterprise rather than with a tour desk.

Dress for the temples, not for the heat

Shoulders and knees must be covered to enter Angkor Wat’s upper level and a number of other sanctuaries, and the rule is enforced. A light long-sleeved shirt and loose trousers are cooler than they sound and solve the problem for the whole day. Bring more water than you think, start at first light, and come back to the pool before the worst of the afternoon.

Tonlé Sap and the floating villages

The great lake south of town is genuinely remarkable, and the boat trips sold to see it vary enormously in honesty and in how much of your money reaches the villages. Ask your hotel which operator it uses and why, agree the price and the route before you set off, and treat any trip that includes a stop at an orphanage or a school as a reason to choose a different one.

Siem Reap Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Siem Reap.

What is the best area to stay in Siem Reap?

For most visitors, Wat Bo and the east bank of the river — small garden hotels with pools, quiet nights, and a five-to-ten minute walk to the Old Market and the restaurants. Choose the Old Market quarter if you want everything on your doorstep and can sleep through Pub Street, and the road to Angkor if you want a full-sized resort and the shortest possible run to the temples at dawn.

How far is the new Siem Reap airport from town?

About 40 kilometres east, and allow roughly an hour door to door. Siem Reap–Angkor International Airport opened in 2023 and replaced the old airfield close to the centre, so journey times quoted in older guidebooks and in some hotel confirmations are badly out of date. Arrange the transfer through your hotel and confirm the price in advance.

How many nights do you need in Siem Reap?

Three is the comfortable answer: one long day on the main Angkor circuit, one slower day for the outlying temples such as Banteay Srei and Beng Mealea, and a third for the town, the markets and a swim. Two nights will show you Angkor Wat, the Bayon and Ta Prohm and leave you tired. A week is no hardship at all if you like temples.

Is Siem Reap easy for older travellers?

Yes, more so than most of Cambodia. The town is flat, hotels are inexpensive enough that a comfortable one is within most budgets, and a car and driver for the day costs little. The temples themselves are the effort: Angkor is enormous, the stone steps are steep and uneven, and the heat is serious. A private driver, an early start, a mid-day break and a guide who will pace the day make all the difference.

Does Siem Reap have adults-only hotels?

A few small boutique properties describe themselves that way, and the picture changes often enough that it is not worth booking on the label alone. Age policies are set by each hotel and are shown on the booking page — check there before you pay. If what you want is a quiet, grown-up stay rather than a formal rule, the low-room-count garden hotels around Wat Bo deliver it reliably.
