Where to Stay in Battambang

Where to Stay in Battambang — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Battambang is the Cambodia people come back for. It is the country’s second city and behaves like a large town: a slow brown river, the best surviving street of French colonial shophouses in the country, an art school that produces a circus, and rice fields that start where the pavement ends. Almost everything worth staying in sits within a few streets of the riverside centre near Psar Nat, with quieter rooms across the Sangker on the east bank and a handful of small lodges out among the fields. Two nights is the minimum that does it justice; three is better.

Choosing Your Area in Battambang

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

The riverside centre & Psar Nat

The heart of it. Street One and the lanes behind the old market hold the colonial shophouses, the cafes, the tour desks and most of the town’s small hotels, all within a flat ten-minute walk of each other. This is where to stay unless you have a reason not to: you can eat, drink and find a driver without ever getting into a vehicle, and the river is at the end of the street.

Across the Sangker — the east bank

A footbridge or a short ride over the river buys quiet and a little more space for the money. Guesthouses and small boutique places sit among residential lanes and wats, five to fifteen minutes from the centre. A good choice for a longer stay or for anyone who would rather hear a monastery bell than a motorbike.

Out among the rice fields

Several small lodges and family-run places lie a few kilometres out, with pools, gardens and a view of nothing but paddy. They are the nicest rooms in the district and they do mean a tuk-tuk for every meal. Ask whether the price includes transfers into town, because at some of them it does.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Battambang trip goes.

What the town is actually for

Battambang is not a sight; it is a place to slow down for two days. The usual shape of a visit is a morning cycling or riding through the villages — rice paper, fish paste, bamboo sticky rice, a prahok kitchen — an afternoon in the colonial streets and the art galleries, and an evening at Phare Ponleu Selpak, the arts school on the edge of town whose circus performances are the best night out in provincial Cambodia and whose ticket money goes back into the school.

Phnom Sampeau and the bats

The limestone hill fifteen kilometres south holds a wat, a long view over the plain, and the killing caves, where people were murdered during the Khmer Rouge years — a small, sober memorial sits at the bottom of the shaft. At dusk, millions of free-tailed bats pour out of a cave in the cliff face in an unbroken ribbon that takes half an hour to pass. Most drivers combine the two; ask to arrive in time to walk the hill before the light goes.

The bamboo railway

The famous bamboo railway no longer runs on the old main line at O Dambong; it operates on a purpose-built track out at Banan, which is a shorter and frankly more touristic ride than the one in older guidebooks. It is still good fun, particularly with grandchildren. Go knowing what it is now rather than expecting what it was.

Battambang Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Battambang.

Where should I stay in Battambang?

In the riverside centre near Psar Nat, where the colonial streets, the cafes and the hotels are all within a flat, walkable few blocks. The east bank across the Sangker is quieter for a longer stay, and the countryside lodges outside town are the prettiest rooms in the province if you do not mind a ride to dinner.

How do you get to Battambang?

By road. It is roughly three hours from Siem Reap and five to six from Phnom Penh by bus or private car, and the Siem Reap run is one of the easiest journeys in the country. There is also a slow, cheap and rather charming train on the Phnom Penh line. Most visitors arrive from Siem Reap and continue to the capital, or the reverse.

How many nights do you need in Battambang?

Two nights, three if you like it — and most people do. One day for the villages and the colonial centre, one for Phnom Sampeau, the hilltop wats and the bat exodus at dusk, with a circus performance at Phare Ponleu Selpak slotted into whichever evening it is running.

Is Battambang worth visiting?

Yes, and it is the part of the trip people are most surprised by. After the crowds at Angkor it is quiet, inexpensive and entirely unhurried, the countryside begins at the edge of town, and the arts scene is real rather than staged for visitors. If your route runs between Siem Reap and Phnom Penh, stopping here costs you almost nothing in travelling time.
