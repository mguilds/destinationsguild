Where to Stay in Quy Nhơn

Where to Stay in Quy Nhơn — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Quy Nhơn is the beach city Vietnam kept quiet about. It has a six-kilometre seafront with four and a half kilometres of sand, a proper beach park and promenade in the middle of town, and none of the crowding that came to Nha Trang and Đà Nẵng. You can walk to dinner here. You can also, in twenty minutes, be standing on an empty cove on a peninsula. Choosing Your Neighbourhood

Five very different places carry the same postmark. Here is the honest lay of the land.

City beach, fishing hamlet, or a cove on the peninsula?

Stay on the city beach along Xuân Diệu if this is your first visit. It is the rare Vietnamese beach city where the town itself is the best base — a walkable promenade, a genuine beach park, coffee roasters and seafood within a few minutes on foot, and taxis when you want them. Bãi Xếp, twelve kilometres south, is a fishing hamlet clinging to a steep hillside; it is beautiful and rustic and the lanes down to the sand are sharply pitched, so it suits the sure-footed and nobody else. Nhơn Lý and the Kỳ Co peninsula, twenty kilometres north-east, is rugged, half-built and thinly served — you will want a motorbike or a driver on call. The Bãi Dài strip south of town and Cát Tiến across the bay are where the self-contained resorts sit: private sand, very little to walk to, and closer to the airport than the city is. One note that will save you confusion: Vietnam merged its provinces in 2025 and Bình Định became part of Gia Lai. Booking sites now say Gia Lai Province. The town has not moved.

Luxury

Quy Nhơn’s top tier sits outside the city, on the quiet sand south of town and on the peninsula across the bay.

Anantara Quy Nhơn Villas

$$$$ LuxuryBãi Dài, southAll villasPrivate pools

The most expensive address in the province and the one that put this coast on international lists — a small all-villa property on its own bay south of the city, run by Minor Hotels. Everything is villa-scale and private-pool, which is the point: guests come here to see nobody. Two honest cautions. There is essentially nothing to walk to, so plan on eating in for the length of your stay; and the villas step down a hillside to the water, so ask about buggy access and the number of steps to your villa if stairs are a consideration.

Boutique & Coastal

Quy Nhơn Hotel FAQ

Should I stay on the city beach or out at a resort?

Stay in the city if you want to walk to dinner and have shops and a pharmacy to hand. Choose Bãi Dài or Cát Tiến if you intend to settle into one resort and barely leave it. Quy Nhơn’s city beach is unusually good for a working city — a real promenade and a real beach park — so the city option is stronger here than it is in most Vietnamese beach towns.

How far is the airport?

Phù Cát sits roughly thirty kilometres north of the city, about forty-five minutes by road. The resorts on the Cát Tiến and Kỳ Co side of the bay are closer to the airport than the city itself, which can be worth a good deal on a short stay.

Is Bãi Xếp suitable for older travellers?

Be careful. Bãi Xếp is a fishing hamlet on a steep hillside twelve kilometres south of the city, and the lanes down to the beach are narrow and sharply pitched. It is a lovely place to stay if you are steady on your feet, and a poor choice if you are not.

Do I need a motorbike?

Not in the city, which is walkable along the beach road and well served by taxis. You do effectively need one at Nhơn Lý and Nhơn Hải on the peninsula, where public transport is thin and taxis are not sitting waiting for you.

Why do the hotel listings say Gia Lai Province?

Vietnam reorganised its provinces in 2025 and Bình Định was merged into Gia Lai. Older guidebooks and a lot of the internet still say Bình Định. Both mean the same place, and Quy Nhơn has not moved.
