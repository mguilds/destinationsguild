When to Go to Malaysia

The Best Time to Visit Malaysia

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: Malaysia is a warm, year-round tropical destination, but the rains move around the country. For Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi on the west coast, roughly December to April is the driest, sunniest window. The east coast islands (Perhentians, Tioman) run opposite, best from March to October, and are largely closed in the Nov–Feb monsoon. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, and the best timing for each region.

Malaysia sits on the equator, so it is hot and humid all year — daytime highs around 31–33°C, warm seas, and short, dramatic downpours rather than all-day rain. What really matters is the monsoon calendar, which differs sharply between the west coast, the east coast and Borneo. Get the timing right for your region and you’ll trade grey skies for blue. The Seasons at a Glance

Malaysia’s ‘seasons’ are really monsoon phases — here’s the quick read for the main travel regions.

West coast dry season — December to April

$$$ PeakWest coast & islandsSunniestBook ahead

The prime window for Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi: the driest, sunniest months, with calm Andaman seas ideal for the west-coast beaches. It coincides with peak visitor numbers and the year-end holidays, so book flights and island resorts well ahead. This is the classic time for a first trip to Peninsular Malaysia.

Inter-monsoon — April–May & October–November

$$ ShoulderAfternoon stormsGreen & lushGood value

Between the monsoons the west coast sees more afternoon thunderstorms, but they are usually short and the mornings often bright. Crowds thin and prices ease, making it a good-value time if you keep plans flexible. The countryside is at its greenest.

Northeast monsoon — November to February

$ East coast lowEast coast wetIslands closedWest coast OK

The northeast monsoon soaks the east coast and its islands (Perhentians, Tioman), which largely close, and brings wetter spells to Borneo. The west coast, however, is entering its best, driest months. Time it right and you simply travel to the sunny side of the peninsula.

Southwest season — June to September

$$ ValueEast coast bestBorneo drierDiving season

The drier stretch for the east-coast islands and much of Borneo, with the calmest seas for diving off the Perhentians, Redang and Sabah. The west coast stays warm with scattered showers. A strong choice for a beach-and-reef holiday.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, aim for the shoulder and green-season weeks.

Green season — May to early November on the west coast

$ Best valueSofter ratesShort afternoon rain

Outside the school holidays, the west coast’s wetter months bring the lowest hotel rates and airfares, with rain usually falling in short afternoon bursts rather than all day. May to early November is the value sweet spot for KL, Penang and Langkawi if you keep mornings for sightseeing.

For the islands, target the shoulder edges of each region’s season — late April–May and September for the east coast — for lower prices with still-decent weather. Avoid Malaysian and Singaporean school holidays and major festivals, and book midweek, to land the best fares.

Beating the Heat & the Crowds

Malaysia’s busy spells and wet spells are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the rains to plan around

Year-end & CNYSchool holidaysNE monsoon Nov–Feb

The sharpest crowds and prices come with the year-end holidays, Chinese New Year (Jan/Feb) and Malaysian and Singaporean school breaks, when the west-coast islands fill up. On the weather side, the northeast monsoon (Nov–Feb) shuts much of the east coast, while the inter-monsoon months bring the wettest west-coast afternoons.

To have the beaches and sights closer to yourself, travel in the shoulder weeks and start early each day — mornings are usually the brightest and calmest. For where to base yourself, see our guides to where to stay in Kuala Lumpur and Langkawi.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to Malaysia.

What is the best time to visit Malaysia?

For the west coast — Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi — the best weather runs roughly December to April, the driest and sunniest months. The east coast islands such as the Perhentians and Tioman are best from March to October and largely close during the northeast monsoon from November to February. Borneo (Sabah and Sarawak) is reasonably dry from March to September. Because Malaysia is equatorial, it is warm and humid all year whenever you go.

What is the cheapest time to visit Malaysia?

The wetter shoulder weeks generally bring the lowest prices. On the west coast, the green season from May to early November — outside the school holidays — tends to have softer hotel rates and airfares, with rain usually arriving in short afternoon bursts rather than all day. Avoid the Malaysian and Singaporean school holidays and major festivals for the best value, and book midweek where you can.

When should I avoid Malaysia because of rain?

It depends where you’re going. The northeast monsoon (November to February) brings heavy rain and rough seas to the east coast and its islands, which mostly close, and wetter spells to Borneo. The west coast is at its wettest during the inter-monsoon months of April–May and October–November, when afternoon thunderstorms are common — though rarely all day. There is no truly ‘dry’ nationwide window; plan by region.

Does it rain all day in Malaysia?

Usually not. Outside the main monsoon, rain typically comes as intense but short afternoon or evening thunderstorms, clearing to sunshine. Mornings are often the brightest part of the day, so it pays to sightsee early and keep a light rain layer and flexible plans. Humidity is high year-round, so pace yourself and drink plenty of water.

When is the best time for Langkawi and the islands?

Langkawi and the west-coast islands are best from December to April, when seas are calm and skies clear. The east-coast islands (Perhentians, Redang, Tioman) shine from April to September and largely shut for the November–February monsoon. For Borneo’s islands off Kota Kinabalu, March to September is the driest, calmest stretch for diving and beaches.
