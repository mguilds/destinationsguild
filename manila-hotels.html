Where to Stay in Manila

Where to Stay in Manila — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Manila is not one city but seventeen of them stitched together, and the single decision that shapes your stay is which district you sleep in. Get it right and the capital is generous: superb food, real history, and hotels that would cost twice as much in Singapore. Get it wrong and you spend your holiday in traffic. Choosing Your Neighbourhood

Four districts hold nearly every hotel worth the trip. Here is the honest difference between them.

Makati, Bonifacio Global City, the Bay Area or Intramuros?

Read this firstMetro ManilaTraffic matters

Makati is the safe answer and usually the right one: the old business district has become the most liveable part of the metro, with proper pavements, the Ayala museum and Greenbelt gardens in the middle of it, and everything from a legendary grand hotel to a clean budget room within a few streets of each other. Bonifacio Global City — everyone says BGC — is what Makati would be if it had been designed all at once: wide, green, orderly, full of new hotels and good restaurants, and a little corporate in the evenings. The Bay Area in Pasay, around the Mall of Asia and the entertainment city casinos, is where you stay for a dawn flight, a cruise sailing, or a view of the famous Manila Bay sunset; it is not a place to walk for pleasure. Intramuros and Ermita, the old Spanish quarter and the strip along Roxas Boulevard, put you among the history and the museums, with fewer hotels and more traffic. One point that overrides all of this: whichever you choose, ask what the drive will be at the hour you plan to move, not what the map says.

Luxury

Manila does grand hotels unusually well, and at prices that would be unthinkable in Hong Kong or Singapore.

The Peninsula Manila

$$$$ LuxuryMakatiLandmark lobbySince 1976

The social heart of Makati as much as a hotel. Its vast, light-filled lobby, with the gilded sunburst above the staircase, is where Manila meets for afternoon tea and has been for fifty years. Rooms are generous and quietly traditional rather than fashionable, the service is superb, and the location on the corner of Ayala and Makati Avenues could not be more central. The pick if you want the city's institution.

Family-friendly

What families need in Manila is a pool, a mall you can reach without a taxi, and a short walk to dinner.

Adults-only

An honest answer here is more use than a list.

Manila has no adults-only hotels worth sending you to

Honest noteMetro ManilaTry the islands instead

Adults-only is a beach-resort format in the Philippines, not a city one, and Trip.com carries no genuine adults-only property in Metro Manila that we would put our name to. Rather than dress up a business hotel as something it is not, here is the honest advice: if you want a grown-up, child-free stay in the capital, book an all-suite hotel such as Raffles Makati or an upper floor at one of the Makati landmarks, where children are simply rare. If the adults-only holiday is the point of the trip, keep it for Boracay, Palawan or Bohol, where the format actually exists. Manila Hotel FAQ

Which district should I stay in on a first visit?

Makati, in almost every case. It is the most walkable part of Metro Manila, it has the greatest concentration of good hotels at every price, and the pavements, restaurants and shopping are all within reach on foot. Bonifacio Global City is newer, cleaner and even easier to walk, but it is quieter in the evenings and further from the historic sights. Choose the Bay Area only if an early flight or a cruise departure makes it sensible.

How long does it really take to get from the airport to Makati?

Anywhere from twenty minutes to an hour and a half, and the difference is entirely the time of day. Ninoy Aquino International is close to Makati as the crow flies, but the roads between them carry some of the heaviest traffic in Asia. Arriving mid-morning or after eight at night is a different experience from arriving at five in the afternoon. Build the delay into your plans rather than hoping.

Is it worth staying inside Intramuros?

For atmosphere, yes; for choice, no. The walled Spanish city is the most interesting square kilometre in the country, but it holds only a handful of hotels and most visitors see it comfortably on a day trip from Makati or the Bay Area. Stay there if the history is the whole point of your visit and you are content with a small, quiet base.

Are Manila hotels good value compared with the rest of Asia?

At the top end, remarkably so. A suite at one of the Makati landmarks costs meaningfully less than an equivalent room in Singapore, Hong Kong or Tokyo, and the service tradition in Philippine hotels is among the warmest anywhere. Value in the middle of the market is more uneven, so read recent reviews rather than the description.

Do I need to worry about the rainy season when booking?

It is worth knowing about rather than worrying about. From June to October the habagat can bring heavy afternoon rain and, occasionally, street flooding in low-lying parts of the metro. Hotels in Makati and Bonifacio Global City sit on higher ground and are rarely affected. Our month-by-month guide sets out the whole year.
