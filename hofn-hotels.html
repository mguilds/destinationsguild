Where to Stay in Höfn

Where to Stay in Höfn

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Höfn í Hornafirði — the name simply means “harbour” — is a town of around two thousand people on a low spit in a lagoon at the south-eastern corner of Iceland, with the white bulk of Vatnajökull, the largest ice cap in Europe, filling the sky behind it. It is six hours from Reykjavík and roughly an hour east of the glacier lagoon at Jökulsárlón, which is the whole reason to know about it: staying here turns the single most punishing day trip in Iceland into a civilised overnight. Add the black peninsula at Stokksnes fifteen minutes east, one of the most photographed places in the country, and a working harbour with a genuine sense of the edge of things, and Höfn earns its night rather better than most people expect.

Choosing Your Ground

The town on its spit, or one of the country hotels strung out along the road towards the glacier.

In Höfn, or out along the glacier road?

OrientationWalkabilityDistancesDark skies

Höfn itself is flat, tiny and entirely walkable — harbour, swimming pool, supermarket, a handful of restaurants, and the long view across the lagoon to the ice. If you want to park the car and walk to dinner, stay in the town; this is the only place for forty miles in either direction where that is possible. West along Route 1 towards Jökulsárlón lie the country hotels and farm guesthouses — Hali, Smýrlabjörg, Skaftafell further on — which put you closer to the glacier lagoon and further from anywhere to eat that is not the hotel’s own dining room. That is a fair trade if your plan is to be at the lagoon at eight in the morning. Wherever you sleep here the skies are dark, the horizon is enormous, and this is one of the better corners of Iceland for the aurora between September and March.

Out towards the glacier lagoon

If your priority is being at Jökulsárlón before the coaches, sleep west of town rather than in it.

Hali Country Hotel

$$$ Country hotelTowards the glacier lagoonRestaurant on siteVery dark skies

A country hotel on the farm at Hali, out along Route 1 west of Höfn and appreciably closer to Jökulsárlón and Diamond Beach. It has its own restaurant, which is essential out here, and a small museum next door devoted to the writer Þórbergur Þórðarson, who was born on this farm. The skies are as dark as anywhere in Iceland. There is nowhere to walk to; that is the point.

Höfn Hotel FAQ

Why stay in Höfn at all?

Because it breaks the drive. Reykjavík to Jökulsárlón and back in a day is around ten hours of driving for perhaps an hour at the lagoon, and people do it, and they regret it. Sleep at Höfn or on the road west of it and the glacier lagoon becomes a morning rather than an ordeal — and you get Stokksnes, the harbour, the ice cap and a proper dinner into the bargain.

How far is Höfn from the glacier lagoon?

About eighty kilometres, or an hour, west along Route 1. Diamond Beach is directly opposite the lagoon across the road. Skaftafell and the national park visitor centre are about two hours west, and Reykjavík is roughly six hours — 460 kilometres — which is why nobody sensible drives it in one go.

What is Stokksnes and is it worth it?

Stokksnes is the black-sand peninsula fifteen minutes east of Höfn beneath the sawtooth peak of Vestrahorn, and it is one of the most photographed places in Iceland. It is private land: you pay a modest entry fee at the Viking Café at the gate, which also runs a small film set built for a production that was never made. On a still day the reflections are extraordinary. On a windy one, the sand blows hard enough to sting.

Is Höfn walkable?

The town is, easily — it is flat, small and laid out along a spit, and you can walk from most accommodation to the harbour, the pool and the restaurants in a few minutes. Everything outside the town needs the car, and there are no footpaths on Route 1.

What about the langoustine?

Höfn built its name on langoustine, holds a summer festival in its honour and still has restaurants devoted to it. Be aware, though, that Icelandic langoustine stocks have been poor in recent years and the catch has been much reduced, so what is on the plate is not always what it once was. Ask what is fresh, and do not be disappointed if the answer is the local fish instead — it will be excellent.

Is the road to Höfn difficult?

In summer, no — it is paved Route 1 the whole way, with a few narrow bridges. In winter it is a different proposition: this stretch crosses open sandur plains where wind and drifting snow close the road with little warning, and it can shut for a day at a time. Check road.is every morning without exception, and build a spare day into any winter itinerary that includes this corner.

Can I see the northern lights from Höfn?

It is one of the better places in the country to try. The town is small enough to escape in five minutes, the horizon is wide, the ice cap reflects what light there is, and the south-east often has clearer skies than the south coast. Season is roughly late September to March; the country hotels outside town are darker still.

Do I need to book ahead?

In June, July and August, yes — there are only a few hundred beds in the whole district and they fill months in advance, because everyone driving the Ring Road needs a bed at roughly the same point on the map. Outside those months you will usually find something, but the choice narrows and some places close for the winter.
