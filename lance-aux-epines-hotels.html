Lance aux Épines & the South Coast

Where to Stay on Grenada’s South Coast

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

South and east of the airport the coast frays into a run of peninsulas and small bays — True Blue, Lance aux Épines, Prickly Bay, Mount Hartman — and this is where Grenada keeps its quieter half. There is no long beach here and no strip of shops. What there is instead is sheltered water, a yachting crowd, the island’s dive shops, and several of its best-known small hotels, all within a few minutes of the airport and ten or fifteen of Grand Anse. Choose it if you would rather be somewhere than at a resort.

The areas, and which one suits you

Where the beds actually are, and the honest word on steps, hills, road noise and how far it really is to the sand.

Lance aux Épines

A long residential peninsula of gardens, villas and a few small hotels, with calm water on both sides and no through traffic to speak of. The best address on the south coast and the quietest, about fifteen to twenty minutes from the airport by the hotels’ own reckoning.

True Blue

The bay just east of the airport, with a marina, restaurants on the water and St George’s University next door — which means more life in the evening than the rest of this coast and a younger crowd. Five minutes from the terminal, and a short hop over to Grand Anse.

Prickly Bay

The yachting heart of Grenada: a sheltered anchorage full of masts, a marina, chandlers, and the charter and dive boats. Not a beach destination, very much a boat one, and a good place to eat within earshot of halyards.

Mount Hartman and Secret Harbour

The next bays east again, narrow and deep and almost entirely given over to boats and a couple of hillside properties. Lovely to look at, steep to walk, and far enough from everything that you will want a car or a taxi habit.

What this coast is not

It is not Grand Anse. The beaches here are small, some are more landing than beach, and there is no line of shops and restaurants to stroll to. If a long swimmable stretch of sand on the doorstep is the point of the holiday, stay at Grand Anse and visit this coast for dinner.

Getting about

Minibuses run the main road but not far down the peninsulas, so plan on taxis, a hire car, or a hotel shuttle — True Blue Bay’s own site advertises a free shuttle to Grand Anse. Fares are set by zone; agree the figure before you set off. Driving is on the left and visitors need a temporary local permit, which the hire company arranges.

What your money is worth on the south coast
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Grenada uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged at EC$2.70 to the US dollar, so a price of EC$27 is about ten US dollars. US dollars are accepted in most hotels, restaurants and taxis, and change often comes back in local money. Cards work in hotels, supermarkets and the larger restaurants; carry cash for minibuses, market stalls and small bars. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills — ask what is included when you book. This coast eats out more than it cooks in: the marina restaurants at True Blue and Prickly Bay are the social centre and are priced for the yachting trade rather than for the town. The supermarkets on the Grand Anse road, ten minutes away, are where self-catering guests shop.

Calabash Hotel

Lance aux Épines36 suitesBeachfront

Calabash’s own site uses both “Calabash Hotel” and “Calabash Grenada”, and its FAQ places it in Lance aux Épines in the south of Grenada, about fifteen to twenty minutes from Maurice Bishop International Airport. It describes thirty-six upscale suites and says the hotel sits directly on a calm, sheltered beach suitable for swimming, paddleboarding and relaxing. Three places to eat are named on its own dining pages — Rhodes Restaurant, the Beach Club and Nori. It does not publish a street address, which is normal enough on this peninsula. The diving it links to is run by a separate company off its own domain, so ask the hotel rather than assuming there is a dive shop on site.

True Blue Bay Boutique Resort

True Blue Bay describes itself on its own site as a waterfront resort on the south coast of Grenada, a convenient five-minute drive from Maurice Bishop International Airport and within walking distance of St George’s University, with Grand Anse beach about five minutes away by car and a free shuttle running to it. Its own description lists four pools, a PADI dive shop and a tree-top open-air studio used for yoga, and names the Dodgy Dock Restaurant and Bar on the water. The accommodation is rooms, suites and villas; the site does not publish a count, and it calls itself family friendly. We did not find a statement of beach frontage on the site, so take it as a waterfront property rather than a beach one and ask where you actually swim.

Lance Aux Epines Cottages

Lance aux Épines11 unitsDirectly on the beachSelf-catering

Lance Aux Epines Cottages gives a post box address — P.O. Box 1187, St George, Grenada, West Indies — and places itself on Grenada’s south-western coast. Its own site states eleven units carefully placed within a sprawling three-acre property, let as apartments and one- and two-bedroom cottages, and says plainly that they are directly on the beach and that the bay is well protected. Complimentary water sports and free wi-fi are advertised, along with an airport transfer service, though no distance is stated. The Sand Bar and Grill in the navigation sits on a separate domain, so check with the cottages what is open when you are there. This is the self-catering option on this coast rather than a hotel, which suits a family or a long stay.

Travelling in your later years

The south coast asks a little more of you than Grand Anse does, and repays it with quiet. The great advantage is the airport: five to twenty minutes from your room depending on which bay you choose, so no long transfer at either end of the trip. The disadvantages are slopes and distance. Several properties here are built up headlands, the peninsulas are long and not much fun to walk in the heat, and there is no parade of shops and restaurants within strolling range, so you will be taking taxis or driving. Lance aux Épines is the flattest and calmest corner, and Calabash and Lance Aux Epines Cottages both state on their own sites that they sit directly on sheltered, swimmable sand — which is the single most useful thing to know if walking on a slope is a consideration. Ask every property about steps between the room and the water before you book.

Questions about staying in The South Coast

South coast or Grand Anse?

Grand Anse for sand and convenience; the south coast for calm, boats and a sense of place. Grand Anse gives you a long swimmable beach, the widest choice of hotels, shops and restaurants you can walk to and a ten-minute taxi into the capital. The south coast gives you sheltered bays, small hotels with gardens, the dive and sailing scene, and considerably less going on after dinner. Many people who have been to Grenada more than once end up here.

How far is the south coast from the airport?

Very close, which is its best practical argument. True Blue Bay's own website calls itself a five-minute drive from Maurice Bishop International, and Calabash, further out on the Lance aux Epines peninsula, puts the drive at fifteen to twenty minutes on its own FAQ page. That is a material difference from islands where the airport is an hour and a half from the hotels, particularly on an overnight arrival.

Is there a beach on this coast?

Several small ones rather than one big one, and the difference matters. Calabash states on its own site that it sits directly on a calm sheltered beach suitable for swimming and paddleboarding, and Lance Aux Epines Cottages says its units are directly on the beach in a well-protected bay. Other properties here face a marina or an anchorage rather than sand. If a swimmable beach outside your door is essential, confirm it with the specific property, and note that Grand Anse is ten or fifteen minutes away whichever bay you pick.

Do I need a hire car if I stay here?

Not strictly, but you will use taxis more than you would at Grand Anse, because the minibuses run the main road rather than the peninsulas and there is no strip of shops within walking distance. True Blue Bay advertises a free shuttle to Grand Anse on its own site, and several properties arrange airport transfers. A car for two or three days to see the interior and the north of the island is a sensible middle course. Driving is on the left and visitors need a temporary local permit, which the hire company arranges when you collect.

Is this a good base for diving and sailing?

It is the base for both. Prickly Bay and True Blue are where the marinas, chandlers, charter operators and dive shops are, and True Blue Bay states on its own site that it has a PADI dive shop on the property. Grenada's reefs, its large wreck off the capital and the underwater sculpture park at Molinere are all reached from here. Grenada Sailing Week is based at Port Louis Marina in St George's, a short drive away.

Is self-catering a good idea here?

It works well, and Lance Aux Epines Cottages is the obvious example: eleven apartments and cottages on a three-acre beachfront plot, by its own description. The supermarkets on the Grand Anse road are well stocked and ten minutes away, the restaurants at True Blue and Prickly Bay cover the evenings you do not want to cook, and a kitchen takes the sting out of a fortnight for a family. Confirm with the property what is within walking distance, because on these peninsulas the honest answer is often nothing.
