A short history

Long inhabited by the Caddo, Osage and Quapaw peoples, Arkansas became part of the United States through the 1803 Louisiana Purchase and was organized as a territory before achieving statehood in 1836 as the 25th state. Hot Springs, valued by Indigenous peoples and early settlers alike for its thermal waters, became a federally protected reservation in 1832, decades before the National Park Service existed.

The state played a painful, pivotal role in civil-rights history: in 1957, nine Black students, the Little Rock Nine, integrated Central High School under federal troop protection after the governor tried to block them, an event that tested the limits of segregationist resistance and drew international attention.

Arkansas's economy today rests on agriculture, timber and major corporate headquarters including Walmart and Tyson Foods, alongside a growing outdoor-recreation and tourism sector. The Ozark and Ouachita mountains, Buffalo National River and the Crater of Diamonds State Park give the self-styled 'Natural State' a distinctive, unhurried appeal.

Bodhi

Hot Springs became a federal reservation way back in 1832 — decades before Yellowstone — because the government wanted to protect the thermal waters that gamblers, gangsters and health-seekers alike would soak in for the next century.

When to go

Spring and autumn are ideal; fall foliage in the Ozarks is a highlight.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Cool and occasionally icy, with mild spells; quiet season for hot-spring soaking.

Spring (Mar–May)

Wildflowers and rising rivers make this prime time for float trips and hiking.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Hot and humid, good for river floating but less pleasant for long hikes.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Crisp air and vivid Ozark foliage make this the standout season.
