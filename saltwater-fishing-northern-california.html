Saltwater Fishing in Northern California — Rockfish, Crab, Salmon & a Cold, Honest Ocean

From the easy harbours of Avila and Morro Bay to the working boats of Fort Bragg and Crescent City: what you can fish, what is closed this winter, and how to come home from it.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the California Department of Fish and Wildlife and each operator, 4 November 2026

North of Point Conception the ocean changes. It is colder, rougher and full of rockfish and lingcod, with Dungeness crab in winter, salmon in the seasons they are allowed, and albacore far offshore when the water warms. The boats are smaller and the days are longer. Most of the fleet is working out of old fishing harbours that have not been tidied up for visitors, which is much of the appeal.

This guide runs from Avila Beach to the Oregon line, by the way you want to fish it: the boats, the piers, the shore and the deep water. It is part of our California fishing guide. This coast also kills people who do not respect it, so please read the safety section. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

What is open and closed this winter

Read this first if you are going between now and spring.

As of 4 November 2026

Rockfish and lingcod: open from boats to 31 December, then closed from 1 January to 31 March 2027.

Dungeness crab: opens Saturday 7 November, but crab traps are restricted from the Oregon line to Point Conception until at least 1 January 2027. Use hoop nets or crab snares; hoop nets must be checked at least every two hours. The opening is delayed from Cape Mendocino to Ten Mile State Marine Reserve because of domoic acid, a natural toxin. Noyo Harbor and Fort Bragg lie close to that line, so check CDFW’s map.

Ocean salmon: closed for the rest of 2026.

Pacific halibut: a report from the operators says north of Point Arena to 15 November or quota; check before you go.

Confirmed with CDFW, 4 November 2026 — Crab notice, 30 October 2026, and the In-Season Changes page. Crab opens Sat 7 November in all three north coast counties through 30 July 2027, and to 30 June 2027 elsewhere. “Limit: Ten.” Minimum “Five and three-quarter inches”. Domoic acid hotline (831) 649-2883. Regional pages updated 2 November 2026 list ocean salmon as closed for the year. In-season notices: CDFW In-Season Changes. Razor clams in Humboldt and Del Norte counties are also closed for domoic acid.

The boats, harbour by harbour

From south to north. Real operators, each with its own website, opened on 4 November 2026. No ratings, no paid placements. Fares change: confirm when you book.

Avila Beach — Patriot Sportfishing

From the Harford Pier, an easy start. Listed at $90 for five hours and $110 for eight, with crab and cod trips in the crab season; a 6% port fee applies; rods $15; licences in the shop. 805-595-7200. patriotsportfishing.com

Morro Bay — Virg’s Landing

Under the great rock. Half-day six hours $90; three-quarter day $110; twelve-hour long-range $175. Sells one-day and two-day licences, cash only. (805) 772-1222. virgslanding.com

Monterey — Old Fisherman’s Wharf

Chris’ Fishing Trips: rock and lingcod $120 weekdays, $130 weekends, 15 and under $85–$95; rod $10; one-day licences in the office. Its site is old, so telephone: 831-375-5951. chrissfishing.com

Open year-round. Nearshore half-day $88, under 16 $68; deep-sea half-day $108; deep-sea cod $131; twilight $88 from May to December; albacore July to November, by telephone. Rods $14–$16. No braided line on board. 831-427-0230. stagnaros.com

Half Moon Bay — Riptide Sportfishing

The nearest ocean fleet to San Francisco, at Pillar Point Harbor. Rockfish $170; salmon $250 in season; tuna $500. Rod $20. Licences are not sold on the boat, so buy online first. (650) 728-8433. riptide.net Also from the harbour: New Captain Pete.

Deep sea — albacore and the long trips

The north coast’s big-game fishing. Long days and a lot of ocean.

Albacore and bluefin

Tuna are open all year; the season is really about warm water reaching the coast. Anchor Charter Boats in Fort Bragg expects tuna from mid to late July until the fish leave, usually October or November, on eight- to ten-hour trips at $500. Stagnaro lists albacore from July to November, and Riptide sails for tuna at $500. In a good year boats go thirty miles or more out to find them. Chris’ boat Caroline reported two bluefin off Monterey on 2 November 2026.

Confirmed with CDFW, 4 November 2026 — 2026 Ocean regulations: albacore 25 north of 34°27′ N, in addition to the 20-fish limit; bluefin tuna 2, in addition to the 20-fish limit.

Salmon, when there is a season

Salmon are the fish everyone here wants, and the fish with the most rules. The ocean season was shut altogether for a time; 2025 had six open days. In 2026 it opened in stages from April to October, closing as quotas were met, and Riptide reported limits off Half Moon Bay in mid-October. It is now over. Dates for 2027 are expected in April 2027, so do not book a salmon trip before then.

Halibut and sturgeon in the Bay

California halibut come into San Francisco Bay in spring and summer, and Bay charters fish for them. White sturgeon are now catch and release only, from 1 October to 30 June, and they are a candidate for state protection; treat them gently. Over 60 inches, a fish may not be taken out of the water.

Surf and shore

A licence is needed on the sand from age 16.

What people fish for

Surfperch are the mainstay, best from October to June. Fish the incoming tide near sand crab beds. Limit 20 in all and 10 of a species, but only 5 in San Francisco and San Pablo bays, which are closed to surfperch from 1 April to 31 July.

Striped bass from the San Francisco beaches: CDFW says summer surf casting there “is occasionally very successful.” Limit 2, 18-inch minimum north of Point Conception.

California halibut from May to August in central California; limit 2 north of Point Sur, 22-inch minimum.

Rock fishing. State Parks names Portuguese and Schoolhouse beaches on the Sonoma Coast, and Salmon Creek for surf fishing. Shore anglers are exempt from the rockfish calendar, but this is the fishing that gets people hurt.

Come home from it

We do not usually lead with warnings. On this coast we do.

Sneaker waves, cold water and bars

Sneaker waves. Bigger waves arrive without warning after long quiet spells. Never turn your back on the ocean, stay much further back than seems necessary, and never stand on logs on the beach. A log can roll on you in a moment.

Do not go in after anything. Not a rod, not a dog. The water is cold enough to stop a strong swimmer, and State Parks says Sonoma Coast beaches are not for swimming.

Rocks. Duncan’s Landing is, in State Parks’ words, the most dangerous point on the Sonoma Coast. Wear a Coast Guard approved life jacket if you wade, as CDFW advises. Fish with someone else.

On a boat. Harbour bars are dangerous on an outgoing tide, and captains cancel when conditions turn. Let them. A cancelled trip is a good captain at work.

Confirmed with the National Weather Service and California State Parks, 4 November 2026 — NWS: “Never turn your back on the ocean.” State Parks’ ocean safety page says most victims were climbing on rocks and cliffs, playing near or in the surf, or shore fishing. Point Arena has warned that this winter’s El Niño could bring bigger waves and strong storms.

Where to stay

Every town below is pinned to its own Trip.com city number, so the button opens that California town’s hotels — not a namesake in another state.

A harbour for every kind of trip

Easiest: Avila Beach, Morro Bay, Monterey and Santa Cruz are all good holiday towns with a boat as a bonus, and Monterey’s four-hour afternoon trips fit a sightseeing day. From San Francisco: Half Moon Bay or Emeryville. For the working coast: Bodega Bay, Fort Bragg, Eureka, Trinidad and Crescent City, where small boats and big fish make the long drive worthwhile.

Three or four of you? Take a house, not four hotel rooms. A cabin at Bodega Bay or Fort Bragg gives you a kitchen, a big pot for the crab, and somewhere to dry your gear. Our vacation homes guide has the questions to ask before you pay.

Questions

When does Dungeness crab season open for sport anglers in 2026?

Saturday 7 November 2026, but with limits. Crab traps are restricted from the Oregon border to Point Conception until at least 1 January 2027; hoop nets and crab snares are allowed. The opening is delayed from Cape Mendocino to Ten Mile State Marine Reserve for domoic acid. The limit is 10, at 5¾ inches.

Is ocean salmon fishing open in California?

Not now. Ocean salmon opened in stages in 2026 after a very short 2025, and it has closed for the rest of the year in every area. CDFW expects to announce 2027 dates in April 2027.

When can you fish for rockfish in Northern California?

From a boat north of Point Conception, 1 April to 31 December, with no depth limit in 2026. It is closed to boats from 1 January to 31 March. Shore anglers are exempt from those dates. The limit is 10 in combination, with zero for quillback, yelloweye, cowcod and bronzespotted.

Is Pacifica Pier open?

No. The City of Pacifica closed the pier on 4 June 2026, and the stabilisation work reported in October 2026 will not reopen it. The Berkeley Pier has also been closed since 2015. CDFW’s older web pages still recommend both.

Can I keep a sturgeon in San Francisco Bay?

No. White sturgeon are catch and release only, with a zero daily and annual limit, a report card and a single barbless hook, and green sturgeon may not be taken at all. Emeryville Sportfishing runs release trips.

Can I catch crab inside San Francisco Bay?

Not Dungeness crab: taking them in San Francisco and San Pablo bays is not allowed. The Park Service says red and rock crabs are caught from the Fort Baker pier at Horseshoe Cove.

How dangerous is fishing from the rocks on the north coast?

It is the most dangerous fishing in this guide. California State Parks says most ocean victims were climbing on rocks, playing near the surf or shore fishing. The National Weather Service warns that sneaker waves can surge dozens of feet higher without warning. Never turn your back on the ocean, and stay well back.
