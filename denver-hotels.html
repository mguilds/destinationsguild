Where to Stay in Denver — LoDo, Downtown, RiNo & Cherry Creek

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Denver is the Mile High City — Visit Denver, the official visitor bureau, puts it at 5,280 feet above sea level — and the centre is compact enough to walk. The airport train runs straight into Denver Union Station, and a free electric bus runs the length of downtown. Where you sleep decides whether you walk to dinner or drive to it. LoDo and downtown are the easy first-visit choice, Capitol Hill is for museums, RiNo is for art and breweries, and Cherry Creek is for shopping and a quieter night. This page sorts the areas and names eight hotels we checked on their own websites today.

Choosing Your Area in Denver

Six bases, each suited to a different trip.

LoDo and Union Station

Lower Downtown, the old warehouse district around Larimer Square and the restored Denver Union Station. Visit Denver describes Victorian buildings now filled with restaurants, galleries, shops, bars, brewpubs and coffee houses.

Stay here for a first visit, for evenings out on foot, and for the train to and from the airport, which ends at Union Station.

Downtown and the 16th Street promenade

The business and shopping core, with the Denver Performing Arts Complex, the Colorado Convention Center and Denver Pavilions. Visit Denver points to the free, fully electric 16th Street FreeRide buses for getting up and down the promenade.

Stay here for shows, conventions and the widest choice of rooms in walking distance of everything.

Capitol Hill and the Golden Triangle

Just southeast of downtown. Visit Denver names the gold-domed Colorado State Capitol, with its “Mile High” marker on the 13th step, the Denver Art Museum, the Clyfford Still Museum and the Molly Brown House Museum among old mansions.

Stay nearby for museum days. We have not listed a hotel here yet; downtown hotels are a walk away.

RiNo — River North Art District

North of downtown. Visit Denver describes historic warehouses and factories that now hold jazz bars, restaurants, brewpubs, galleries and working studios, with street-art murals around almost every corner.

Stay here for an arty, lively base with a younger feel. Expect a ride or a longer walk into the centre.

The Highlands and LoHi

Across the river to the northwest. Visit Denver describes Victorian-era homes, independent shops, galleries and some of the city’s most inventive restaurants, with views back to downtown from Lower Highland.

A good area to eat in, close to LoDo. We have not listed a hotel here yet.

Cherry Creek

Visit Denver puts Cherry Creek five minutes from downtown, with Cherry Creek North and the Cherry Creek Shopping Center offering more than 300 stores and 75 cafes and restaurants between them.

Stay here for shopping, a calmer evening and a more residential feel. A car or a short ride makes it easiest.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Denver details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Denver

The airport is Denver International (DEN). Visit Denver explains that the A Line airport train runs from the airport directly to Denver Union Station in the heart of downtown, and that buses, taxis, rideshares and shuttles also serve the airport (read 26 November 2026). If you stay in LoDo or downtown, the train is the simple way in.

Getting around

Downtown is walkable, and the free 16th Street FreeRide buses run the length of the promenade. RTD buses and light rail reach further out. RiNo, the Highlands and Cherry Creek are a short ride from the centre. If you bring a car, ask your hotel about parking before you book.

When to go

Visit Denver says the city sees more than 300 days of sunshine a year and only 8 to 15 inches of rain and snow. It gives an average winter daily high of 45°F, with 60-degree days not unusual, and an average August high of 86°F with low humidity and cool evenings (read 26 November 2026). Visit Denver adds that snow does not stay on the ground long in the city, so a city stay works in any season.

A word on the altitude

Visit Denver notes that the air at a mile high is thinner and drier, though most visitors barely notice. Drink plenty of water on your first day and go easy on hard exercise until you feel at home.

Denver Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in Denver?

LoDo or downtown for a first visit and easy walking. Capitol Hill for the museums. RiNo for art, murals and breweries. Cherry Creek for shopping and a quieter evening.

How do I get from Denver airport to downtown?

Take the A Line airport train from Denver International Airport to Denver Union Station in downtown. The Crawford Hotel is inside Union Station, and LoDo and downtown hotels are a short walk or ride from it.

Do I need a car in Denver?

Not if you stay downtown or in LoDo: the centre is walkable and the 16th Street FreeRide is free. A car helps for Cherry Creek, the Highlands and day trips into the mountains. Ask your hotel about parking before you book.

Will the altitude bother me in Denver?

Denver sits at 5,280 feet. Visit Denver says the air is thinner and drier but most visitors barely notice. Drink plenty of water and take it easy on your first day.

Is there a budget place to stay in downtown Denver?

Yes. Hostel Fish, listed above, offers bunk beds and private rooms downtown, with a guest kitchen and lounge.
