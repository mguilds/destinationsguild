Soufrière

Where to Stay in Soufrière

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Soufrière is the reason most people remember Saint Lucia. The old French capital sits in the collapsed crater of a volcano on the south-west coast, with Petit Piton rising straight out of the sea at one end of the bay and Gros Piton behind it — the pair protected as a UNESCO World Heritage site since 2004. Around the town are the Sulphur Springs with their steaming vents, Diamond Falls and its botanical garden, cocoa estates in the hills and the dark volcanic sand of Anse Chastanet and Anse Mamin. The hotels here have the finest views in the Caribbean and a great many steps to reach them, and the evenings are quiet. It is scenery first, convenience second.

The areas, and which one suits you

Soufrière is a small town with hotels scattered across the hills and bays around it, and where you land changes the holiday a good deal.

The town itself

A working waterfront town of French colonial streets, a church square, a fish market and small guesthouses. Cheap, friendly and real, with the Pitons at the end of the street. You will hear the town rather than the sea, and a tender from a cruise ship occasionally fills it for a few hours.

Anse Chastanet and Anse Mamin

Two dark-sand bays north of the town, reached by a rough road, with good snorkelling straight off the beach and a rainforest valley behind. This is the quiet, barefoot end of Soufrière: little to do in the evening beyond dinner where you are staying, and all the better for some people.

The Piton hillsides

The ridges between and above the two peaks, where the famous open-walled rooms are built looking straight down at the sea. The views are the reason to come and they are not exaggerated. Everything here involves steps, slopes and a shuttle, and you are a drive from anywhere else.

The estates in the hills

Old cocoa estates behind the town, several of which take guests in cottages among the trees. Cooler, green, full of birds, and a short drive from both the town and the springs. Good value and the most restful option.

Malgretoute and the Sugar Beach bay

The bay directly between the Pitons, with imported pale sand and a resort behind it. The setting is as good as it gets on the island; the access road is steep.

Getting about

Minibuses run to Castries and to Vieux Fort from the town, slowly. Water taxis go along the coast and are the pleasantest way to arrive. Taxi fares are set by zone — agree them first. Reckon forty-five minutes to the international airport at Vieux Fort, an hour and a quarter to Castries and an hour and a half to Rodney Bay.

What your money is worth in Soufrière
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Saint Lucia uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged at EC$2.70 to the US dollar, so EC$27 is about ten US dollars. Soufrière is a town of two economies: the hillside hotels are among the most expensive rooms in the Caribbean, while the restaurants, guesthouses and market in the town below are priced for the people who live there. Nearly every attraction here is ticketed — the Sulphur Springs, Diamond Falls, the Tet Paul trail, the Gros Piton climb — and each is a modest fee rather than a large one. Guides expect to be tipped. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills; ask what is included when you book, because here more than anywhere else the headline rate is not the total.

Ladera’s own site gives its address as Rabot Estate, Soufrière, and states plainly what makes it unusual: it was built by hand between the two Pitons and is the only resort inside the UNESCO World Heritage site itself. Travelling in your later years

This is the hardest part of Saint Lucia to get around, and the most beautiful. Both facts matter.

Soufrière is built on the inside of a crater and its hotels are built down hillsides. Rooms are reached by steps and paths, restaurants are often on a different level from the bedrooms, the beach is usually below both, and buggies help but do not remove the climb. Before booking anywhere here, ask these questions and get real answers: how many steps lie between the room and the dining room, is there a lift, is there a buggy and does it run in the evening, and how far is it to the sand. If the answers are discouraging, the estates in the hills and the small hotels in the town sit on gentler ground than the famous ridge properties, and the flattest, easiest base on the island is up north at Rodney Bay. The compensation for all this is the one thing nowhere else has: the two peaks out of your window. Also worth knowing — Soufrière is only about forty-five minutes from the international airport at Vieux Fort, which makes arrival here far easier than arrival in the north.

Fishing here

The water off Soufrière drops away steeply within a short distance of the shore, which is why the coast here is known for diving and snorkelling rather than for shallow-water fishing. Charter boats out of the bay and from Rodney Bay in the north fish the blue water for marlin, sailfish, wahoo and dorado. What this coast does not have is the sand flats that bonefish need — for those, see our guide to fishing the flats, and The Guild Fishing Library for the whole shelf: lakes, rivers and salt.

Questions about staying in Soufriere

Is Soufriere worth staying in, or just visiting for the day?

Worth staying, if the scenery is why you came. A day trip from the north means three hours in a car for a few hours on the ground, and the town and the Pitons are at their best early in the morning and in the last hour of light, which a day trip misses entirely. Two or three nights here, with the rest of the week in the north, is the trip we would book.

Which hotels have the Piton view?

The ones built on the ridges and hillsides between and above the two peaks, and the bay directly between them. That view is what you are paying for, and it is genuinely one of the great hotel views anywhere. It comes with steep ground, steps and a shuttle, so read the access description carefully before you book - the view is on the terrace, not at the bottom of the hill.

Can you swim at Soufriere?

Yes, on this coast the water is calm and deep. The sand is dark volcanic grey rather than white, and the bays - Anse Chastanet, Anse Mamin, the bay between the Pitons - have good snorkelling close in, with reef not far from the shore. It is not the long pale beach of the north; it is better snorkelling and a far better setting.

What is there to do in Soufriere besides look at the Pitons?

A good deal within half an hour: the Sulphur Springs with its vents and mud baths, the Diamond Falls Botanical Gardens, the Toraille waterfall, the Tet Paul Nature Trail with the classic view of both peaks, the guided climb up Gros Piton, cocoa estate tours with the chocolate at the end, and boat trips along the coast. Most are ticketed and most are quietest early or late.

Are the Sulphur Springs busy?

In the middle of the day, yes, particularly when a large ship is in Castries and the excursion buses come down. The site is small and the car park fills. Go in the first hour it is open or in the late afternoon and it is a different place. The same advice applies to Diamond Falls.

How long is the drive from the airport?

About forty-five minutes from Hewanorra International at Vieux Fort, which is much the shorter transfer and a real argument for starting or ending a trip here rather than in the north. Castries is about an hour and a quarter away, Rodney Bay about an hour and a half, both on a winding coast road that takes longer than the distance suggests.
