Why see Washington from the water

The Potomac gives you the view of the capital that most visitors never see.

From the deck of a boat on the Potomac River, Washington's landmarks line up one after another: the Lincoln Memorial and the Kennedy Center, the Jefferson Memorial and the Washington Monument, with Arlington and the hills of Virginia on the other shore. It is a relaxing way to see the city, and a welcome rest for tired feet after a day on the National Mall.

The river is busiest between spring and autumn. In winter, many sightseeing sailings run less often or stop for the season, while dinner and holiday cruises carry on, so check the schedule before you plan your day around a boat.

Water taxis on the Potomac

Water taxis link several of the region's waterfronts, including the Wharf on Washington's Southwest waterfront, Georgetown and Old Town Alexandria in Virginia. They are a pleasant and practical way to reach Old Town's brick streets, shops and restaurants, with the city skyline behind you.

Routes and timetables change with the season. The bus-and-water-taxi pass above is the easiest way to book both together through our partner.

By boat to Mount Vernon

George Washington's home at Mount Vernon sits on the Potomac south of Old Town Alexandria. In season, sightseeing boats sail there from the Alexandria waterfront, which makes a memorable day out. These sailings are not sold through our partner, so check Mount Vernon's official website, mountvernon.org, for current boat options and admission.

Tips from the Guild

Sit on the right side

Ask which side of the boat faces the monuments on your route, and get there early for a seat by the rail.

Bring a layer

It is always cooler and windier on the water than on shore, even in summer.

Time it for sunset

An evening cruise lets you see the monuments by day and lit up at night on the same sailing.

Check the season

Many sightseeing boats run from spring to autumn only. Dinner and holiday cruises run more of the year.

Potomac River tours FAQ

Can you take a boat tour of the monuments in Washington, D.C.?

Yes. Narrated sightseeing cruises on the Potomac River pass many of Washington's landmarks, including the Lincoln Memorial, the Kennedy Center and the Jefferson Memorial.

Are there dinner cruises in Washington, D.C.?

Yes. Several dinner and brunch cruises sail on the Potomac, many from the Wharf on Washington's Southwest waterfront.

Is there a water taxi from Washington, D.C. to Old Town Alexandria?

Yes. Water taxis link waterfronts including the Wharf, Georgetown and Old Town Alexandria. Routes and timetables change with the season.

Can you take a boat to Mount Vernon?

In season, sightseeing boats sail to Mount Vernon from the Old Town Alexandria waterfront. Check mountvernon.org for current options.

Do Potomac River cruises run in winter?

Many sightseeing sailings run less often or pause in winter, while dinner and holiday cruises continue. Check the schedule before you book.
