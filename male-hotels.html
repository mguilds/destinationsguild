Where to Stay in Malé

Where to Stay in Malé — The Capital Island

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Malé is not what people picture when they picture the Maldives, and that is the reason to spend a night in it. A hundred thousand people live on a single square mile of reclaimed coral, in a city of tall buildings, narrow streets, scooters and mosques, with the Indian Ocean on every side and almost no sand. It is the country’s only city, and it is where you see how Maldivians actually live. Stay on the north waterfront for the view and the ferries, behind the harbour for the cheapest rooms and the best food, and remember that the airport is on a different island, five minutes away across a bridge.

Choosing Your Area in Malé

The island is small enough to walk across, so this is about aspect and noise more than distance.

The north waterfront (Henveiru)

The strip facing the harbour and the airport island, and the pleasantest part of the city to stand in. The ferry terminals, the fish market, the presidential quarter and most of the better-known hotels are along here, and the rooms that face north look across the water to the runway lights.

It is also the busy side: boat engines start early and the road behind it runs all day. Ask for a high floor.

Behind the harbour and the inner streets

A few blocks in, the city tightens into narrow lanes of shops, cafes and apartment blocks. This is where the cheapest decent rooms are, where Maldivians eat, and where a short-eats cafe will feed you properly for the price of a coffee at a resort.

No views to speak of and no beach, but you are five minutes from everything and paying a fraction of the waterfront rate.

The south side and the artificial beach

The southern and eastern shore has the seawall promenade and the stretch of imported sand the city uses as its beach. It is a genuinely nice place at six in the evening, when half of Malé comes out to sit on the wall, and there are rooms within a short walk of it.

It is calmer than the harbour side. It is still a city beach, not an island one — if swimming matters, that is what Hulhumalé is for.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a night in Malé goes.

The airport is on another island, and a bridge now connects them

Velana International Airport is on Hulhulé, not on Malé. Since the Sinamalé Bridge opened, the capital and the reclaimed island of Hulhumalé are both a short road journey from the terminal rather than a ferry crossing. Taxis queue outside arrivals; a public bus and a passenger ferry also run and cost very little.

If your onward island is reached by seaplane, remember those fly in daylight only — a night in Malé or Hulhumalé is often not a choice but the schedule.

It is a Muslim capital, and the rules are real

No alcohol is sold on the island and none may be brought in; it is held at customs and returned on departure. Dress is modest in the street — shoulders and knees covered is the simple rule — and swimwear belongs only on the marked part of the artificial beach.

Nobody will be rude to you about any of this. It is simply the country’s own law, and knowing it before you pack saves an awkward first hour.

What the Guild would actually do with the time

Go to the fish market in the late afternoon when the dhonis land and the tuna come up the quay on men’s shoulders. See the old Friday Mosque, built out of carved coral stone, from the outside at least. Walk the seawall at dusk. Eat in a short-eats cafe.

That is an evening, and it is the most Maldivian evening in the country. Then go to your island in the morning.

Malé Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in the capital.

Is it worth staying in Mal&eacute; itself?

Yes, for a night or two, and no as a beach holiday — they are different questions. Malé is a working capital city, not a resort: a square mile of tall buildings, scooters, mosques and a very good fish market, with no beach to speak of. It is worth a night at the start or end of a trip to see how the country actually lives, and it is cheap by Maldivian standards. If what you want is sand outside the door, stay on Hulhumalé or go straight out to an island.

Is Mal&eacute; the same island as the airport?

No, and this surprises people. Velana International Airport sits on its own island, Hulhulé, with Malé on one side and the reclaimed island of Hulhumalé on the other. All three are now joined by the Sinamalé Bridge, so a taxi from the terminal to a hotel in Malé is a short drive rather than a boat. A public ferry runs as well and costs very little.

Can you drink alcohol in Mal&eacute;?

No. Malé is an inhabited island in a Muslim country and alcohol is not sold there, nor may you bring it in — it is taken at customs on arrival and returned when you leave. The exceptions in the greater Malé area are the hotel on the airport island and the resorts and liveaboards further out. Plan around it rather than being caught out by it.

How long do you need in Mal&eacute;?

An afternoon and a night covers it comfortably. The old Friday Mosque built of carved coral stone, the fish market at the hour the boats come in, the waterfront, the artificial beach and a walk through the narrow streets behind the harbour are the whole of it, and the island is small enough to cross on foot in twenty minutes. Anything longer and you are using Malé as a base rather than seeing it.
