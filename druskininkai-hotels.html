Druskininkai

Where to Stay in Druskininkai

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Druskininkai is Lithuania’s oldest and largest spa town, set in the pine forest of Dzukija where the Nemunas makes a wide bend in the far south of the country, about two hours from Vilnius by coach and close to the Belarusian border. Its salt-mineral springs have been treated as medicine since the early nineteenth century — the name comes from the Lithuanian word for salt — and the sanatoria and spa hotels that grew up around them are still the business of the place. M.K. Ciurlionis, the composer and painter, grew up here and the family house is a museum. It is flat, quiet, green and unlike anywhere else in the country.

The spa centre and Vilniaus aleja

Most centralWalk to the springsLevel

The heart of the town is a few flat, tree-lined streets between the mineral-water pavilion, the park and the pedestrian stretch of Vilniaus aleja: the sanatoria and spa hotels, the restaurants, the church, the little lake and the drinking fountain where people still come to take the water. Almost all of the town’s larger places to stay are here, most of them running treatment and spa programmes rather than simply having a pool, and everything is within a level ten-minute walk. For a first visit or a short one, stay here.

The Nemunas riverside and the park

QuietestRiver walksShort walk in

The Nemunas makes a wide bend below the town and the ground between the two is park, pine wood and promenade, with paths along the water, a sculpture trail and the Ciurlionis family house — the composer and painter grew up here — a few minutes away. Guesthouses and apartments sit among the trees. It is five to ten minutes into the centre on the flat, it is quiet at night, and it is the nicest side of Druskininkai for a stay of more than two or three days.

The Snow Arena and water park side

Best for familiesIndoor snow all yearCable car or bus

Across the river stands an indoor ski slope that runs all year, with a water park and an adventure park near it, and a cable car crossing the Nemunas to reach them. It is, frankly, an unusual thing to find in a nineteenth-century spa town in a pine forest, and it is the reason a lot of Lithuanian families come. Accommodation on this side is modern and geared to that, which suits children and suits anybody looking for a sanatorium rather less.

Out of town: Liskiava, Grutas and the Dzukija forest

Furthest outCar neededForest and river

Druskininkai sits inside the pine forest of Dzukija, and the countryside around it is the best reason to have a car here. Liskiava, a few kilometres up the Nemunas, has a baroque church on a bluff above a river loop. Grutas Park, twenty minutes away, is where the Lenin and Stalin statues taken down in 1991 were collected and set out among the trees, and it is a stranger hour than it sounds. Dzukija National Park runs north from the town through villages that still live partly on mushrooms and honey. Getting around Druskininkai

On foot

The centre is small, flat and almost entirely walkable, and the paths through the park and along the river are the point of being here. Nothing in the town itself needs transport.

Bicycles and the forest trails

Druskininkai has a good network of cycle paths that run out along the Nemunas and into the pine forest, and bicycles are easy to hire. It is flat, sandy-soiled and shaded, which makes it one of the easier places in the country to ride with children or after a long gap.

The cable car

A cable car crosses the Nemunas from near the centre to the Snow Arena side, which saves a long way round by road and is a pleasant five minutes in its own right. Hours are seasonal, so check before counting on it for a return trip in the evening.

Coaches, and the missing railway

Druskininkai has no useful passenger train, so coaches do the work: roughly two hours from Vilnius and a similar time from Kaunas, running through the day. The coach station is in the middle of town. For the national park, Grutas and the villages, a car makes a real difference, and the roads are quiet.

The border

The town is close to the Belarusian frontier, which is an external border of the European Union and closed to casual crossing. If a map shows you a short cut east or south, ignore it, and keep to the Lithuanian side of the river where the signs tell you to.

When to come

Druskininkai is a year-round town, which few places in Lithuania are, because the sanatoria do not close. January to April is the busiest spa season, when Lithuanians come for treatment and the forest is under snow; it is also the cheapest time for an ordinary hotel room. May and June bring the forest into leaf, the cycle paths into use and light until late. July and August are warm, green and busy with families, and the Snow Arena is at its most incongruous. September is our pick: warm, dry, the pines turning at the edges and the mushroom season beginning, which in Dzukija is close to a civic event. October to December is grey and wet, and best spent indoors in a bathhouse, which is of course what the town is for.

Getting in: what changed, and what has not

Europe’s Entry/Exit System is now in force. Since 10 April 2026 it has operated at every Schengen border crossing and passport stamps have been discontinued. On your first crossing you will have your fingerprints taken and your photograph made at the border, which adds a few minutes; after that the check is quicker. There is nothing to apply for and nothing to pay.

ETIAS - the travel authorisation you may have read about, expected to cost around EUR 20 and last three years - is not in force yet. No applications are being taken, and the European Union has not announced the exact start date; it has said it will give several months’ notice before the system goes live. It has been postponed repeatedly and no start date is confirmed, so treat it as coming rather than cancelled, and ignore any website offering to sell you one today - nobody can issue one yet. When it does begin there will be six months in which you can travel without one, then six months in which only first-time arrivals are exempt.

What this means for your trip right now: you do not need ETIAS. You do need a passport issued within the last ten years and valid for at least three months beyond the date you plan to leave.

Lithuania is in the European Union, the Schengen area and the euro, so arriving from anywhere else in Schengen there is no check at all. Month-by-month weather and the dates worth planning around are in Best Time to Visit Lithuania, and the country guide is The Lithuania Travel Guide.

Druskininkai — questions

What actually happens at a Lithuanian spa town?

Rather more medicine than pampering, and that is the appeal. Druskininkai grew up in the early nineteenth century around salt-mineral springs and peat mud, and the sanatoria still run programmes of baths, mud, physiotherapy, inhalations and massage, often booked by the week and often with a doctor’s consultation at the start. You can equally book a hotel room and use a day spa. If you want treatment rather than a sauna, say so when you book, because the programmes fill.

Is it worth coming if I do not want a spa?

Yes, if you like walking in forest. The town is a pleasant, quiet, flat place in the middle of the Dzukija pine woods with the Nemunas running round it, the paths are excellent, Grutas Park and Liskiava are close, and the Ciurlionis house is a genuinely interesting hour. Two nights is about right on those terms. If you want nightlife or architecture, go elsewhere.

How do I get there, and how long does it take?

By coach from Vilnius or Kaunas, roughly two hours either way, several times a day, landing in the middle of town. There is no useful passenger railway. By car it is about an hour and three quarters from Vilnius on good roads. There is no airport; Vilnius is the one to fly into.

Is the Snow Arena any good, or is it a gimmick?

It is a real indoor slope that runs all year, with lifts, lessons and hire, and it is the reason a great many Lithuanian families book this town in July. It is not a mountain and it will not satisfy an experienced skier for a week. As a day for children, or a first lesson, or a strange hour in the middle of a hot summer, it does the job.

When should I come?

The spa season runs all year and the sanatoria are busiest in winter and early spring, when Lithuanians come for treatment. For the walking and the river, May to September is the time, and September is the best of it: warm, dry, the forest turning and the mushroom season beginning, which here is a national pastime rather than a hobby.

Is Grutas Park disrespectful?

It is argued about locally, which is worth knowing before you go. The statues of Lenin, Stalin and the rest were pulled down in 1991 and a private collector gathered them here rather than let them be melted, and the park presents them with the history of the deportations and the resistance alongside. Some people think an open-air collection trivialises what those figures did; others think the only mistake would have been to forget. Go, read the panels, and make up your own mind.

Can I combine it with anything else?

It pairs naturally with Vilnius, two hours away, and with the Dzukija forest and the Nemunas loop villages around it. Going on from here to the coast means crossing the whole country, so Druskininkai is better treated as the southern end of a trip than as a stop in the middle of one.
