Adelaide’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Adelaide — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Adelaide is the easiest Australian capital to read, because it was drawn on a grid and ringed with parkland, and the whole centre is a mile square. North Terrace is the cultural edge of that square — the art gallery, the museum, the state library and the university stand in a row, and most of them are free to enter. The East End, around Rundle Street, is where the restaurants and the small bars are; Rundle Mall is the shopping spine one block west. North Adelaide, over the Torrens and the parklands, is quieter, leafier and holds Adelaide Oval. Nothing in the square mile is more than a twenty-minute walk from anything else, so the address matters less here than in Sydney or Melbourne — choose by the kind of room you want. Luxury

Adelaide only recently acquired a hotel that competes with the eastern capitals, and it sits on the river between the city and the Oval.

Eos by SkyCity

$$$$ LuxuryRiverbankFestival DriveRiver and parkland views

The best rooms in South Australia, in a tower on Festival Drive between the Torrens and North Terrace, with large, quiet, properly finished rooms, a serious spa and pool, and several of the city’s most ambitious restaurants in the building. The footbridge to Adelaide Oval is at the door, the Festival Centre and the art gallery are a two-minute walk, and the railway station is next door for the Glenelg tram and the airport bus. For a special few days in Adelaide, this is the address.

Boutique

Adelaide kept its sandstone, and two of the city’s most interesting rooms are inside buildings that were never meant to be hotels.

Mayfair Hotel Adelaide

$$$ UpscaleCBD45 King William StreetRestored 1930s building

Trip.com files this one as Mayfair Hotel Adelaide by IHG. It occupies a restored heritage building on the corner of King William Street and Hindley Street, in the exact centre of the grid, with high ceilings, dark joinery and a rooftop bar that looks along King William. Rooms are individually shaped rather than uniform, which is much of the appeal. Rundle Mall is a block east, the Central Market a few minutes south, and the tram stops outside the door for Glenelg and the Entertainment Centre.

Oval Hotel at Adelaide Oval

$$$ UpscaleNorth AdelaideKing William RoadBuilt into the Oval

Built into the western stand of Adelaide Oval itself, so a good number of rooms look straight down at the ground — which is either the best idea in Australian hospitality or an irrelevance, depending entirely on whether you follow the cricket. Budget & Value

Adelaide is the best-value mainland capital for a hotel room, and the two below are the dependable central choices.

Crowne Plaza Adelaide

$$ Mid-rangeEast End27 Frome StreetRooftop pool

Trip.com files this one as Crowne Plaza ADELAIDE by IHG. A newer tower on Frome Street at the eastern edge of the grid, with a rooftop pool and bar, crisp modern rooms and views over the parklands from the upper floors. Rundle Street’s restaurants are around the corner, the Botanic Garden is a two-minute walk, and the whole cultural row on North Terrace is five minutes away. Consistently the most comfortable room for the money in the city.

Family-friendly

Families want a kitchen and a second bedroom here, because Adelaide is a city you base yourself in for a week and drive out of each day.

Adina Apartment Hotel Adelaide Treasury

$$$ UpscaleCBD2 Flinders StreetApartments with kitchens

Apartments inside the old State Treasury building on Flinders Street, a block from Victoria Square, with proper kitchens, laundries and separate bedrooms in the larger configurations, and an indoor pool in the vaulted basement. The heritage shell means the rooms vary in shape and some are more interesting than others. The tram stops in Victoria Square, the Central Market is a five-minute walk, and the city is flat in every direction.

Adults-only

Adelaide has no significant hotel that enforces a strict adults-only age policy, and we will not invent one. For a quiet, grown-up stay, ask for a high parkland-facing room at Eos or the Crowne Plaza, or one of the shaped heritage rooms at the Mayfair, and request a floor away from the pool and the function rooms. Adelaide is a quiet city at night by Australian standards, which does most of the work for you.

Spa & pool

The spa and pool at Eos by SkyCity are the best in South Australia, and the rooftop pool at the Crowne Plaza is the pleasantest cheap swim in the city; both are described above. If you want the real South Australian water cure, take the tram to Glenelg — about half an hour from Victoria Square — and swim off the jetty beach instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask us most when choosing where to stay in Adelaide.

What is the best area to stay in Adelaide for first-time visitors?

Anywhere inside the square mile, which is the useful answer, because the grid is flat and small. If you want to be precise: the East End around Rundle Street for restaurants and small bars, North Terrace for the museums and the galleries, and North Adelaide over the parklands if you want quiet and Adelaide Oval. Avoid choosing on distance alone — nothing in the centre is far from anything else.

How much do Adelaide hotels cost?

Expect roughly 110 to 180 Australian dollars a night for clean value rooms, about 180 to 300 for comfortable four-star and apartment stays, and from around 400 upward for the best suites. Adelaide is the cheapest of the mainland capitals most of the year. The exceptions are real: rates jump hard for the Adelaide Festival and Fringe in late February and March, for the Gather Round football weekend, and for Test cricket at the Oval.

Is Adelaide a good base for the wine regions?

It is the best in Australia. The Adelaide Hills start twenty minutes from the city, McLaren Vale is about forty-five minutes south and the Barossa Valley about an hour north-east, so you can sleep in the city all week and drive to a different region each day. Hire a car or take a tour, and do not plan on driving yourself to more than two or three cellar doors in a day.

What is free to visit in central Adelaide?

More than in any other Australian capital. The Art Gallery of South Australia, the South Australian Museum and the State Library stand in a row on North Terrace and have long been free to enter, and the Adelaide Botanic Garden at the east end is free as well. The Adelaide Central Market costs nothing to walk through and is the best hour in the city. Confirm opening days before you go, because they vary.

Which Adelaide hotels take dogs?

We are not naming one until we have read the policy on the property’s own site, because the fees, weight caps and room conditions vary too much for a general answer to help. Shortlist from the city list on this page and telephone to confirm.

Do I need a car in Adelaide?

Not for the city itself — the grid is walkable, the tram runs from North Terrace through the centre to Glenelg, and the free City Connector bus has long looped the square mile; confirm the current fares when you arrive. You do need a car, or a tour, for the wine regions and the Fleurieu Peninsula, and that is the main reason to visit South Australia at all.
