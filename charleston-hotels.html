Where to Stay in Charleston — Historic District, Marion Square & the Beaches

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Charleston is a small city with a very old heart. The city government gives its founding as 1670, and most visitors spend their days on foot in the historic streets of the peninsula, between the City Market, King Street and the harbour. Where you sleep decides how much of that you can walk to. Stay in the Historic District to step out of the door into it; stay near Marion Square for King Street shopping and a little more room; or stay out on the beaches and drive in. This page sorts the areas and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in Charleston

Four bases, each suited to a different trip.

The Historic District, by the City Market

The lower peninsula: Meeting Street, Queen Street, the City Market and the waterfront. The City of Charleston’s own visitor pages point to its parks, museums and “seven miles of waterfront”, and to the Dock Street Theatre, which it calls America’s first purpose-built theatre.

Stay here for a first visit and for walking everywhere. Most of the hotels are historic buildings, many arranged around courtyards. The trade-off is parking: the city runs garages and lots on the peninsula, and many hotels charge for parking, so ask before you book.

Marion Square, Upper King Street and Ansonborough

A few blocks north of the Market, around Marion Square and the long run of shops and restaurants on King Street. Ansonborough, between Meeting Street and the harbour, is a quieter residential pocket. A good choice for shoppers, for couples and for anyone who wants the old town on foot with a slightly calmer street outside at night.

One practical note from the city: there is no street parking on King Street between Spring and John Streets on Thursday, Friday and Saturday nights from 6 PM to 3 AM; the city points drivers to its Midtown and Marion Square garages instead.

Mount Pleasant, across the harbour

The town across the harbour from the old city. The National Park Service runs Fort Sumter ferries from two places, one of them Patriots Point Naval and Maritime Museum in Mount Pleasant, and it recommends Patriots Point to travellers in RVs or other large vehicles. A practical base if you are driving and want easier parking than downtown.

The beaches: Folly Beach and Isle of Palms

Charleston has barrier-island beaches within a short drive. Two of the best known are Folly Beach and Isle of Palms; the City of Isle of Palms runs its own beach pages, including a beach shuttle and beach wheelchairs. Choose a beach base for a summer family holiday and plan to drive or take a ride into town for the historic sights.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Charleston details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Charleston

The airport is Charleston International Airport (CHS), at 5500 International Boulevard. Its own website lists the ways out: Uber and Lyft from a covered pick-up area across the roadways from baggage claim, a taxi stand in the centre median outside baggage claim, where a starter quotes the fare for your destination, and courtesy shuttles run by many local hotels. Ask your hotel whether it runs one.

Getting around

The Historic District is best on foot; the streets are short and the sights close together. If you bring a car, the city runs public garages and lots on the peninsula, and a hotel with its own parking is worth paying attention to. For Fort Sumter, the National Park Service says the ferries leave from the Fort Sumter Visitor Center at Liberty Square downtown and from Patriots Point in Mount Pleasant; private boats may not dock at the fort.

When to go

Charleston is a year-round city, and many travellers aim for spring or autumn for walking weather. Summer is hot and humid. The US National Hurricane Center gives the Atlantic hurricane season as 1 June to 30 November. If you travel in those months, especially to a beach hotel, choose a rate you can change and keep an eye on the forecast.

Charleston Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in Charleston?

The Historic District, near the City Market and Meeting Street, for a first visit and for walking everywhere. Marion Square and upper King Street for shopping and a slightly calmer night. Mount Pleasant if you are driving and want easier parking. Folly Beach or Isle of Palms for a beach holiday with the city a drive away.

Do I need a car in Charleston?

Not if you stay in the Historic District and spend your days downtown; the old town is best on foot. A car helps for the beaches, plantations and day trips. If you bring one, ask your hotel about parking before you book, because downtown parking is limited and usually charged.

Can I stay on the beach near Charleston?

Yes. Folly Beach and Isle of Palms are barrier-island beach towns within a short drive of the city. Tides Folly Beach, listed above, is an oceanfront hotel on Folly Beach where every room has an ocean view, by its own account.

How do I get to Fort Sumter from my hotel?

By ferry. The National Park Service says the ferries leave from the Fort Sumter Visitor Center at Liberty Square in downtown Charleston and from Patriots Point in Mount Pleasant, and there is a fee for the ride. Private boats may not dock at the fort.

When is hurricane season in Charleston?

The US National Hurricane Center gives the Atlantic hurricane season as 1 June to 30 November. Travellers in those months should book a rate they can change and watch the forecast.
