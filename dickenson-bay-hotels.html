Dickenson Bay

Where to Stay on Dickenson Bay

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Dickenson Bay is the beach most people picture when they picture Antigua: a long, gentle curve of pale sand on the north-western corner of the island, with calm shallow water, a line of hotels and beach bars set back behind the palms, and the sun going down straight out to sea. It is the busiest stretch of coast in the country and still not busy by Caribbean standards. It is also the easiest place on the island to spend a week with no car at all: you can swim, eat, drink, hire a paddleboard and get a taxi into St John’s ten minutes away without ever leaving the bay.

The areas, and which one suits you

Several distinct places go under this name, and they are not interchangeable.

The main beach

The middle of the curve, where the larger hotels and the beach bars are and where the water sports operate. Shallow, calm, shaded in patches, and lively in a mild way — vendors, music at the weekend, boats coming and going. This is the part people mean.

The north end

Quieter, with smaller properties and apartments and a stretch of sand that often has almost nobody on it. A short walk from everything, a good deal less expensive, and our pick for anyone who wants the bay without the bustle.

Runaway Bay

The next bay south, separated by a low headland. Longer, emptier and more exposed, with fewer hotels and a more local feel. Prices are lower and the water is just as calm; the trade-off is less on the doorstep.

Inland at Cedar Grove and Marble Hill

Up the slope behind the bay: apartments, villas and small guesthouses with a view and no beach, five to ten minutes’ walk down. Much cheaper, and an easy compromise if you do not mind the climb back up in the heat.

What Dickenson Bay is not

It is not remote, it is not undiscovered, and it is not the place for a wild Atlantic beach — for that you want Half Moon Bay or Long Bay on the east coast. It is also not the island’s restaurant quarter: that is English Harbour, forty-five minutes south.

Getting about

St John’s is about ten minutes by taxi and the airport about fifteen. Minibuses run along the main road at the top of the bay rather than along the beach. Taxi fares are set by zone — agree the figure before you get in. Most people here walk everywhere and hire a car for a day or two.

What your money is worth in Antigua
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Antigua and Barbuda uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged to the US dollar at EC$2.70 — a rate that has held for many years, so a price of EC$27 is about ten US dollars and no app is needed. US dollars are taken almost everywhere, but change often comes back in Eastern Caribbean dollars and paying in local money is usually very slightly better. Cards are fine in hotels, supermarkets and the larger restaurants; keep cash for minibuses, beach bars and the market. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills, so the final total sits above the headline rate — ask what is included when you book, and at an all-inclusive ask exactly what the word covers here, because it varies a great deal from one resort to the next.

Travelling in your later years

An honest assessment of this corner of the island.

Dickenson Bay is one of the gentlest bases in the eastern Caribbean for anyone who would rather not climb. The land behind the beach is flat, the hotels are laid out along the sand rather than up a slope, the water shelves gradually from a sandy bottom with no rocks and almost no surf, and the walk from most rooms to the sea is level and short.

The airport is about fifteen minutes away, which means you are in a chair by the water within the hour of landing. St John’s and the hospital are ten minutes in the other direction. And because so much is within walking distance of the bay — restaurants, bars, a pharmacy, water sports — you can have a full week here without once getting into a car.

What to watch: the inland apartments at Cedar Grove and Marble Hill are cheaper for a reason, and that reason is the walk back up the hill in the afternoon heat. Ask for a ground-floor room if stairs are a problem, since several of the smaller properties here have no lift. And take the heat seriously between July and October — the shade on this beach is patchy.

Questions about staying in Dickenson Bay

Is Dickenson Bay the best beach in Antigua?

It is the best all-round beach to stay on, which is not quite the same thing. It is long, calm, shallow, sheltered and has everything you need along it. For sheer beauty, Half Moon Bay on the east coast and the bays on the Five Islands peninsula are finer — but they have little or nothing built behind them, which is exactly why Dickenson Bay is where people sleep.

Is it crowded?

By Antiguan standards yes, by Caribbean standards no. In February there will be people on the sand, boats on the water and music at the beach bars at the weekend; it is cheerful rather than packed, and the north end is quiet even in high season. In September you may have most of it to yourself.

Can I manage here without a car?

Easily, and most people do. Restaurants, bars, water sports and a pharmacy are within walking distance, St John’s is a ten-minute taxi ride, and minibuses run along the main road. Hire a car for a day if you want to see English Harbour and the east coast, and give it back.

How is the swimming and snorkelling?

The swimming is excellent — calm, clear, shallow and sandy, with no surf most days, which is why families and nervous swimmers do well here. The snorkelling off the beach itself is modest; the good reefs are reached by boat, and trips run from the bay.

Is it noisy?

In the middle of the bay there is music from the beach bars, particularly at the weekend, and the water sports boats run during the day. It is not nightclub noise and it stops at a reasonable hour. If quiet matters, book at the north end or at Runaway Bay next door.

What about Runaway Bay instead?

A good choice if you want the same calm water for less money. It is the next bay south, longer and emptier, with fewer hotels and a more local feel, and the sunsets are the same ones. The trade-off is that there is much less within walking distance, so you will use taxis more.
