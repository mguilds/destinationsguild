Before you go

Acadia is compact, coastal and very busy in summer, and a few reservations and a free bus make all the difference.

Acadia is one of the smaller national parks, but few pack so much into so little ground. Most of it lies on Mount Desert Island, reached by a short causeway from the town of Trenton, with the town of Bar Harbor at its door. Granite mountains rise straight from the sea, and the 27-mile Park Loop Road strings together Sand Beach, Thunder Hole, Otter Cliffs and Jordan Pond. Smaller pieces of the park lie across the bay on the Schoodic Peninsula and offshore on Isle au Haut.

Everyone needs an entrance pass, whatever part of the park you visit. If you plan to drive to the top of Cadillac Mountain, you also need a separate vehicle reservation for the summit road from late May to late October, bought online in advance. Sunrise reservations go fast.

Summer traffic and parking are the park's biggest headache. Parking lots at Jordan Pond, Sand Beach and the Cadillac summit fill early on summer days. The answer, for many visitors, is the free Island Explorer bus, which links Bar Harbor, the other island villages and the park's main stops from late spring to mid-October.

Then there are the carriage roads: 45 miles of broken-stone roads built by John D. Rockefeller Jr., closed to cars and open to walkers, bicyclists and horses. They are the gentlest way into the park's interior, and one of the great pleasures of any national park.

Cadillac Summit Road reservation (2026): required for every vehicle, including motorcycles, from May 20 through October 25, 2026. It costs $6 and is sold only online at recreation.gov, never at the park, and you still need a park entrance pass. Thirty percent of each day's Sunrise and Daytime reservations go on sale at 10 a.m. Eastern 90 days ahead, and the other 70 percent at 10 a.m. two days ahead. Sunrise reservations give a 90-minute entry window (4 to 5:30 a.m. from May 20 through July, getting later through the fall); Daytime reservations give a 30-minute window. You are limited to one Sunrise reservation per vehicle every seven days and one Daytime reservation per vehicle per day. There is no set time to leave, but the road closes to vehicles at 9 p.m., and there is no re-entry. Save or print your QR code, because cell service is poor up there.

Size limits on Cadillac: vehicles over 21 feet long (counting bike racks and hitch attachments), RVs and trailers are not allowed on Cadillac Summit Road, even with a reservation. No reservation is needed if you walk or bicycle up, and the Island Explorer does not go to the summit.

Right now (late November 2026): the 2026 campgrounds, Hulls Cove Visitor Center and the Jordan Pond House have closed for the season, and the gravel park roads closed November 15. The paved Park Loop Road is scheduled to close to vehicles for the winter after December 1, 2026, and any section can close early for storms or ice. The carriage roads often close to all users during spring mud season, typically March and April.

Visitors from outside the U.S.: since January 1, 2026, Acadia is one of 11 national parks that charge non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older an extra $100 per person on top of the entrance fee. The America the Beautiful pass costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for nonresidents, and a pass covers that extra fee.

Best things to do in Acadia National Park

Six experiences to build your visit around, most within a short drive or bus ride of Bar Harbor.

Scenic drive

Park Loop Road

Twenty-seven miles past Sand Beach, Thunder Hole, Otter Cliffs and Jordan Pond. Much of the coastal stretch is one-way, so plan to stop as you go. Start early or ride the Island Explorer.

Highest point

Cadillac Mountain

At 1,530 feet, the top of Mount Desert Island, with views across Frenchman Bay. Sunrise here is famous; book a vehicle reservation, or watch sunset instead.

Car-free

The carriage roads

Forty-five miles of graded gravel roads and handsome stone bridges, for walking, bicycling and horses. Everyone yields to horses; only Class 1 e-bikes are allowed.

Tea & popovers

Jordan Pond

A clear lake under the twin Bubbles. The Jordan Pond House restaurant serves its traditional tea and popovers on the lawn in season (May 16 to October 23 in 2026).

Quiet side

Schoodic Peninsula

The only part of the park on the mainland, an hour's drive from Bar Harbor, with a loop road past surf-pounded granite and far fewer people.

Horse-drawn

Wildwood Stables

The park's stables near Jordan Pond, open late May to mid-October, offer horse-drawn carriage rides on the carriage roads, the way Rockefeller meant them to be seen.

From the Guild

Leave the car at your hotel. The Island Explorer is free, it stops at Jordan Pond, Sand Beach and the Village Green in Bar Harbor, and it saves you circling full parking lots. For Cadillac, set a reminder for 10 a.m. Eastern two days before your date, when most of the reservations are released.

When to visit Acadia

Summer (late June through August) is warm, green and crowded, with cool evenings and the occasional day of thick fog. Everything is open, the Island Explorer runs its full schedule, and Bar Harbor is at its liveliest. Book rooms and campsites well ahead.

Fall is Acadia's finest season. Leaves begin to turn in September and the park says color usually peaks in the first to third week of October. It is also very busy, especially on weekends, and the Island Explorer cuts back to a reduced fall schedule from mid-August and stops in mid-October.

Late spring (May to mid-June) is quieter, with blackflies in the woods and some facilities still opening; the carriage roads may be closed for mud season into spring. Winter is for the hardy: most of the Park Loop Road closes to cars after December 1, the campgrounds and visitor center are closed, and the park becomes a place for snowshoes, cross-country skis and solitude.

How to get to Acadia National Park

By car

From Boston, follow I-95 north to Augusta and Route 3 east through Ellsworth to Mount Desert Island, or stay on I-95 to Bangor and take Route 1A to Ellsworth, then Route 3. Mount Desert Island is about 50 miles from Bangor. The Park Loop Road begins at Hulls Cove Visitor Center, just north of Bar Harbor.

For the Schoodic Peninsula, follow US 1 north from Ellsworth for about 17 miles to Route 186, then follow the signs to the Schoodic Loop Road.

By air & bus

Hancock County Airport in Trenton, about 10 miles from the park, has direct flights from Boston's Logan Airport. Bangor International Airport is about an hour's drive. Both have rental cars.

In 2026 the free Island Explorer began spring service on May 20 between the Acadia Gateway Center in Trenton, the Village Green in Bar Harbor and the Park Loop Road, with full summer service from June 23 into mid-October. You still need a park entrance pass to ride into the park.

Where to stay near Acadia National Park

Bar Harbor puts you at the park's front door and on the Island Explorer routes. Ellsworth, on the mainland, is the budget-minded choice, with chain hotels and an easy drive onto the island.

Hotels & vacation homes

About 3 miles from Hulls Cove Visitor Center

The park's own town, with inns, motels, B&Bs and harbor-view hotels, plenty of restaurants, and the Island Explorer's hub on the Village Green. Rooms book up months ahead for summer and October.

Lodges inside the park

Acadia has no hotels or lodges inside the park. Overnight stays in the park itself are limited to its campgrounds: Blackwoods and Seawall on Mount Desert Island, Schoodic Woods on the Schoodic Peninsula, and the lean-to shelters at Duck Harbor on Isle au Haut. All other lodging is in the surrounding towns.

The Jordan Pond House is a restaurant, not an inn, but it is the park's one classic place to sit down to a meal, and lunch or afternoon popovers there is a tradition. For historic hotels elsewhere in the state, see our Historic Hotels of Maine guide.

RV travel & camping at Acadia

Three drive-in campgrounds, a 35-foot limit at the two on Mount Desert Island, and hookups only at Schoodic Woods.

Blackwoods campground, about 5 miles south of Bar Harbor, was open May 1 to October 19 in 2026. Seawall, on the quieter west side of the island near Southwest Harbor, and Schoodic Woods, on the Schoodic Peninsula, were open May 20 to October 12. Reserve on recreation.gov; summer and fall weekends sell out early. The park had not posted 2027 dates when we checked.

At Blackwoods and Seawall, RVs may be no more than 35 feet long and 11 feet high. Neither has hookups, but each has a dump station. Schoodic Woods is the RV-friendly choice, with water and electric hookups at its RV sites and a dump station that also supplies water for on-board tanks.

None of the park campgrounds has showers; pay showers are available nearby, including in Otter Creek near Blackwoods. Remember that RVs, trailers and any vehicle over 21 feet are not allowed on Cadillac Summit Road, so leave the rig at camp and see the summit by car, by bicycle or on a guided bus tour.

For full hookups, the private RV parks and campgrounds along Route 3 between Ellsworth and Bar Harbor and around Mount Desert Island have many full-hookup sites, several on the water. Book early for July, August and foliage season.

Fishing in Acadia

A valid Maine fishing license is required to fish fresh water in the park; under the park's rules, Maine residents need one from age 16 and nonresidents from age 12. Licenses are sold by the State of Maine, not the park. Most of the island's larger lakes and ponds (Maine's "Great Ponds") are regulated by the State of Maine rather than by park rules.

Freshwater streams in the park on Mount Desert Island are closed to fishing from fall to spring (park pages give October 1 or October 31 to March 31), and Upper Hadlock Brook is closed all year. Saltwater fishing from the shore is managed by the State of Maine; anglers without a Maine freshwater license generally must sign up for the Maine Saltwater Recreational Fishing Registry. Ice fishing on the ponds is popular from about January through March. Check the state's current rules before you go.

Accessible travel at Acadia

What is available for visitors with limited mobility, and the passes that help.

The free Island Explorer buses all have wheelchair lifts (the Bicycle Express vans do not), and riders with disabilities can ask for a pickup up to three-quarters of a mile off the regular route. Bus stops are paved and generally level, though not always near accessible parking.

At Jordan Pond, the restaurant area and the boat launch are accessible, with five accessible parking spaces; the path around the pond itself is not. On the Ocean Path, the stretch from Sand Beach to Thunder Hole is concrete and packed gravel, and the Thunder Hole parking area is the easiest way onto it; beyond that the path turns rougher, with granite steps.

The park lists the Cadillac Summit Loop, Compass Harbor, the Jesup Path and Hemlock Path loop, Ocean Path, Ship Harbor and Wonderland trails among its most approachable walks, and Schoodic Woods as its most approachable campground. In summer an all-terrain beach wheelchair with large inflatable tires is available at Echo Lake Beach. The NPS app carries current accessibility details, and the park asks for accommodation requests by email at least two weeks ahead.

Driving the Park Loop Road is the easiest way to see the park from a seat; many of the coastal turnouts have views straight from the car. The narrated bus tours that hold park permits are another comfortable option; ask them directly about wheelchair access.

Park passes that save you money. U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability can get the free lifetime Access Pass. Visitors 62 and over can buy the Senior Pass ($80 for life, or $20 a year). Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass. Each covers entrance fees at every national park.

Traveling on dialysis? The nearest dialysis clinic is in Ellsworth, about 20 miles from Bar Harbor, with more in Bangor, about 50 miles away. Book your treatment at a clinic there several weeks before you go; the large dialysis companies run travel desks that will arrange it with your home unit.

Acadia by cruise ship

Bar Harbor is a regular port of call on New England and Canada cruise itineraries, most often on summer and fall sailings between Boston or New York and Quebec City, Montreal or Halifax. Larger ships anchor in Frenchman Bay and bring passengers ashore by tender to the downtown waterfront, a short walk from the Village Green and the Island Explorer stop.

A port day is long enough to see the highlights: a bus tour of the Park Loop Road and Cadillac Mountain, a ride on the free Island Explorer to Jordan Pond or Sand Beach, or a few miles on the carriage roads by rented bicycle. Remember that you still need a park entrance pass, and that a private car going up Cadillac needs its own vehicle reservation.

Bar Harbor voters approved a daily limit on cruise passengers coming ashore in 2022, and it has been argued over in court since, so the town now sees fewer big-ship calls than it once did. If Bar Harbor is the reason you are booking a particular cruise, ask the cruise line whether the call is confirmed. For a longer visit, a land stay in Bar Harbor before or after a Boston cruise is easy to arrange.

Acadia National Park FAQ

How much does it cost to get into Acadia National Park?

An Acadia pass costs $35 per vehicle, $30 per motorcycle or $20 per person on foot or bicycle, each valid for 7 days. The Acadia annual pass is $70. Everyone needs a pass, in any part of the park.

Do I need a reservation for Cadillac Mountain?

Yes, to drive up Cadillac Summit Road between May 20 and October 25, 2026. The vehicle reservation costs $6 and is sold only on recreation.gov, 30 percent 90 days ahead and 70 percent two days ahead, at 10 a.m. Eastern.

Can I take an RV up Cadillac Mountain?

No. RVs, trailers and any vehicle over 21 feet long, including bike racks and hitches, are not allowed on Cadillac Summit Road.

When does the Island Explorer bus run?

In 2026 spring service began May 20, full summer service began June 23, and service ran into mid-October. The buses are free, but you still need a park entrance pass.

Is there a hotel inside Acadia National Park?

No. The only overnight stays in the park are its campgrounds. Most visitors stay in Bar Harbor or Ellsworth.

What is the RV length limit at Blackwoods and Seawall campgrounds?

RVs may be up to 35 feet long and 11 feet high. Neither campground has hookups, but each has a dump station. Schoodic Woods has water and electric hookups.

When is the best time to see fall foliage in Acadia?

Leaves start turning in September, and the park says color usually peaks in the first to third week of October.

Do cruise ships stop at Bar Harbor?

Yes. Bar Harbor is a port of call on many New England and Canada cruises, with passengers brought ashore by tender to the downtown waterfront, close to the free Island Explorer bus.

Plan your Acadia trip

Book your room in Bar Harbor or Ellsworth early, mark the Cadillac reservation dates on your calendar, and let the Island Explorer do the driving.
