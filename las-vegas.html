Las Vegas Travel & Adventure Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

A city of glass and neon dropped into the Mojave — and, for a Canadian in January, warm desert afternoons at some of the cheapest room rates of the year. The Grand Canyon is up the road.

Find Las Vegas Hotels Search Flights to LAS

Las Vegas is easy to sneer at and very hard to dislike. It is a monument to appetite — a strip of casino resorts built in a desert that cannot really support them, selling a fantasy of every other city on earth at once. But strip away the mythology and what a Canadian actually gets is this: a four-and-a-bit-hour flight to a place where it is 15°C in January, where the hotel rooms are cheaper than almost any comparable city, where the eating is now genuinely world-class, and where you are two hours from red rock and four from the Grand Canyon. You do not have to gamble a dollar to have an excellent week. But you do need to understand two costs that nobody puts on the billboard — and one of them, most Canadians never claim back.

A quirk worth knowing: most of the famous Strip is not actually in the City of Las Vegas at all. The great resorts sit in unincorporated Clark County — in townships called Paradise and Winchester — which is why the city proper feels like a different place entirely. That city proper is Downtown, around Fremont Street: older, smaller, cheaper, and a good deal more human. Many repeat visitors end up preferring it.

The airport is astonishingly close — Harry Reid International sits at the southern end of the Strip, and you can be in your hotel twenty minutes after landing. And the desert begins the moment the buildings stop: Red Rock Canyon is half an hour west, and it is a genuine shock the first time, to be among sandstone cliffs and Joshua trees before your coffee has gone cold.

The two costs nobody warns Canadians about

1. Resort fees. Nearly every Las Vegas hotel adds a compulsory daily "resort fee" on top of the room rate you booked — covering Wi-Fi, the pool, the gym, whether you use them or not. It is charged per night in US dollars and it is not optional. A cheaper headline rate can easily become the more expensive stay. Always check the resort fee before comparing two hotels, and check for parking charges too.

2. The 30% withholding on winnings — and how to get it back. If you win above certain thresholds, a US casino will generally withhold 30% of the winnings from you as a non-resident, on the spot. What most Canadians don't know is that under the Canada–US tax treaty, you may be able to recover some or all of it — by filing a US non-resident return and offsetting your gambling losses in the same year against your winnings. You'll need to keep records of those losses and obtain a US taxpayer identification number.

This is a real refund, and it goes unclaimed all the time. It is also a tax matter, not a travel tip: use a qualified cross-border tax professional, and keep your paperwork. Nothing on this page is tax advice.

Why visit Las Vegas

Four reasons that have nothing to do with a slot machine.

Winter warmth, cheap

December to February: mild, dry, thin crowds and some of the lowest room rates of the year. It is 12°C on the Strip and −15°C at home.

The desert on the doorstep

Red Rock in 30 minutes, Valley of Fire in an hour, Zion in under three, the Grand Canyon in four and a half.

The eating

Vegas quietly became one of America's great restaurant cities. The buffet cliché is a generation out of date.

Everything on one street

Shows, architecture, bars and pools — all within a walk. No car needed until you leave town.

Top things to do in Las Vegas

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Walk the Strip after dark

It's free, it's absurd, and it's the whole point. Start at the Bellagio fountains and just keep going.

● The Grand Canyon

Grand Canyon West and the Skywalk is the 2½-hour day trip. The South Rim, 4½ hours out, is the real thing — go, and stay the night.

● Red Rock Canyon

Thirty minutes from the Strip: a scenic loop drive through sandstone cliffs. The best cheap morning in Nevada.

● See a show

Cirque du Soleil has several resident productions here, alongside residencies from the biggest names in music. Book ahead.

● Downtown & Fremont Street

Old Vegas — cheaper tables, better neon, more character. Take the bus or a cab; it's a different city.

● Hoover Dam

Forty-five minutes out: an engineering monument from the Depression that is genuinely moving to stand on.

Where to stay in Las Vegas

Location on the Strip matters more than the hotel. The Center Strip — roughly Bellagio to the Venetian — is the sweet spot: you can walk to most of what you came for, which matters more than you think, because the Strip is far longer than it looks and the blocks are enormous. The South Strip is closest to the airport and often cheaper. The North Strip has been the bargain end, though it is changing fast. Downtown / Fremont Street is the value and character play: markedly cheaper rooms, lower table minimums, better neon, and a 15-minute cab from the Strip. Wherever you book, add the resort fee to the nightly rate before you compare — it is the single most common way visitors misjudge the cost of a Vegas hotel.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Spring and autumn have the best weather outright. But for Canadians, December to February is the quiet secret: mild, dry, thin crowds and the cheapest rooms of the year — pack a jacket for the evenings, because the desert gets cold once the sun drops. Summer is genuinely dangerous heat, regularly above 40°C, and no amount of pool will fix it.

Getting there

Harry Reid International (LAS) sits at the foot of the Strip — twenty minutes to your hotel. Non-stops run from Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary, Edmonton and Winnipeg, and it is one of the most competitive routes out of Canada, so seat sales are common. You need no car for the Strip; rent one only for the day you drive out to the canyon.

Las Vegas travel FAQ

What is a resort fee, and can you avoid it?

A mandatory daily charge most Las Vegas hotels add on top of the room rate you booked, covering Wi-Fi, the pool and the gym whether you use them or not. It's charged per night in US dollars and it is not optional. Always check the resort fee before comparing two hotels — a cheaper headline rate can end up the more expensive stay. Many hotels now charge for parking too.

Are Canadians taxed on gambling winnings in Las Vegas?

Yes — US casinos generally withhold 30% of certain winnings from non-residents at the moment you're paid. But under the Canada–US tax treaty you may be able to recover some or all of it, by filing a US non-resident return and offsetting your gambling losses in the same year against the winnings. You'll need records of those losses and a US taxpayer identification number. It's a real refund that many Canadians never claim — but it's a tax matter, so use a qualified cross-border tax professional.

Can you visit the Grand Canyon from Las Vegas?

Yes. Grand Canyon West, with the Skywalk, is about 2½ hours away and is the usual day trip. The far more spectacular South Rim is roughly 4½ hours each way — doable in a very long day, but much better with a night in Tusayan, Williams or Flagstaff so you can catch sunrise at the rim.

When is the best time for Canadians to visit?

Spring and autumn have the best weather. But winter is the Canadian's secret: December through February brings mild, dry days, thin crowds and some of the cheapest rooms of the year. Summer is genuinely dangerous heat, often above 40°C.
