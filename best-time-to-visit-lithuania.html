Lithuania through the year

Best Time to Visit Lithuania

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Lithuania has four seasons that behave like four separate countries. Midsummer brings light that never quite goes out and a national holiday the whole population disappears into; autumn turns the forests gold and empties the hotels; winter brings real snow, a frozen lagoon and the cheapest rooms of the year; and spring is late, muddy and then suddenly green. Nothing here is unusable and nothing here is interchangeable, so the month you pick decides what sort of trip you get. Here is the year as it actually runs.

The short answer

Come in June for the light, the flowers and the warm evenings, planning around midsummer on the 23rd and 24th. Come in September for the best combination of warm sand, turning forest and empty paths — this is our own pick. Come in July or August if you want to swim, because the shallow Baltic takes that long to warm, and accept that the coast will be full and dear. Come in January or February if you want snow, churches, bathhouses, an empty city and the lowest prices of the year, and accept that it will be dark by four. Think twice about March and November, the thaw and the dark, which are the only two months we would steer a first visit away from.

Lithuania month by month

January and February

Deep winter, and in a normal year the real thing: snow lying for weeks, temperatures below freezing for most of it, the Curonian Lagoon icing over and ice fishermen out on it. The light is gone by the middle of the afternoon. This is the indoor country — the Vilnius churches and museums, the Kaunas galleries, the Druskininkai bathhouses, long lunches — and it is the cheapest time of the year to do all of it. Out of town the forests under snow are beautiful and very quiet. Bring boots with grip: the cobbles ice up.

March

The thaw, and the least attractive month in the country. The snow turns to slush and then to brown, the sky stays grey, the countryside is mud and the trees are still bare. Prices are low and the cities are yours. The one reason to come is the Kaziukas fair in Vilnius around St Casimir’s day, which fills the Old Town with craft stalls and is the oldest and best of the country’s markets. Otherwise, make it a city trip rather than a country one.

April

Spring arrives, late and then quickly. The forest floor comes up before the canopy does, the storks come back to their poles in the fields, and by the end of the month the parks are green. It can still snow. Days are lengthening fast, the cities are quiet, the prices are still low, and the coast is cold and empty. A good month for walking and a poor one for sitting outside.

May

One of the two best months, and the better one for flowers. The lilac goes over in the Vilnius parks, the forests fill in, the light already runs late, and the terraces open. It is warm without being hot, the midges have not started and the summer visitors have not arrived. The sea is still far too cold to swim in. If you want one month for a first visit and cannot take June, take this one.

June

The prize. Light until nearly midnight, linden in flower, courtyards and riverbanks in use, warm evenings and the whole country outdoors. The spit is at its best — warm pine, empty cycle path, cool sea. The one thing to plan around is Jonines on 23 and 24 June, midsummer, when the cities empty into the countryside, a great many businesses close and the night is spent round a bonfire. Wonderful if you are invited to one, bewildering if you expected a working city.

July

The warmest month and the busiest. This is when the Baltic finally becomes swimmable, which means Palanga, Nida and the Klaipeda beaches are full and dear, and when the cities are a little emptier because Lithuanians have gone to them. Statehood Day on 6 July is a public holiday and a genuinely moving one, with the national anthem sung at nine in the evening wherever Lithuanians are. Klaipeda’s Sea Festival takes the city’s last free room in late July.

August

Warm, green and still busy on the coast, with thunderstorms more likely than in July and the water at its warmest. Inland it is the mushroom and berry month, which in Dzukija is close to a civic activity. The Baltic Way anniversary falls on 23 August and is marked rather than celebrated. Towards the end of the month the coast begins to empty and the prices come off, which makes the last week better value than the first.

September

Our own pick for the country as a whole. The sand on the spit is still warm, the air is cool and clear, the forests are starting to turn, the students are back and the festivals are running, and there is nobody on the paths. Rooms on the coast fall to a fraction of their August price. The weather is less reliable than in June, which is the only argument against it, and a wet week is a real possibility.

October

The best colour and the most rain. The first half of the month is the autumn at its height in the Nemunas valley and the southern forests, with long gold light on a good day. The second half is where it tips — darker, wetter, the leaves down, the coast shutting up for winter. Cheap, quiet and worth it for a walker with waterproofs; not a beach month under any circumstances.

November

The dark, and the one month we would steer anybody away from. Grey, wet, bare and short-dayed, with the Christmas markets not yet open. Two things redeem it: All Souls’ on 1 and 2 November, when the cemeteries across the country fill with candles after dark and it is one of the more beautiful national habits anywhere; and the lowest prices of the year outside January.

December

Dark by four in the afternoon and lovely anyway. The Christmas markets open by the Cathedral in Vilnius and on the Town Hall Square in Kaunas, the Vilnius tree has become a minor international attraction in its own right, and the churches and concert halls are at their fullest. Snow is likely but no longer certain. Book ahead for the weekends before Christmas and for New Year, and expect much of the country to shut for a few days around the holiday itself.

The weather, and what it does to a trip

The coast and the inland are two climates

The Baltic moderates everything near it: Klaipeda and the spit are milder in winter and cooler in summer than Vilnius, and the south-east and the Aukstaitija lakes are the other way round — colder winters, warmer summers, more snow. A forecast for Vilnius is a fair guide for Kaunas and Trakai and a poor one for Nida.

The light matters more than the temperature

Lithuanians will tell you the cold is manageable and the dark is the thing. In late June the sky never goes properly black; in late December you get around seven hours of grey daylight. Plan outdoor sightseeing for the middle of a winter day and let the evenings be churches, museums, concerts and dinner, which is how the country itself does it.

Snow is normal, and so is a brown winter

A Lithuanian winter usually brings weeks of lying snow, and when it does the forests and the frozen lagoon are the reason to come. But mild winters have become common enough that a December visit may find rain and brown grass instead. If snow is the point of your trip, come in late January or February rather than at Christmas, and keep your plans loose.

Ticks and mosquitoes, honestly

If you walk in forest, long grass or near water between about May and September you will meet mosquitoes, and ticks are present across the region. Cover your legs, use repellent and check yourself after a walk. Lithuanians treat this as ordinary rather than alarming, and so should you — but if you plan a lot of time in the woods, ask your own doctor well before you travel what they advise for the region.

Dates worth planning around

The days that fill the hotels, close the shops or are simply worth arranging a trip around.

Independence Day — 16 February

The proclamation of the modern Lithuanian state in 1918. Flags on every building, concerts and ceremonies in the cold, and a public holiday. Together with 11 March it tells you most of what you need to know about the last hundred years here.

Restoration of Independence — 11 March

The 1990 declaration restoring independence from the Soviet Union, the first by any Soviet republic. A public holiday, marked with processions and a good deal of quiet pride rather than spectacle.

The Kaziukas fair — around 4 March

St Casimir’s fair, held in Vilnius since the early seventeenth century, fills the Old Town streets with craft stalls, wooden toys, verbos — the dried-flower palms — and food, over a weekend around the saint’s day. It is the one unmissable thing in a thin month, and the dates shift with the calendar, so check them.

Jonines — 23 and 24 June

Midsummer, and the biggest holiday of the Lithuanian year by a distance: bonfires, oak and flower wreaths, beer, and a night nobody sleeps through. The cities empty into the countryside and a great many businesses close. Marvellous if you are invited to a country celebration, and bewildering if you turn up expecting a working city.

Statehood Day — 6 July

The coronation of Mindaugas as the only king of Lithuania in 1253. A public holiday, and the one with the best custom attached to it: at nine in the evening, Lithuanians sing the national anthem together, in squares at home and in halls and gardens wherever in the world they happen to be. If you are here, go and stand in a square for it.

The Klaipeda Sea Festival — late July

Three days that fill the whole Klaipeda waterfront with ships, stalls, concerts and crowds, and take every room in the city and a good many up the coast. Dates shift from year to year. It is a wonderful weekend to be there and an impossible one to arrive without a booking.

The Baltic Way anniversary — 23 August

In 1989 around two million people formed a human chain from Tallinn through Riga to Vilnius against Soviet rule. The anniversary is marked rather than celebrated, and in some years with events along the old route. Worth knowing the date before you ask anybody about it.

All Souls’ — 1 and 2 November

Both days are public holidays, and on the evenings around them the cemeteries of every town and village fill with candles until the whole ground glows. It is a family matter rather than a spectacle, so go quietly if you go at all. It is also the single most beautiful thing in the Lithuanian calendar.

The Song Celebration — every few years

Tens of thousands of singers and dancers from across the country converge on Vilnius for a week of massed choirs, folk dancing and processions, in a tradition whose centenary was marked in the summer of 2024 and which UNESCO lists as intangible cultural heritage. The intervals have run to four and five years rather than to a fixed rule, and the next dates are announced well ahead, so check them before fixing a summer trip: the city fills completely.

The Christmas markets — late November to early January

The Cathedral Square in Vilnius is the main one, with the city’s tree, and Kaunas puts a smaller one on the Town Hall Square. Weekends draw visitors from across the region; a weekday evening is far better. Dates shift a little each year, so check before fixing travel around them.

What it costs, and when

Lithuania is among the cheaper countries in the euro area and the season moves the price far more than the place does. In Vilnius and Kaunas the swing is modest: a lift over Christmas, New Year and the big public holidays, and otherwise much the same all year. On the coast it is a different country. Nida, Palanga and Klaipeda in July and August cost a multiple of their May price, because the supply of beds does not grow and on the Curonian Spit, inside a national park, it cannot. January to March and November are the cheapest weeks of the year everywhere, and the spa hotels in Druskininkai are the one exception, because that is their busy season. The practical rule: for the cities, come when you like; for the spit in high summer, book months ahead or go in September instead.

Fishing here

The fishing year runs with the seasons too: pike and zander on the Curonian Lagoon and in the Nemunas delta through the open-water months, trout and grayling on the Neris and the Zeimena, the Aukstaitija lakes in summer, and ice fishing on the lagoon in a hard winter, which is an institution rather than a novelty. An angler’s card is required, the salmon and sea-trout waters have their own seasons, and parts of the delta sit inside a regional park with rules of their own — arrange it locally rather than from abroad. Getting in: what changed, and what has not

Europe’s Entry/Exit System is now in force. Since 10 April 2026 it has operated at every Schengen border crossing and passport stamps have been discontinued. On your first crossing you will have your fingerprints taken and your photograph made at the border, which adds a few minutes; after that the check is quicker. There is nothing to apply for and nothing to pay.

ETIAS - the travel authorisation you may have read about, expected to cost around EUR 20 and last three years - is not in force yet. No applications are being taken, and the European Union has not announced the exact start date; it has said it will give several months’ notice before the system goes live. It has been postponed repeatedly and no start date is confirmed, so treat it as coming rather than cancelled, and ignore any website offering to sell you one today - nobody can issue one yet. When it does begin there will be six months in which you can travel without one, then six months in which only first-time arrivals are exempt.

What this means for your trip right now: you do not need ETIAS. You do need a passport issued within the last ten years and valid for at least three months beyond the date you plan to leave.

Lithuania is in the European Union, the Schengen area and the euro, so arriving from anywhere else in Schengen there is no check at all. Where to sleep in each city, quarter by quarter, is in The Lithuania Travel Guide.

Lithuania’s seasons — questions

What is the single best month to visit Lithuania?

June, for most people. The light runs until nearly midnight, the forests and courtyards are at their freshest, it is warm without being hot and the summer crowds have not arrived. Our own pick is actually September: a little cooler, the sand on the spit still warm, the forests beginning to turn, nobody on the paths and rooms cheaper than in July. Both are better than August, which is the busiest and the dearest.

Is winter actually a bad idea?

No, as long as you know what you are buying. It is cold, it is often properly snowy and the light is gone by the middle of the afternoon, so a winter trip is a city trip: the Vilnius Christmas market and the churches, the Kaunas museums and the basilica terrace, the Druskininkai bathhouses, long lunches. It is also the cheapest and emptiest time of the year by a wide margin, and the frozen lagoon and the snowy pine forests are genuinely beautiful. Bring boots with grip for the cobbles.

When can I swim?

July and August in the Baltic, and only then — the sea off Palanga, Klaipeda and the Curonian Spit is shallow but takes a long time to warm, and a swim in June is for the committed. The lagoon side of the spit is warmer and calmer than the sea side. Inland, the lakes of Aukstaitija and the rivers warm faster and are pleasant from late June.

When is the autumn colour?

Late September into the middle of October, with the exact fortnight moving by a week or two each year. The forests of Dzukija and Aukstaitija, the Nemunas valley around Kaunas and the parks of Vilnius are all worth the trip. It is also the wettest part of the year, so bring waterproofs and treat a bright day as the bonus it is.

Which months would you steer a first visit away from?

March and November. March is the thaw: grey sky, slush, then mud, with the trees still bare and nothing at its best. November is the dark, before the Christmas markets open and after the colour has gone. Both are cheap and both are perfectly fine for a city break with museums in it, but if you have a free choice, almost any other month gives you more.

Does the Song Celebration affect my trip?

Only if your year is one of the celebration years, and then enormously. The national Song Celebration brings tens of thousands of performers into Vilnius for a week and fills the city completely; its centenary was marked in the summer of 2024. The cycle has run at four- and five-year intervals and the next dates are announced well in advance rather than fixed by a rule, so check them before you book anything in a Lithuanian summer. If you land in that week deliberately, it is the best thing you will see here.

How much does the season change what I pay?

In the cities, less than you would expect: Vilnius and Kaunas are moderately priced all year, with a lift at Christmas and over the big public holidays. On the coast it is a different country — Nida, Palanga and Klaipeda in July and August cost a multiple of what they cost in May, because the number of beds does not change, and Nida inside a national park is the sharpest case of it. If high summer on the spit is fixed for you, book months ahead.
