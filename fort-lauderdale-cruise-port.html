🗺️Harbor map — where everything is

Port Everglades is a working seaport, not a waterfront promenade. The eight cruise terminals sit inside a secured perimeter shared with oil berths and container cranes, and there is no way out on foot. Everything a visitor wants — the beach, Las Olas, the restaurants on 17th Street — is two to three miles away by road. The airport, unusually, is nearer than any of it.

🏨Stay the night before you sail

Fly in the same morning and you are betting your cruise on one flight. Fort Lauderdale is the easiest port in America to arrive into the night before — the airport is two miles from the ship — and a night ashore turns a fraught morning into a slow breakfast. Three honest choices, depending on what you want the evening to be.

Closest to the ship — the Intracoastal

Minutes from the terminals, on the water, with the 17th Street restaurants on the doorstep. The choice if you want the shortest possible morning.

Two miles north on A1A, where the beach begins. Worth it if you are coming a day or two early and want the ocean, not the port.

🚌Getting to the ship on sailing morning

This is where Fort Lauderdale quietly costs people money, so here is the arithmetic before the sales pitch.

Which hotels actually run a port shuttle. Visit Lauderdale, the county’s own tourist board, lists the Courtyard Fort Lauderdale Airport & Cruise Port and the Comfort Suites Fort Lauderdale Airport & Cruise Port as offering complimentary transfers to the port — those two are the genuine article. It lists the Hyatt Place Fort Lauderdale Cruise Port and the Four Points by Sheraton Fort Lauderdale Airport/Cruise Port among the hotels that run a shuttle for a fee. Dozens of other hotels along the airport and 17th Street corridors sell “park, stay and cruise” packages that bundle a room, parking for the week and a seat on the van.

What it really costs. Do not take “complimentary” at face value. Across the Fort Lauderdale park-and-cruise hotels the reported shuttle fare runs from about $12 to $20 per person, each way, with $15 the commonest figure; hotel parking alongside it typically runs $10 to $18 a day. A packaged room-plus-parking-plus-shuttle deal is usually priced above the plain room rate rather than instead of it. And a listing that advertises a “free shuttle” very often means free to the airport — not to the ship.

The Guild math. Take the common $15 per person, each way. Two of you: $30 to the ship, $60 there and back. A family of four: $60 each way, $120 round trip. One taxi carries all four of you for a metered fare of roughly $16 to $25 — the meter does not care how many are in the car. So: travelling alone, the shuttle usually wins. From two people upward, a taxi or rideshare is almost always cheaper, and it leaves when you do rather than at a fixed hour. The shuttle’s real advantage is not price but luggage and certainty — a driver who does this run every morning and knows which terminal is which.

The fixed departures. Hotel vans do not run on demand. Expect three or four set slots across the late morning — typically somewhere between about 9:30 a.m. and 1 p.m. — and expect to put your name down at the front desk the night before. Miss your slot and you are paying for a taxi anyway. Ask two questions when you book: what time is the last run, and does the van call at other hotels first?

Parking at the port instead. The port’s own garages charge a $20 daily maximum for a standard car and $25 a day for oversized vehicles; a short visit is $4 for up to an hour and $8 for up to five. There are no reservations and no pre-payment — it is first come, first served on the day. For a seven-night cruise that is about $140, against a hotel package at $10 to $18 a night plus the shuttle fare. Run both numbers for your party size; for a couple the port garage often wins outright, and for a family it usually does.

Which garage is yours: the Heron Garage (1,818 spaces, 7-foot clearance) serves Terminals 2 and 4; the Palm Garage (1,966 spaces, same clearance) serves Terminals 19, 21, 25, 26 and 29; Lot A serves Terminals 19 and 26, Lot B serves Terminals 18 and 19, and Lot C serves Terminal 18. If you drive anything taller than seven feet, the garages are out — ask the port about surface parking before you arrive.

⚓Arriving at the terminal — what it is actually like

The unflattering truth first: you cannot walk out of this port. Port Everglades is a secure, working seaport — petroleum berths, container cranes, a naval visit or two — and the cruise terminals sit inside the fence on causeways with no pedestrian exit. There is no promenade, no row of bars outside the gate, nothing to stroll to. If you are picturing Cozumel or Nassau, picture an airport instead. Everyone leaves by vehicle.

Find your terminal before you set off. Eight terminals, and several of them shared, so “Port Everglades” typed into a satnav is not good enough — use your own terminal’s street address. Northport holds Terminal 2 (Princess, 1801 SE 20th Street) and Terminal 4 (Disney, 1800 SE 20th Street, opened in autumn 2023 and connected directly to the Heron Garage). Midport holds Terminal 18 (Royal Caribbean, 1901 SE 32nd Street), Terminal 19 (Holland America, 2019 Eller Drive), Terminal 21 (Holland America and Princess, 2021 Eller Drive), Terminal 25 (Celebrity, 2025 Eller Drive), Terminal 26 (Holland America, 2026 Eller Drive) and Terminal 29 (Celebrity, Holland America and Royal Caribbean, 2200 SE 35th Street). The port’s general address, 1850 Eller Drive, is the administrative one — it will get you through the gate but not to your door.

At the gate. Have your cruise documents and government-issued photo identification in your hand at the security checkpoint, not in a case in the boot. Arrive inside the boarding window printed on your pass; showing up three hours early does not get you aboard three hours early, it gets you a chair in a queue. If someone is dropping you off and coming back later, the port keeps a free cell-phone waiting lot.

Coming back at the end. Disembarkation mornings are brisk here. Terminals empty onto the same few roads, so if you have a flight, remember that FLL is close enough that a mid-morning departure is genuinely realistic — one of the few ports where that is true.

💵Money, parking and tipping

This is the United States: US dollars, cards everywhere, contactless normal. There is no currency to change and no reason to carry much cash beyond tips.

Taxis run on the meter. Broward County sets a $13 minimum fare with a $3 airport fee on trips starting at FLL, so $16 is the floor from the airport and about $20 is typical to the terminals. Rideshare is often less — but surge pricing on a Saturday morning, when six ships are turning around at once, can wipe out the saving entirely. If the app quotes something startling, a taxi rank is a fixed, boring, metered alternative.

Tipping, as everywhere in America, is expected rather than optional: 18–20% in restaurants, a couple of dollars a bag for porters at the terminal — and porters are worth it, because they take the heavy cases off you at the kerb. Shuttle drivers appreciate a few dollars a head.

Parking rates are covered in full above: $20 a day standard, $25 oversized, no reservations. Bring a credit card.

🏝️A spare day in Fort Lauderdale

Arriving early or staying on? Fort Lauderdale rewards a day far more than most departure ports do — it is a real city with a real beach, not just a place ships leave from.

  • The beach at Las Olas — where the sand strip begins, about two miles from the terminals. Wide, clean, lifeguarded, with the low wave-wall promenade running north. The most reliable good afternoon in the city.
  • Las Olas Boulevard — downtown’s dining and gallery street, about 2.6 miles away. Boutiques, courtyards and restaurants, best in the early evening.
  • The waterways — Fort Lauderdale calls itself the Venice of America and the canals are genuinely the thing that makes it different. A water-taxi hop or a sightseeing cruise shows you the city from the angle it was built for.
  • 17th Street — the causeway strip between the port and the beach, thick with restaurants and the most convenient dinner if you are staying near the ship.
  • Hugh Taylor Birch State Park — a strip of old coastal hammock between the Intracoastal and A1A, and the quietest green space near the beach.
Only have half a day? Beach first, Las Olas after dark. Do not try to add the Everglades — the airboat operators are an hour’s drive west and back, and it is the classic way to turn a relaxed day into a rushed one.

🚌Getting around, with real fares

Taxi: metered, $13 minimum, $3 more if you start at the airport. Terminals to the beach or downtown is a short hop; expect something in the region of $15–$25 depending on traffic and where exactly you are going.

Rideshare: Uber and Lyft both operate and both have designated pickup points at the airport and the port. Usually cheaper than a taxi, occasionally much dearer during a surge.

Water taxi: a hop-on service along the New River and Intracoastal linking downtown, Las Olas and the beach. Slow, scenic, and the nicest way to see the city — but it is sightseeing transport, not a way to make a sailing time.

Rental cars: every major desk is at FLL, two miles away, and several have downtown branches. Sensible if you are touring before the cruise; pointless for a single night, once you have paid to park it.

Buses: Broward County Transit runs the city and is inexpensive, but it does not serve the cruise terminals inside the port fence. Do not plan an embarkation morning around it.

👕What to wear & the weather

Subtropical, and warm all year. Winter — the heart of the cruise season — is the pleasant half: warm days, cooler evenings, low humidity, the reason the northern half of the continent comes here in January. Summer is hot, very humid, and reliably wet in the afternoons, when a heavy shower arrives, soaks everything for twenty minutes and clears.

Pack light layers and something with sleeves for the ship’s air conditioning, which is fierce. If you are sailing between June and November, you are sailing in hurricane season: it rarely spoils a cruise, but it is the reason to fly in the night before rather than the morning of, and the reason travel insurance is worth having.

📶Staying connected

You are in the United States, so an American phone works normally and a foreign one bills at whatever your roaming plan says. Free Wi-Fi is provided in every cruise terminal, and in nearly every hotel and coffee shop. Download your boarding pass, your terminal address and this page before you leave the hotel — the port’s mobile signal is fine, but the kerbside is not the place to discover otherwise.

🛍️What to buy — and what to skip

Worth it: anything you forgot, bought properly. There is a full-sized supermarket and pharmacy within a couple of miles of the port, and sunscreen, seasickness tablets, a hat and a bottle of something for the cabin all cost a fraction ashore of what they cost aboard. This is the single best-value stop of the whole cruise and almost nobody makes it.

Skip: duty-free bought at the terminal, souvenir T-shirts on the beach strip, and anything sold as a “cruise essential” in the airport. You are not somewhere exotic yet.

⚠️Scams & hassles to dodge

Fort Lauderdale is a straightforward American city and there is no pier hustle here. The traps are commercial rather than criminal.

  • The “free” shuttle that is not. The commonest one. Ask the price per person, each way, before you book the room.
  • Unofficial parking touts off the port property offering to shuttle you in. Some are legitimate operators; some are not. If you have not booked it in advance from a company with a real address, use the port’s own garages.
  • Surge pricing on turnaround Saturdays. Check the taxi rank before accepting a rideshare quote that has doubled.
  • Booking Miami by mistake. They are different ports 23 miles apart. Check which one your ship actually sails from — especially if you are sailing Disney, which moved here from Miami in 2023.

🩹Health, water & sun

Tap water is safe to drink throughout Broward County. Standard American pharmacies are everywhere and stock everything you might have forgotten. The sun is the real hazard: this is latitude 26 north, the light is stronger than it feels with a sea breeze on you, and the classic cruise mistake is a bad burn on the embarkation day that ruins the first three days at sea. Hat, high-factor sunscreen, and shade in the middle of the day.

Mosquitoes appear at dusk in the wet months; repellent is worth carrying if you are eating outside. Medical care is excellent and expensive — travel insurance is not optional for visitors.

♿Accessibility & families

Port Everglades is one of the easier American cruise ports for anyone with limited mobility. The terminals are modern, air-conditioned, step-free, and fully accessible, with free Wi-Fi and seating; wheelchair assistance is available at the terminal — arrange it through your cruise line in advance rather than on the day. Terminal 4 connects directly to the Heron Garage, which is as short a walk from car to ship as any American port offers.

For families: the drop-off kerbs are busy but orderly, porters take the heavy bags at the door, and the short distance from FLL means small children are not enduring a long transfer at the end of a flight. Disney sails from Terminal 4, which was built for exactly this traffic. If you are four or more, re-read the Guild math above before you book a per-person shuttle.

⭐Best things to do, ranked

  • An hour on the beach at Las Olas — the simplest and the best. Two miles from the ship, and the reason people come to Florida.
  • A water-taxi loop — the canals, the yachts, the houses. The one genuinely distinctive thing Fort Lauderdale has.
  • Dinner on Las Olas Boulevard — the best evening in the city, and easy to fit in the night before you sail.
  • The Bonnet House or Hugh Taylor Birch State Park — old Florida, ten minutes from the beach, and quiet.
  • A provisioning run — unglamorous and the best-value hour of your trip. See above.

🍴Where to eat

17th Street Causeway is the practical answer if you are staying near the ship: a solid strip of restaurants between the port and the beach, walkable from most of the port-side hotels, and busy enough that you can turn up without a booking.

Las Olas Boulevard is the good answer if you have an evening to spend: courtyard restaurants, a proper street to walk down, and the only part of Fort Lauderdale that feels like a town centre rather than a highway.

The beach strip on A1A is where to eat if you want to look at the sea while you do it. Prices reflect the view.

Florida on a plate means stone crab in season, grouper sandwiches, key lime pie and Cuban coffee — all of them better here than they will be aboard.

🧮Ship transfer or do it yourself?

Cruise lines sell an airport-to-pier transfer for FLL. For a journey of two miles it is rarely good value against a $20 taxi — but it does come with one real benefit: if your flight is late, the line knows you are coming. Buy it for the insurance, not the economics, and only if you are flying in on sailing day, which we would rather you did not do at all.

For everything else — hotel to ship, ship to airport — a taxi or rideshare is cheaper and faster for any party of two or more, and the arithmetic is set out above.

⏰Don’t miss the ship

Check-in closes well before the published sailing time — on most lines two hours before — and the gangway goes up before that. Work backwards from the time on your boarding pass, not from the sailing time, and add half an hour for the security queue on a busy Saturday.

Because Port Everglades is a departure port rather than a stop, the risk here is not a late excursion — it is traffic. Six ships turning round at once puts a great many cars on the same few roads between about 10 a.m. and 1 p.m. If your slot is late morning, leave earlier than the distance suggests.

🇺🇸Emergency numbers & staying safe

911 reaches police, fire and ambulance anywhere in the United States. Port Everglades can be reached on 954-468-3720, and the port’s parking management team on 954-468-3680. Broward Health and Holy Cross are the main hospitals in the city and both are close.

Fort Lauderdale is a safe city by American standards, with the usual sense applied after dark away from the busy strips. Keep your cruise documents and passport on you rather than in checked luggage on embarkation day.

❓Good to know — quick answers

Can I walk out of Port Everglades into town?
No. Port Everglades is a secure working seaport with no pedestrian exit, and the terminals sit on causeways ringed by cargo berths and fuel tanks. Every passenger leaves by vehicle — taxi, rideshare, hotel shuttle or a booked transfer. Downtown Fort Lauderdale is about 2.6 miles away and the beach about 2 miles; both are short drives, but neither is a walk.

Do any hotels offer a free shuttle to the ship?
A few genuinely do — Visit Lauderdale, the county tourist board, lists the Courtyard Fort Lauderdale Airport & Cruise Port and the Comfort Suites Fort Lauderdale Airport & Cruise Port as running complimentary port transfers. Most others charge. The going rate reported across Fort Lauderdale park-and-cruise hotels is about $12 to $20 per person each way, most commonly $15, and “free shuttle” in a listing often means free to the airport only. Always ask the price per person, each way, before you book.

How much does it cost to park at Port Everglades?
The port’s published rate is a $20 daily maximum for a standard vehicle and $25 a day for oversized vehicles, with short stays charged $4 for up to one hour and $8 for up to five. The port does not take parking reservations or pre-payment, so spaces are first come, first served. The Heron Garage serves Terminals 2 and 4; the Palm Garage serves Terminals 19, 21, 25, 26 and 29; Lots A, B and C serve Terminals 18, 19 and 26.

Which terminal will my ship use?
Check your boarding pass — it is the only reliable answer, because several terminals are shared. As a guide: Terminal 2 is Princess, Terminal 4 is Disney, Terminal 18 is Royal Caribbean, Terminals 19 and 26 are Holland America, Terminal 21 is Holland America and Princess, Terminal 25 is Celebrity, and Terminal 29 is shared by Celebrity, Holland America and Royal Caribbean.

How far is Fort Lauderdale airport from the cruise port?
Barely two miles — it is the closest major airport to any cruise port in the United States, and the drive is usually under fifteen minutes. A taxi from FLL runs on the meter with a $13 minimum plus a $3 airport fee, so $16 is the floor and around $20 is typical. Rideshare is often cheaper outside surge periods.

Is Fort Lauderdale the same as Miami for cruises?
No, and mixing them up is the classic Florida cruise mistake. They are separate ports about 23 miles apart, each with its own terminals and its own airport. Disney Cruise Line moved its South Florida operation from Miami to Port Everglades in November 2023, so a Disney sailing now means Fort Lauderdale, not Miami.

Should I take the hotel shuttle or a taxi?
It depends entirely on how many of you there are. At a typical $15 per person each way, two people pay $30 and four pay $60 — while one taxi carries the whole party for a metered fare of roughly $16 to $25. Travelling alone, the shuttle usually wins. From two people up, a taxi or rideshare is normally cheaper and always more direct, because the shuttle runs on fixed departures and may call at other hotels first.
