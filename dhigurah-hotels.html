Where to Stay in Dhigurah

Where to Stay in Dhigurah — Whale Sharks & Sand

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Dhigurah is a long splinter of an island on the southern rim of Ari atoll: a small village at one end and then roughly two miles of palms and white sand running out to a sandbank at the other. It sits inside the protected water where whale sharks are seen all year rather than in a season, which is why divers and snorkellers come, and it is a good deal quieter than the better-known guesthouse islands. Stay in the village for the cheapest rooms, toward the beach end for the sand, and with a dive guesthouse if the water is the whole reason you are going.

Choosing Your Area on Dhigurah

There is one small settlement and then a very long island. That is really the whole choice.

The village

Everything on Dhigurah that is not sand is in the village at the northern end: the guesthouses, the two or three cafes, the shop, the mosque and the harbour. Rooms here are the cheapest and you are a few minutes from the bikini beach and the boats.

It is a quiet place. There is no nightlife and there is not meant to be.

Toward the beach and the sandbank

The guesthouses at the southern edge of the village put you closest to the bikini beach and to the track that runs down the spine of the island. Walking that track to the sandbank at the far end, with nobody else on it, is the thing most visitors remember about Dhigurah.

Take water and something for your head; there is no shade for long stretches and no shop once you set off.

The dive guesthouses

Several of the island’s small hotels are built around a dive operation, with their own boat, a compressor and a daily schedule out into South Ari. If you intend to be in the water every day, staying with one is simpler and usually cheaper than booking dives separately.

Ask what certification they expect for the channel dives, and what happens to your money if the weather closes the sites.

Know Before You Book

The things worth knowing before you commit a week to a two-mile sandbank.

The whale sharks are wild animals, and so are the rules

The South Ari Marine Protected Area is where whale sharks are reliably present year-round, and Dhigurah is inside it. Reliable is not the same as guaranteed: boats sometimes search all morning and find nothing, and the honest operators say so before you pay.

There are rules about distance, numbers in the water and never touching or blocking an animal. A good boat will brief you on them unprompted. If yours does not, that tells you something about the boat.

Getting here takes planning, and it is worth asking the cost first

The fast route is a domestic flight to Maamigili at the south of Ari and a short boat transfer. A speedboat from the capital takes a couple of hours; the public ferry costs very little, takes most of a day and does not sail daily.

Transfers are a real part of the price of a Maldives trip and they are charged per person each way. Ask what yours will be before you book the room, not after.

An inhabited island, with inhabited-island rules

Swimwear on the marked bikini beach only, shoulders and knees covered in the village, and no alcohol sold on the island. The trips to a floating bar or a nearby resort exist here as they do elsewhere.

Dhigurah is smaller and quieter than Maafushi, and that cuts both ways: fewer places to eat, fewer boats to choose from, and a great deal more peace.

Dhigurah Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a guesthouse on Dhigurah.

Can you really see whale sharks at Dhigurah?

Dhigurah sits on the southern rim of Ari atoll, inside the protected stretch of water where whale sharks are seen through the year rather than in a season — which is unusual anywhere in the world. That is not a guarantee on any given morning: they are wild animals in open water and a boat can look all day and find nothing. What it does mean is that the odds here are as good as they get, and that you can try again tomorrow.

How do you get to Dhigurah?

The quickest way is a domestic flight from the airport to Maamigili, the airstrip at the southern end of Ari atoll, and then a short boat ride. There is also a speedboat from the capital, which takes a couple of hours, and a public ferry that is very cheap, takes most of a day and does not run daily. Your guesthouse will arrange whichever you choose; ask the cost both ways before you book the room.

What is the island itself like?

Long and thin — a village at one end and then roughly two miles of sand, scrub and palms running away to a sandbank at the other, which is a rarity in a country of small round islands. The village is small and quiet, there is a bikini beach, and the walk down the spine of the island to the sandbank at the far end is the thing most people remember. It is considerably quieter than Maafushi.

Is Dhigurah better for diving or for snorkelling?

Both work, and the split is unusually even. Divers come for the channels and cleaning stations of South Ari and for the chance of a whale shark on a dive rather than a snorkel. Snorkellers do well because the whale-shark trips are snorkelling trips by design, and because the house reef and the surrounding shallows are good. If you are a mixed party, this is an easier island than most.
