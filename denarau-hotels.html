Where to Stay on Denarau Island

Where to Stay on Denarau Island — Resorts & Marina

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Denarau Island is where most of Fiji’s big-brand resorts sit: a flat, largely man-made island joined to Viti Levu by a causeway, about twenty minutes from Nadi airport, with a row of large hotels along one beach, a golf course behind them and a marina at the far end. It is the easiest holiday in Fiji to organise and the one that asks least of you. It is also the one most often described afterwards as ‘not what I pictured’ — so the most useful thing this page can do is tell you plainly what Denarau is and is not, before you book it.

Choosing Your Resort on Denarau

They are all on the same island and the same beach. What differs is the shape of the stay.

The beachfront resort row

The large resorts run in a line along Denarau’s west-facing beach, which means every one of them gets the sunset and none of them is more than a short walk from the next. Between them you can eat at any of the others, and the difference between them is less about location than about pools, kids’ clubs and how formal the place feels.

Sofitel Fiji Resort & Spa Sheraton Fiji Golf & Beach Resort Hilton Fiji Beach Resort and Spa

The quieter, adult-leaning end

Denarau is a family island by default — school holidays fill it, and the pools are loud by eleven in the morning. Several of the properties are deliberately calmer than the rest, and if you are here as a couple it is worth choosing one on purpose rather than finding out at breakfast.

The Westin Fiji Golf Resort & Spa Radisson Blu Resort Fiji Denarau Island

Villas and self-catering

Denarau also has villa and apartment stock, which is the sensible answer for families of more than four, for anyone staying a week or more, and for anyone who would rather not eat every meal at a resort restaurant. Kitchens matter here: the island’s food is convenient and not cheap.

Sheraton Denarau Villas

The marina end

Port Denarau sits at the island’s eastern tip: the marina, a cluster of shops and restaurants, the day-cruise desks and the catamaran berths. Staying near it means walking to dinner and being first on the boat; staying at the far end of the beach means a shuttle ride to both.

Know Before You Book

Four things about Denarau that are better learned here than on arrival.

The beach is not the postcard, and that matters

This is the single thing to understand before booking. Denarau’s sand is grey-brown rather than white, and the water close to shore is often cloudy — the island sits beside a river mouth and mangrove flats, and the sediment is simply what that coast is made of. The pools are magnificent; the beach is for walking on.

If the image in your head is white sand and clear turquoise water, that is the Mamanuca and Yasawa islands offshore, not Denarau. Many people book Denarau for the beach and are disappointed, then take a day boat out to the islands and understand. The better plan is to decide which one you want and book accordingly, or split the trip and do both.

Everything to the islands leaves from here

Port Denarau is the departure point for the catamarans to the Mamanucas and Yasawas, for day cruises, and for most island-resort transfers. If an island is your real destination, a night on Denarau first is the usual way of catching a morning boat without a dawn drive.

Check your boat’s departure time against your flight before you book the first night. The long-haul arrivals and the island departures are not designed to connect.

Getting around the island

A shuttle bus loops Denarau between the resorts and the marina through the day, and taxis to Nadi town and the airport are plentiful and short. You do not need a hire car to stay here, and most people who take one find it sits in the resort car park.

Denarau is a gated island with a single causeway in. That makes it easy and quiet; it also means the Fiji outside it has to be gone and found.

Golf, and the rest of the dry side

There is an eighteen-hole championship course on the island, which is one of the genuine reasons to choose Denarau over an offshore resort. Beyond the causeway, the Sabeto hot springs and mud pools, the orchid gardens below the Sleeping Giant ridge, and Nadi’s market and temple are all inside half an hour.

Denarau Island Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Is Denarau Island worth staying on?

Yes, for the right trip. It is the easiest base in Fiji: twenty minutes from the international airport, a row of large resorts with everything on site, a marina for day trips, and a golf course. It suits families, short stays, first visits and anyone who does not want a boat transfer after a long-haul flight. It does not suit anyone whose picture of Fiji is a white-sand island, because that is not what Denarau looks like.

Why is the water at Denarau not clear?

Because of where the island sits. Denarau is beside a river mouth and mangrove flats on the west coast of Viti Levu, and the fine sediment that comes with them is what makes the sand grey-brown and the shallows cloudy. It is not pollution and it is not seasonal — it is simply that coastline. The clear turquoise water is out at the Mamanuca and Yasawa islands, an hour or so offshore by catamaran.

How far is Denarau from Nadi airport?

About twenty minutes by road, across the causeway west of Nadi town. Resorts run transfers, and taxis are plentiful and inexpensive. It is the shortest airport-to-resort hop in Fiji, which is a large part of why Denarau exists in the form it does.

Should you stay on Denarau or go out to the islands?

If you have a week or more, do both: a night or two on Denarau at each end for the flights and the boats, and the middle of the trip out at an island. If you have only a few nights and want beaches, go straight out to the Mamanucas. If you have only a few nights and want convenience, a pool and a golf course, stay on Denarau and take a day cruise to see the islands.

Is Denarau good for families?

It is the most family-shaped place in Fiji. The resorts have kids' clubs, large shallow pools, and food that children will actually eat; the island is flat, gated and walkable; and there is no boat transfer to manage with tired children after a long flight. Villas and apartments are the sensible choice for larger families.
