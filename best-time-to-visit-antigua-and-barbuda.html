Best Time to Visit Antigua

Best Time to Visit Antigua and Barbuda

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Antigua has one of the steadier climates in the Caribbean and a clear split down the middle of the year. From the middle of December into April it is dry, bright and breezy, and the trade wind takes the edge off the heat; from June into November it is hotter, closer and wetter, with heavy showers rather than all-day rain, and it is inside the Atlantic hurricane belt. Prices follow the weather almost exactly, which is why the two shoulders — early December, and late April into May — are where the value sits. Below is the year month by month, then the events, the prices and the weeks we would choose ourselves.

Antigua month by month

Twelve months, with the honest word on each — including the ones the brochures leave out.

January

Peak dry season. Highs in the low eighties, nights in the low seventies, a steady trade wind and the lowest humidity of the year. Busy and dear, and about as reliable as Caribbean weather gets.

February

The same weather and the highest prices of the year. If you want a guaranteed dry fortnight and do not mind paying for it, this is the month. Book a long way ahead.

March

Still dry, still breezy, a degree or two warmer than February, and from the middle of the month the sailing fleets start arriving on the south coast. Our pick of the peak months.

April

Warming up and still dry for most of the month. The last week belongs to Sailing Week and the harbours fill. Prices begin to ease after Easter, which makes late April one of the best bargains in the year.

May

The shoulder, and underrated. The first proper showers arrive but they are short, the island is green again, the sea is warming and the crowds and the rates have both dropped.

June

Hot and humid, with afternoon showers. Hurricane season opens on the first of the month, although June very rarely produces anything. Good value and plenty of sunshine between the showers.

July

The hottest month, with highs in the high eighties and little wind some days. Carnival begins at the end of it and St John’s is full. Expect a heavy shower most days and bright sun either side of it.

August

Hot, wet and lively — Carnival finishes in the first days of the month. Storm risk starts to climb in the second half. Cheap, but watch the forecast.

September

The wettest month and the peak of the hurricane risk, and the quietest and cheapest time to be here. Some hotels and restaurants close for a refit. A gamble, and an inexpensive one.

October

Still wet and still inside the season, with the risk easing through the month. The sea is at its warmest. Prices stay low and the island is empty.

November

Hurricane season ends on the thirtieth and the weather is drying out through the month. Independence Day on the first is a national holiday with parades and a week of events around it. A good month, and not yet expensive.

December

The dry season returns. The first half is one of the two best-value windows in the year — proper dry-season weather at shoulder prices — and then from about the twentieth the Christmas and New Year peak arrives and rates double.

Rain, wind and hurricanes

The part of the year that needs planning rather than avoiding.

Antigua is one of the drier islands in the eastern Caribbean — it has no mountain to wring the clouds out — and in a normal year the wet season means a heavy shower in the afternoon and sun on either side of it, not days indoors. That same dryness is why much of the island’s water is desalinated and why hotels ask guests to be sparing with it.

The trade wind blows from the east almost all year and is the reason the heat is bearable. It also means the Atlantic coast is breezy and choppy while the western and northern bays stay calm, so if the wind is up, change coast rather than changing plans.

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November. Antigua sits in the belt and has been hit: Hurricane Irma passed over in September 2017 and destroyed most of the buildings on Barbuda, which was evacuated and has been rebuilding ever since, while Antigua itself came through comparatively lightly. In practice the risk in any single week is small, and it is concentrated from late August to early October. If you travel then, take insurance that names storms specifically, pick a fare you can move, and keep an eye on the forecast in the week before you fly.

Events through the year

Dates move, so confirm them with the organisers before you book a trip around one.

Antigua Racing Cup — March

A newer regatta based at Nelson’s Dockyard. The organisers give 17 to 21 March for the 2027 edition. Smaller than Sailing Week and growing.

Antigua Sailing Week — late April

The island’s oldest and biggest regatta, and one of the best known in the Caribbean. The published dates are 21 to 25 April 2027, with the same late-April slot in the years after.

Carnival — late July into August

About ten days of calypso, steel band, J’ouvert and costumed parades in St John’s, marking emancipation. The biggest event in the Antiguan year.

Independence — 1 November

National day, marking independence in 1981, with parades, a food fair and a week of events around it. A good week to be here and not an expensive one.

The yachting winter

From December to April the harbours on the south coast fill with charter and racing yachts. Falmouth and English Harbour are at their liveliest and their fullest.

Cricket

Test and regional cricket at the Sir Vivian Richards Stadium when the international calendar brings it. Worth checking if you care — an Antiguan crowd at a match is an experience in itself.

Prices and what is open

The spread between seasons here is wide. Christmas and New Year, then February and the Easter weeks, are the peak: the best-known resorts charge their highest rates and the good rooms go months ahead. From May the rates step down, and by September they are a fraction of the February figure.

The trade-off in the cheapest months is not only weather. A number of hotels and restaurants close for several weeks in September and October to refit, some excursions run on a reduced timetable, and the island is noticeably quiet. Check that the place you want is actually open before you book a flight.

Whatever the month, a government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills, so the total sits above the advertised rate. Ask what is included when you book — particularly at the all-inclusive resorts, where what counts as included varies a great deal from one to the next.

Travelling in your later years

Which months we would choose, and which we would avoid.

For most older travellers the best weeks here are the dry-season shoulders: the first half of December, and late April into May. The weather is as good as February, the island is quieter, the prices are lower, and — the point that matters most — the heat and humidity are far gentler than in the summer.

We would be cautious about July to early October. It is not the temperature so much as the humidity, which is heavy and persistent and makes walking any distance hard work. If you do travel then, insist on a room with reliable air conditioning, plan the day around the mornings, and carry water on any beach trip — several of the finest beaches here have no shade and no facilities whatsoever.

One practical note on flights: Antigua’s airport is fifteen to twenty-five minutes from the main hotel coasts, which is a shorter transfer than almost anywhere in the Windwards. After a long flight that is worth a great deal, and it is a good reason to choose this island over a steeper one.

Questions about when to visit Antigua

What is the best month to visit Antigua?

If the weather is all that matters, February and March: dry, breezy, warm without being heavy, and the lowest chance of a wet afternoon in the year. If value matters too, the best weeks are the first half of December and the back half of April into May — still in the dry season, still reliably sunny, and a good deal cheaper than the February peak.

When is hurricane season in Antigua?

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, and Antigua is in the belt. The risk is not spread evenly: it is small in June and July, climbs through August, and peaks from late August through September into early October. Most wet-season days are simply hot with a heavy shower, but if you travel in September, buy insurance that covers a named storm and choose a flexible fare.

Is September really a bad time to go?

It is the cheapest and the riskiest month, and some hotels and restaurants close for part of September and October to refit. If you are relaxed about the weather and want an almost empty island at a third of the February price, it can be a bargain. If this is a once-in-a-decade trip you have saved for, do not book it in September.

How hot does Antigua get?

Less than people fear, because of the trade wind. Daytime highs sit in the low to mid eighties Fahrenheit through the dry season and the high eighties in the summer, with nights in the seventies all year. The difference between January and August is not so much the temperature as the humidity — in August it is close and still, in January there is a breeze all day.

How warm is the sea?

Warm enough to swim in every month. The water sits around the high seventies Fahrenheit in the coolest part of the winter and the mid to upper eighties by late summer. The sheltered western and northern bays are calm nearly all the time; the Atlantic side of the island has surf and a breeze whatever the month.

When is Antigua Sailing Week, and is it a good time to visit?

The organisers' published dates for the next edition are 21 to 25 April 2027, in the traditional late-April slot, with subsequent years following the same pattern. There is also a separate newer regatta, the Antigua Racing Cup, set for 17 to 21 March 2027 at Nelson's Dockyard. Both are a fine time to be on the south coast if you like a crowd and a party, and the wrong time if you want a quiet beach — rooms around English and Falmouth Harbours book out and go up in price. Always confirm dates with the organisers before booking a trip around them.

When is Carnival?

Antigua's Carnival runs for about ten days from the end of July into the first days of August, finishing with the costumed parades, and it marks emancipation rather than Lent, which is why it falls in summer here and not in February. St John’s is the centre of it. It is hot, loud and genuinely local rather than staged for visitors; book a room early if you want to be in town for it.
