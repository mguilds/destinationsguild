Where to Stay in Mendoza

Where to Stay in Mendoza — Best Hotels & Wine Lodges

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Mendoza asks you one question before any other: city or vineyards? Mendoza city is low-rise, leafy and pleasantly walkable, built around the Plaza Independencia, with restaurants, a casino and no need for a car. The wine itself is elsewhere. Luján de Cuyo and neighbouring Chacras de Coria lie about half an hour south, close enough for easy day trips; the high Uco Valley is an hour and a half further and feels genuinely remote. The vineyard lodges are among the loveliest places to stay in Argentina, but nearly all of them require a car, a driver or a hotel transfer for every meal. Below are our picks by style, each with a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Two practical warnings. First, do not plan to drive yourself between tastings — hire a driver or take a tour; Argentina’s drink-drive limit is strict and the vineyard roads are dark. Second, the Uco Valley sits at roughly 1,100 metres and some wineries higher still. Mendoza city itself, at about 750 metres, is low enough that altitude is a non-issue.

Mendoza Hotels — Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of Mendoza should I stay in?

Stay in Mendoza city if you want to walk out to dinner, do not want to arrange a car, or are only here for a night or two — the plaza hotels are genuinely convenient. Stay in Luján de Cuyo or Chacras de Coria, about half an hour south, for vineyards within easy reach of the city. Stay in the Uco Valley, an hour and a half further, only if you have three nights or more and are content to eat where you sleep — it is beautiful and genuinely remote.

Which Mendoza hotels have a lift?

The city hotels do: Trip.com confirms lifts at the Park Hyatt, the Sheraton, the Diplomatic, the Huentala, the Argentino and the Amerian Executive. The vineyard lodges mostly do not — Trip.com lists no lift for Cavas, Villa Mansa, Club Tapiz, Susana Balbo, Posada Salentein or Atamisque. Most are low-rise, so this is less of a problem than it sounds, but the grounds are large and often gravelled. Casa de Uco is the one vineyard resort where Trip.com does list a lift.

Do I need a car in Mendoza?

Not in the city, where everything is walkable. Out in the vineyards, yes — but hire a driver or take a tour rather than driving yourself. You will be tasting wine, the drink-drive limit is strict, and the vineyard roads are unlit at night. Lares de Chacras and the Susana Balbo house are the two exceptions on this page: one is in a walkable village, the other includes airport transfers.

When is the best time to visit Mendoza?

Harvest, the vendimia, falls in March and is the most atmospheric time to come, with the festival at the start of the month. October to April is warm and reliable. Winter, June to August, is cold and quiet in the vineyards but clear, and it is ski season up at Las Leñas.
