Lake Fishing in Georgia & Alabama — Six Big Southern Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Lake Guntersville, Wheeler Lake, Lake Eufaula, Lake Lanier, Lake Seminole and Lake Oconee: what swims in each, the rules from the Alabama and Georgia wildlife agencies, the licence a visitor needs, where to launch a boat, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division and the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources, 16 November 2026

Georgia and Alabama are bass country, and these six lakes are the reason. In north Alabama the Tennessee River is dammed by the Tennessee Valley Authority into Lake Guntersville and Wheeler Lake, each about 67,000 acres by TVA’s count, with grass beds, crappie and famous bass water. On the state line the Chattahoochee becomes Lake Eufaula, where a licence from either state is good on the lake itself. North of Atlanta, Lake Lanier is the striped bass and spotted bass lake. In the far south-west corner of Georgia, Lake Seminole sits where the Flint and Chattahoochee meet Florida. And between Atlanta and Augusta, Lake Oconee holds hybrids, stripers and crappie.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, and anyone who wants a guide for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 16 November 2026 from the agency that sets it — the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources (Wildlife and Freshwater Fisheries) and the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division — and is dated. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The best months, in plain words

Southern lakes: long warm seasons, short mild winters, and fishing all year. The hottest weeks of July and August push fish deep and anglers out early or at night.

Lake Guntersville — Alabama’s biggest lake

TVA gives Guntersville about 67,900 acres of water, almost 890 miles of shoreline and a length of 76 miles, on the Tennessee River in north-east Alabama.

Lake Guntersville, from Scottsboro to Guntersville Dam

What swims there. Largemouth and smallmouth bass are why most visiting anglers come, and the Tennessee Valley Authority calls crappie “the number one sport fish” on the lake. Local guides also fish for sauger and walleye, white bass, stripers and bream.

How people fish it. Mostly from a boat: bass anglers work the grass beds and creek mouths with lures, crappie anglers fish brush and bridges, and guides offer half- and full-day trips. There is bank fishing and a fishing dock at Lake Guntersville State Park.

Keeping a fish. Guntersville has its own bass rule: any largemouth or smallmouth bass you keep from Guntersville Reservoir or Town Creek must be at least 15 inches. Otherwise Alabama’s statewide limits apply — 10 black bass a day (no more than 5 smallmouth), 30 crappie at least 9 inches long.

Confirmed with the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources, 16 November 2026 — freshwater creel and size limits page: Guntersville 15-inch minimum on largemouth and smallmouth bass; statewide black bass and crappie limits. Lake size from the Tennessee Valley Authority.

Boat ramps. Lake Guntersville State Park has ramps at the Short Creek and Town Creek bridges, at Trail of Tears (with a fishing dock) and near the campground. Its Town Creek Fishing Center has two ramps and rents canoes, kayaks, pontoons and bass boats.

Nearest towns: Guntersville, which sits on the lake, and Scottsboro for the upper lake and Goose Pond.

Wheeler Lake — the long lake by Decatur and Huntsville

TVA gives Wheeler about 67,070 acres of water and 1,027 miles of shoreline, from below Guntersville Dam west past Decatur to Wheeler Dam.

Wheeler Lake, from Ditto Landing to Joe Wheeler State Park

What swims there. Largemouth and smallmouth bass, crappie, catfish and the other Tennessee River fish. Wheeler’s own rule is for smallmouth, which tells you they are worth protecting here.

How people fish it. From a boat on the main river channel and in the creek arms, and from the bank and piers at the parks. Bass guides who fish the north Alabama lakes include Wheeler in their trips.

Keeping a fish. Any smallmouth bass kept from Wheeler Reservoir must be at least 15 inches. Otherwise Alabama’s statewide limits apply. Alabama allows only one catfish over 34 inches a day, and live catfish of 34 inches or more may not be taken out of the state.

Confirmed with the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources, 16 November 2026 — freshwater creel and size limits page: Wheeler 15-inch smallmouth minimum; statewide catfish rule. Before you eat what you keep: the Alabama Department of Public Health’s fish advisory (released June 2026) has entries for several Wheeler embayments, including “Do Not Eat Any” for largemouth bass in Round Island Creek and for all species in Baker’s Creek. Read it before you keep fish here.

Boat ramps and marinas. Joe Wheeler State Park near Rogersville has a marina with fuel, transient slips and boat rentals. Ditto Landing on the Huntsville reach and Lucy’s Branch near Athens are working marinas (listed below).

Nearest towns: Decatur and Huntsville for the most hotels; Athens for the north shore; Rogersville for Joe Wheeler State Park and the lower lake.

Lake Eufaula — one lake, two states, one licence

Its official name is Walter F. George Reservoir. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers says it runs 85 miles up the Chattahoochee with 640 miles of shoreline; the Alabama agency gives 45,181 acres.

Lake Eufaula, from Eufaula to Fort Gaines

What swims there. Largemouth bass and crappie are the lake’s best-known fish; local guides also run trips for shellcracker and other bream, and for catfish.

How people fish it. Boat fishing for bass along the creek channels and standing timber, crappie fishing over brush, and bank and pier fishing at the state parks. Guides here run night bass trips in summer.

One licence. Alabama and Georgia recognise each other’s fishing licences on the Chattahoochee where it forms the state line and on its impoundments, Lake Eufaula among them — but not on the creeks and rivers that flow in. Both states set a 14-inch minimum on largemouth bass on Lake Eufaula. Alabama’s 9-inch crappie minimum does not apply on these shared waters.

Confirmed with the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources and the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division, 16 November 2026 — Alabama regulation 220-2-.122 (2025–2026 regulation book) and Georgia’s agreements with bordering states: licences recognised on the boundary waters and impoundments, tributaries excluded; 14-inch largemouth minimum on Walter F. George in both states. The two states print the shared limits differently — Georgia says its statewide limits apply on these waters, Alabama’s regulation sets 10 black bass and 15 striped, white and hybrid bass (only 2 of 22 inches or longer) — and the figures agree. Live blueback herring may not be used in the Alabama waters of the agreement.

Boat ramps. Lakepoint State Park, north of Eufaula, has a marina with bait, tackle, licences and boat rentals, two ramps, and a lodge and cabins. On the Georgia shore Florence Marina State Park has a marina, ramp, bait shop, lighted fishing pier and fish-cleaning station.

Nearest towns: Eufaula, Alabama, for most beds; Fort Gaines, Georgia, for the dam end. (We left out Georgetown, Georgia: Trip.com’s search sends that name to Guyana and Malaysia.)

Lake Lanier — stripers and spotted bass north of Atlanta

The Corps of Engineers gives Lanier 39,000 acres and more than 692 miles of shoreline, with about 40 Corps park areas around it. The Corps says more than 7.5 million people visit Lanier a year.

Lake Lanier, from Gainesville to Buford Dam

What swims there. Striped bass, spotted bass and largemouth, crappie, walleye, white bass, catfish and bream. Georgia’s white bass record — 5 lb 1 oz, caught in 1971 — came from Lake Lanier.

How people fish it. Striper anglers pull live bait and troll from boats, and Lanier’s striper guides fish all year. Bass anglers work points, docks and timber for spotted bass.

Keeping a fish. Lanier has its own bass rule: largemouth and spotted bass must be at least 14 inches. Georgia allows 10 black bass a day, and 15 striped, hybrid and white bass in total, only 2 of them 22 inches or longer.

Confirmed with the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division, 16 November 2026 — 2026–2027 Georgia fishing regulations: Lake Lanier 14-inch minimum on largemouth and spotted bass; statewide black bass and striped/hybrid/white bass limits. Lake facts from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. Eating what you keep: Georgia EPD’s fish guidelines suggest limits on some Lanier bass, walleye and white bass; crappie, catfish and stripers carry no restriction in its 2023 booklet.

Boat ramps. The Corps runs about 40 park areas on the lake, many with ramps; we could not confirm an exact ramp count. Marinas with fuel and stores ring the lake (one is listed below).

Nearest towns: Gainesville for the north and middle lake, Flowery Branch for the middle, Buford for the south end and the dam, and Cumming for the west shore.

Lake Seminole — where the Flint and the Chattahoochee meet

The Corps of Engineers gives Lake Seminole 37,500 acres and 376 miles of shoreline, in Georgia’s south-west corner with its dam, Jim Woodruff Dam, on the Florida line.

Lake Seminole, from Bainbridge to Jim Woodruff Dam

What swims there. Largemouth bass and crappie, with hybrid and striped bass, shoal bass from the rivers, catfish and bream.

How people fish it. Mostly from boats, for bass and crappie, with guides on the lake; from the bank and the fishing dock at Seminole State Park.

Rules to know. Georgia closes all fishing, including spear fishing, in the marked areas around five fish refuges in Lake Seminole from 1 May to 31 October. A Georgia or Florida licence is valid on most of the lake under the two states’ agreement; Florida’s rules apply on the Florida portion. Georgia’s statewide limits apply on the rest.

Confirmed with the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division, 16 November 2026 — 2026–2027 Georgia fishing regulations: the five Lake Seminole fish refuges closed 1 May – 31 October; the Georgia–Florida agreement and its boundaries. We did not check Florida FWC’s own wording — if you fish the Florida side, check with Florida FWC.

Boat ramps. Seminole State Park near Donalsonville has three ramps, a fishing dock and kayak and canoe rental.

Nearest towns: Bainbridge for the Flint River arm and the most hotels; Donalsonville for the state park and the Spring Creek side; Chattahoochee, Florida, for the dam end. Our Florida lake guide covers the waters south of the line.

Lake Oconee — hybrids, stripers and crappie between Atlanta and Augusta

The lake behind Georgia Power’s Wallace Dam: about 19,000 acres and 374 miles of shoreline, by Georgia Power’s own figures.

Lake Oconee, from Greensboro to Eatonton

What swims there. Hybrid and striped bass, largemouth bass and crappie are what local guides fish for, with catfish and bream.

How people fish it. Guides run hybrid and striper trips, often watching the electronics for schools, and crappie trips around brush and bridges. Marinas rent boats and run launches.

Keeping a fish. Largemouth bass kept from Lake Oconee must be at least 14 inches. Georgia’s statewide limits cover the rest: 15 striped, hybrid and white bass a day in total, only 2 of them 22 inches or longer; 30 crappie.

Confirmed with the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division, 16 November 2026 — 2026–2027 Georgia fishing regulations: Lake Oconee 14-inch largemouth minimum; statewide limits. Lake size from Georgia Power.

Boat ramps. Fish Tale Marina and Anchors Marina near Eatonton both list launches or rentals (below). We did not confirm every public ramp on the lake today.

Nearest towns: Greensboro for the north and the Greene County shore; Eatonton for the south and the Putnam County shore.

The licence and the limits, plainly

Two states, two licences — except on the shared Chattahoochee lakes, where either one will do.

Georgia fishing licences

Who needs one. Georgia residents aged 16 to 64, and non-residents 16 and older. Children under 16 fish without one. A separate trout licence is needed only for mountain trout.

For a visitor. Non-resident annual licence $50; one-day licence $10, plus $3.50 for each extra day.

For a Georgian. Annual licence $15; seniors 65 and over can buy a Senior Sportsman’s annual licence for $7.

Where to buy. Online at Go Outdoors Georgia, by phone on 1-800-366-2661, or from a licence agent. Georgia adds a transaction fee ($3 online and at retail, $5 by phone).

Free fishing days. Georgia residents may fish public waters without a licence on its free fishing days; the Wildlife Resources Division lists 5 June and 12 June 2027 as the next ones.

Confirmed with the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division, 16 November 2026 — WRD licence price list and licence pages: the prices and age rules above. Prices change. Buy from the agency or a licensed agent.

Alabama fishing licences

Who needs one. Non-residents 16 and older — with no exemption for non-residents over 65. Alabama residents 65 and over are exempt, and residents may fish from the bank with hook and line in their own county without a licence (carry your driver’s licence).

For a visitor. Non-resident annual freshwater licence $68.20 ($70.60 for Louisiana residents and $75.42 for Mississippi residents); seven-day trip licence $38.05; family three-day trip licence, up to four immediate family members, $38.05.

For an Alabamian. Resident freshwater licence $17.45. Alabama licences expire on 31 August each year.

Where to buy. Online, by phone on 1-888-848-6887, at county probate offices and licence commissioners, at licence agents, or at Wildlife and Freshwater Fisheries district offices. Lakepoint State Park’s marina sells licences.

Confirmed with the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources, 16 November 2026 — resident and non-resident freshwater licence pages: the prices and rules above. The price pages carry no year label; the creel-limit pages are still headed 2025–2026. Prices change. Buy from the agency or a licensed agent.

Boats and life jackets

Georgia. Anyone born on or after 1 January 1998 must take a DNR-approved boater education course to operate a boat. Children under 12 may not operate a vessel over 16 feet. Every person aboard needs a wearable life jacket, and children under 13 must wear one while the boat is under way unless inside a fully enclosed cabin.

Alabama. You must be at least 12 to operate a motorised boat, and operators need a vessel (“V”) endorsement on their licence; under 14, only a learner licence with a licensed adult of 21 or older aboard. Every person aboard needs a wearable life jacket; children under 8 must wear one at all times; boats 16 feet and longer must also carry a throwable cushion or ring.

Confirmed with Georgia DNR Law Enforcement and the Alabama Law Enforcement Agency, 16 November 2026. Visiting boaters: check how each state treats your home state’s boating card before you launch.

Eating what you keep

Alabama’s advice is the Alabama Department of Public Health’s fish consumption advisory (released June 2026); Georgia’s is the Environmental Protection Division’s Guidelines for Eating Fish from Georgia Waters (2023 edition). Both list advice by lake, fish and size, mostly about largemouth bass and mercury. Guntersville’s listed sites carry no restriction; parts of Wheeler and Lake Eufaula carry strict ones. Read the current advice for the water you fish before you keep fish to eat.

Questions

Do I need two licences to fish Lake Eufaula, on the Alabama–Georgia line?

No. Under the agreement between the two states, a valid Alabama or Georgia fishing licence is good on the waters of the Chattahoochee River where it forms the state line and on its impoundments, which include Walter F. George Reservoir (Lake Eufaula), Lake George W. Andrews and the part of Lake Seminole outside the Florida agreement. The agreement does not cover the creeks and rivers that flow in: on those you need the licence of the state you are standing in. On Lake Eufaula both states set a 14-inch minimum on largemouth bass.

What does a fishing licence cost a visitor in Georgia and Alabama?

Georgia, from the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division price list: non-resident annual $50, non-resident one-day $10 and $3.50 for each extra day; residents pay $15 a year. Children under 16 need no Georgia licence. Alabama, from the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources: non-resident annual freshwater licence $68.20 ($70.60 for Louisiana residents, $75.42 for Mississippi residents), seven-day trip licence $38.05, and a family three-day trip licence for up to four family members, $38.05; residents pay $17.45. Alabama licences expire on 31 August. Prices were read on 16 November 2026 and do change.

What are the bass size limits on these lakes?

Lake Guntersville: largemouth and smallmouth bass must be at least 15 inches (Alabama). Wheeler Lake: smallmouth bass at least 15 inches (Alabama). Lake Eufaula: largemouth at least 14 inches in both states. Lake Lanier: largemouth and spotted bass at least 14 inches. Lake Oconee: largemouth at least 14 inches. Elsewhere in Georgia the largemouth minimum is 12 inches. Both states allow 10 black bass a day in total, with limits on how many of certain species.

Can I fish Lake Seminole with a Florida licence?

In part. Under Georgia's agreement with Florida, a valid Georgia or Florida licence covers freshwater fishing on the part of Lake Seminole bounded by Florida State Road 271 to the west, Jim Woodruff Dam to the south, and the Herman Talmadge Bridge to the north. Florida's rules apply on the Florida portion of the lake. Georgia closes five marked fish refuges in Lake Seminole to all fishing from 1 May to 31 October.

Can I use live blueback herring for bait?

Not in the Alabama waters covered by the Georgia–Alabama agreement: Georgia's regulations say it is illegal to possess and fish with live blueback herring there, which includes Lake Eufaula. Blueback herring are sold as striper bait around Lake Lanier. Bait rules change and differ by water, so check with the Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources Division or the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources before you buy bait for another lake.

Ramp counts and fees. We could not confirm how many Corps ramps there are on Lanier, or every ramp on Oconee, and we give no launch fees. Low water closes ramps.
