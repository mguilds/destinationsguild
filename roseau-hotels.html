Roseau, Dominica

Where to Stay in Roseau

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Roseau is the capital of Dominica and the island’s front door: a small, busy, unglamorous town of narrow streets and a harbour front on the south-west coast, with the mountains standing up behind it. It is not where you come for a beach. It is where you stay to reach everything else — the World Heritage park inland, the dive sites a few minutes offshore, the sulphur springs to the south and the road north to Cabrits. This page covers the areas in and around the town, the hotels we were able to read on their own websites, and the ones we have deliberately left off.

The areas, and which one suits you

Roseau is a small working capital on the south-west coast, and it is the island’s gateway rather than its beach. Stay here for the parks, the dive boats and the short drives; go north or south for sand.

The waterfront

The sea side of the town, along Victoria Street, where the harbour front and the island’s best-known hotel are. This is the most convenient base on the island for a first visit: you can walk to the market and the Botanic Gardens, and the drives up into the mountains start from your door.

The old town grid

Behind the front, a tight grid of narrow streets, shopfronts, churches and the market. Lively on a weekday morning and on Saturday, quiet by evening. Very few beds for visitors — come for an hour on foot rather than for a week.

The coast just south

The shoreline immediately south of town is where several of the island’s dive operations work from, because the drop-off is close in. If diving is the reason you are coming, ask your operator which pier they leave from before you choose a hotel — a ten-minute difference on land is worth more than it sounds at seven in the morning.

Canefield, towards the airport

Canefield Airport is three miles north of Roseau, and the small strip of coast between the two is near enough to town to be practical. Worth a thought for a single night either side of a light-aircraft hop, not for a holiday.

Soufrière and the south

Half an hour or so down the coast, past the sulphur springs, the land rises steeply and the island’s best-known wellness resort sits above the sea. It is a different holiday from a town hotel and we have put it on this page because anyone comparing the south-west wants it in the comparison — but it is not in Roseau, and the card below says so.

Getting about

Taxis in Dominica are not metered, and the UK Foreign Office notes you can usually pay in US dollars — so agree the fare before you set off. The same page says to arrange car hire before you arrive rather than on the day, that the roads are sometimes narrow and in poor condition and that there are few road signs. Fort Young Hotel & Dive Resort

The hotel writes its own name as Fort Young Hotel & Dive Resort and gives its address as Victoria Street, Roseau. It calls the setting “our landmark waterfront location in the capital” and describes itself as a full-service, four-star hotel on the Caribbean Sea. Rooms are listed by type rather than by number — Fort Mountain View, Fort Ocean View, Oceanfront, Deluxe Oceanfront, Junior Ocean Suite and Oceanfront Suite — and the site does not publish a total room count, so we are not going to invent one. There is a dive operation on the premises, which the hotel calls Fort Young Dive and which it says runs daily dives. One thing we deliberately have not written: the name invites you to assume the building stands on an eighteenth-century fort, and that may well be so, but the hotel’s own site does not say it, so neither do we. Ask which room category you are being quoted; mountain-view and oceanfront are a different proposition at the same address.

Jungle Bay Dominica

Soufrière, not Roseau60 bungalowsWellness

Where it actually is, first: Jungle Bay is at Soufrière, down the coast in the south of the island, not in Roseau. It is on this page because anyone weighing up the south-west coast wants it in the comparison, not because it is in town. Its own site states the figure plainly — sixty luxury eco-bungalows — and describes itself as bespoke wellness and eco-luxury above the Caribbean Sea. It names its spa as the Bamboo Spa, and its Adventure and Wellness package is described as having all meals included, which is worth knowing because it changes how the nightly rate compares with a room in town. The site’s footer carries a 2026 copyright and live booking links. If you want the mountains and the sea and are content to drive, or not to drive at all, this is the island’s best-known property of its kind; if you want to walk to a market, it is the wrong base.

Travelling in your later years

Roseau is the sensible base on this island if you are travelling in your seventies or eighties, and the reason is simple: almost everything worth seeing is a drive from it, so you unpack once. The waterfront is flat and walkable, the market and the Botanic Gardens are close, and a hotel in town means you are not negotiating a steep unlit drive at the end of a long day. Three things to weigh honestly. First, the island’s great sights are mountain walks, and the famous ones are hard going — there is a great deal of Dominica you can enjoy from a car, a boat and a short level path, and no shame in planning it that way. Second, the Foreign Office is blunt about the roads: narrow, sometimes in poor condition, few signs, and the main highway from Douglas–Charles Airport prone to landslides after bad weather with sheer drops where the road has fallen away, and it says not to drive that road at night. Arrange a driver for the airport run and the mountain days. Third, on health: the Foreign Office says the government hospital in Roseau, the Dominica China Friendship Hospital, can cope with many types of treatment but that serious cases may require emergency evacuation, and that private treatment can be expensive. Take insurance that covers evacuation, and carry your medicines in your hand luggage.

Questions about staying in Roseau

Is Roseau a good base for seeing Dominica?

It is the best one for a first visit, and the reason is geography rather than charm. Roseau sits on the south-west coast about a third of the way up the island, so the mountain parks, the sulphur springs, the west-coast dive sites and the road north are all day trips from one bed. Stay here and you unpack once. What Roseau is not is a beach holiday: it is a small working capital with a harbour front, a market and a grid of narrow streets, and if sand is the point of the trip you want the north or the south coast instead.

How far is Roseau from the airports?

There are two, and they are very different. Canefield Airport is three miles, or five kilometres, north of Roseau and is the small one - its runway is 3,130 feet, it operates only between sunrise and sunset, and the aircraft are turboprops and light planes. Douglas-Charles Airport, on the other side of the island, is the one with the jets, and it is a long drive: the airport's own Wikipedia entry puts it about an hour from Portsmouth, and the drive to Roseau is longer still. Neither page we read publishes a Roseau drive time, so we are not going to print a figure we cannot source. Arrange your transfer in advance, and do not plan to drive that road after dark.

Which hotels in Roseau can the Guild actually vouch for?

One in the town itself: Fort Young Hotel and Dive Resort, on Victoria Street on the waterfront, which we read on its own website. We have also put Jungle Bay on this page, but it is at Soufriere in the south of the island rather than in Roseau, and its card says so in its first line. Two other Roseau-area properties have websites that refuse automated readers, so we could not read them and we have not named them. That is a short list for a capital city, and we would rather you knew it was short than pad it from a booking site.

Do I need a car in Roseau?

Not to stay in town, and possibly not at all. Taxis are everywhere, and the UK Foreign Office notes they are not metered and that you can usually pay in US dollars, so agree the fare first. For the mountain days a driver is often better value than a hire car once you have read what the Foreign Office says about the roads: narrow, sometimes in poor condition, few road signs, and easy to get lost. If you do want a car, that same page says to arrange the hire before you arrive rather than on the day.

Is Roseau a good cruise day ashore?

Dominica is one of the better port days in the eastern Caribbean for anyone who likes landscape rather than shopping, because the island's great sights are close to the coast. We have to be straight with you about the detail, though: the Dominica Air and Sea Ports Authority's own website refuses automated readers, so we could not confirm the berth arrangements at Roseau from the authority that runs them, and we are not going to describe a cruise terminal we could not read about. Your cruise line's port information is the version that counts. What we can say is what is near: the market, the Botanic Gardens and the town on foot, or a drive up into the hills.

What should I see from Roseau in one day?

Pick one direction and do it properly. Inland takes you up towards Morne Trois Pitons National Park, which UNESCO inscribed as a World Heritage site in 1997 and which holds the Boiling Lake, the Valley of Desolation and the island's two largest lakes, Boeri and Freshwater. North along the coast is the road to Portsmouth and Cabrits. South is the sulphur springs at Soufriere and the island's tapering tail. What does not work is trying to combine two of those: the roads are slow, and the day disappears through a car window.

What money will I need?

The East Caribbean dollar, which Dominica shares with seven neighbouring territories as a member of the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank - the bank lists it among its members as the Commonwealth of Dominica. The EC dollar is pegged to the US dollar at EC$2.70, a fixed rate the central bank's own website marks the fiftieth anniversary of, so divide a local price by rather less than three. US dollars are widely accepted and the Foreign Office says taxi fares can usually be paid in them, though change may come back in local money. Carry cash for small places, and note that the Foreign Office says cash or travellers cheques of EC$10,000 or more must be declared on arrival.

Is there a departure tax?

Yes, and it catches people out. Both the UK Foreign Office and the US State Department give it as 86 East Caribbean dollars, or 33 US dollars. The Foreign Office says it is usually included in the cost of a plane ticket but that you also have to pay it if you leave by ferry, which is the trap for anyone island-hopping to Guadeloupe or Martinique by boat. The State Department adds that children under twelve are exempt. Check your ticket before you reach the airport rather than at the desk.
