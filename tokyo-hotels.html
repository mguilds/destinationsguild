Tokyo’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Tokyo — Best Hotels by Category

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Tokyo is really a constellation of cities, and the stay that suits you depends less on a single neighborhood than on what you want the hotel to do. Are you after a sky-high luxury tower with the skyline at your feet, a design-led boutique with real character, a smart value room near the trains, a family base with room to spread out, grown-up calm, or somewhere that welcomes your dog? We’ve grouped Tokyo’s most enduring, well-run hotels by category, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can just choose your dates.

Luxury

Tokyo does luxury with a quiet, almost obsessive perfectionism — flawless service, hushed high-floor rooms and city views that go on forever. These are hotels where the stay is as memorable as the sightseeing, and where the famous Japanese hospitality, omotenashi, is felt in every small detail.

Aman Tokyo

$$$$ LuxuryOtemachiTop-floor sanctuarySpa & onsen

Occupying the upper floors of a tower above Otemachi, Aman Tokyo feels like a serene mountain ryokan lifted into the sky, with soaring stone-and-paper interiors and vast rooms wrapped in city views. Its two-story spa, indoor pool and camellia-scented calm are a world away from the streets below, yet the Imperial Palace gardens are a short walk away. This is the choice when you want luxury as stillness rather than show. Expect the very top of the market in both polish and price.

Mandarin Oriental, Tokyo

$$$$ LuxuryNihonbashiSkyline viewsMichelin dining

Crowning a tower in the historic Nihonbashi merchant district, the Mandarin Oriental pairs large, warmly modern rooms with some of the finest views and restaurants in the city. Its spa is a perennial award-winner and the dining is studded with Michelin stars, all delivered with gracious, unhurried service. It is central for the traditional heart of old Tokyo yet effortlessly refined. A superb pick for travelers who want landmark luxury with real warmth.

Boutique

Tokyo’s boutique hotels are where the city’s design instincts really shine — small, distinctive places with a strong point of view, whether that’s a modern ryokan with its own hot spring or a lifestyle hotel woven into a fashionable backstreet. Choose these when you want personality and a sense of place over a big-brand lobby.

Hoshinoya Tokyo

$$$$ Luxury boutiqueOtemachiModern ryokanRooftop onsen

A luxury ryokan reimagined as a slender tower in the business district, Hoshinoya Tokyo asks you to leave your shoes at the door and sink into tatami floors, sliding screens and floor-level beds. Each level has its own intimate lounge, and a top-floor open-air onsen draws hot-spring water up from deep beneath the city. It is a genuinely immersive taste of Japanese ryokan tradition without leaving the capital. Best for travelers who want culture and craft folded into their stay.

Trunk Hotel

$$$ UpscaleShibuya / Cat StreetDesign-ledGreat bar scene

Tucked just off Cat Street between Shibuya and Harajuku, Trunk Hotel is the original of Tokyo’s socially minded lifestyle boutiques, with reclaimed-material interiors, a buzzy street-level lounge and a young, creative crowd. Rooms are compact but full of thoughtful, locally made touches, and the location puts the city’s best shopping and cafés on the doorstep. It leans lively rather than hushed, so light sleepers should ask about quieter rooms. Ideal for travelers who want to feel plugged into contemporary Tokyo.

Budget & Value

Tokyo can be surprisingly kind to a careful budget: clean, compact, well-run rooms beside a train line cost a fraction of the towers, and put the whole network at your feet. You trade square footage for location and reliability — and spend the savings on ramen, temples and day trips.

Hotel Gracery Shinjuku

$$ Mid-rangeShinjuku / KabukichoThe Godzilla hotelOn the train hub

Famous for the giant Godzilla head that peers over its terrace, Hotel Gracery Shinjuku is a cheerful, dependable mid-range tower right in the middle of Shinjuku’s dining and nightlife. Rooms are modern and tidy if snug, and you are minutes on foot from one of the world’s busiest stations, which makes day trips effortless. The Kabukicho setting is lively and a little raffish, so it suits the curious more than the retiring. A reliable, well-priced base for first-time explorers.

Family-friendly

Traveling with children in Tokyo is easier from a base with a little room to breathe — larger rooms, a pool, and easy reach of the big attractions. The bayfront around Odaiba and the Disney resort area at Maihama are the natural family choices, with space, views and things for younger travelers to do close at hand.

Grand Nikko Tokyo Daiba

$$$ UpscaleOdaiba bayfrontPoolFamily suites

Set on the Odaiba waterfront with sweeping views over Tokyo Bay and the Rainbow Bridge, Grand Nikko Tokyo Daiba is a large, comfortable hotel built for families, with roomy layouts, dedicated family suites and a seasonal outdoor pool. A monorail station at the door and the beach, malls and museums of Odaiba nearby mean the children are rarely bored. It is a relaxed, resort-like escape while still being firmly inside the city. A dependable, spacious pick for a family holiday.

Adults-only

Pet-friendly

Tokyo is a dog-loving city, but relatively few hotels take pets, so the ones that do are worth knowing. The Prince group leads here, with a handful of properties offering dedicated dog-friendly rooms and amenities. Always confirm the size limit, fees and booking rules directly, as pet rooms are limited and go quickly.

Good to know before you book

What is the best area to stay in Tokyo?

For first-time visitors, Shinjuku is the most convenient base — it is Tokyo’s great transport hub, with trains and subways fanning out across the city. Shibuya suits younger travelers and night owls, Ginza and Marunouchi offer polished central luxury near the Imperial Palace, and Asakusa gives you old-Tokyo temple charm at gentler prices. Pick the neighborhood that matches your pace, then let the train network do the rest.

Is Tokyo walkable?

Each Tokyo neighborhood is wonderfully walkable, with sights, shops and restaurants clustered close together. But the city as a whole is enormous, so you will lean on the superb train and subway network to move between districts. Buy a Suica or Pasmo IC card on arrival, tap in and out, and you can cross the city cheaply and easily without a word of Japanese.

When is the best time to visit Tokyo?

Spring, from late March into April, brings the famous cherry blossoms and mild, pleasant days. Autumn, from October through November, is equally lovely, with crisp air and golden foliage. Summer is hot and humid with a rainy spell in June, while winter is cold but often bright and clear — and the quietest, best-value time to visit.
