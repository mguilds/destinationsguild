Lake Fishing in Wyoming — Yellowstone Lake, Mountain Water and the Towns Beside Them

Yellowstone Lake, Fremont Lake, Boysen, Glendo and the Wyoming end of Flaming Gorge: what swims in each, the rules from the agencies that set them, the licence or permit a visitor needs, where the boats go in, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Wyoming Game and Fish Department, the National Park Service, Wyoming State Parks and the U.S. Forest Service, 22 November 2026

Wyoming is better known for its rivers, but its lakes are some of the biggest and coldest in the West. Yellowstone Lake, high in the national park, is the home of the native Yellowstone cutthroat trout — and of lake trout that are not native there, which the park requires every angler to kill. Fremont Lake, just outside Pinedale, is a glacier-cut lake about 600 feet deep where lake trout grow large. Boysen and Glendo are reservoirs in open country, the state’s walleye lakes. And at the south-west corner, Flaming Gorge runs from the town of Green River down into Utah, a lake known for big lake trout, kokanee salmon and burbot fished through the ice at night.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, and anyone who wants a guide for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 22 November 2026 from the body that sets it, and is dated. Three things to know before you plan: Yellowstone has its own fishing permit and its own season, which closed on 31 October and reopens in late May; every boat that comes into Wyoming from another state must be inspected before it touches the water; and on Flaming Gorge the lake trout and burbot rules are designed to thin those fish out, so read them before you release anything. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

Yellowstone Lake — native cutthroat, and lake trout you must keep

Yellowstone National Park, run by the National Park Service. The park sets its own fishing rules and sells its own permit.

Yellowstone Lake, from Bridge Bay to Grant Village

What swims there. Yellowstone cutthroat trout, the native fish, and lake trout, which are not native to the lake. The park’s rules turn on that difference.

Keeping a fish. The park’s 2026 regulations say all lake trout caught in Yellowstone Lake must be killed — it is illegal to release them alive — and there is no possession limit on non-native fish. All native fish, which includes the cutthroat, must be released unharmed.

How people fish it. From the shore and by wading, with flies and lures, for cutthroat; and from boats, trolling deep for lake trout. Only lead-free artificial lures and flies are allowed in the park; the regulations make an exception for lead-core line and heavy downrigger weights used for lake trout on Yellowstone Lake. Bait is not allowed.

The season. In 2026 the park’s general season opened on the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend (23 May) and closed on 31 October, and Yellowstone Lake follows it. The streams that run into the lake, and the water within 100 yards of their mouths, open on 15 July. Fishing is from sunrise to sunset. The season is closed now; expect it to reopen on the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend 2027, and check the park’s regulations when they are published.

Places you may not fish. The Bridge Bay and Grant Village marinas and harbours and their channels, the shoreline from West Thumb to Little Thumb Creek, and the lowest 2 miles of Pelican Creek.

Boats. Every boat — even a float tube — needs a park boat permit and an inspection for invasive species before it goes in. The park’s boating page (updated 20 October 2026) gives the permits as $20 for seven days or $30 for the season without a motor, and $40 for seven days or $60 for the season with one; some sailboats and inboard boats need 30 days of drying first. Motorboats are allowed only on Yellowstone and Lewis lakes. In 2026 the Bridge Bay ranger station issued permits from 23 May to 20 September and the Grant station from 23 May to 31 October. There is a fishing platform for wheelchair users at the Grant Village boat launch. No car? The park concessioner rents small motorboats and runs guided fishing trips from the Bridge Bay Marina in summer (see Guides & marinas).

Confirmed with the National Park Service, 22 November 2026 — Yellowstone’s 2026 fishing regulations, its boating page (updated 20 October 2026) and the Superintendent’s Compendium (September 2026). The park fishing permit’s 2026 price did not show on any park page we could read; buy it on Recreation.gov or at a park visitor centre and check the price there.

Nearest towns: inside the park, the concessioner’s Lake Yellowstone Hotel and Lake Lodge Cabins sit right on the shore in summer. Outside it, West Yellowstone in Montana is the nearest town to the lake by road; Cody is the gateway from the east, over Sylvan Pass; and Jackson is the gateway from the south, through Grand Teton National Park. Check which park roads are open before you drive in spring or autumn.

Fremont Lake — deep, cold and full of lake trout

In the Bridger-Teton National Forest, a few miles from Pinedale, at the foot of the Wind River Range.

Fremont Lake, Pinedale

What it is. The Forest Service calls Fremont Lake the second largest lake in Wyoming and one of the deepest in the United States, at about 600 feet. It was carved by glaciers coming down out of the Wind Rivers.

What swims there, and how people fish it. Lake trout (mackinaw) are the fish people come for. The Wyoming Game and Fish Department’s spring 2026 forecast said fishing is best shortly after the ice goes off, trolling or jigging over shoals and reefs, and that its sampling turned up some of the largest lake trout it had seen there in 25 years, including one of 39 inches. It also says kokanee are caught by trolling open water. Rainbows and other trout are taken from the shore.

Keeping a fish. Fremont Lake has no special rules of its own, so Wyoming’s statewide limits apply: six trout a day from lakes, and lake trout and splake six combined with no more than one over 24 inches. In this corner of the state (the Green River drainage, Game and Fish Area 4), any burbot, walleye, yellow perch or northern pike you catch must be killed immediately.

Ice fishing. Fremont Lake is not on Wyoming’s list of waters where you may fish with more than two lines through the ice, so the ordinary line limit applies.

Boat ramps and camping. The Forest Service’s Lower Fremont Lake boating site has two paved ramps, no fee and is open day and night, though low water can limit access. Fremont Lake Campground, on the east shore about 7 miles north-east of Pinedale at 7,400 feet, has 54 sites ($15 single, $30 double, $50 group), its own ramp and dock, and opens on Memorial Day. Lakeside Lodge on the south shore runs a marina with boat rentals (see Guides & marinas).

Confirmed with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department and the U.S. Forest Service, 22 November 2026 — Chapter 46 fishing regulations (effective 1 January 2026), the Game and Fish spring and summer forecast (27 April 2026), and the Bridger-Teton National Forest’s Fremont Lake Campground (updated 16 September 2026) and Lower Fremont Lake boating pages.

Nearest town: Pinedale, the town at the foot of the lake, with places to stay, an outfitter’s shop, and the Lakeside Lodge cabins on the lake itself.

Boysen Reservoir — walleye in the middle of Wyoming

Boysen State Park, run by Wyoming State Parks, on the Wind River above the Wind River Canyon.

Boysen Reservoir, Shoshoni to the canyon

What it is. A Bureau of Reclamation reservoir of roughly 20,000 surface acres with about 76 miles of shoreline, in open sagebrush country between Shoshoni and Thermopolis.

What swims there. Walleye and sauger, rainbow trout, yellow perch and crappie. Game and Fish’s spring 2026 forecast called spring ideal for rainbows from the shore and said the walleye fishing was very good.

Keeping a fish. Boysen has no lake-specific limits, so the statewide limits apply: six walleye, two sauger (counted separately), six trout, and perch, crappie and other panfish within a combined fifty. In this part of the state (Area 2), walleye and sauger must be kept whole until you are off the water or the ice.

Ice fishing. Boysen is on Wyoming’s special winter list: while it is ice-covered you may fish up to six lines, each line, pole or tip-up must carry your name, and you must stay within 300 yards of all of them. The regulations also allow live baitfish here; check Chapter 46 for which ones.

Boat ramps. Wyoming State Parks lists Brannon, Tough Creek and Cottonwood (with floating docks), Fremont (concrete dock) and Lakeside (ramp, no dock), open all year. A privately run marina with a store operates from May to October; we could not find a website of its own. Day use is $7 a vehicle for Wyoming residents and $12 for visitors.

Water advisories. The park posted a cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) advisory on 13 July 2026 and a toxin advisory on 18 August 2026, including at Brannon and Tough Creek. Check the park page for the current status before you swim, let a dog drink, or clean fish in the lake water.

Confirmed with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department, Wyoming State Parks and the Bureau of Reclamation, 22 November 2026 — Chapter 46 fishing regulations (effective 1 January 2026), the spring forecast (27 April 2026), the Boysen ramps and marina pages, the 2026 park fee schedule and Reclamation’s Boysen recreation page.

Nearest towns: Shoshoni sits at the south end of the lake but is very small, so its button is left off; most anglers stay in Riverton, to the south-west, or in Thermopolis, below the Wind River Canyon to the north, with its hot springs.

Glendo Reservoir — one of the state’s best walleye lakes

Glendo State Park, run by Wyoming State Parks, on the North Platte River in south-east Wyoming.

Glendo Reservoir, from Elkhorn to Whiskey Gulch

What it is. About 12,000 acres of water below Laramie Peak. Wyoming State Parks calls it one of the best walleye fisheries in the state.

Keeping a fish. Glendo has its own walleye rule: any walleye under 15 inches must be released, and walleye must be kept whole until you are off the water. The daily limit is the statewide six. Fishing contests are not allowed from 15 to 30 June.

How people fish it. Trolling and jigging for walleye from boats, and from the shore in spring. Glendo is also on the special winter list, so through the ice you may fish up to six lines, each marked with your name, staying within 300 yards of them.

Boat ramps. Wyoming State Parks lists Elkhorn, Bennett Hill, Reno Cove, Whiskey Gulch, the Marina and Indian Point ramps; several have separate high-water and low-water lanes, and some are usable only for a short season, depending on the level. The park’s own water-level figure was out of date when we checked, so ask the park before you tow. Day use is $7 a vehicle for Wyoming residents and $12 for visitors.

Water advisory. The park posted a toxin advisory on 15 September 2026 covering the marina area and Custer Cove. Check the park page for the current status.

Confirmed with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department and Wyoming State Parks, 22 November 2026 — Chapter 46 fishing regulations (effective 1 January 2026), the Glendo park, boat-ramp and marina pages, and the 2026 park fee schedule.

Nearest towns: the little town of Glendo is beside the park, and B & A Marina at the south end has a motel of its own in season. For a wider choice, stay in Wheatland, to the south, or Douglas, to the north-west, both on Interstate 25.

Flaming Gorge — the Wyoming end, for lake trout, kokanee and burbot

Flaming Gorge National Recreation Area, run by the U.S. Forest Service (Ashley National Forest). The reservoir crosses into Utah; this guide covers the Wyoming end.

Flaming Gorge: Buckboard Crossing, Firehole and Anvil Draw

What swims there. Lake trout, some of them very large, kokanee salmon, rainbow trout, smallmouth bass and burbot.

Keeping a fish — Wyoming’s special rules. Trout: four a day, of which no more than three may be kokanee, and every kokanee caught from 10 September to 30 November must be released. Lake trout are treated as nongame: there is no limit on lake trout of 28 inches or smaller, and you may keep only one over 28 inches. Burbot must be killed immediately, with no limit. All smallmouth bass must be returned to the water immediately. Gaffs are not allowed, and fish must stay whole until you are off the water; if you fillet them afterwards, each fillet keeps a one-inch square of skin. Fishing from any of the concrete boat ramps on the reservoir is prohibited.

How people fish it. Game and Fish says spring brings rainbows of 14 to 18 inches to the shore, and early-season kokanee are trolled from the surface down to about 30 feet. In winter, burbot fishing through the ice is a popular outing: at night, off rocky points, in 20 to 50 feet of water. Flaming Gorge is on the special winter list, so up to six lines are allowed through the ice.

Licences across the state line. A Wyoming resident licence covers the Wyoming part of the reservoir; with a Utah reciprocal stamp added, it covers the whole lake. A Utah resident licence plus a Wyoming reciprocal stamp ($30) also covers the whole lake. Where both states’ limits are the same, you may take only one day’s limit from the whole reservoir. The rules do not say what a visitor from a third state needs to fish both ends; ask Wyoming Game and Fish or the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources before you cross the line.

Boat ramps and passes. Buckboard Crossing is open all year and has a fish-cleaning station. The reservoir has been low in 2026: the Forest Service’s alert of 30 July 2026 says the level is lower than normal because of drought and higher releases from the dam, affecting ten ramps including Buckboard, Firehole and Anvil Draw, and Anvil Draw has been closed since 7 July. A recreation pass is $5 a day, $15 a week or $35 a year, and is not sold online.

Confirmed with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department and the U.S. Forest Service, 22 November 2026 — Chapter 46 fishing regulations (effective 1 January 2026), the Game and Fish Flaming Gorge fishing page, and the Ashley National Forest’s passes, boat-launch, water-level (alert of 30 July 2026, updates of 18 August and 21 September 2026) and ramp-closure pages.

Nearest towns: Green River, at the top of the reservoir, and Rock Springs, a short drive east on Interstate 80. Buckboard Marina, on the west shore, rents boats and slips; we could not open a website of its own, so we do not link it.

The licence and the limits, plainly

Two different papers: a Yellowstone park permit inside the park, and a Wyoming fishing licence everywhere else on this page.

Wyoming fishing licence

For a visitor. Non-resident 12-month licence $102; five days $56; one day $14; youth $15.

For a Wyoming resident. 12-month licence $27; one day $6; youth $3.

The Conservation Stamp. $21.50. Game and Fish says it is needed with an annual licence but not with a daily licence, and it runs for 12 months from purchase.

Children. Wyoming residents under 14 need no licence. Visiting children under 14 need none either when they fish with an adult who holds a valid Wyoming licence, but their fish count towards that adult’s limit.

How long it lasts, and where to buy. An annual licence runs for 365 days from the day you buy it. Buy online from Game and Fish or from a licence agent anywhere in the state.

Confirmed with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department, 22 November 2026 — its licence fee list and fishing questions page. The fee list carries no effective date, and the stamp rule comes from an older Game and Fish answer page; check the price when you buy.

Boats: the invasive-species decal and inspection

The decal. Boats need a Wyoming aquatic invasive species decal. Revised rules that took effect on 1 November 2026 set it at $20 for a resident motorboat and $50 for a non-resident one, and $5 and $15 for boats without motors. Inflatables of 10 feet or less and paddleboards need no decal but must still be inspected; float tubes and similar water toys are exempt. Decals run for the calendar year.

The inspection. From 1 March to 30 November, a boat brought into Wyoming by road must be inspected before it touches the water, unless it has not been in any suspect or infested water for 30 days, has not passed a check station and has an inspection seal receipt from after it last left the water. From 1 December to the end of February the same applies without the seal rule. A boat that has been in mussel-infested water in the past 30 days must be inspected in every month, and every boat must stop at any check station that is open. In 2026 Wyoming added golden mussels to its list of prohibited species.

Confirmed with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department, 22 November 2026 — its aquatic invasive species and decal pages, the revised Chapter 62 rule (dated 14 July 2026, effective 1 November 2026) and the Commission’s news release of 15 July 2026. The Game and Fish fee list still showed the older decal prices ($10 and $30) when we read it.

Yellowstone National Park permit

Who needs one. Everyone 16 and older fishing in the park. Children 15 and under get a free permit, signed by an adult. The park’s permit is sold on Recreation.gov and at places in and around the park.

And a Wyoming licence? The park’s 2026 regulations mention a state licence only for one stretch of the Yellowstone River in Montana; on Yellowstone Lake you fish on the park permit. We could not read the 2026 permit price on any park page, so check it when you buy.

Confirmed with the National Park Service, 22 November 2026 — Yellowstone’s 2026 fishing regulations and the Superintendent’s Compendium.

Questions

Do I need a Wyoming fishing licence in Yellowstone National Park?

No, you fish on a Yellowstone park permit. Everyone 16 and older needs one; children 15 and under get a free permit signed by an adult. Permits are sold on Recreation.gov and in and around the park. The park’s 2026 regulations mention a state licence only for one stretch of the Yellowstone River in Montana. Checked with the National Park Service on 22 November 2026.

Can I release a lake trout on Yellowstone Lake?

No. Yellowstone’s 2026 regulations say every lake trout caught in Yellowstone Lake must be killed, and it is illegal to release one alive; there is no possession limit on non-native fish. Native fish, including the Yellowstone cutthroat trout, must be released unharmed. Checked with the National Park Service on 22 November 2026.

What does a Wyoming fishing licence cost a visitor?

A non-resident 12-month licence is $102, five days $56 and one day $14; a non-resident youth licence is $15. An annual licence also needs the $21.50 Conservation Stamp; a daily licence does not. Visiting children under 14 need no licence when they fish with a licensed adult, but their fish count towards that adult’s limit. Checked with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department on 22 November 2026.

Which licence do I need for Flaming Gorge?

On the Wyoming end, a Wyoming licence. To fish the whole reservoir, a Wyoming resident adds a Utah reciprocal stamp, and a Utah resident adds a Wyoming reciprocal stamp ($30). Where the two states’ limits are the same, only one day’s limit may be taken from the whole reservoir. The rules do not say what a visitor from a third state needs, so ask Wyoming Game and Fish before you cross the line. Checked on 22 November 2026.

Does my boat need an inspection before I launch in Wyoming?

Usually, yes. From 1 March to 30 November, a boat brought into Wyoming by road must be inspected before it touches the water unless it meets all three exemptions in the rules, and any boat that has been in mussel-infested water in the past 30 days must be inspected in every month. Boats also need a Wyoming invasive-species decal: from 1 November 2026, $20 for a resident motorboat and $50 for a non-resident one. Checked with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department on 22 November 2026.
