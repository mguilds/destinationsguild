Tanzania — The Serengeti, the Mountain and the Spice Islands

Tanzania Travel Guide — Where to Stay, Region by Region

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Tanzania is the country that holds the Serengeti, the Ngorongoro Crater, Kilimanjaro and Zanzibar, and a well-planned fortnight can take in all four. That combination is the reason it is near the top of so many lists, and the reason it rewards planning more than most places do.

This guide sets out the regions and what each is for, names in full the one part of the country official advice says to avoid, and prints the two entry rules that catch visitors out — including a compulsory government insurance policy you must buy even if you already have your own.

Where to stay — the Guild’s Tanzania city guides

Five guides, each one about choosing between districts rather than between hotels, plus a month-by-month page for the timing.

Arusha

The gateway to the northern safari circuit: the town centre for the briefing, the coffee-farm lodges on the Moshi road for the night before.

Moshi

Directly under Kilimanjaro, and where to be the day you come down. The trailhead lodges at Machame and Marangu, too.

Stone Town

Zanzibar’s old merchant city. The real choice here is access and stairs, not price.

Nungwi

The northern tip, where the water stays deep at low tide — which is not true of much of the island. Kendwa next door.

Dar es Salaam

The harbour city and the ferry to Zanzibar, with the specific streets official advice names.

When to go

Four seasons, not two. The migration month by month, the Zanzibar monsoon and the weeks where the price drops for no good reason.

Why go

Tanzania holds the Serengeti, the Ngorongoro Crater, the highest mountain in Africa and the old Swahili trading town of Zanzibar, and it is entirely possible to put all four into a fortnight. Few countries offer that range in one set of borders, and none of them do it with the sheer density of animals the northern plains carry in the dry season.

It is not a cheap country, and anyone telling you otherwise is quoting the hotel and not the park fees. What you are buying is the best big-game viewing on earth, and the honest advice is to go for fewer days and do them properly rather than stretch the budget over a longer, thinner trip.

Getting in

Julius Nyerere International at Dar es Salaam, Kilimanjaro International between Arusha and Moshi for the northern circuit, and Zanzibar’s Abeid Amani Karume airport. Most safari visitors fly into Kilimanjaro.

Money

The Tanzanian shilling. US dollars are widely accepted for park fees, lodges and tours, and notes must be reasonably new to be taken. Cards work in the cities and in the better lodges; carry cash beyond them.

Language

Swahili and English are both official. English is widely spoken in the tourist trade and much less so in the villages. A dozen words of Swahili will be met with real warmth.

Altitude

Arusha sits near 1,400 metres and the Crater rim around 2,300. Nothing dangerous, but the first evening is more tiring than you expect. Kilimanjaro is a different matter entirely.

Health

Malaria is a risk countrywide, and dengue, cholera and mpox are all named in official health advice. Medical facilities are limited outside Dar es Salaam, so insurance covering evacuation matters. Emergency number 112.

Park fees

Set by Tanzania National Parks, with the Ngorongoro Conservation Area charged separately by its own authority. Both are revised, so we print no figure. Ask your operator in writing whether fees are inside the quote.

The regions

The northern circuit — Serengeti, Ngorongoro, Tarangire

This is what most people mean by Tanzania. From Arusha the road runs west past Lake Manyara and Tarangire to Karatu on the Crater rim, and on into the Serengeti. It is the densest concentration of accessible big game anywhere, and in the dry season it is busy with vehicles to match. Karatu is the practical overnight stop for the Ngorongoro gate.

Kilimanjaro and the Pare mountains

East of Arusha the mountain dominates everything. Moshi is the base, the climb takes five to nine days depending on route, and the days matter more than the fitness. The lower slopes are coffee and banana country and worth a day even if you are not climbing.

Zanzibar — Stone Town and the beaches

An hour’s flight or two hours’ ferry from Dar es Salaam, and effectively a different country in feel: Omani doors, Swahili food, a mainly Muslim island with a long trading history that includes a hard one. Two nights in Stone Town and the rest on the coast is the shape that works.

Dar es Salaam and the mainland coast

The commercial capital, the main port of entry and the ferry terminal, with beach hotels strung north of the city. South of it, Mafia Island is the quiet alternative to Zanzibar for diving and whale sharks in season.

The southern circuit — Nyerere and Ruaha

Nyerere National Park, carved out of the old Selous, and Ruaha far inland are enormous, emptier and wilder than the north, with a fraction of the vehicles. They cost more to reach and reward longer stays. This is where a returning visitor goes, and the Guild has no city book here yet because there is no town to write one about.

The lakes and the west

Lake Victoria in the north-west, Lake Tanganyika in the far west with the chimpanzees at Gombe and Mahale, and Kigoma as the regional town. Remote, slow and expensive to reach by air. Note the specific official advice about armed robbery around Kigoma, below.

The parts official advice says to avoid — stated in full

We print this rather than summarise it away. As at 22 November 2026, the UK Foreign Office advises against all but essential travel to any area within 20km of the Tanzanian border with the Cabo Delgado province of Mozambique, in the far south-east, because of attacks by groups linked with Islamic extremism. That is the only area under advice against travel in the country.

Separately, and short of advice against travel, there have been armed robberies including vehicle hijackings in the area bordering Burundi and in the Kigoma region. Official advice there is to drive only in daylight, and notes there are few facilities for visitors.

None of the five places the Guild writes about in Tanzania is in or near either area. Dar es Salaam, Arusha, Moshi, Stone Town and Nungwi are all hundreds of kilometres from the Mozambique border strip and from Kigoma, and we checked each against the list rather than assuming it.

On the parks themselves, official advice is worth repeating: some are extremely remote and emergency access and evacuation can be difficult, camping should be at official sites only, viewing wildlife on foot or at close range carries real risk, and you need the correct permit before entering a national park.

Getting in — the rules as they now stand

Two of these catch people out, and both are recent. Source throughout: UK Foreign Office entry requirements for Tanzania, still current at 22 November 2026, last updated 30 October 2026.

Government travel insurance is compulsory

All visitors to mainland Tanzania and to Zanzibar, residents excepted, must hold inbound travel insurance provided by the Tanzanian government — even if you already hold a personal policy. Buy it online beforehand or at your port of entry. It covers your stay up to 92 days. You buy it for your first port of entry only: Zanzibar first means the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation, the mainland first means the National Insurance Corporation. You may be refused entry without it.

Visa, and the e-visa route

A tourist or business visa is required. Apply for the e-visa online before you travel; it is no longer possible to obtain one from the Tanzanian High Commission in London. A single-entry visa is available on arrival at main ports of entry subject to requirements, and you may be asked for your return or onward ticket. A multiple-entry visa cannot be obtained on arrival.

Passport

Your passport must have an expiry date at least six months after the date you arrive, and at least one blank page. You will be refused entry on a document reported lost or stolen.

Health screening and the surveillance form

On 17 October the Ministry of Health announced heightened screening for travellers from high-risk regions and affected countries at all points of entry. Every traveller must complete a Travellers Health Surveillance Form before departure or on arrival. Mpox screening, by body temperature check, applies at all points of entry. Check the current list of affected countries before you fly.

The honest safety picture

Tanzania is a rewarding country to travel in and a safe one for most visitors most of the time. It would be dishonest to leave out the following, all of it from official advice updated on 30 October 2026.

Crime. There is a risk of armed crime, including armed robbery, throughout the country, and particular care is advised in places frequented by tourists. In Dar es Salaam, visitors and residents have been targeted in the city centre, on the Masaki and Oysterbay peninsula — Coco Beach especially — and along Toure Drive. On Zanzibar, incidents have occurred in Stone Town and at hotels and on popular tourist beaches across the island. Bag snatching by criminals working from cars and motorbikes is a known tactic: walk towards the traffic and as far from the road as you can. Advice added on 30 October 2026 covers using the security measures your accommodation provides against burglary — in practice, use the safe.

Politics, and a change in mood. The general election of 29 October 2025 was followed by protests and a security response that caused deaths and injuries in many areas of mainland Tanzania; previous elections in Zanzibar have also turned violent. Further demonstrations are possible at any time, including on public holidays. Official advice notes an increase in anti-foreigner rhetoric. Stay away from demonstrations and large crowds, and leave immediately if you come across one. The authorities have limited internet access during and after protests before now, so keep your airline, insurer and embassy numbers written down somewhere offline.

Your phone and what you photograph. This is the warning we would most want a reader to take away, because it is easy to break by accident. It is illegal to take or share photographs or video of protests and violence. Tanzanian security services may ask to inspect your phone and other electronic devices for content relating to the events around the elections. The police have stated it is an offence to hold or share images that “cause panic or degrade a person’s dignity”. Keep clear of anything that could be read as political, in public and online.

Terrorism. Attacks cannot be ruled out and could be indiscriminate, including in hotels, restaurants, bars, transport hubs and crowded places. Extremists linked to Al-Shabaab are thought to be active in Tanzania, and attacks by IS-Mozambique are possible near the Mozambique border.

LGBT+ travellers. Same-sex sexual activity is illegal in Tanzania, including Zanzibar, and official advice is plain that same-sex relationships are not tolerated in a conservative society. Showing affection in public — holding hands or kissing in a public place — could lead to arrest or imprisonment. That is something to know before booking rather than after, and the Foreign Office’s general guidance for LGBT+ travellers is worth reading alongside this. Source: UK Foreign Office travel advice for Tanzania, read on 22 November 2026.

What to do

The Crater floor at first light, before the vehicles arrive. The northern Serengeti in August and September if the herds have reached the river. Tarangire for elephants and baobabs, which almost everybody rushes. A night walk in a private concession, which you cannot do inside the national parks. Stone Town’s lanes with no particular destination, and the Forodhani food stalls after dark. A spice farm, done with a good guide rather than a bored one. Chimpanzee tracking at Mahale if you have the days and the budget. And in the Pare and Usambara mountains, the walking that almost no visitor to Tanzania ever does.

Fishing here

Tanzania has serious fishing — Nile perch on Lake Victoria, the extraordinary endemic species of Lake Tanganyika, and billfish off Mafia and Pemba in the deep water of the Zanzibar channel. The Guild has no Tanzania fishing book yet, and we would rather say so than pretend otherwise. What we do have is in the Guild Fishing Library, which covers lakes, rivers and salt water across the shelf as it grows.

Travelling in your later years

Tanzania works well for older travellers, with two caveats worth knowing before you book. Safari days are long hours on corrugated roads, and the vehicle matters more than the lodge — ask for a window seat guaranteed, ask about the suspension, and consider flying between parks rather than driving the whole circuit. In Stone Town, the characterful hotels are old merchant houses with steep, narrow stairs and no lifts; the Shangani seafront has the larger hotels and is the easier choice. Medical facilities are limited outside Dar es Salaam, so insurance that genuinely covers evacuation is not optional here, and it is separate from the compulsory government policy.

Questions readers actually ask

Do I need anything new to enter Tanzania?


Two things catch people out. First, compulsory government-provided inbound travel insurance: every visitor to the mainland and to Zanzibar, residents aside, must hold it even if they already have their own policy, bought online or at the port of entry and valid up to 92 days. You buy it for your first port of entry only, from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation if you land on Zanzibar or the National Insurance Corporation on the mainland. Second, the visa is now an e-visa applied for online; it can no longer be obtained from the Tanzanian High Commission in London, and a visa on arrival is single-entry only. Your passport needs six months' validity beyond arrival and one blank page.

Which parts of Tanzania does official advice say not to visit?


Only one area is under advice against all but essential travel: any area within 20km of the border with the Cabo Delgado province of Mozambique, in the far south-east, because of attacks by groups linked with Islamic extremism. Separately, there have been armed robberies and vehicle hijackings around the Burundi border and in the Kigoma region in the far west, where the advice is to drive in daylight only. None of the places in this guide is in or near either area.

How long do I need for a first trip to Tanzania?


Ten days is the shortest trip we would call worthwhile, and a fortnight is the right answer. A workable fortnight is a night in Arusha, five or six days on the northern circuit taking in Tarangire, the Ngorongoro Crater and the Serengeti, then a flight to Zanzibar for two nights in Stone Town and four or five on the north coast. Adding Kilimanjaro means another week; do not try to compress the climb into a safari itinerary.

Is Tanzania safe for tourists?


For most visitors, most of the time, yes, and millions go without trouble. The honest qualifications: armed crime including armed robbery is a risk countrywide, and official advice names the city centre, the Masaki and Oysterbay peninsula and Toure Drive in Dar es Salaam, and Stone Town and tourist beaches across Zanzibar, as places where visitors have been targeted. Demonstrations have turned deadly since the October 2025 election and there is an increase in anti-foreigner rhetoric. It is also illegal to photograph protests, and the security services may inspect your phone. Travel with that in mind rather than without it.

Can I combine a safari and Zanzibar?


Yes, and most people should. The northern circuit from Arusha and then a short flight to Zanzibar is the standard shape of a Tanzanian holiday, and the weather suits both from June to October and again in January and February. Allow a full day for the move. Because the compulsory government insurance is bought for your first port of entry only, arriving through Kilimanjaro or Dar es Salaam covers you for Zanzibar as well.

What will the park fees add to my trip?


More than most people budget for. National park entry is set by Tanzania National Parks and the Ngorongoro Conservation Area is charged separately by its own authority, with vehicle, concession and camping fees on top in some cases. Both bodies revise their rates, so we deliberately print no figure that would be out of date by the time you read this. Get it in writing from your operator whether park and conservation fees are included in the quoted price, and confirm current rates with the authorities before you commit.
