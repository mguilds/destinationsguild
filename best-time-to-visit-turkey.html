When to Visit Türkiye — Month by Month

Best Time to Visit Türkiye — Month by Month

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Türkiye runs from a humid city on the Bosphorus to high desert valleys to a Mediterranean coast, so “when to go” depends on which of them you are going to. The two windows that work almost everywhere are late April to early June and September to October. Below is the month-by-month in plain terms, then each region on its own, then the holidays that fill the flights and the hotels — including the two big ones whose dates move every year.

The short answer

Late April to early June, and September to October. Those two windows give you warm days without the summer heat, open water on the coast, comfortable walking weather in Istanbul and at the archaeological sites, and Cappadocia at its best for both ballooning and valley walks.

The rest of the year is not wasted, it is simply a different trip. July and August are hot — the coast is lovely in the water and hard work out of it, and Ephesus at two in the afternoon is an endurance test. They are also the full months and the dear ones. November to March is quiet, inexpensive and often rewarding: Istanbul under a grey sky is a serious city to walk about in, Cappadocia under snow is a sight few visitors see, and the coast is mild, green and mostly closed.

Month by month

Türkiye is big enough that a single national month-by-month is a half-truth, so each month below says which part of the country it is describing. Our yardsticks are Istanbul, Cappadocia and the Mediterranean coast.

January and February

Cold in Istanbul, with grey days, rain off the Black Sea and the occasional snowfall that makes the whole city worth photographing. Cappadocia is properly cold and often snow-covered; balloon flights run when the wind allows and are cancelled more than at other times of year. The Mediterranean coast is mild and wet, the sea is too cold for swimming, and a good many seafront hotels close or run quietly. The cheapest months of the year, and the emptiest monuments.

March

The turn. Istanbul begins to thaw and the parks come out; it is still a coat month and still showery. Cappadocia is cold but brightening. On the coast the almond and citrus are out and the days are warm enough to sit outside, though the sea is not ready. Good value, and a reasonable gamble if you are going for cities and ruins rather than beaches.

April

One of the two best months, especially the second half. Istanbul has its tulips and its best light; the Aegean and Mediterranean sites are green, flowering and comfortable to walk; Cappadocia’s valleys are at their prettiest. The sea is still bracing for most people. Expect rain some days everywhere — this is spring, not summer.

May

Arguably the best single month in the country. Warm rather than hot everywhere in this guide, long days, the sea warming to swimmable by the end of it on the south coast, and the archaeological sites still bearable in the afternoon. Busy but not overwhelmed. If you can only travel once and want everything to work, come in May.

June

Early June still belongs to spring; by the end of it the summer has arrived. The coast is excellent — hot days, warm sea, long evenings — and the sites are getting hard in the middle of the day. Istanbul is pleasant and busy. Cappadocia is hot and dry by day and cool at night, which suits the dawn balloons.

July and August

The hot months, the full months and the expensive ones. The Mediterranean is at its best for swimming and at its worst for walking around ruins. Istanbul is humid; the Bosphorus is where the city goes to breathe. Cappadocia is dry and very bright. This is also when Turkish families take their own holidays, so the coast roads, the resorts and domestic flights all fill. If this is when you can travel, plan early mornings, long lunches and water.

September

The other best month. The heat eases, the sea is at its warmest of the whole year after a summer of sun, the crowds thin after the first week or two, and the light is beautiful. Everything in this guide works in September, which is why we would book it first alongside May.

October

Excellent into the second half. Warm days on the coast and swimmable water for much of the month; perfect walking weather in Istanbul and at the ruins; Cappadocia in autumn colour. Rain becomes more likely as the month goes on, and by the end of it the seafront season is winding down.

November and December

Winter arrives. Istanbul is cold, wet and atmospheric and the museums are blissfully quiet; Cappadocia is cold with the first snow; the coast is mild, green and largely out of season. Prices fall sharply. A good time for a city trip, a poor time for a beach one.

By region

Istanbul

Best in April to June and September to October. Summer is humid and crowded; winter is cold and grey but genuinely rewarding and the cheapest time to see the monuments without queueing. There is no bad month for the food.

Cappadocia

April to June and September to October for walking and the best ballooning odds. Summer is hot and very bright; winter is cold and photogenic under snow, with more weather cancellations. Nights are cool at altitude in every season — bring a layer even in July.

The Mediterranean and Lycia

Late May to early July, and September to mid-October: hot days with a swimmable sea. Midsummer is excellent beside the water and punishing on the Lycian Way. Winter is mild, wet and quiet, with many hotels shut.

The Aegean and Ephesus

April to early June and September to October. The great ruins here are unshaded, so a July afternoon at Ephesus or Pamukkale is an ordeal rather than a visit. Go at opening time in any season.

The Black Sea coast

Green because it rains, and it rains in every season. High summer is the usual time to go and even then expect showers; the mountain roads and yaylas are at their best from June to September.

Eastern Anatolia

A short season: roughly June to September, because the winters are severe and long. Several governments publish specific advice about provinces near the Syrian and Iraqi borders — read your own government’s current advice before planning anything there.

Holidays and dates that fill the country

Two of Türkiye’s biggest holidays, Ramazan Bayramı (Eid al-Fitr, at the end of Ramadan) and Kurban Bayramı (Eid al-Adha), follow the lunar calendar and so fall about eleven days earlier each year. We deliberately do not print dates for them here, because any date we printed would be wrong within a year — look them up for your own travel year. What matters practically is that both are multi-day national holidays: domestic flights, coaches and coastal hotels fill, city traffic changes completely, and some small businesses close for several days. They are a wonderful time to be in the country if you have booked ahead, and a difficult one if you have not.

The fixed national days are easier to plan around: 1 January, 23 April (National Sovereignty and Children’s Day), 1 May (Labour and Solidarity Day), 19 May (Commemoration of Atatürk, Youth and Sports Day), 15 July (Democracy and National Unity Day), 30 August (Victory Day) and 29 October (Republic Day, the national day, marked with ceremonies and often crowds in the cities). Turkish school holidays run through high summer, which is part of why July and August fill the coast.

Questions

What is the single best month to visit Türkiye?

May, with September a very close second. Both give you warm rather than hot days in every region in this guide, a sea that is comfortable to swim in on the south coast, archaeological sites you can walk around in the afternoon without suffering, and Cappadocia at its best for both walking and ballooning. September has the warmer sea; May has the greener landscape.

Is it worth visiting Türkiye in winter?

For cities and landscape, yes. Istanbul in winter is cold and grey and the great monuments are nearly empty, which is an experience the summer simply cannot offer, and prices are at their lowest. Cappadocia under snow is remarkable. For a beach holiday, no — the Mediterranean is too cold to swim and a good proportion of seafront hotels close or run at half pace.

When is the sea warm enough to swim on the south coast?

Roughly late May to late October, with the water at its warmest in August and September after a whole summer of sun. September is the month that gets the balance best: the air has cooled a little, the sea has not, and the crowds have begun to thin. In April and early May the sea is swimmable for the hardy and bracing for everyone else.

How do the Bayram holidays affect travel?

Ramazan Bayramı and Kurban Bayramı are multi-day national holidays that move about eleven days earlier each year, so check the dates for your own travel year rather than relying on any guide. During both, domestic flights, intercity coaches and coastal hotels fill with Turkish families, city traffic changes and some small businesses close for several days. Book well ahead and the holidays are a fine time to be there; arrive without a booking and they are hard work.

When is Cappadocia best for hot-air balloons?

Spring and autumn give the best combination of settled air and comfortable temperatures, with April to June and September to October the usual advice. Flights are weather-dependent in every season and are cancelled for wind more often than visitors expect, so whenever you come, book your flight for the first morning of your stay rather than the last — that way a cancellation leaves you another chance.
