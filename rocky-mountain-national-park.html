Rocky Mountain National Park

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

A Visitor's Guide · Northern Colorado · Trail Ridge Road tops out at 12,183 feet

Rocky is a summer park that asks you to plan ahead: a timed-entry reservation from late May to mid-October, a high road that is open only about five months a year, and no lodging inside the park. Here is how it all works.

Before you go

Two things decide a good Rocky Mountain visit: when you enter the gate, and how well you handle the altitude.

Rocky Mountain National Park sits on the Continental Divide in northern Colorado, a drive of an hour and a half to two hours from Denver. Its east side opens from the town of Estes Park, its west side from the lakeside village of Grand Lake, and Trail Ridge Road climbs between them over the top of the Rockies, with eleven miles above 11,500 feet.

From late May to mid-October, Rocky runs a timed-entry reservation system. You can still get in without one if you enter before 9 a.m. or after 2 p.m., and that early start is the best advice we can give anyone, reservation or not: the light is better, the wildlife is out, and the parking lots have room.

Take the altitude seriously. Bear Lake sits at 9,475 feet and the high point of Trail Ridge Road at 12,183 feet. Visitors coming up from the desert or from sea level should drink plenty of water, go easy the first day, and talk to their doctor first about heart or lung conditions. Weather on the tundra can turn from sunshine to sleet in an hour, even in July, so bring a warm layer.

There is no lodging inside the park. You will sleep in Estes Park or Grand Lake, or in one of the park's campgrounds.

Timed entry (2026, now over for the season): standard Timed Entry was required to enter from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. daily, May 22 through October 12, 2026, for every area except the Bear Lake Road Corridor. Timed Entry+ Bear Lake Road was required to enter that corridor from 5 a.m. to 6 p.m., May 22 through October 18, and also covered the rest of the park. Reservations are booked only on recreation.gov, with a non-refundable $2 processing fee, on top of your entrance pass. They give you a two-hour window to enter, with no set time to leave. In 2026 they were released at 8 a.m. Mountain time on the first of each month for the month ahead, with more released at 7 p.m. the night before each date. They are not sold at the gates. The park had not announced its 2027 rules when we checked; expect a similar system and watch the park's timed-entry page in early spring.

No reservation? Enter before 9 a.m. or after 2 p.m. and you do not need one for most of the park. The Bear Lake Road Corridor is stricter: during the season a Timed Entry+ reservation is needed there from 5 a.m. to 6 p.m., and drivers who miss their entry window are turned around. Campers with a park campground reservation and backpackers with a wilderness permit should read the park's timed-entry questions and answers, which explain when those reservations count as your entry.

Right now (late November 2026): Trail Ridge Road is closed to through travel between Many Parks Curve on the east side and the Colorado River Trailhead on the west, and to drive between Estes Park and Grand Lake you must go around the park outside it. Bear Lake Road stays open all winter. Old Fall River Road is closed to vehicles until about early July. The free park shuttles ended October 18 and return in 2027. Colorado's traction law can take effect on park roads at any time in winter.

Visitors from outside the U.S.: since January 1, 2026, Rocky Mountain is one of 11 national parks that charge non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older an extra $100 per person on top of the entrance fee. The America the Beautiful pass costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for nonresidents, and a pass covers that extra fee.

Best things to do in Rocky Mountain National Park

Six experiences to build your visit around, from the roadside to an easy lakeshore stroll.

Scenic drive

Trail Ridge Road

Forty-eight miles from Estes Park to Grand Lake over the top of the Rockies, reaching 12,183 feet, the highest continuous paved road in the country. Stop at the overlooks and at the Alpine Visitor Center at 11,796 feet.

Easy walk

Bear Lake

At the end of Bear Lake Road, a level trail circles a small lake under Hallett Peak. It is the start of the walks to Nymph, Dream and Emerald lakes, and the busiest spot in the park, so come early or ride the summer shuttle.

Accessible

Sprague Lake

A flat loop of packed gravel and boardwalk around a lake with mountain reflections, about as easy as a walk in the high country gets.

Wildlife

Moraine Park & the elk

A wide meadow along the Big Thompson River where elk gather. In September and early October the bulls bugle at dawn and dusk; stay in your car or well back.

Summer only

Old Fall River Road

The park's original road to the top: 9.4 miles of one-way, uphill gravel with tight switchbacks, open to vehicles from about early July to early October. No vehicles over 25 feet and no trailers.

West side

Kawuneeche Valley

The quieter side of the park, where the Colorado River begins as a meadow stream near Grand Lake. Look for moose in the willows early and late in the day.

From the Guild

If the timed-entry reservations are gone for your date, do not give up. Be at the gate before 9 a.m. (before 5 a.m. for Bear Lake Road) and you are in, or arrive after 2 p.m. for a late afternoon and sunset on Trail Ridge Road. Book a 7-day pass rather than a 1-day pass if you will be in the area more than one day; it costs only $5 more.

When to visit Rocky Mountain

Summer (late May to early September) is when everything is open: Trail Ridge Road usually opens to through travel in the last week of May, Old Fall River Road in early July, and the free shuttles run from late May to mid-October. It is also when timed entry is in effect and crowds peak. Afternoon thunderstorms are routine in July and August, so plan high-country stops for the morning and get off exposed ridges by early afternoon.

September and early October are, to our mind, the finest weeks of the year: golden aspens, crisp mornings, and the elk rut in Moraine Park and Horseshoe Park. Weekends are busy, and Trail Ridge Road can close for a day at a time after an early snow.

Winter (mid-October to late May) is quiet. Trail Ridge Road closes to through travel, so the two sides of the park are reached separately from Estes Park and Grand Lake. Bear Lake Road stays open, there is snowshoeing and cross-country skiing, and Moraine Park Campground stays open for winter camping. Expect snow, ice and strong winds, and carry chains or good snow tires.

How to get to Rocky Mountain National Park

By car

From Denver, the usual route to the east side is US 36 through Boulder and Lyons to Estes Park, about 1.5 to 2 hours. The Beaver Meadows and Fall River entrances are both just west of town. To reach Grand Lake and the west side, take I-70 west, US 40 over Berthoud Pass to Granby, and US 34 north, also about 1.5 to 2 hours from the interstate.

Between mid-October and late May, Trail Ridge Road is closed, and there is no way across the park by car. Driving from one side to the other then means a long loop outside the park.

By air & shuttle

Denver International Airport (DEN) is the closest major airport. There is no public transit from the airport to the park, but commercial shuttles run from DEN to Estes Park.

Inside the park in summer, free shuttles run along the Bear Lake Road Corridor from the Park & Ride on Bear Lake Road. The Hiker Shuttle from the Estes Park Visitor Center to the Park & Ride needs a $2 ticket booked on recreation.gov and a park pass. In 2026 the shuttles ran May 22 to October 18; the 2027 schedule will be posted in spring.

Where to stay near Rocky Mountain National Park

Estes Park is the obvious base: two entrances at its edge, the most rooms, and the Bear Lake shuttle a short drive away. Grand Lake is smaller and quieter, and the right choice for the west side and the Kawuneeche Valley.

Lodges inside the park

Rocky Mountain National Park has no lodges, hotels or cabins inside the park; the park's own guidance is to stay in Estes Park or Grand Lake. In summer the Trail Ridge Store at the Alpine Visitor Center has a cafe and coffee bar, open from about late May to early October, but there are no overnight rooms anywhere in the park.

RV travel & camping at Rocky Mountain

Five campgrounds you can drive to, four of them by reservation, and room for rigs up to 40 feet in one of them.

In summer, Moraine Park, Glacier Basin and Aspenglen on the east side and Timber Creek on the west side all require reservations on recreation.gov. Longs Peak campground is first come, first served, for tents only, and usually open from early July to early September. The park had not yet posted its summer 2027 campground dates when we checked.

The park lists maximum RV lengths of 40 feet at Moraine Park, 35 feet at Glacier Basin, and 30 feet at Aspenglen and Timber Creek. Many individual sites are shorter than the campground maximum (some Glacier Basin sites take only 20 feet), so check the length listed for the exact site you book.

Hookups are scarce. Moraine Park has electric sites in Loops B and C, at a higher nightly rate; there are no water or sewer hookups anywhere in the park. Access and Senior Pass holders get half off standard campsite fees. The park takes cards only, no cash.

Winter camping: Moraine Park is the park's winter campground, first come, first served, from October 19, 2026 to mid-May 2027, and its dump station is closed in winter. Remember that RVs and trailers are not allowed on Old Fall River Road, and that Trail Ridge Road, though fine for RVs in summer, is a long, steep climb with few guardrails.

For full hookups and showers, Estes Park and the Grand Lake area have private RV parks and campgrounds outside the park. They fill in summer and during the elk season, so reserve ahead.

Fishing in Rocky Mountain

Rocky's lakes and streams hold brown, brook and rainbow trout and native cutthroat trout. Anglers 16 and older need a valid Colorado fishing license, which is not sold in the park, so buy one in town or online before you go. One rod or line per person, and Colorado's second-rod stamp is not honored here.

Only artificial flies and lures are allowed, except that children 12 and under may use worms or preserved fish eggs in waters that are not catch-and-release. Many lakes and streams that hold native cutthroat trout are catch-and-release only, with barbless hooks; some waters, including Bear Lake and its inlet and outlet, are closed to fishing. Possession limits vary by species and size, so read the park's fishing page for the list of open, closed and catch-and-release waters before you cast.

Accessible travel at Rocky Mountain

What is available for visitors with limited mobility, and the passes that help.

The park's free summer shuttles are all wheelchair accessible, with kneeling buses and ramps, and each bus holds up to two wheelchairs. The shuttles usually do not stop at the accessible parts of Sprague Lake, so tell the driver where you want to go and when you need to be picked up.

Sprague Lake has the park's best-documented accessible trail, with packed gravel, a wood bridge and a minimum width of 40 inches; watch for small rises onto the bridges and for snow into mid-June. Electric mobility devices may be used on the Lily Lake, Sprague Lake and Coyote Valley trails, and on the west side Coyote Valley is an easy stroll along the young Colorado River.

All the visitor centers have accessible parking: Beaver Meadows (with a lift to the lower level), Fall River, Kawuneeche, the Moraine Park Discovery Center (with an elevator) and the Alpine Visitor Center at 11,796 feet, which closes in winter. All-terrain wheelchairs can be borrowed free by reservation through the Estes Park Mountain Shop, and in summer a mobility scooter is available at Hidden Valley.

Accessible campsites are set aside at Aspenglen, Glacier Basin, Moraine Park, Timber Creek and Longs Peak, and there is an accessible wilderness campsite near Sprague Lake, open May through October by reservation. Remember the altitude: at 9,000 to 12,000 feet, everything takes more breath than at home.

Park passes that save you money. U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability can get the free lifetime Access Pass. Visitors 62 and over can buy the Senior Pass ($80 for life, or $20 a year). Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass. Each covers entrance fees at every national park.

Traveling on dialysis? The nearest dialysis clinics are in Loveland, about 30 miles east of Estes Park down the Big Thompson Canyon. Book your treatment at a clinic there several weeks before you go; the large dialysis companies run travel desks that will arrange it with your home unit.

Rocky Mountain National Park FAQ

Do I need a reservation for Rocky Mountain National Park?

From late May to mid-October, yes, if you enter between 9 a.m. and 2 p.m. (5 a.m. to 6 p.m. for the Bear Lake Road Corridor). Reservations are booked on recreation.gov for a $2 fee. Outside those hours, and the rest of the year, no reservation is needed.

How much is the entrance fee at Rocky Mountain National Park?

$30 per vehicle for one day or $35 for seven days. Motorcycles are $25 or $30, visitors on foot or bicycle $15 or $20, and the park's annual pass is $70. The park takes cards only, not cash.

When is Trail Ridge Road open?

Usually from the last week of May to mid-October, weather permitting. In 2026 it opened May 29 and closed to through travel for the winter on November 13.

Is there lodging inside Rocky Mountain National Park?

No. There are no hotels or lodges in the park. Visitors stay in Estes Park or Grand Lake, or in the park's campgrounds.

Can I take an RV into Rocky Mountain National Park?

Yes. Moraine Park takes RVs up to 40 feet, Glacier Basin 35 feet, and Aspenglen and Timber Creek 30 feet. RVs, trailers and vehicles over 25 feet are not allowed on Old Fall River Road.

Is there a shuttle in Rocky Mountain National Park?

In summer, free shuttles run along Bear Lake Road from the Park and Ride, and a Hiker Shuttle runs from the Estes Park Visitor Center with a $2 ticket from recreation.gov. In 2026 they ran May 22 to October 18.

Can I drive from Estes Park to Grand Lake in winter?

Not through the park. Trail Ridge Road closes to through travel from mid-October to late May, so you must drive around the park.

When is the elk rut in Rocky Mountain National Park?

September and early October, when bull elk bugle at dawn and dusk in meadows like Moraine Park and Horseshoe Park. Watch from a distance or from your car.
