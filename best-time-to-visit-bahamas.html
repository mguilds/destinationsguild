Best Time to Visit the Bahamas

Best Time to Visit the Bahamas — Month by Month

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

The Bahamas has no bad weather month in the way that northern countries do — the coldest afternoon of the year is still around 25°C and the sea never drops below swimmable. What changes across the calendar is humidity, rain, wind, price and risk, and those five things vary enormously between February and September. Here is the whole year honestly: what the air and the sea actually do, when the trade winds will cancel your boat trip, which weeks double the price of a room on Paradise Island, and a plain account of the hurricane season rather than either scaremongering or silence.

The short answer

Go in late April, May or early June. That window gives you dry-season weather with the winter winds gone, a sea that has warmed into the eighties Fahrenheit, the clearest water of the year over the Exuma banks, negligible hurricane risk — and rates that have dropped away from the March peak. November is the second answer and the underrated one: warm, dry, quiet and inexpensive once the season closes on the thirtieth, with only American Thanksgiving week to avoid.

If your dates are fixed by school holidays, February and March are the most reliable weather of the year and the most expensive, with American spring break landing on Nassau through March. July and August are hot, humid, cheap and family-friendly if you plan around the heat. September is the cheapest month in the calendar and the riskiest — the peak of hurricane season — and is a fine choice for a flexible, well-insured traveller and a poor one for anybody else.

One thing that is not seasonal and catches people out: on any day with four or five cruise ships alongside, downtown Nassau is shoulder to shoulder between ten and four, in January as much as in July. Our Nassau guide explains how to work around it.

Season by season

Two real seasons rather than four, and the shoulder weeks between them are where the value is.

Winter — mid-December to April — high season, and it earns it

This is what people are paying for: afternoons of 25 to 27°C, humidity low enough that walking around Nassau is a pleasure, steady trade winds, and rainfall of only 40 to 60 millimetres a month. There is no hurricane risk at all. The trade-offs are price and company — rates are at their annual peak, Christmas and New Year are extraordinary, and March brings American spring break to New Providence. Evenings in January and February are cooler than most visitors expect, so pack one warm layer.

Spring — late April to early June — the value window

Our pick, and not by a small margin. The weather is still essentially dry-season, the sea has warmed from the low seventies Fahrenheit to the eighties, the winds have dropped so small boats run and the water over the Exuma banks is at its clearest, and rates fall away as soon as the spring-break crowd goes home. Hurricane risk in this window is negligible. The only real caution is that late May onwards brings afternoon showers — short, heavy and rarely a problem.

Summer — June to August — hot, cheap and increasingly humid

The sea reaches 29°C and stays there, the light is enormous, and rates are far below winter. The costs are humidity that makes the middle of the day hard work, a heavy shower most afternoons, and a hurricane season that is officially open from 1 June and genuinely active from about mid-August. It is a good family window if you plan around the heat and insure properly, and a poor one if you dislike being damp.

Autumn — September to November — the gamble, then the reward

September and the first half of October are the statistical peak of the hurricane season and the cheapest weeks of the year; a good number of Out Island hotels and restaurants simply close. From the third week of October the odds shift quickly, and November is one of the best-kept secrets in the Caribbean — warm, dry, quiet and inexpensive, with the exception of American Thanksgiving week.

Month by month

Typical daytime highs and sea temperatures for Nassau. The southern islands run a degree or two warmer, Grand Bahama a degree cooler in winter.

January — 25°C / 77°F · sea 24°C / 75°F

Peak dry season and, after the New Year rush clears, one of the pleasant surprises of the year. Bright, breezy and comfortable rather than hot, with very little rain. Two caveats: the trade winds are at their strongest, which is glorious on a beach and occasionally cancels a small-boat excursion, and a northern front can push evenings down into the high teens Celsius. Bring one long-sleeved layer and do not assume a January sea will feel warm on the first step in.

February — 25°C / 77°F · sea 24°C / 75°F

Much like January and arguably the most reliable month of the year: dry, sunny, low humidity and no hurricane risk whatsoever. Rates are firmly in high season and Presidents’ Day weekend brings a short American spike. This is the month for walking Nassau’s old town without wilting, and the best of the year for whale and dolphin watching off the deeper drop-offs.

March — 26°C / 79°F · sea 25°C / 77°F

Excellent weather and the busiest, priciest month on New Providence, because American spring break runs through it. Nassau, Cable Beach and Paradise Island fill with a young crowd and rates hold at their annual peak. If that is not your holiday, the Out Islands feel entirely different in March — the Exumas and Eleuthera are busy but civilised — or move your dates three weeks later and pay noticeably less.

April — 27°C / 81°F · sea 26°C / 79°F

The best month of the year, and we will defend that. The spring-break crowd goes home, the weather is still firmly dry-season, the sea has warmed to a genuinely inviting temperature and rates start easing from the second half of the month. The Family Island regattas begin, with the traditional wooden sloops racing at George Town in the Exumas — worth planning a trip around. Easter, wherever it falls, is a short local peak.

May — 29°C / 84°F · sea 27°C / 81°F

The value sweet spot. Officially the wet season has begun, but May rain is typically a short afternoon shower rather than a washout, the humidity has not yet become oppressive, hurricane risk is negligible this early, and rates have come down meaningfully from March. Water clarity in the Exumas is at its best with the lighter winds. If someone asks us for one month, this is usually it.

June — 31°C / 88°F · sea 28°C / 83°F

Hot, increasingly humid, and the official start of hurricane season on the first — though June storms affecting the Bahamas are rare and the practical risk this early is low. Prices are good, the sea is warm enough to stay in for hours, and the winds are light, which makes it a strong month for snorkelling and for boat days out to the cays. Expect a heavy shower most afternoons, usually gone within the hour.

July — 32°C / 89°F · sea 29°C / 84°F

Properly hot and properly humid, with the sea at bath temperature. American and Canadian school holidays lift family demand and rates rise a little from June without approaching the winter peak. Independence Day on the 10th and Emancipation Day at the start of August bring local celebrations and summer Junkanoo “rushes”. Plan the day around the heat: water in the morning, shade in the afternoon.

August — 32°C / 89°F · sea 29°C / 84°F

The hottest, stickiest month, and the point at which hurricane season becomes a genuine planning consideration rather than a footnote — the second half of August starts the real peak. Rates are low and the sea is spectacular. If you go, buy insurance that covers hurricane disruption when you book and pick a hotel with a written rebooking policy. Sargassum can affect Atlantic-facing beaches at this time of year.

September — 31°C / 88°F · sea 29°C / 84°F

The cheapest month and the riskiest. This is the statistical peak of the Atlantic hurricane season, and it is the month in which Dorian struck in 2019. Most September trips are completely unaffected and the value is remarkable — the same room can be a third less than in March. Some Out Island hotels and restaurants close for the month entirely, so confirm your specific property is trading. Keep the plan flexible and watch the forecast.

October — 29°C / 85°F · sea 28°C / 83°F

The first half still carries real hurricane risk; from about the third week the odds improve quickly, the humidity starts to break and the country begins to feel like itself again. Rates stay low until November. A good gamble month for travellers who are flexible, well insured and happy to book late — and a poor one for a trip that has to happen on fixed dates.

November — 27°C / 81°F · sea 27°C / 80°F

Season ends on the thirtieth and in practice the weather has usually settled well before then. Warm, drying out, quiet and still inexpensive for most of the month — one of the best-value windows in the calendar. American Thanksgiving week is the exception and books up early. Anything closed for September and October is reopening, so check dates if you are heading to a small island.

December — 26°C / 78°F · sea 25°C / 77°F

Two different months in one. The first two weeks are lovely and still reasonably priced — dry season properly underway, no hurricane risk, comfortable heat. From about the 18th the country goes to its annual peak: Christmas and New Year rates are the highest of the year, Harbour Island and the best Exuma hotels have been booked for a year, and minimum-stay rules appear everywhere. In return you get Junkanoo on Boxing Day and New Year’s morning, which is worth seeing once in a lifetime.

Rain, wind and hurricanes — plainly

Three things decide what a Bahamian week feels like, and only one of them is temperature.

The rain is short and heavy, not grey and long

The dry season, December to April, sees roughly 40 to 60 millimetres a month. The wet season, May to October, sees 150 to 230. That sounds decisive and mostly is not, because the rain arrives as a heavy convective downpour in the afternoon that lasts twenty minutes to an hour and then clears to bright sun. Northern fronts in winter can bring a genuinely grey day or two, which is rarer but flatter. Plan water activities for the morning between June and October and you will hardly notice the difference.

The wind decides whether the boat goes out

This is the seasonal factor visitors never think about. The northeast trades blow hardest from December to March, which is delightful on a hot beach and is also the reason a small-boat excursion in the Exumas gets cancelled at eight in the morning. If your trip is built around a sandbar-and-pigs day, or around snorkelling, either build in a spare day or come between April and June when the winds ease and visibility over the banks is at its best.

Hurricane season, without the drama

The Atlantic season runs 1 June to 30 November. The practical risk is not spread evenly across it: June and July are low, the real peak is mid-August to mid-October, and the second half of November is usually settled. The overwhelming majority of trips in the peak are unaffected. But 2019’s Dorian sat over Abaco and Grand Bahama as a category five, and the recovery is still visible, so treat the risk as real and manageable rather than remote. Insure when you book, not later; choose a hotel with a written rebooking policy; and confirm your property is open, because some Out Island hotels close for September altogether.

Sargassum and the sea itself

Sea temperature runs from about 24°C (75°F) in February to 29°C (84°F) in August, so every month of the year is swimmable. Two smaller things worth knowing: sargassum seaweed can wash up on Atlantic-facing shores in spring and summer, affecting east-facing beaches far more than the sheltered western ones, and water clarity on the banks is remarkable year-round — it is the surface chop, not the water, that varies.

The dates that move prices

A small country with a lot of demand: a handful of dates can double a room rate or empty an island of beds.

Christmas, New Year and Junkanoo

18 December to 2 January. The single biggest price event of the Bahamian year. Rates on New Providence and Paradise Island reach their annual maximum, minimum-night stays appear everywhere, and the good small hotels on Harbour Island and in the Exumas are commonly booked a year in advance. What you get for it is real: Junkanoo parades down Bay Street in the small hours of Boxing Day and New Year’s Day, with costumed groups of hundreds, cowbells and brass, judged and fiercely competitive. Book the room first and the flight second.

American spring break — March

roughly the first three weeks. Not a single date but a rolling month of US university and school holidays, and it lands squarely on Nassau, Cable Beach and Paradise Island. Rates hold at their peak, the beach bars are young and loud, and availability tightens. The Out Islands are barely touched by it. Moving to mid-April keeps the same weather at a meaningfully lower price.

The Family Island regattas — April onwards

and the Exuma regatta in particular. The traditional wooden Bahamian sloops race island by island through the spring and summer, and the National Family Island Regatta at George Town in the Exumas in April is the great one. It fills every room on Great Exuma and turns a very quiet town into a very busy one for a week. Worth planning a trip around, or worth avoiding — but not worth stumbling into unbooked.

American long weekends and Thanksgiving

and why they matter here. The Bahamas runs on the American holiday calendar as much as its own. Presidents’ Day in February, Memorial Day at the end of May, Independence Day on 4 July and Labor Day in early September each bring a short, sharp bump on New Providence, and Thanksgiving week in November is the one genuine exception to that month’s otherwise excellent value. Check the US calendar before fixing dates.

Bahamas timing FAQ

What is the best month to visit the Bahamas?

Late April to early June, if you can choose freely. The weather is still dry-season, the sea has warmed to the low eighties Fahrenheit, the winds have dropped so boat trips run reliably and the water in the Exumas is at its clearest, hurricane risk is negligible, and rates fall as soon as the American spring-break crowd goes home. February and March give marginally more reliable weather at the highest prices of the year, and November is the underrated alternative — warm, dry, quiet and cheap, except during American Thanksgiving week.

What is the rainy season in the Bahamas, and does it ruin a trip?

The wet season runs roughly May to November, and it very rarely ruins anything. Rain in the Bahamas usually arrives as a heavy afternoon downpour that lasts twenty minutes to an hour and then clears to sunshine, rather than as days of grey drizzle. The dry season, December to April, sees only about 40 to 60 millimetres a month; the wettest months, from May to October, can see 150 to 230. Plan water activities for the morning in summer and you will barely notice it.

When is hurricane season, and should I avoid it?

1 June to 30 November, with the genuine peak from mid-August to mid-October. You do not need to avoid the whole six months — June and July carry low practical risk, and the second half of November is usually settled. What matters is how you book in the peak weeks: buy travel insurance covering hurricane disruption at the moment you book rather than later, choose a hotel with a written rebooking policy, confirm your property is actually open because some Out Island hotels close in September, and keep the plan flexible.

How warm is the sea in the Bahamas in winter?

About 24°C (75°F) in January and February, rising to 26°C (79°F) by April. That is comfortably swimmable — it is roughly the temperature of an unheated outdoor pool in a warm climate — but it is not the bath-warm water of August, and the first few seconds will feel brisk on a windy morning. Sea temperature peaks at about 29°C (84°F) from July to September.

When are the Bahamas cheapest?

September and early October, by a wide margin — the same room can cost a third less than in March. The reason is the hurricane peak, so the saving comes with real conditions attached: insure at the time of booking and confirm the hotel is trading. The best value without that gamble is early November or late April into May, when the weather is reliable and the winter rates have not yet returned. Grand Bahama is the least expensive island in any month.

Is it worth timing a trip around Junkanoo?

If you have any interest in seeing it, yes — it is unlike anything else in the region. The two major parades run down Bay Street in Nassau in the small hours of Boxing Day and New Year's Day, from about two in the morning until well after sunrise, with costumed groups of hundreds competing for a judged prize that takes a year of building to contest. The cost is that you are travelling in the single most expensive week of the Bahamian year. Smaller summer Junkanoo rushes around Independence Day in July offer a cheaper taste.
