When to Go to Japan

The Best Time to Visit Japan

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: spring — late March into April for the cherry blossom — and autumn — October and November for the foliage — are the two sweet spots. Both bring mild, mostly dry days that are ideal for Tokyo, Kyoto and the mountains. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the crowds, and the best timing for Tokyo, Kyoto, Hokkaido and Okinawa.

Japan stretches some 3,000 kilometres from subtropical Okinawa to the snows of Hokkaido, so it has a genuine four-season climate and timing shapes the whole trip. The country you meet under a canopy of cherry blossom in early April — petals on the breeze, everyone outdoors — is a world away from the humid rains of late June or the powder-deep ski slopes of February. Choose your dates well and everything is easier. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$$$ PeakCherry blossomMild & brightBook early

The headline season. From late March, the cherry blossom sweeps north over a few weeks, reaching Tokyo and Kyoto in early April with warm days around 15–20°C and cool evenings — picture-perfect for gardens, temples and picnics under the trees. The trade-offs are the country's biggest crowds and highest prices, sharpest during the blossom peak and the Golden Week holiday at the very end of April, so plan and book well ahead.

Summer — June to August

$$ MixedHot & humidRainy then festivalsHead north

A season of two moods. June to mid-July brings the tsuyu rainy season — humid, showery and quieter, with softer prices. Then July and August turn hot and sticky, with city highs around 31–35°C and lively festivals and fireworks after dark. Cool, dry Hokkaido is the summer escape and largely skips the rains. Watch the calendar for the Obon travel peak in mid-August.

Autumn — September to November

$$$ ShoulderFoliageCrisp & clearGreat value

Many travelers' favourite. September can still be warm and carries the tail of the typhoon season, but October and November settle into crisp, clear days around 14–20°C, with the autumn foliage turning the maples of Kyoto and the mountains fiery red and gold. The weather rivals spring, the crowds are a touch thinner and the light is superb — one of the best-value windows of the year.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold & clearBest for snowCheapest

The quiet, good-value season, New Year fortnight aside. The Pacific cities like Tokyo and Kyoto get crisp, sunny days around 8–12°C and cold nights, perfect for sightseeing without the crowds. Meanwhile the Japan Sea side and Hokkaido pile up world-class powder for skiing, and the hot springs are at their most inviting. Pack warm layers, especially for the north.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ Low (post-NY)Cold & clearSki season

After the New Year peak passes, January is cold, dry and quiet in the cities — excellent for crowd-free temples and museums under bright skies. Up north it is deep ski country. Prices are among the year's lowest. Pack proper winter layers, especially for evenings and the Japan Sea coast.

February

$ Low seasonColdestPowder snow

The coldest month and prime time for Hokkaido powder and the Sapporo Snow Festival. The big cities stay crisp and quiet, with the first plum blossoms appearing late in the month. Great value and thin crowds make it a smart choice for skiers and unhurried city sightseeing alike.

March

$$ Value → risingWarming upEarly blossom

Winter loosens its grip and the first cherry blossoms open in the warm south and, late in the month, in Tokyo. Early March is still good value and pleasantly quiet; prices and crowds climb sharply as the blossom approaches. A lovely, softly-warming month to travel before the spring rush.

April

$$$$ PeakCherry blossomGolden Week starts

The blossom peak in Tokyo and Kyoto, and the busiest, priciest sightseeing of the year — magical, but book far ahead. Warm, bright days are ideal for gardens and parks. The Golden Week holiday begins at the very end of the month, so aim for early-to-mid April to pair the blossom with slightly calmer travel.

May

$$$ ShoulderFresh & greenGolden Week spike

Once Golden Week (roughly the 29th of April to the 5th of May) is over, May is one of the finest months: warm, dry, fresh green everywhere and comfortable for walking. Northern blossoms linger in Hokkaido. Skip the holiday week itself, when domestic travel and prices surge, and May rewards you handsomely.

June

$$ ValueRainy seasonLush & quiet

The tsuyu rainy season settles over most of the country, bringing humid, showery spells — but also hydrangeas, misty temple gardens and noticeably lower prices and crowds. Rain rarely lasts all day. Hokkaido largely escapes the wet and is a fine choice. A quietly rewarding month for flexible travelers.

July

$$ MixedHot & humidFestival season

The rains ease around mid-July and summer turns hot and sticky, with highs in the low-to-mid 30s°C and lively matsuri festivals, including Kyoto's famous Gion Matsuri. Cool Hokkaido and the highlands are the comfortable escapes, and the beaches of Okinawa are at their best. Plan indoor breaks in the midday heat.

August

$$$ Obon peakPeak heatFireworks

Hot, humid and festive, with spectacular fireworks and summer festivals nationwide. The Obon holiday around mid-August is a major domestic travel peak — trains and flights fill and prices jump — so plan around it. Head to the mountains, Hokkaido or the sea to beat the heat, and watch for the first typhoons.

September

$$ ValueEasing heatTyphoon watch

The heat begins to fade, especially late in the month, and prices soften after the summer holidays. The main caveat is typhoon season, which peaks now and can briefly disrupt travel, so keep the itinerary flexible. Between storms, early autumn brings pleasant, clearing weather and good value.

October

$$$ Prime valueCrisp & clearSweet spot

Arguably the best all-round month: comfortable, dry days around 18–22°C, stable weather as typhoons wind down, and the first autumn colours arriving in the north and the mountains. Everything is open and pleasant, crowds are moderate and prices reasonable. A superb time to combine cities, countryside and hot springs.

November

$$$ RisingPeak foliageBeautiful

The classic foliage month, when the maples of Kyoto, Nikko and the mountains blaze red and gold, usually peaking mid-to-late month. Cool, clear and comfortable for walking, it draws leaf-viewing crowds to the famous gardens, so book ahead. One of the loveliest and most photogenic times to travel.

December

$$ → $$$$ SplitCold & clearNew Year peak

A month of two halves. Early-to-mid December is cold, clear and quiet, with illuminations lighting the cities and great value before the holidays. The New Year period from around the 28th onward is a sharp domestic peak, with many sights closing briefly and prices high — so aim for the calmer first three weeks.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Winter — January and February, plus the June rains

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSkip New Year

City hotels and long-haul airfares are at their lowest in January and February, once the New Year holiday is behind you, when cold, clear weather makes for crowd-free sightseeing. Early-to-mid June, during the rainy season, is the other bargain window — humid and showery, but quiet and green. The one period to avoid for value is the New Year fortnight, when domestic demand spikes.

Want fine weather and a better deal? Target the shoulder edges: early December before the holidays, late May after Golden Week, and late September as summer fades. You get comfortable conditions at well below blossom-and-foliage peak prices. As a rule, midweek flights beat weekends, and booking rail passes and popular hotels a month or two out lands the best availability.

Crowds & the Holidays to Plan Around

Japan's busiest travel weeks are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks to sidestep

Cherry-blossom peakGolden WeekObonNew Year

Three domestic holidays drive the sharpest crowds and prices: Golden Week (roughly the 29th of April to the 5th of May), Obon around mid-August, and the New Year period from late December to the 3rd of January. Add the cherry-blossom peak in early April, when the popular cities are at their busiest. During these windows, bullet trains and flights fill, and top hotels sell out months ahead.

To have the great sights closer to yourself, travel in the quieter shoulders — late May and June, early December, or January and February — and start early at famous spots like Kyoto's Fushimi Inari and Arashiyama, which are calmest at opening. Best Time by Region

Japan is long and varied — the ideal month depends on where you're headed.

Tokyo, Kyoto & the Honshu Heartland

Best: Mar–May, Oct–NovPeak: blossom & foliageAvoid: Jun rains, Aug heat

The classic sightseeing circuit shines in spring and autumn, when mild, dry weather suits temples, gardens and long city walks. Cherry blossom peaks in early April; the maples turn in mid-to-late November. Summer is hot and humid and June is wet, while winter is cold but crisp and wonderfully quiet. Hokkaido & the Northern Highlands

Best: Jul–Aug, Dec–FebCool summersWorld-class powder

The far north runs on a different clock. Summer is its finest hour — cool, dry and green, with lavender fields around Furano and no rainy season to speak of — a genuine escape from the southern heat. Winter brings legendary powder snow to Niseko and the Sapporo Snow Festival in February. Spring blossom arrives late, in early May.

Okinawa & the Southern Islands

Best: Apr–Jun, Oct–NovSubtropical beachesAvoid: peak typhoons

Subtropical Okinawa is a beach-and-diving destination with warm seas from late spring through autumn. April to June and October to November are the sweet spots — hot, mostly sunny and outside the worst of the summer typhoon peak (August to September). Winters are mild and pleasant for sightseeing, though the sea is cooler and breezier.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to Japan.

What is the best time to visit Japan?

Spring, roughly late March to April for the cherry blossom, and autumn, October to November for the foliage, are the two finest windows: mild, mostly dry, and beautiful. Spring is the busier and pricier of the two, so many travelers prefer autumn, which offers similar comfortable weather with slightly thinner crowds and glorious red-and-gold landscapes in Kyoto and the mountains.

When is cherry blossom season in Japan?

The blossom typically opens in late March and reaches full bloom in early April in Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, with the front moving north over several weeks. It comes earlier in warm Kyushu in the south and does not reach Hokkaido until early May. Full bloom lasts only about a week and the dates shift a week or two each year with the weather, so build in some flexibility.

What is the cheapest and least crowded time to visit Japan?

Winter, especially January and February outside the New Year holiday, is the quietest and best value, with crisp, clear days in the cities and superb snow up north. Early-to-mid June, during the rainy season, is another low-price window if you don't mind showers. Avoid the New Year period itself, which is a sharp domestic peak.

When should I avoid Japan because of crowds?

Golden Week in late April to early May, the Obon holiday around mid-August, and the New Year period from late December to January 3 bring huge domestic travel, packed bullet trains and peak prices. Cherry-blossom peak in early April is also very busy in the popular cities, so book accommodation and rail seats well ahead for any of these.

What is the weather like during Japan's rainy and typhoon seasons?

The tsuyu rainy season runs from about June to mid-July across most of the country, bringing humid, showery spells; Hokkaido in the far north largely escapes it. Typhoon season peaks from August to October and can occasionally disrupt flights and trains, though storms usually pass within a day or two. Neither need ruin a trip, but they reward a flexible itinerary.
