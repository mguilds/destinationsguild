Where to Stay in Rhodes

Where to Stay in Rhodes — Old Town, Beaches & Lindos

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Rhodes gives you a complete medieval city and a proper beach holiday on the same island, and the only real decision is which one you want to wake up in. The Old Town is a walled, car-free UNESCO site of cobbled lanes and knights’ houses — wonderful to stay inside, and hard going underfoot. The New Town is flat, modern and ten minutes’ walk from the walls, with lifts, taxi ranks and the town beach. The west-coast strip at Ixia and Ialysos holds the big international resorts and catches the summer breeze; the sheltered east coast at Kallithea and Faliraki has calmer water. And Lindos, an hour south, is the prettiest village on the island and entirely stepped. Below are our picks by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Old Town cobbles and no cars

The medieval city is closed to vehicles on almost every lane, and the surfaces are worn cobble and pebble mosaic, often sloping and slippery when wet. Taxis can only drop you at a gate, and hotels move luggage the rest of the way by hand cart. Wear closed shoes with grip. If walking is difficult, stay in the New Town instead — it is flat and lift-served, with the walls ten minutes away.

Lindos is beautiful, and it is all steps

Lindos village is entirely pedestrian: you are dropped above it and walk down and back up stepped, whitewashed alleys, and the acropolis is reached by a steep stepped path that is punishing in midday heat. Donkeys carry visitors part of the way but not to the summit, and many travellers now decline them on welfare grounds. If steps are a problem, admire the acropolis from the beach or from St Paul’s Bay, base yourself at Vlicha Bay just north, and visit the village early in the morning.

Which coast, and why it matters

Ixia and Ialysos face west and catch the summer meltemi, so the sea there is often lively and the beaches are pebbly — splendid for a breeze, less so for swimming. Kallithea, Faliraki and Lindos on the east coast are sheltered and noticeably calmer, which matters if you actually want to get in the water. Both sides are twenty to thirty minutes from the Old Town by taxi or bus.

Fly rather than sail, and go in the shoulder season

Athens to Rhodes takes about an hour with many daily departures; the ferry from Piraeus takes eleven to fifteen hours, usually overnight, which is a long haul with luggage. May, early June, September and the first half of October give warm sea, temperatures in the seventies and eighties, thinner crowds and materially lower rates. Many resorts close for the winter and run roughly April or May to October.

Rhodes Hotels — common questions

Should I stay in Rhodes Old Town or the New Town?

The Old Town is a walled medieval city and staying inside it is a real pleasure — but it is closed to cars, the lanes are worn cobble and pebble mosaic, often sloping and slippery when wet, and hotels move luggage by hand cart from the nearest gate. The New Town is flat, modern and lift-served, with taxi ranks, the town beach and a ten-minute level walk to the walls. If walking is at all difficult, choose the New Town and visit the Old Town on foot during the day.

How hard is Lindos to get around?

Harder than the photographs suggest. The village is entirely pedestrian: you are dropped above it and walk down and back up stepped, whitewashed alleys, and the acropolis is reached by a steep stepped path that is punishing in the midday heat. Donkeys carry visitors part of the way but not to the summit, and many travellers now decline them on welfare grounds. If steps are a problem, view the acropolis from the beach or from St Paul’s Bay, stay at Vlicha Bay just north of the village, and go early in the morning before the heat and the coaches.

Which side of Rhodes has the better beaches?

It depends what you want. The west coast at Ixia and Ialysos faces the summer meltemi wind, so it is breezy and the sea is often lively, with pebbly beaches — splendid if you want a cooling wind, less so if you want to swim. The east coast at Kallithea, Faliraki and Lindos is sheltered and noticeably calmer, and the sand is better. Both coasts are twenty to thirty minutes from the Old Town by taxi or bus, so you are not cut off either way.

Should I fly or take the ferry from Athens to Rhodes?

Fly. Athens to Rhodes takes about an hour and there are many daily departures. The ferry from Piraeus runs most days but takes eleven to fifteen hours, usually as an overnight crossing, which is a long haul with luggage unless you book a cabin. Rhodes airport is on the west coast, twenty to thirty minutes from Rhodes Town and Ixia, and about an hour from Lindos.

Were the Rhodes wildfires a problem, and is the island recovered?

The 2023 wildfires affected parts of the southern and eastern interior around Kiotari, Gennadi, Lardos and Laerma. Lindos was evacuated as a precaution but was not burned. The main resort areas, including Lindos, Kiotari and Lardos, have been operating normally since, and none of the hotels on this page sits in the affected area. As always, check current conditions before you travel in high summer.

When is the best time to visit Rhodes?

May, early June, September and the first half of October give warm sea, temperatures in the seventies and eighties Fahrenheit, thinner crowds and materially lower room rates. August brings heat above ninety, peak prices and the busiest beaches, and it is also the height of the meltemi wind, which makes the west coast breezy and choppy while the east coast stays calmer. Note that many resorts close for the winter and run roughly from April or May to October.
