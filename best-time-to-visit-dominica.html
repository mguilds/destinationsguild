When to visit Dominica

Best Time to Visit Dominica

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Dominica has two seasons rather than four, and on an island this mountainous they feel further apart than the calendar suggests. The dry season, about January to May, is the settled and expensive half: sunshine, a steady trade wind, firm footing on the trails. The green season, about June to November, is hotter, wetter, far cheaper and extraordinarily green, and it carries the Atlantic hurricane season. This page goes through all twelve months, sets out the hurricane record without softening it, gives the festival dates that have actually been published — and is straightforward about the ones nobody has published yet.

The short answer

February to April is the most reliable, and March is our pick. The dry season runs from about January to May: steadier sunshine, lower humidity, a dependable trade wind, the best walking and diving conditions, and the highest room rates. June to November is the green season — hotter, wetter, far cheaper, with the waterfalls at their best — and it carries the Atlantic hurricane season, 1 June to 30 November. If value matters more than certainty, go in the first half of December or in late April into May, the two edges of the dry season, when the weather is still good and a room costs noticeably less than in February. Avoid September unless you have a particular reason and good insurance: it is the statistical peak of the hurricane season and the month Maria struck in 2017.

Dominica month by month

A note on what is not in these cards, because you deserve to know. There is not a single temperature or rainfall figure below, and that is deliberate rather than lazy. We could not find a published monthly climate table for Dominica from the island’s own meteorological service or any other source we were able to read, and we are not willing to borrow a neighbouring island’s numbers from fifty miles away and present them as Dominica’s — nobody would notice, and it would still be wrong. So these cards say what each month is actually like to travel in. If a Guild desk ever gets a sourced climate table for the island, there is a slot here waiting for it.

January

The heart of the dry season and one of the best months to be here. Steadier sunshine, lower humidity and a reliable trade wind, with the hills still green from the autumn rains rather than burnt off. Rooms are at or near their highest. Carnival season opens this month: the island’s tourism authority publishes the Mas Domnik 2027 season as running from 9 January to 10 February.

February

Peak dry season and peak price. The most settled weather of the year and the busiest the island gets, which on Dominica still means uncrowded by Caribbean standards. Carnival builds through the month towards its climax — see the events section, because the parade dates themselves are not published where we could read them.

March

Our pick of the twelve if you want certainty. Dry, breezy, still green, and the carnival crowds have gone home. Walking and diving conditions are at their most dependable and the long mountain days are more comfortable than they will be again until December.

April

The last of the reliable dry weather, and the first of the better value. Prices begin to come off the winter peak in the second half of the month while the weather is still largely settled. A very good compromise fortnight.

May

The shoulder, and the start of the green season. Showers return, the island gets greener and wetter, and rates soften noticeably. If you do not mind an afternoon downpour and you want the place close to yourself, this is a shrewd month.

June

Hotter, wetter and quieter, and the Atlantic hurricane season opens on the first of the month. Rain here is usually a heavy shower that clears rather than a grey day, but the humidity is real and the mountain trails get slippery. Prices are low.

July

Full green season: hot, humid and showery, with the lowest rates of the year alongside August and September. The waterfalls and rivers are at their most impressive, which is a genuine argument for coming now if you can take the heat.

August

The wettest, stickiest stretch and statistically the start of the busier half of the hurricane season. Dive Fest falls at the end of the month — the tourism authority published the 2026 edition as 29 to 30 August. Cheap, lush and humid.

September

The month to think hardest about. This is the statistical peak of the Atlantic hurricane season, and it is the month Hurricane Maria struck the island in 2017. Many smaller places take their annual break. If you travel now, take insurance that covers weather disruption and watch the forecast in the week before you fly.

October

The turn. Rain eases through the month and the island’s biggest event falls at the end of it: the World Creole Music Festival, which the tourism authority published for 2026 as 23 to 25 October at Windsor Park, in its twenty-sixth year. No 2027 date had been published anywhere we could read when this page was written, so check before you book flights around it.

November

A good month and an under-rated one. The rains are tailing off, the hurricane season closes on the thirtieth, the island is at its greenest and rates have not yet climbed to Christmas levels. Independence Day falls on 3 November, the anniversary of independence in 1978, and the Creole season around it is the most purely Dominican time to be here.

December

The first half of the month is the best value week-for-week in the whole year: dry-season weather arriving, green hills, and rates still below the Christmas peak. From about the third week, prices jump to their highest of the year and stay there into the new year.

The dry season, the green season, and hurricanes

Dominica is a mountainous island and that shapes its weather more than its latitude does. The interior rises to Morne Diablotins at 1,448 metres and catches a great deal more rain than the coasts; UNESCO notes that the World Heritage park inland holds at least five forest types, including rare elfin or cloud forest at the highest elevations, which is a polite way of saying the mountain tops are frequently in cloud. The practical consequence for a visitor is that a bright morning in Roseau and a wet afternoon on a mountain trail are the same day, in every season.

The dry season, roughly January to May, is the settled half: more sun, less humidity, a steady trade wind and the firmest footing on the trails. The green season, roughly June to November, brings heavier and more frequent rain — typically a hard shower that passes rather than a day of drizzle — along with higher humidity, lower prices and the island at its most spectacularly green.

On hurricanes, the Guild does not soften it. The Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, and Dominica is not outside the belt. Hurricane Maria made a direct Category 5 landfall on the island on 18 September 2017 with maximum sustained winds of 165 miles an hour — the first Category 5 hurricane on record to strike the country — and 65 people died there. Early estimates suggested ninety per cent of the buildings on the island had been damaged or destroyed. Published damage figures differ depending on which source you read: one gives EC$3.69 billion, about US$1.37 billion, and another about US$930 million, though both describe the loss as around 226 per cent of the country’s annual economic output, which is the figure that tells you the most. UNESCO’s own files record that major hurricanes in 1979 and 1980 badly damaged the island’s forests too. Dominica has rebuilt, and travelling here in the green season is an entirely reasonable thing to do — but do it with insurance that covers weather disruption, and watch the forecast in the week before you fly.

Festivals — what is published, and what nobody has published yet

This section is shorter than we would like, and the reason is worth stating: the island’s official festivals website was carrying a 2022 copyright notice and still serving the 2023 line-up on its music-festival page when we checked. We have taken dates only where a page actually printed them.

Mas Domnik — the carnival

Season published, parade dates not. The tourism authority publishes Dominica Carnival 2027 as running 9 January to 10 February and describes it as “the Real Mas”, with bouyon, calypso and costume. It does not publish the Carnival Monday and Tuesday street-parade dates, the opening gala or the Calypso Monarch final anywhere we could read, and the one news report said to carry an official 2027 calendar would not load for us. If carnival is the reason you are coming, write to the Dominica Festivals Committee for the parade dates before you book flights — do not take them from an events aggregator.

World Creole Music Festival

2026 published, 2027 not. The tourism authority gave the 2026 festival as 23 to 25 October at Windsor Park, returning for its twenty-sixth year. That edition has now passed. No 2027 date was published on any page we could read. One caution for anyone planning ahead on a rule of thumb: the festival is often described as beginning on the last Friday in October, but the published 2026 dates did not follow that rule, so the rule is not safe to plan by.

Independence and the Creole season

Dominica became independent on 3 November 1978, and Independence Day is 3 November. The weeks around it are the island’s own cultural season rather than a visitor event, and for many people they are the most rewarding time to be here. We could not find published 2027 dates for Creole Day or the Reunion events on any page we could read, so we have given none.

Dive Fest

The tourism authority published the 2026 edition as 29 to 30 August and calls it Dominica’s longest-running celebration of the deep. No 2027 dates were published when we checked.

Events with no published date at all

Said plainly, because a guide that invents a date is worse than a guide that admits a gap: we found no published dates for the Nature Island Literary Festival, Domfesta, the Giraudel Flower Show, hiking events on the Waitukubuli National Trail, or Emancipation Day. They may well be happening. Nobody we could read has announced when.

How to use this section

Book the weather first and the festival second. If a specific event is the whole purpose of the trip, confirm its dates in writing with the organiser before you buy a flight, and build in a day either side. That advice applies more than usual on an island whose official festival calendar is out of date.

What a room costs, by season

Dominica does not have the big resort inventory that drives the sharp seasonal swings on Barbados or Saint Lucia, so the ladder is gentler here than it is elsewhere in the eastern Caribbean — but it is real, and it runs roughly like this.

Highest — late December to February

From about the third week of December through to the end of February. Christmas and New Year are the single dearest nights of the year, and February is the busiest settled-weather month. Book months ahead, particularly for the small places, where there may be only a handful of rooms in total.

High but easing — March and April

Still dry-season rates, but the pressure comes off after carnival and again after Easter. March is the sweet spot for weather against price.

Best value with good weather — late April to May, and early December

The two edges of the dry season. Largely settled weather, visibly lower rates, and far fewer people. If we had to pick one fortnight on value alone it would be the first half of December.

Lowest — June to early November

Green season. The cheapest rooms of the year, the greenest island and the fullest waterfalls, against heat, humidity, heavier rain and the hurricane season. September is the cheapest and the riskiest; some small properties close.

What moves the price here

Not the beach. On Dominica the premium sits on seclusion and on the small high-end properties — a villa with a plunge pool or a cottage up a mountainside costs what it costs in any season. A town hotel in Roseau swings more with the calendar than those do.

A cost nobody budgets for

The departure tax: 86 East Caribbean dollars, or 33 US dollars, per person. The UK Foreign Office says it is usually included in the price of an air ticket but that you also pay it leaving by ferry, which catches island-hoppers. The US State Department notes children under twelve are exempt. Check your ticket, not the airport desk.

Travelling in your later years

If you are choosing a month in your seventies or eighties, choose the dry season and do not agonise over the rest. February to April gives you the firmest footing on paths, the lowest humidity and the least chance of a day lost to weather, and on an island this steep those three things matter more than they would in Barbados. March is the best single month: dry, breezy and quiet. The green season is not unsafe, but heat and humidity are tiring, wet volcanic rock is genuinely slippery, and a heavy shower turns a short walk into a careful one. The other reason to favour the dry months is practical: September is the peak of the hurricane season, and the Foreign Office notes that while the government hospital in Roseau can cope with many types of treatment, serious cases may require emergency evacuation — and evacuation is exactly what a storm week makes difficult. Travel in the settled half of the year, take insurance that covers both weather disruption and medical evacuation, and ask any small property directly how many steps stand between the car and the bed.

Questions about when to visit Dominica

What is the best month to visit Dominica?

March, if you want one answer. The dry season runs from about January to May and March sits in the middle of it with the carnival crowds gone: settled sunshine, lower humidity, a steady trade wind and the firmest footing on the mountain trails, which matters on an island where the best days out are walks. February is marginally more reliable and noticeably dearer. If value counts more than certainty, take the first half of December or late April into May - the two edges of the dry season, when the weather is still good and rooms cost less.

When is the rainy season in Dominica?

Roughly June to November, and the island calls it the green season rather than the rainy one, which is fair. The rain usually comes as a heavy shower that passes rather than a grey day, and it leaves the island spectacularly green with the waterfalls and rivers at their fullest. The costs are heat, humidity, slippery trails and the hurricane season. One thing to understand about Dominica specifically: it is mountainous, the interior catches far more rain than the coast, and the high ground is often in cloud in any season - a sunny morning in Roseau and a wet afternoon on a summit are the same day.

Is Dominica in the hurricane belt?

Yes, and we are not going to dress it up. The Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, and Dominica has been hit hard. Hurricane Maria made a direct Category 5 landfall on 18 September 2017 with maximum sustained winds of 165 miles an hour - the first Category 5 on record to strike the country - and 65 people died. Early estimates suggested ninety per cent of buildings were damaged or destroyed, and the loss was put at around 226 per cent of the country's annual economic output. UNESCO's files also record major hurricane damage to the island's forests in 1979 and 1980. The island has rebuilt. Travel in the green season if you like, but take insurance that covers weather disruption and watch the forecast the week before you fly.

What is the cheapest time to go to Dominica?

June to early November, with September the cheapest of all - and September is also the statistical peak of the hurricane season and the month Maria struck, so the saving comes with a real risk attached. The better-value answer for most people is the first half of December or late April into May: largely settled dry-season weather, visibly lower rates and far fewer visitors. From about the third week of December to the end of February is the dearest stretch, with Christmas and New Year the single most expensive nights of the year.

Are there temperature figures on this page?

No, and that is on purpose. We could not find a published monthly climate table for Dominica from the island's own meteorological service or from any other source we were able to read. We were not prepared to take a neighbouring island's figures from fifty miles away and print them as Dominica's - nobody would have spotted it and it would still have been wrong. So the month cards describe what each month is like to travel in instead. If a Guild desk ever sources a real climate table for the island, we will add it and say where it came from.

When is carnival in Dominica, and when is the music festival?

Carnival is Mas Domnik, and the tourism authority publishes the 2027 season as 9 January to 10 February - but it does not publish the Carnival Monday and Tuesday parade dates, which are what you would actually book around, and the one news report said to carry the official 2027 calendar would not load for us. Write to the Dominica Festivals Committee for those dates rather than trusting an events aggregator. The World Creole Music Festival was published for 2026 as 23 to 25 October at Windsor Park, in its twenty-sixth year; that edition has passed and no 2027 date had been published anywhere we could read. Do not plan on the common rule of thumb that it starts on the last Friday in October - the 2026 dates did not follow it.

Is November a good month?

Better than most people assume, and it is our quiet recommendation. The rains are tailing off, the hurricane season closes on the thirtieth, the island is at its greenest after six months of rain, and rates have not yet climbed to the Christmas peak. Independence Day falls on 3 November - Dominica became independent on that date in 1978 - and the Creole season around it is the most genuinely Dominican time of year to be on the island, less a visitor event than a national one you are welcome to watch.

Does the weather affect whale watching or diving?

Conditions are generally calmer and visibility better in the dry season, which is the practical reason to prefer January to May for anything on the water. On the whales themselves, we will give you only what we could source: a group of sperm whales lives in the waters off Dominica year-round, which is unusual and is the island's real distinction for anyone interested in large marine mammals. We could not read a published whale-watching season or any operator rules from an official page, so we are not printing months, sighting rates or swim-with rules. Ask the operator directly, and ask what happens if a trip is cancelled for weather.
