The Gold Coast’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay on the Gold Coast — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

The Gold Coast is not one town but a thirty-mile run of beaches, and which one you pick changes the holiday completely. Surfers Paradise is the bright, busy middle: towers on the sand, the Cavill Avenue mall, and everything open late. Broadbeach, one tram stop south, is the grown-up version — the same beach, better restaurants, fewer crowds — and it is where we send most first-time visitors. Main Beach and the Spit, to the north, are quieter still, with the marina, Sea World and a long patrolled beach. Burleigh Heads further south is the one the locals keep, with a headland walk and a proper village. Coolangatta sits at the far southern end beside the airport. The G:link tram runs the length of the central strip, which is why staying near a stop is worth more than staying near anything else. Luxury

The Gold Coast has spent the last few years finally building hotels to match its real estate. These are the two best, and they sit a mile apart on the same beach.

The Langham, Gold Coast and Jewel Residences

$$$$ LuxurySurfers Paradise38 Old Burleigh RoadAbsolute beachfront

The best hotel on the coast, in the middle tower of the Jewel development with the sand directly in front of it — one of very few genuinely absolute-beachfront hotels in Queensland. Rooms are large, quiet and finished to a standard the Gold Coast did not previously have, there are several pools on a terrace above the beach, and the spa and the afternoon tea are both worth the money. It is a short walk north to Surfers Paradise and south to Broadbeach, with a tram stop a few minutes inland.

JW Marriott Gold Coast Resort & Spa

$$$$ LuxurySurfers Paradise158 Ferny AvenueSaltwater lagoon with reef fish

A long-established resort on Ferny Avenue with the Gold Coast’s most unusual swimming: a saltwater lagoon and pool system stocked with reef fish, alongside freshwater pools and a good spa. Rooms are generous and most look either over the ocean or back across the Nerang River. The beach is a three-minute walk through the park, Cavill Avenue is five minutes south, and the tram stop is at the corner. Families and couples both do well here, which is rarer than it sounds.

Boutique

The coast does not really do small hotels, but it does one design hotel properly.

QT Gold Coast

$$$ UpscaleSurfers Paradise7 Staghorn AvenuePlayful design, big pool

The most characterful hotel in Surfers Paradise, a cheerfully over-designed sixties-surf-motel fantasy on Staghorn Avenue with a large pool deck, a good Japanese restaurant and a bar that the locals actually use. The beach is two blocks east, Cavill Avenue a few minutes south, and the tram runs along the Gold Coast Highway behind it.

Budget & Value

There are thousands of apartment towers here, and the trick is picking one close to both the sand and a tram stop rather than the cheapest rate in the listings.

Mantra on View Surfers Paradise

$$ Mid-rangeSurfers Paradise22 View AvenuePool, two blocks from the sand

Trip.com files this one as Mantra on View Surfers Paradise. A tall, plain, well-run tower on View Avenue, two short blocks from the beach and a minute from the Cavill Avenue tram stop, with a pool and spa on a podium deck and a choice of hotel rooms or one- and two-bedroom apartments with kitchens. Nothing here is fashionable and the rates reflect it; what you get is a central address, a lift and a place to cook. The best plain money in Surfers Paradise.

Family-friendly

The Gold Coast is Australia’s family holiday, and the hotel decision is almost entirely about pool, kitchen and how far the children must walk to the sand.

Sheraton Grand Mirage Resort, Gold Coast

$$$$ LuxuryMain Beach71 Seaworld DriveLagoon pools, direct beach access

The quietest of the big resorts, on the Spit at Main Beach with its own lagoon pools, tropical gardens and a path straight through to a long patrolled beach. Sea World is next door and the Marina Mirage shops and restaurants are across the road, which together solve several days with children without needing a car. It is a few minutes’ drive north of Surfers Paradise rather than in it, which is the point of staying here.

Adults-only

Several Gold Coast towers are adults-only by building rules rather than by hotel policy, and we are not going to name one as adults-only on a policy we have not read. If you want a quiet, grown-up few days here, the honest advice is geographic rather than contractual: stay at Broadbeach, Main Beach or Burleigh Heads rather than in Surfers Paradise, ask for a high floor away from the pool deck, and the schoolies and stag parties will never trouble you.

Spa & pool

The best swimming here is the sea, and it is patrolled: Surfers Paradise, Broadbeach, Main Beach, Burleigh Heads and Coolangatta are all flagged beaches in season, and you should swim between the flags. For hotel water, the saltwater reef lagoon at the JW Marriott is the most interesting pool on the coast, the Sheraton Grand Mirage’s lagoon pools are the best for small children, and the Langham’s beachfront terrace is the best place to do nothing. Banks, cards and ATMs add their own margins. The coast is effectively cashless and tap-to-pay works on the tram, the buses and the beachfront cafes alike. Hotel rates quoted on Trip.com already include Australia’s GST, and tipping is not expected. Queensland does not use daylight saving, so from October to April the Gold Coast runs an hour behind Sydney even though the New South Wales border is twenty minutes away — which catches out a great many people at Coolangatta airport.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask us most when choosing where to stay in Gold Coast.

Which Gold Coast suburb should I stay in?

Broadbeach for most visitors: the same beach as Surfers Paradise, better restaurants, a quieter night and a tram stop. Surfers Paradise if you want the bright lights and everything open late. Main Beach and the Spit for quiet, the marina and Sea World. Burleigh Heads for the headland walk and the village the locals kept. Coolangatta if you are flying in and out and want to be near the airport.

How much do Gold Coast hotels cost?

Expect roughly 130 to 220 Australian dollars a night for clean apartment-tower rooms, about 220 to 400 for the better resorts, and from around 500 upward for beachfront luxury. No Australian destination swings as hard by date: Christmas and January, Easter and the Queensland school holidays can double the rate, and the Gold Coast 600 and the marathon weekends fill the central strip. February and the cooler weeks of May and August are the bargains.

Do I need a car on the Gold Coast?

Not if you stay near the tram. The G:link light rail runs from Broadbeach through Surfers Paradise and Main Beach up to Helensvale, where it meets the Brisbane train, and it covers most of what visitors want. You need a car for the theme parks further inland, for Burleigh and Currumbin, and for the hinterland at Springbrook and Lamington. Hire it for the days you need it rather than the whole week.

How far are the theme parks from the beach hotels?

Close, but not walkable. Sea World is on the Spit at Main Beach, next door to the Sheraton Grand Mirage. Warner Bros. Movie World, Wet’n’Wild and Dreamworld are inland along the Pacific Motorway, roughly twenty to thirty minutes’ drive from Surfers Paradise, and reachable by bus or shuttle. Buy multi-park passes before you travel, and check each park’s opening days, which vary by season.

Which Gold Coast hotels take dogs?

We are not naming one. Most of the towers here are managed apartment buildings where the body corporate sets the pet rule, not the hotel, so even a hotel’s own answer can be overridden. Shortlist from the list on this page and telephone the building’s reception before you pay.

Is the Gold Coast suitable for older travellers?

Very, if you choose the suburb rather than the brochure. Broadbeach, Main Beach and Burleigh Heads are flat, quiet, well-served by the tram and full of good restaurants, and the beachfront paths are level and shaded in parts. Surfers Paradise is louder and busier at night, and November brings the school leavers’ celebrations, which are worth avoiding. Ask for a high floor and a room away from the pool deck.
