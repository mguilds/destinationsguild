Lake Fishing in Michigan — Six Fine Fishing Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Lake St. Clair, Saginaw Bay, Houghton Lake, Higgins Lake, Burt Lake and Torch Lake: what swims in each, how people fish them in summer and through the ice, the 2026 rules, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Michigan Department of Natural Resources, 6 November 2026

Michigan touches four of the five Great Lakes and holds thousands of inland lakes besides, so “lake fishing” here means two different things. There are the big, open waters joined to the Great Lakes — Lake St. Clair on the edge of Detroit and Saginaw Bay off Lake Huron — where walleye and perch may be kept all year round. And there are the inland lakes of the northern Lower Peninsula — Houghton, Higgins, Burt and Torch — cottage-country lakes with their own seasons, where a family can rent a cabin with a rowboat tied to the dock.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers, ice anglers and anyone who wants a lake, a cabin and perhaps a perch fry for supper. Michigan’s rules differ between the Great Lakes waters and the inland lakes, and some lakes carry their own exceptions, so we have quoted the Michigan DNR wherever a number is involved.

Lake St. Clair — the big water next to Detroit

A broad, shallow lake between the St. Clair and Detroit rivers, shared with Ontario, with St. Clair Shores, Harrison Township and New Baltimore on the Michigan shore.

Lake St. Clair, from St. Clair Shores and New Baltimore

What swims there. Smallmouth bass and muskellunge are what draw anglers from far away; walleye, northern pike and yellow perch are what most people take home. In winter the DNR lists the lake for yellow perch, bluegill and crappie through the ice.

How people fish it. From boats — drifting and casting for bass on the flats, trolling for muskie and walleye — and from piers and the shore at the Metropark. In a cold winter the bays freeze for ice fishing. It is a big, shallow lake, and wind kicks up a steep chop quickly: watch the forecast in a small boat.

Keeping a fish. Walleye: six a day, at least 15 inches, all year. Bass: five a day, at least 14 inches, from the third Saturday in June to 31 December. Muskellunge: one per angler per licence year, at least 42 inches, from the first Saturday in June to 31 December, and it must be registered within 24 hours. Northern pike: five a day, each at least 24 inches. Perch and panfish: 25 a day of each group.

Confirmed with the Michigan DNR, 6 November 2026 — walleye regulations page: “St. Clair River and Lake St. Clair — Six fish per day, Open all year, 15″ minimum size limit, Three rods per angler”; bag limits “are updated each year on May 1”. 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations, General Fishing Regulations: bass “3rd. Sat. in June – Dec. 31: L. St. Clair, St. Clair R., Detroit R.”, 14″, 5; muskellunge 42″, “Only 1 per angler per license year (Mandatory Registration)”, “1st Sat. in June – Dec. 31: L. St. Clair”; northern pike “Waters where up to 5 Northern Pike 24″ or greater allowed in the daily possession limit: Lake St. Clair, St. Clair R., and Detroit R.”

The Canadian side. The eastern part of the lake is Ontario, which sets its own limits and sells its own licence. A Michigan licence covers Michigan’s waters only.

Boat launches. Lake St. Clair Metropark at Harrison Township has a boat launch area and an accessible fishing pier; there are DNR and municipal launches along the shore as well.

Nearest towns: St. Clair Shores on the south-west shore and New Baltimore on Anchor Bay to the north. Detroit is close by to the south-west.

Saginaw Bay — walleye country on Lake Huron

The great bay in the “thumb” of Michigan’s mitten, with Bay City at its foot.

Saginaw Bay, from Bay City, Au Gres and East Tawas

What swims there. Walleye above all — the DNR says walleye is now the most harvested fish in Michigan’s Great Lakes waters, followed by yellow perch, a change it ties to Lake Huron’s shifting food web.

How people fish it. Trolling and jigging for walleye from boats, perch fishing over the shallow inner bay, and, in winter, ice fishing from the bay’s shores when conditions allow. Open-bay ice can shift and crack with wind and current; go with someone who knows that day’s ice.

Keeping a walleye. Eight a day, each at least 13 inches, open all year — more generous than the statewide five at 15 inches. Northern pike: two a day, at least 24 inches, all year on these Great Lakes waters. Yellow perch: 25.

Confirmed with the Michigan DNR, 6 November 2026 — 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations, Note 3: “L. Huron waters of MH-4 including Saginaw Bay and lower Saginaw R. … The daily possession limit for Walleye is 8 with a 13″ minimum size limit and open all year. Any changes will be announced online at Michigan.gov/Fishing and in a prerecorded message at 888-367-7060.” News release, 4 June 2026: “Walleye is now the most harvested species in the Michigan waters of the Great Lakes, followed by yellow perch.”

Boat launches and the shore. Bay City State Park, on the bay’s south-east shore, has a fishing pier and the Saginaw Bay Visitor Center. Farther north, Tawas Point State Park sits at the end of the sand spit that forms Tawas Bay; the DNR says the bay is popular for open-water and ice fishing, with several boating access sites nearby.

Nearest towns: Bay City at the south end, Au Gres on the west shore, and East Tawas on Tawas Bay to the north.

Houghton Lake — Michigan’s largest inland lake

A wide, shallow lake in Roscommon County, in the middle of the northern Lower Peninsula, ringed with cottages and small resorts.

Houghton Lake, from the town of Houghton Lake

What swims there. Walleye, bluegill, crappie and sunfish are the DNR’s own list for the ice; northern pike are common enough that the lake has its own pike rule.

How people fish it. Boats and pontoons in summer — many resorts include a rowboat or rent pontoons — and a busy ice season in winter, when the town holds its Tip-Up Town winter festival.

Keeping a fish. Walleye: five a day, at least 15 inches, from the last Saturday in April to 15 March. Northern pike here follow a special rule: up to five of any size, but only one of them 24 inches or longer. Bass: five, at least 14 inches, from the Saturday before Memorial Day to 31 December. Bluegill, crappie and other sunfish: 25 a day together.

Confirmed with the Michigan DNR, 6 November 2026 — 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations: walleye “Last Sat. in April – March 15: Lower Peninsula inland waters”, 15″, 5; “Waters where up to 5 Northern Pike of any size may be taken with only 1 pike 24″ or greater allowed in the daily possession limit … Roscommon: … Houghton L.”; Exceptions by county, Roscommon: “Denton Cr. from the Lake James Dam downstream to Houghton L. closed to fishing March 23 – April 23.” DNR ice-fishing page: “Houghton Lake (Roscommon County) – walleye, bluegill, crappie, sunfish.”

Spring closures. Two streams that feed the lake, Denton Creek below the Lake James Dam and part of the Cut River, are closed to fishing from 23 March to 23 April.

Nearest town: Houghton Lake itself, strung along the south and west shores. Grayling is the next larger town, to the north.

Higgins Lake — deep, clear and cold

Houghton Lake’s neighbour to the north-west, much deeper and clearer, with a state park at each end.

Higgins Lake, from Grayling and Roscommon

What swims there. The DNR lists lake trout, smelt, lake whitefish and northern pike for the ice.

How people fish it. Jigging deep water for lake trout and whitefish, from a boat in summer and through the ice in winter, and smelt fishing through the ice. The water is cold and deep a short way from shore: wear a life jacket in a small boat.

Keeping a fish. Higgins Lake is a Type E trout lake: open all year, any tackle, three trout and salmon a day in total, lake trout and brown trout at least 15 inches. Whitefish and cisco: five together. Smelt: two gallons. Pike: two, at least 24 inches, from the last Saturday in April to 15 March.

Confirmed with the Michigan DNR, 6 November 2026 — 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations, Inland Trout & Salmon Lake Regulations, Type E: “Open All Year … All [tackle] … 3”, minimum 15″ for brook, brown and rainbow trout, splake and lake trout, 10″ for coho, chinook and pink salmon, 15″ for Atlantic salmon; county listing, Roscommon: “Higgins Lake — E”; cisco and whitefish “5 in any combination on other inland waters”; smelt “2 gallons”. News release, 10 September 2026: salmon added to the Type E possession limit. DNR ice-fishing page: “Higgins Lake (Roscommon County) – lake trout, smelt, lake whitefish, northern pike.”

Boat launches. South Higgins Lake State Park, near Roscommon, has a mile of shoreline and a popular boat launch; North Higgins Lake State Park also has a launch. Both need a Recreation Passport on the car.

Nearest towns: Roscommon, just south-east, and Grayling to the north. Trip.com’s search sends “Roscommon” to the county in Ireland, so we have pinned Grayling and Houghton Lake instead and given Roscommon no button.

Burt Lake — walleye and perch in the north

A large lake in Cheboygan County, near the top of the Lower Peninsula, with Indian River at its south end.

Burt Lake, from Indian River and Cheboygan

What swims there. The DNR lists yellow perch and walleye for the ice. Muskellunge swim here too — the lake has its own 46-inch rule.

How people fish it. Drifting and trolling for walleye from boats, perch fishing in summer and winter, and casting the shoreline in summer.

Keeping a fish. Walleye: five, at least 15 inches, from the last Saturday in April to 15 March. Yellow perch: 25. Muskellunge: Burt Lake is a 46-inch water, so any muskie you keep must be at least 46 inches, from the first Saturday in June, one a year, registered within 24 hours.

Confirmed with the Michigan DNR, 6 November 2026 — 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations, Special Muskellunge Regulations, “46″ Minimum Size Limit Waters … Cheboygan: … Burt L.”; walleye and perch from the General Fishing Regulations table. DNR ice-fishing page: “Burt Lake (Cheboygan County) – yellow perch, walleye.” Burt Lake State Park page: “situated on the southeast corner of beautiful Burt Lake … boating, fishing”.

Boat launches. Burt Lake State Park, at the south-east corner by Indian River, has a boat launch and a sandy beach.

Nearest towns: Indian River at the south end, and Cheboygan, on Lake Huron to the north-east, with more choice.

Torch Lake — the long blue lake, and a new salmon fishery

A long, deep, strikingly clear lake in Antrim County, north of Traverse City. Not to be confused with the smaller Torch Lake in Houghton County in the Upper Peninsula, which has different rules.

Torch Lake, from Bellaire, Elk Rapids and Traverse City

What swims there. Lake trout in the deep water, muskellunge (the lake has its own 50-inch rule) and, now, Atlantic salmon, which the DNR says have grown into an emerging fishery after successful stocking.

How people fish it. Trolling and jigging deep water from a boat for trout and salmon, and casting the shallower edges.

Keeping a fish. Torch Lake is a Type E trout lake: open all year, any tackle, three trout and salmon together a day, lake trout at least 15 inches. Muskellunge: a 50-inch minimum here. Walleye and pike follow the inland rules (last Saturday in April to 15 March).

Confirmed with the Michigan DNR, 6 November 2026 — news release, 10 September 2026: “The Type E classification in Fisheries Order 254 does not list salmon and refers to the daily possession limit as three trout per day. However, recent successful Atlantic salmon stocking activities have resulted in an emerging fishery in Torch Lake (Antrim County) and some connected waters. Adding salmon to the possession limit under Type E-designated lakes will help clear up confusion”; “these regulation changes are not reflected in the printed version of the 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations.” 2026 Regulations: county listing, Antrim: “Torch Lake — E”; “50″ Minimum Size Limit Waters — Antrim: … Torch L. (including tribs)”.

Boat hire. Butch’s Torch Lake, near Bellaire, rents tritoons and other craft; ask before booking whether a rental may be used for fishing.

Nearest towns: Bellaire, just east of the lake, Elk Rapids at its south-western end, and Traverse City, to the south, with the most choice.

The licence and the limits, plainly

One Michigan licence covers every lake on this page, in Michigan’s waters. The 2026 licences and rules run to 31 March 2027.

Who needs one, and what it costs

From seventeen. Anyone 17 or older, resident or visitor, needs a licence. Under 17 you may fish free but must keep the rules; an adult actively helping a young angler needs a licence. A $2 voluntary youth licence exists for those who want one.

Buy online at Michigan.gov/DNRLicenses, on the Michigan DNR Hunt Fish app, or from a licence agent — many bait shops in these towns sell them. Michigan runs two Free Fishing Weekends a year, one of them in February, when no licence is needed (all other rules still apply).

Bringing a boat? Drain all water from the live well and the bilge when you leave any lake, release fish only into the water they came from, and never dump bait. That is Michigan’s rule, not just advice. State parks and DNR boating access sites need a Recreation Passport.

News release, 23 April 2026: “The 2026 annual fishing licenses are valid through March 31, 2027.” 2026 Regulations, VHS rules: “A person who trailers a boat over land shall drain all water from the live well(s) and the bilge of their boat upon leaving any body of water.”

On the ice

The Michigan DNR says plainly that your safety is your own responsibility and that there is no reliable thickness that tells you ice is safe. Test it as you go with a spud, needle bar or auger. Clear ice with a bluish tint is strongest; milky, refrozen snow ice is weak; slush ice is only about half as strong as clear ice; ice covered by snow should always be treated as unsafe. Carry ice picks and a life jacket, and ask a local bait shop what it has heard that morning.

Questions

Do I need a fishing licence to fish a lake in Michigan, and what does it cost?

Yes, from age 17, resident or visitor. Under 17 you may fish without one but must follow the rules, and an adult helping a child must be licensed. The Michigan DNR’s 2026 regulations list a resident annual licence at $26, a non-resident annual at $76, a senior annual (Michigan residents 65 and over) at $11 and a 24-hour licence at $10 for anyone. The 2026 licences are valid to 31 March 2027.

When is walleye season on Michigan’s inland lakes?

On Lower Peninsula inland lakes, such as Houghton, Higgins, Burt and Torch lakes, walleye and northern pike may be kept from the last Saturday in April to 15 March. In 2026 that opener was Saturday 25 April. On Lake St. Clair and Saginaw Bay, which count as Great Lakes waters, walleye are open all year.

How many walleye can I keep on Saginaw Bay?

Eight a day, each at least 13 inches, all year, under the 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations. That rule covers the Lake Huron waters of Saginaw Bay and the lower Saginaw River. The DNR says any change is announced online and on its fishing message line, 888-367-7060.

When can I keep bass and muskie on Lake St. Clair?

Bass may be kept from the third Saturday in June to 31 December on Lake St. Clair (20 June in 2026), five a day, at least 14 inches. Muskellunge may be kept from the first Saturday in June to 31 December, one per angler per licence year, at least 42 inches, and every muskie you keep must be registered with the DNR within 24 hours. Catch-and-release fishing for both is open all year.

Can I keep an Atlantic salmon from Torch Lake?

Yes, within the trout and salmon limit. Torch Lake is a Type E trout lake: open all year, any tackle, three trout and salmon a day, each at least 15 inches (10 inches for coho, chinook and pink salmon). On 10 September 2026 the DNR added salmon to the Type E possession limit because Atlantic salmon stocking has produced an emerging fishery in Torch Lake. That change is not in the printed 2026 booklet, so check the DNR’s digital regulations.
