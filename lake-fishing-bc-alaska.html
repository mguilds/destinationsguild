Lake Fishing in British Columbia & Alaska — the Kamloops Lakes to Kenai and Iliamna

The stocked trout lakes of the Kamloops plateau — Lac Le Jeune, Roche Lake, Tunkwa and Leighton — and four Alaska lakes: Kenai Lake and Skilak Lake on the Kenai Peninsula, Big Lake in the Mat-Su, and Iliamna, the biggest of them all. What swims in each, the rules from the agencies that set them, the licence a visitor needs, where the boats go in, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Government of British Columbia and the Alaska Department of Fish and Game, 25 November 2026

This is the lake-fishing companion to our Fly Fishing British Columbia & Alaska book. That book follows the rivers — steelhead and the five Pacific salmon. This one stays on still water, and it is for a wider crowd: families with a spinning rod, trollers, anglers who like a small boat and an anchor, and people who fish through the ice.

Two very different kinds of lake. Around Kamloops, in the dry interior of British Columbia, hundreds of small plateau lakes are stocked with rainbow trout every year, and they are fished from car-top boats, pontoons and float tubes; we cover four of the best known. In Alaska the lakes are bigger, colder and wilder: Kenai and Skilak are glacial lakes on the Kenai River with lake trout, rainbows and Dolly Varden; Big Lake is the Mat-Su’s summer and ice-fishing lake, north of Anchorage; and Iliamna, 77 miles long, is reached only by air.

Every rule, limit and price below was read on 25 November 2026 from the body that sets it, and is dated. Three things to know before you plan: British Columbia and Alaska each sell their own licence, and you need the right one for each side of the border; most Kamloops lakes carry their own special rules (engine limits, single hooks, a two-trout quota on Roche), so read the line for your lake; and in Alaska, salmon fishing is closed in Kenai Lake and Big Lake, and king salmon are closed in Skilak. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The best months, in plain words

These are northern lakes that freeze. The calendar turns on ice-off in spring and ice-up in late autumn.

The Kamloops trout lakes — Lac Le Jeune, Roche, Tunkwa and Leighton

Stocked rainbow trout lakes on the plateau south and west of Kamloops, in B.C.’s Thompson-Nicola region (Region 3). Fishing rules: the Government of British Columbia. Parks: BC Parks.

Lac Le Jeune

What swims there. Rainbow trout. The Freshwater Fisheries Society of B.C. stocks the lake every year with Pennask rainbow trout yearlings.

Keeping a fish. No special quota on this lake, so the Region 3 rule applies: 5 trout and char a day, no more than 1 over 50 cm. The one special rule is a 20 km/h speed limit on the water.

How people fish it. Our guidance: from a small boat or float tube with a fly or a trolled lure, and from shore. BC Parks describes a 76-metre fishing wharf at the day-use area, which makes this the easiest of the four for children and for anyone who cannot get into a boat; the Region 3 rules list Lac Le Jeune as wheelchair accessible.

Boat launch and camping. A boat launch (Freshwater Fisheries Society of B.C.) and a provincial park campground, which can be booked through BC Parks. The park sits at 1,280 metres, at the Lac Le Jeune exit of Highway 5, 37 km south of Kamloops and 47 km north of Merritt; a 26 km paved road also runs up from Kamloops.

Nearest towns: Kamloops to the north and Merritt to the south. Lac Le Jeune Resort, on the lake, has rooms, cabins and boat rentals — see lodges & guides.

Confirmed with the Government of British Columbia and BC Parks, 25 November 2026 — the 2025–2027 B.C. Freshwater Fishing Regulations Synopsis, Region 3 (the regulations page was last updated 26 August 2026), BC Parks’ Lac Le Jeune Park page, and the Freshwater Fisheries Society of B.C.’s GoFishBC site.

Roche Lake

What swims there. Rainbow trout. GoFishBC says the lake is stocked each spring with Pennask and Fraser Valley rainbows. BC Parks says Roche Lake Park holds seven stocked trout lakes, some of them aerated.

Keeping a fish. Roche has its own rules: no ice fishing; rainbow trout daily quota 2; single hook; and in the south arm (south of a line from the largest island), electric motors only, 7.5 kW at most. A separate B.C. government page about this lake still shows a change from “single barbless hook” to “single hook” as pending; the synopsis in force says single hook. If in doubt, use a barbless single hook — it satisfies both.

How people fish it. Our guidance: a quiet, fly-and-boat lake. Bring a small boat, a pontoon or a float tube; an electric motor if you want to fish the south arm.

Boat launch and camping. A boat launch and rustic campsites on Roche and Horseshoe lakes; GoFishBC counts 33 campsites, first come, first served. The park is about 36 km south-east of Kamloops, by a gravel road off Highway 5A at the north end of Trapp Lake.

Nearest town: Kamloops.

Confirmed with the Government of British Columbia and BC Parks, 25 November 2026 — the 2025–2027 synopsis, Region 3; the B.C. government’s Roche Lake angling-regulation proposal page; BC Parks’ Roche Lake Park page; and GoFishBC (5 January 2024).

Tunkwa Lake and Leighton Lake

What swims there. Rainbow trout in both. GoFishBC says Tunkwa is stocked with 40,000 Pennask rainbow trout a year and calls it “an excellent winter fishery”.

Keeping a fish. The Region 3 quota applies (5 trout and char a day, no more than 1 over 50 cm). Tunkwa: engines limited to 7.5 kW (10 hp). Leighton: no fishing within 100 metres of the mouth of the inlet stream, or within 100 metres of the Tunkwa Creek outlet, from 1 May to 30 November.

How people fish it. Our guidance: small boats and float tubes in open water; through the ice in winter on Tunkwa. Check the ice yourself before you walk out — nobody publishes a daily ice report for these lakes.

Boat launch and camping. Tunkwa Park has campgrounds on both lakes, run for BC Parks by Shuswap Adams Parks Ltd. It lies between Savona and Logan Lake, about 40 km south-west of Kamloops: 24 km of paved road from Savona, 16 km from Logan Lake.

Nearest towns: Logan Lake is the nearest village, with few rooms; Kamloops is the place to stay. Tunkwa Lake Resort rents cabins and boats on the lake; its website showed nothing dated 2026 when we checked, so we do not list it below — call before you go.

Confirmed with the Government of British Columbia and BC Parks, 25 November 2026 — the 2025–2027 synopsis, Region 3; BC Parks’ Tunkwa Park page; and GoFishBC’s Thompson-Nicola ice-fishing guide (updated 16 December 2025).

Kenai Lake — the head of the Kenai River

A long, glacial-blue lake in the Chugach National Forest, between Moose Pass and Cooper Landing, where the Kenai River begins. Fishing rules: the Alaska Department of Fish and Game (ADF&G).

Kenai Lake, from Cooper Landing to the south end

What swims there. ADF&G’s rules for Kenai Lake set limits for lake trout, rainbow trout, Arctic char and Dolly Varden, Arctic grayling and burbot. Salmon pass through the lake to spawn, but salmon fishing is closed all year in Kenai Lake and the other lakes of its drainage.

Keeping a fish. Lake trout: 2 a day 20 inches or longer, plus up to 10 under 20 inches. Rainbow trout (in unstocked lakes such as Kenai): 2 a day, only 1 of them 20 inches or longer, and a total of 2 rainbows 20 inches or longer a year in this part of Alaska, which you must record on your licence when you keep one. Dolly Varden and char in lakes: 2 a day, only 1 of them 20 inches or longer. Grayling: 5 a day. Burbot: 2 a day.

How people fish it. Bait and multiple hooks are allowed all year on the lake, except within a quarter mile of its inlet streams, where the stream rules apply. All flowing water in the drainage is closed from 1 May to 10 June. Our guidance: troll or jig for lake trout from a boat, and cast from shore near the campgrounds; the lake is cold and big, so keep an eye on the wind.

Boat launch and camping. Quartz Creek Campground, run by the U.S. Forest Service at the north end of the lake near Cooper Landing, has 45 sites ($23 a night single, $33 double, bookable on Recreation.gov up to 180 days ahead) and a boat launch that the Forest Service keeps open all year, with intermittent plowing in winter.

Nearest towns: Cooper Landing at the north end, and Moose Pass near the south.

Confirmed with the Alaska Department of Fish and Game and the U.S. Forest Service, 25 November 2026 — ADF&G’s 2026 Southcentral sport fishing regulations, Kenai River Drainage (Kenai Lake & Tributaries), and the Chugach National Forest’s Quartz Creek Campground page (updated 18 November 2026).

Skilak Lake — big water in the Kenai National Wildlife Refuge

About 25,000 acres, on the Kenai River below Cooper Landing, inside the Kenai National Wildlife Refuge. Fishing rules: ADF&G. Campgrounds and boating rules: the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

Skilak Lake, from the upper to the lower campground

What swims there. Skilak sits on the Kenai River, and ADF&G manages it with the Lower Kenai River, “upstream to and including Skilak Lake”. Those rules set limits for rainbow trout, Dolly Varden, lake trout, burbot and sockeye salmon. King salmon fishing is closed in Skilak Lake.

Keeping a fish. Rainbow trout: 1 a day, and it must be under 16 inches. Dolly Varden: 1 a day, under 16 inches. Lake trout: 2 a day 20 inches or longer, plus up to 10 under 20 inches. Burbot: 2 a day. Sockeye: from 20 June to 15 August, 6 a day (16 inches or longer), 12 in possession, in line with the Kenai late-run king salmon plan; from 16 August to 19 June, 3 a day and 6 in possession, combined with chum and coho. Read the Lower Kenai pages of the booklet before you keep a sockeye — the river rules are long.

How people fish it. Bait and multiple hooks are allowed all year on Skilak, except within half a mile of the upper Kenai River inlet, where the river rules apply. Our guidance: most anglers troll or jig from a boat. Skilak is a big, cold lake fed by glaciers, and wind can rise quickly; small boats should stay near shore and watch the weather.

Boat launches and refuge rules. The refuge runs two campgrounds with boat launches: Upper Skilak Lake (25 units, $10 a night for walk-in tent sites and $15 for others) and Lower Skilak Lake (14 units, free). Motorboats on Skilak must have four-stroke or direct-fuel-injection engines. The rocky islands in the lake, and the water within 100 yards of them, are closed from 15 March to 30 September for nesting birds.

Nearest towns: Cooper Landing, nearer the upper campground, and Soldotna, the bigger town down the river.

Confirmed with the Alaska Department of Fish and Game and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, 25 November 2026 — ADF&G’s 2026 Southcentral regulations, Lower Kenai River (general rules and the Skilak Lake entry), and the Kenai National Wildlife Refuge’s camping, fishing and rules pages.

Big Lake — the Mat-Su’s summer and ice lake

About 2,500 acres and 89 feet deep at its deepest, west of Wasilla off the Parks Highway, north of Anchorage. Fishing rules: ADF&G. Launches: Alaska State Parks.

Big Lake, from the north to the south launch

What swims there. ADF&G’s Big Lake rules cover burbot, Arctic char and Dolly Varden, and northern pike, which are not native here: ADF&G’s Big Lake page records pike caught in its test nets in 2006, 2020 and 2021. Salmon fishing is closed all year.

Keeping a fish. Arctic char and Dolly Varden: catch and release only. Burbot: 5 a day from 1 May to 31 October; 2 a day from 1 November to 14 March; none may be kept from 15 March to 30 April. Northern pike: you may not release a live pike — keep it.

How people fish it. Through the ice you may use two lines, each with one single hook. From 1 November to 30 April only one unbaited, single-hook lure or fly is allowed (burbot included), and chumming is banned — with one exception: from 1 November to 15 March you may fish for pike through the ice with up to 5 lines, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., using a whole bait fish on a large single hook. Read the Big, Mirror and Flat Lakes entry in the booklet before you set lines.

Boat launches and camping. Alaska State Parks runs Big Lake North State Recreation Site (60 walk-in campsites, first come, first served, with a boat launch) and Big Lake South (20 sites and a launch). The state parks fee page lists $7 a day for parking, $20 for camping and $25 to launch, with no effective date shown; ADF&G’s lake page notes the south launch ramp needs repair.

Nearest towns: the community of Big Lake itself, and Wasilla, the nearest town with hotels.

Confirmed with the Alaska Department of Fish and Game and Alaska State Parks, 25 November 2026 — ADF&G’s 2026 Southcentral regulations, Knik Arm (Big, Mirror and Flat Lakes), ADF&G’s Big Lake information page, and the Alaska State Parks pages for Big Lake North and South and its unit fees.

Iliamna Lake — Alaska’s biggest lake, by air only

About 1,600 square miles and 77 miles long, in the Bristol Bay country south-west of Anchorage. Fishing rules: ADF&G (Southwest region, Bristol Bay).

Iliamna Lake and its rivers

What swims there. ADF&G calls Iliamna Alaska’s largest body of fresh water, and spawning ground for Chinook, coho and sockeye salmon; the National Park Service calls it North America’s largest spawning ground for sockeye. The lake and its rivers are famous for big rainbow trout, and the Bristol Bay rules also set limits for lake trout, grayling, char and Dolly Varden.

Keeping a fish. Rainbow trout near the streams: 1 a day from 8 June to 31 October, no size limit; 5 a day from 1 November to 9 April, only 1 of them 20 inches or longer. Lake trout under the general Bristol Bay rules: 4 a day. Some waters are catch and release for rainbows — for example the mouth of Lower Talarik Creek from 8 June to 31 October, and the Kvichak River below the lake’s outlet at Igiugig from 8 June to 30 September.

How people fish it. Within half a mile of any inlet or outlet stream, and in all flowing water, only unbaited, single-hook lures or flies may be used, all year, and those waters are closed from 10 April to 7 June. Farther out on the open lake, the general Bristol Bay rules apply. Our guidance: almost everyone fishes Iliamna from a lodge, with a guide and a boat or floatplane each day.

Getting there. There is no road. The National Park Service’s Lake Clark directions page says the region is reached mainly by small plane, about 120 air miles south-west of Anchorage, and lists Iliamna as a floatplane departure point. Regional airlines fly Anchorage to Iliamna; the lodges arrange the rest.

Nearest town: Anchorage, for the night before you fly. Iliamna village has an airport but no rooms we can sell you; the lodges are booked directly.

Confirmed with the Alaska Department of Fish and Game and the National Park Service, 25 November 2026 — ADF&G’s 2026 Southwest sport fishing regulations, Bristol Bay (Iliamna Lake, Kvichak River and general rules), ADF&G’s May 2012 article on Iliamna Lake, and the NPS Lake Clark pages (directions updated 29 June 2026).

The licence and the limits, plainly

British Columbia and Alaska each sell their own licence. Buy it online before you go, and carry it on the water.

British Columbia (prices in Canadian dollars)

Who needs one. Everyone 16 and older fishing fresh water. B.C. residents under 16 need no licence. Non-residents under 16 may fish without one if a licence holder 16 or older is with them, but the fish they keep count toward that holder’s quota.

For a visitor. B.C. sells two visitor licences, “non-resident” and “non-resident alien”; the province’s residency definitions decide which you are. Non-resident: annual $62.87, eight days $41.15, one day $22.86. Non-resident alien: annual $91.44, eight days $57.14, one day $22.86.

For a B.C. resident. Annual $41.15, eight days $22.86, one day $11.43; residents 65 and older, $5.71 a year.

Where and when. Since 1 April 2026, licences are sold online through the province’s WILD system (you need a Fish and Wildlife ID), or in person from licence vendors. Licences run from 1 April to 31 March. None of the conservation surcharge stamps applies to these four lakes.

Region 3 quota. 5 trout and char a day, no more than 1 over 50 cm (and the lake’s own rules on top: 2 rainbows a day on Roche). Possession is two daily quotas.

Confirmed with the Government of British Columbia, 25 November 2026 — the recreational freshwater fishing licence page (last updated 27 October 2026), the conservation surcharges page, the 2 March 2026 news release on the move to WILD, and the 2025–2027 synopsis, Region 3.

Alaska (prices in US dollars)

For a visitor. Nonresident sport fishing licence: one day $15, three days $30, seven days $45, fourteen days $75, a year $100. Nonresidents under 16 need no licence.

For an Alaska resident. $20 a year, for residents 18 and older.

King salmon stamp. Sold at the same prices as the nonresident licence (resident $10 a year), but king salmon fishing is closed at Kenai Lake, Skilak Lake and Big Lake, so most lake anglers on this page will not need one.

Where. ADF&G’s online store, its mobile app, ADF&G offices and licence vendors. Before you cast, ADF&G asks anglers to check its emergency orders, which can change a season overnight.

Confirmed with the Alaska Department of Fish and Game, 25 November 2026 — the 2026 regulation booklets’ licence page and the Kenai River Drainage section.

Questions

What fishing licence does a visitor need for the Kamloops lakes?

A British Columbia basic freshwater fishing licence, for everyone 16 and older. On the B.C. government’s licence page on 25 November 2026 the fees were: non-resident annual $62.87, eight days $41.15, one day $22.86; non-resident alien annual $91.44, eight days $57.14, one day $22.86; B.C. resident annual $41.15. The province’s residency definitions decide which one you are. Licences are sold online through the WILD system and run from 1 April to 31 March. A non-resident under 16 may fish without a licence alongside a licence holder, but any fish kept count toward that holder’s quota.

What are the special rules on Roche Lake?

The 2025–2027 B.C. Freshwater Fishing Regulations Synopsis, Region 3, lists for Roche Lake: no ice fishing, a rainbow trout daily quota of 2, single hook, and electric motors only (7.5 kW at most) in the south arm. Checked on 25 November 2026.

What does an Alaska fishing licence cost a visitor?

In the Alaska Department of Fish and Game’s 2026 regulation booklet, a nonresident sport fishing licence costs $15 for one day, $30 for three days, $45 for seven days, $75 for fourteen days and $100 for a year. Nonresidents under 16 need no licence. An Alaska resident licence is $20 a year. A king salmon stamp is also sold, but king salmon fishing is closed at Kenai Lake, Skilak Lake and Big Lake. Checked on 25 November 2026.

Can I fish with bait on Kenai Lake and Skilak Lake?

On the open lakes, yes. The 2026 Southcentral booklet allows bait and multiple hooks year-round in Kenai Lake except within a quarter mile of its inlet streams, and in Skilak Lake except within half a mile of the upper Kenai River inlet. In those inlet areas, and in all the flowing water of the Kenai, the river rules apply instead. Salmon fishing is closed in Kenai Lake all year, and king salmon fishing is closed in Skilak Lake. Checked on 25 November 2026.

How do you get to Iliamna Lake?

By air. Iliamna is not on Alaska’s road system; most anglers fly from Anchorage to the village of Iliamna or straight to a lodge by floatplane, and the lodges arrange the rest. Book your Anchorage hotel for the night before the flight. Near the inlets and outlets of the lake, only unbaited single-hook lures and flies are allowed, and those waters are closed from 10 April to 7 June (2026 Southwest booklet, checked 25 November 2026).

What we could not check today, and will not pretend to have

The part of a fishing page most sites leave out.

Seven honest gaps

Roche Lake’s hook rule. The synopsis in force says “single hook”; a B.C. government proposal page still lists the change from “single barbless hook” as pending. A barbless single hook keeps you legal either way.

Which B.C. visitor licence is yours. We did not read the province’s residency definitions page, so we do not tell you whether a visitor from the United States buys the “non-resident” or the “non-resident alien” licence. Check it in WILD before you buy. We also could not confirm whether the B.C. fees include tax.

Alaska limits on Skilak. The booklet sets Skilak’s limits through the Lower Kenai River section, written mostly for the river. We have given them as printed; read the Lower Kenai pages of the 2026 Southcentral booklet before you keep a fish, and check ADF&G’s emergency orders.

Parks, fees and dates. BC Parks did not show 2026 operating dates for these parks, and the Alaska State Parks fee page shows no effective date. Check before you tow a boat.

Best months and ice. No agency publishes ice-off dates or a best-month calendar for these lakes. Where the months table says “our guidance”, it is ours. Never trust ice you have not checked.

Businesses we could not list. Tunkwa Lake Resort and the two Roche Lake Resort websites showed nothing dated 2026, and the Roche sites disagree about boat rentals; we found no Kamloops fly shop, no Big Lake fishing guide and no Kenai Lake boat rental with a current site of its own. Several Iliamna lodges and air services have live sites with no dated season. We list none of them. That is not a judgement on them.

Small towns and hotel lists. Cooper Landing and Moose Pass are small, so we added Soldotna. Logan Lake, Big Lake and Iliamna have no booking button, because we could not show that our partner holds rooms there.
