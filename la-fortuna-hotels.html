Where to Stay in La Fortuna

Where to Stay in La Fortuna & Arenal — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Arenal is the volcano everybody pictures when they picture Costa Rica, and where you sleep here comes down to one question: do you want the hot springs on your own property, or are you happy to buy a day pass? The resorts strung along the volcano road west of town mostly own their thermal water and include it in the rate; the friendly little hotels in La Fortuna town have none, cost a third as much, and put you within a two-minute stroll of the church, the sodas and the tour desks. One more thing worth saying plainly for anyone travelling with a stiff knee: much of this valley is built on a slope. We have noted the steep properties below. Luxury

The volcano road’s two grandest addresses, both with their own thermal water included in the rate.

The Springs Resort & Spa at Arenal

$$$$ LuxuryVolcano road28 mineral poolsVolcano viewsWildlife sanctuary

Perched on a ridge with what may be the most theatrical volcano view in Costa Rica, The Springs spreads twenty-eight mineral pools down a forested hillside, all included in the room rate rather than sold as a day pass. Five restaurants, a wildlife sanctuary and a full spa mean you never need to leave, which is fortunate, because you will want the hotel shuttle for the steep switchback approach. Be candid about the terrain: the property is built on a slope and the walk from the rooms to the lower pools is a genuine descent, so ask for an upper-level room near the lobby if stairs are a concern.

Nayara Gardens

$$$$ LuxuryVolcano roadRainforest casitasSloths in the treesSpring-fed pools

Nayara Gardens is the family-welcoming half of the Nayara estate, a collection of casitas tucked into secondary rainforest where sloths genuinely hang in the cecropia trees outside your door. Service is the quiet, anticipatory kind, the breakfast is excellent, and thermal water feeds the pools and many private outdoor tubs. The grounds are gently rolling rather than punishing, but they are spread out — ask for a casita near the main restaurant if long walks in humidity are not your idea of a holiday.

Boutique

Smaller, quieter and family-run, with thermal water that belongs to the hotel rather than to a crowd down the road.

Hotel El Silencio del Campo

$$$ UpscaleVolcano roadOwn thermal poolsLevel groundsFamily-run

A small cluster of freestanding casitas set on flat, well-kept farm ground with an unobstructed volcano view and hot-spring pools that belong to the hotel, not to a day-pass operator. The scale is human, the staff know your name by the second morning, and the on-site farm supplies the breakfast table. For travellers past middle age it has an underrated virtue: the property is essentially level, with no stair-climbing between your room and the warm water.

Chachagua Rainforest Hotel & Hot Springs

$$$ UpscaleChachaguaRainforest immersionOwn hot springsSuperb birding

An old working ranch turned rainforest retreat some twenty-five minutes south of town, Chachagua trades the volcano panorama for deep green immersion, with a river running through the grounds and its own modest thermal pools. Birders and quiet-seekers love it; the toucans arrive at breakfast without being asked. Two honest caveats — you need a rental car or arranged transfers because nothing is walkable, and the paths between the casitas roll up and down over uneven ground.

Budget & Value

Neither of these has hot springs, and that is exactly why they cost a third of what the volcano road does. Buy a day pass and come out well ahead.

Hotel Las Colinas

$ BudgetLa Fortuna townWalk to everythingRooftop volcano viewFamily-owned

A plain, spotless, family-owned hotel that has been quietly out-performing its price for decades, half a block from the central park and a two-minute stroll from the church, the sodas and the tour desks. There are no hot springs here — you buy a day pass at Paradise or Tabacón and still finish well ahead on the arithmetic. The building has three floors and no lift, and the volcano view is from the rooftop terrace, so request a lower floor if stairs are an issue.

Essence Arenal Hotel Biofarm & Spa

$ BudgetEl CastilloHilltop viewsOrganic farm dinnersUnpaved access

Essence sits on a hillside above El Castillo with a full sweep of volcano and lake, serving three-course organic dinners from its own farm for the price of a modest restaurant meal in town. It has grown up considerably from its hostel origins and now offers private rooms and glamping tents alongside the dorms. Be clear-eyed about access: the last stretch is unpaved, you need a car or a paid shuttle, and the property itself is steep enough that luggage and legs both get a workout.

Family-friendly

Warm water, slides and space to run — and one of them has the gentlest ground on the volcano road.

Los Lagos Hotel Spa & Resort

$$ Mid-rangeVolcano roadWater slidesOwn hot springsFrog & butterfly exhibits

If you are travelling with grandchildren, Los Lagos solves the problem outright: thermal pools with water slides, a frog and butterfly exhibit, a crocodile pond and enough acreage that nobody feels penned in. The springs belong to the hotel, so children can drift between warm pools and cool ones all afternoon without anyone paying admission twice. The trade-off is size and slope — the resort sprawls across a hillside with long walks and stair sections between the rooms and the water, so ask for a room in the lower blocks.

Adults-only

Arenal does adults-only properly — one of these is among the most celebrated hotels in the Americas, the other is the easiest on the legs.

Nayara Springs

$$$$ LuxuryVolcano road16 villasPrivate plunge poolsReached by footbridge

Sixteen villas, each with its own spring-fed plunge pool screened by rainforest, reached across a suspension bridge that firmly separates the adults from everyone else. It is consistently ranked among the finest hotels in the Americas, and the outdoor shower with a sloth overhead is not a marketing invention. Bring your wallet and your walking shoes — dining is priced separately and can startle, and the villa paths involve bridges, steps and grades, though golf-cart transport is available on request.

The Royal Corin Thermal Water Spa & Resort

$$$ UpscaleVolcano roadTen thermal poolsLift serviceCompact and level

Royal Corin is a contemporary, low-slung hotel with ten thermal and natural pools on its own grounds, two of them with waterfalls, and a rooftop terrace for the sunset volcano show. The design is modern rather than rustic, which appeals to travellers who have had their fill of thatch and hardwood. Practically speaking it is the friendliest of the adults-only options for anyone with mobility concerns: compact, with lift service, and the pools a short level walk from the rooms.

Pet-friendly

Honestly, this is the thinnest category in Arenal. The big hot-springs resorts — Tabacón, The Springs, Nayara, Kioro — do not accept pets, and most ‘pet-friendly La Fortuna’ listings you will find elsewhere are private holiday rentals rather than hotels. These two are real hotels with stated policies.

Hotel Arenal Lodge

$$ Mid-rangePrivate hilltopDogs welcomeSuperb birdingCar essential

An old-school lodge on a private hilltop with a commanding view of both the volcano and the valley, and one of the very few properties in the region that will take your dog — one at no charge, a modest fee for a second. The restaurant is honest, the birding on the grounds is superb and the pace is deliberately unhurried. You absolutely need a car: the access road climbs steeply for well over a mile and there is no walking to anything, ever.

A small, pretty riverside bed-and-breakfast on the edge of town, with a good pool, a generous included breakfast and a genuinely relaxed policy toward dogs of any size at no extra charge. The setting by the water is cooler and quieter than the main street, but you can still walk to restaurants in a few minutes. The grounds are level and compact, making this the easier of the two pet options for anyone who would rather not drive after dark.

Hot Springs, Spa & Pool

The reason most people come to Arenal in the first place. Both of these own genuine thermal water.

Tabacon Thermal Resort & Spa

$$$$ LuxuryVolcano roadGenuine geothermal riverAdults-only gardensMany steps

Tabacón is the real article: its springs are fed by a genuine geothermal river running through landscaped gardens, not by a boiler, and hotel guests get unlimited access plus the adults-only Shangri-Lá Gardens reserved for them alone. The spa, with treatment palapas set beside running thermal water, is the best in the region. Two honest cautions — the gardens involve a great deal of walking on stone paths, bridges and steps with hot water underfoot, and the resort sells day passes to non-guests, so the main gardens can be busy in the late afternoon.

Hotel Arenal Kioro Suites & Spa

Every suite here faces the volcano through a wall of glass and includes two jetted tubs, which sounds excessive until the first rainy evening. The hotel owns the Titokú hot springs, a set of pools in a ravine below the main building, and guests have private access. The important practical detail is that the springs are a substantial descent from the rooms; the hotel runs a shuttle, and you should plan to use it rather than the path.

All-inclusive

Read this before you compare prices. True Caribbean-style all-inclusive barely exists in Arenal. What is sold here as all-inclusive is almost always an optional meal-and-drinks plan added to a normal room rate at a mid-range hotel — not an unlimited-everything resort. Several luxury names that appear under ‘all-inclusive’ filters on booking sites are miscategorised, and guests routinely report being surprised by separate dining bills. These two genuinely publish and sell such plans.

Volcano Lodge Hotel & Thermal Experience

$$ Mid-rangeVolcano roadReal all-inclusive planOwn thermal waterIn-room tubs

Volcano Lodge sells a straightforward all-inclusive package — meals, domestic drinks and access to its thermal circuit — at a price that makes the arithmetic easy for travellers who dislike totting up dinner cheques. Many rooms have private thermal baths on the terrace, which is an unusual luxury at this tier. Do not expect resort theatre or gourmet cooking; expect decent buffet food, warm water on your own patio and a bill with no surprises.

Springs, cars and stairs — the practical truth

Before you book

Three things decide whether you enjoy Arenal. First, the springs: Tabacón, The Springs, Nayara, Arenal Kioro, Los Lagos, Arenal Springs, El Silencio del Campo, Volcano Lodge and Chachagua all have their own thermal water; hotels in La Fortuna town have none, and their guests buy day passes at Paradise, Baldi or Tabacón. Of the day-pass options, Paradise Hot Springs is the quiet, level, uncrowded one and Baldi is the loud, busy one. Second, transport: everything on the volcano road needs a car or arranged transfers, and nothing out there is walkable to a restaurant; only the town hotels are. Third, stairs. In rough order of severity the steepest are Essence Arenal, Arenal Lodge, The Springs, Arenal Kioro, Los Lagos and Tabacón; the easiest walking is at Paradise, El Silencio del Campo, Royal Corin, Casa Del Río and Arenal Springs.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travellers ask most about staying in La Fortuna & Arenal.

Should I stay in La Fortuna town or on the volcano road?

Stay on the volcano road if hot springs on your own property matter to you, and expect to need a car or transfers for every meal out. Stay in town if you would rather walk to dinner, spend a third as much, and buy a day pass at one of the springs. Neither is wrong; they are simply different holidays.

Which Arenal hotels have their own hot springs?

Tabacón, The Springs Resort, Nayara Springs and Nayara Gardens, Hotel Arenal Kioro, Los Lagos, Arenal Springs, Hotel El Silencio del Campo, Volcano Lodge and Chachagua all have thermal water on the property, included in the room rate. Hotels in La Fortuna town do not — their guests buy day passes at Paradise, Baldi or Tabacón, which is a perfectly good arrangement and much cheaper.

Is La Fortuna difficult if you don’t walk well?

Parts of it are, and the brochures rarely say so. Many of the finest resorts are terraced down steep hillsides with long outdoor stair runs between the rooms and the pools. The gentlest ground belongs to Paradise Hot Springs, Hotel El Silencio del Campo, Royal Corin, Arenal Springs and Casa Del Río; where a property is steep, ask at booking for a room near the lobby and use the shuttle rather than the path.

How long should I stay in Arenal?

Two to three nights suits most travellers — one day for the hanging bridges or the volcano trails, one for the hot springs and a slow afternoon, and a spare morning for a waterfall or a coffee farm. Arenal pairs naturally with Monteverde by way of the jeep-boat-jeep across Lake Arenal, which is far pleasanter than the long road around.
