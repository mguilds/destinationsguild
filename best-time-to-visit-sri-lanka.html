Best Time to Visit Sri Lanka

Best Time to Visit Sri Lanka

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Sri Lanka is the one country where “when should I go?” has two answers at once, because two monsoons cross the island at opposite ends at opposite times of year. There is almost always a dry, sunny half — it is just not always the same half. Get that right and the trip works; get it wrong and you will spend a fortnight looking at a rough grey sea. Here is the year, month by month, with the festivals and the whale seasons set against it.

The two monsoons, and why they decide everything

The southwest monsoon, locally the Yala, runs from about May to September and wets the west coast, the south coast and the hill country — which is to say Colombo, Negombo, Galle, Mirissa, Kandy and the tea estates, the places most visitors want.

The northeast monsoon, the Maha, runs from about November to February and wets the east and the north — Trincomalee, Pasikuda, Arugam Bay, Jaffna. Between the two sit the inter-monsoon weeks of October and November and again in March and April, when thunderstorms can arrive anywhere from either direction.

The mountains in the middle of the island do the dividing, and they do it sharply: it is entirely normal for Galle to be under warm rain while Trincomalee, four hours away, is dry and flat calm. So the rule for planning is simple. Pick your dates, work out which coast is dry, and build the trip on that coast rather than on the one in the photograph you saw.

December to April — go south and west

Colombo, Galle, Unawatuna, Mirissa, Tangalle, the hill country and the Cultural Triangle. Dry, hot, calm seas, whales off Mirissa. Peak season and peak prices, especially over Christmas.

May to September — go east

Trincomalee, Pasikuda, Arugam Bay and the north. Dry and calm while the south-west is wet, with the elephant Gathering at Minneriya and much lower prices in the rest of the country.

Month by month

What each month actually gives you, on both sides of the island.

January

Peak, and excellent on the west and south coasts: dry, sunny, calm sea, whales off Mirissa. The hill country is clear in the mornings and cold at night. The east is wet. The best single month for the classic route.

February

Often the driest month of all in the south and west, and the pick of the year for Galle, Mirissa and the beaches. Hill country dry and clear. The Navam Perahera lights up Colombo at the February full moon.

March

Still dry in the south and west and getting hot; the hills are at their best. Thunderstorms creep in late in the month as the inter-monsoon begins. The last comfortable month before the real heat.

April

The hottest month, and the one with the New Year in it. Sinhala and Tamil New Year in mid-April empties the cities, fills the roads and trains for several days and shuts a good deal. Inter-monsoon thunder on both coasts; the east is beginning to open up.

May

The southwest monsoon arrives. The west, the south and the hills turn wet and the sea rough, and prices fall sharply. The east coast is coming good. Vesak, at the May full moon, is the most important Buddhist festival of the year.

June

Firmly monsoon in the south-west and firmly dry in the east. Trincomalee, Pasikuda and Arugam Bay are at their best and the Arugam Bay surf season is running. A good month, if you go east.

July

The east at its peak, dry and calm, and the Gathering of elephants at Minneriya building. Esala Perahera in Kandy falls in July or August by the moon and takes the town over for ten nights — book months ahead, or avoid it deliberately.

August

As July: east coast excellent, south-west wet, the Gathering at its height. Esala Perahera often falls here. It is also the European holiday season, so the east coast is busier than the weather alone would suggest.

September

The southwest monsoon starts to ease and the east is still dry. One of the quietest and best-value months of the year, with the trade-off that the weather is unsettled almost anywhere.

October

The wettest and least reliable month on the island, with inter-monsoon thunderstorms on both coasts at once. Also the cheapest. We would not choose it for a first trip, and whale and park trips are patchy.

November

The northeast monsoon sets in over the east and north while the west and south dry out and improve week by week. The south-coast whale season opens. By late November the classic route is working again.

December

The west, the south and the hills are dry and bright and the season is open; the Adam’s Peak pilgrimage begins. The east is wet. Christmas and the New Year are the busiest and dearest fortnight of the year, so book early.

Festivals and dates to plan around

Several of these move with the moon, so check the year before you book.

Sinhala and Tamil New Year — mid-April

The biggest domestic travel week of the year. Families go home, the cities empty, the trains and buses fill, and many shops and some restaurants close for several days. Worth seeing, awkward to travel in.

Vesak — the May full moon

The Buddha’s birth, enlightenment and death, marked with paper lanterns, illuminated pandals and free food stalls everywhere. A beautiful few days, and a strict one: no alcohol and no meat on sale in many places.

Esala Perahera, Kandy — July or August

Ten nights of torchlit processions with caparisoned elephants, drummers, dancers and whip-crackers, the largest Buddhist festival in the country. Dates follow the moon, so check the year. Rooms on the route are let months ahead at several times the usual price.

Poya — every full moon

Each full moon is a public holiday. Transport and shops run normally, but the sale of alcohol is restricted, bars and bottle shops close, and some attractions are busy with local visitors.

Adam’s Peak — December to May

The pilgrimage season on Sri Pada. Thousands climb through the night to reach the summit for sunrise, and the steps are lit and lined with tea stalls. Out of season the route is unlit and often in cloud.

Deepavali and Christmas

Deepavali, in October or November, matters most in the Tamil north and east and in Colombo. Christmas is widely and genuinely celebrated on this strongly Catholic west coast, and the fortnight around it is the peak of the tourist year.

Our own pick

If the usual route is what you want — a night near the airport, the ancient cities, Kandy, the train through the tea country and a week on the south coast — then late January to early March is the stretch we would book, and February if we had to choose one month. The south and west are at their driest, the sea is calm enough for the beaches and the whale boats, the hill country is clear in the mornings, and the Christmas rush has gone home.

If you would rather have the quieter, cheaper island and you are happy to go east, July and August are very good indeed: Trincomalee and Arugam Bay dry and calm, the Gathering at Minneriya at its height, and Esala Perahera in Kandy if you plan for it. The catch is that the hill country and the south will be wet, so this is an east coast trip rather than a tour of the whole island.

And the honest warning: October is the month to avoid on a first visit. Both coasts get inter-monsoon thunderstorms, the boats and some park drives are called off, and while it is the cheapest time of the year, it is cheap for a reason. Mid-April is worth sidestepping too, not for the weather but for the New Year holiday, when the whole country is travelling at once. The full country guide is our Sri Lanka travel guide.

Best time to visit Sri Lanka FAQ

What is the single best month to visit Sri Lanka?

February, if you want one answer and you are doing the usual route. The south and west coasts are at their driest, the sea is calm, the hill country is clear and the whales are off Mirissa. January and March are barely behind it. If your trip is built around the east coast instead, the answer is July or August.

Is there a time when the whole island is good?

Not really, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. The nearest you get is late March to early April and late November, when one monsoon has eased and the next has not set in hard, but those are also thundery inter-monsoon weeks and the weather is a lottery. Plan for one half of the island and enjoy it properly.

Which months should I avoid?

October is the one month we would steer a first-time visitor away from: inter-monsoon storms on both coasts at once, the least reliable weather of the year, and boat trips and park drives often called off. Mid-April is awkward for a different reason - the New Year holiday fills every train, bus and road in the country for several days.

When can I see whales?

Off Mirissa on the south coast from roughly November to April, which is the better-known and more reliable season for blue whales; and off Trincomalee on the east coast from roughly May to October. Out of season the boats simply do not run, so the dates are the whole trick. Mornings are calmer than afternoons either side of the island.

When is the east coast good?

Roughly May to September, which is exactly when the south and west are wet. Trincomalee, Pasikuda and Arugam Bay are dry, hot and calm then, Arugam Bay's surf season runs through it, and the elephant Gathering at Minneriya peaks from about July to September. From November to February the east is the wet half instead.

Does the monsoon mean it rains all day?

Usually not. The normal monsoon pattern here is a bright morning and a heavy downpour in the afternoon or evening, often over within a couple of hours. What the monsoon reliably does change is the sea: it turns rough, swimming becomes poor or unsafe on the affected coast, and the boat trips stop. That, rather than the rain itself, is the reason to follow the calendar.
