Where to Stay in Austin — Downtown, Rainey Street, South Congress & Beyond

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Austin calls itself the Live Music Capital of the World, and most of what visitors come for sits close to Lady Bird Lake: the music clubs of downtown and Sixth Street, the bars of Rainey Street, and the shops and cafes of South Congress just over the river. Where you sleep decides how much of the night you can walk. Downtown is the easy first-visit choice, South Congress has the most Austin character, the Domain is calmer, and the hills to the west are for a resort stay. This page sorts the areas and names eight hotels we checked on their own websites today.

Choosing Your Area in Austin

Six bases, each suited to a different trip.

Downtown and Sixth Street

Visit Austin, the official visitor bureau, names downtown as best for first-time visitors, live music, nightlife and walkability, with music venues, historic theatres and restaurants close together. Sixth Street is the loud, lively strip; the Red River Cultural District is where the independent music scene lives.

Stay here to walk to shows and dinner. Rooms facing Sixth Street can be noisy late at night — ask for a quiet side if you sleep lightly.

Rainey Street

At the southeast corner of downtown, near the lake. Visit Austin describes rows of restored bungalows turned into bars and restaurants.

Stay here for evenings out and for the hike-and-bike trail along the lake.

South Congress (SoCo)

Just south of the river. Visit Austin points to coffee on South Congress, the “i love you so much” mural, boot shops and boutiques along the avenue.

Stay here for the most Austin feel: shops, cafes and music in walking distance.

East Austin

East of the interstate. Visit Austin calls it one of the fastest-changing parts of the city, best for global food, craft cocktails, live music and local culture, with East Sixth Street its main corridor for eating and drinking.

A good area to eat in. We have not listed a hotel here yet.

North Austin and The Domain

Visit Austin describes The Domain as North Austin’s upscale outdoor shopping hub, walkable and well designed, with Domain NORTHSIDE alongside for dining and nightlife.

Stay here for shopping, business in the north of the city and an easier drive. Downtown is a drive away.

West Austin and the Hill Country edge

To the west the city climbs into wooded hills. Visit Austin points visitors to the Greenbelt for hiking and to the Texas Hill Country beyond.

Stay here for a quiet retreat with a pool and a spa. You will want a car.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Austin details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Austin

The airport is Austin-Bergstrom International (AUS), southeast of downtown. The airport and CapMetro, the city’s transit agency, both point to CapMetro Route 20, which starts at the airport just outside baggage claim and runs every 15 to 30 minutes, seven days a week, along Riverside Drive to downtown and the University of Texas campus (read 26 November 2026). Taxis, rideshares and rental cars are also at the terminal.

Getting around

Downtown, Rainey Street and the lakeshore trail are walkable, and South Congress is a short walk or ride across the river. The Domain and the western hills are best by car. If you bring a car, ask your hotel about parking before you book.

When to go

Visit Austin says the city has nearly 300 sunny days a year, temperate winters and hot summers, with spring and autumn the most comfortable balance. It quotes the National Weather Service for an average daily temperature of 70°F, with August the warmest month and January the coldest (read 26 November 2026). In summer, plan outdoor time for the morning and evening and drink plenty of water.

The bridge bats

Visit Austin explains that from late March through early autumn, North America’s largest urban bat colony lives under the Congress Avenue Bridge and flies out around sunset. Arrive early for a spot on the bridge or along the lake trail, and face east (read 26 November 2026). Hotels downtown and on South Congress are a walk from the bridge.

Austin Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in Austin?

Downtown for a first visit, live music and walking. Rainey Street for bars and the lakeside trail. South Congress for local character, shops and cafes. The Domain for shopping and an easier drive. West Austin for a quiet resort stay.

How do I get from Austin airport to downtown?

CapMetro Route 20 starts at Austin-Bergstrom International Airport, just outside baggage claim, and runs every 15 to 30 minutes, seven days a week, to downtown and the University of Texas campus. Taxis and rideshares are also at the terminal.

When can I see the bats in Austin?

From late March through early autumn the bats fly out from under the Congress Avenue Bridge around sunset. Arrive early for a spot on the bridge or along the lake trail.

Do I need a car in Austin?

Not if you stay downtown, on Rainey Street or on South Congress. A car is useful for The Domain, the Hill Country and day trips. Ask your hotel about parking before you book.

Is there a budget place to stay in downtown Austin?

Yes. Firehouse Hostel, listed above, offers dormitory beds and private rooms in a historic fire station steps from Sixth Street.
