When to Go to Italy

The Best Time to Visit Italy

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Short answer: the shoulder seasons — roughly April–May and September–October — are the sweet spot. You get warm, settled weather and full opening hours without the summer heat, queues, and peak prices. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the crowds, and the best timing for cities, the coast, and the Alps.

Italy is a year-round destination, but it is not the same destination year-round. The country you meet in a golden October — harvest tables, warm stone, room to breathe in the Uffizi — is a very different place from mid-August, when the mercury climbs past 35°C, the coast is elbow-to-elbow, and half of Rome has locked up and left town. Choosing your dates well is the single biggest lever you have over the trip. Here is how to pull it.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$ ShoulderMild & bloomingBest for citiesFewer crowds

The countryside greens up, wildflowers and wisteria arrive, and city sightseeing is a pleasure at 18–24°C. Late April and May are prime — everything is open, gardens are at their peak, and crowds haven't yet surged. Watch two spikes: Easter week (a major domestic peak, especially in Rome) and the run around Italy's April holidays. March can still be cool and showery, but it's cheap.

Summer — June to August

$$$$ PeakHot & busyBeach & lakesBook early

High season, and it feels like it. June is the most bearable of the three — long days, warm seas, festivals — before July and August bring serious heat and the biggest crowds and prices of the year. This is the time for the coast, the lakes, and the Dolomites for hiking, not for marathon city sightseeing in the midday sun. If summer is your only window, book flights and hotels months ahead and start your days early.

Autumn — September to November

$$ ShoulderWarm & mellowHarvest & foodGreat value

Many travelers' favorite season. September still has warm water and summer light with the peak crowds ebbing; October is mellow, golden, and one of the best-value months of the year, with the grape and olive harvests and truffle season in full swing. Rain increases as November arrives, but the cities are quiet and prices soft — ideal for Rome, Florence, and Venice.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold & quietSki & marketsCheapest

The bargain season, and a genuinely lovely one for the right trip: crowd-free art cities, Christmas markets in the north, and top-tier skiing in the Alps and Dolomites. Days are short and cold, and most coastal resorts — Amalfi, Cinque Terre, the smaller islands — largely shut down. Sidestep the two-week Christmas–New Year spike and January and February are the cheapest weeks in Italy.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ CheapestColdSki season

The quietest, cheapest month once the New Year holiday passes. Cold and often crisp; ideal for near-empty museums in Rome and Florence, winter sales, and prime skiing in the Dolomites. Venice is atmospheric and damp. Book cities, not the coast.

February

$ CheapColdCarnival

Still low season and low prices, with one big exception: the Venice Carnival, which fills the city and lifts rates for about ten days. Good skiing continues. Almond blossom starts in the far south. A great month for a quiet, affordable city break.

March

$$ Value shoulderCool, variableSpring begins

Shoulder season opens. Weather is variable — mild days, cool evenings, some rain — but crowds are thin and prices low. Cities shine; the coast is only just stirring. If Easter falls in late March, expect a short surge around it.

April

$$ ShoulderMild & greenEaster peak

One of the loveliest months: green hills, blossom, comfortable sightseeing weather. The catch is Easter and the April 25 / May 1 holiday cluster, which bring domestic crowds and higher rates. Outside those dates, superb value and mood.

May

$$$ PrimeWarm & settledEverything open

Arguably the best all-round month. Warm, stable weather, long days, gardens at their peak, the sea warming for a first swim, and the full summer machinery open before the crowds and heat arrive. Popular — book ahead — but worth it.

June

$$$$ Peak beginsHot, long daysCoast & lakes

Summer proper, and the most pleasant of the hot months. The longest days of the year, warm seas, and festival season, with crowds and prices climbing steadily toward the July–August peak. Excellent for the Amalfi Coast, the lakes, and Dolomite hiking.

July

$$$$ PeakVery hotBusy

Hot, crowded, and pricey, especially at the coast and in the honeypot cities. Great for beach and lake holidays and mountain trails; hard work for midday city sightseeing. Reserve accommodation, trains, and headline sights well in advance.

August

$$$$ PeakHottestFerragosto

The hottest, busiest month — and the trickiest. Around Ferragosto (August 15) Italians take their own holidays: coast, lakes, and islands are packed and expensive, while cities can feel half-shuttered as family-run shops and trattorias close for a week or two. Doable, but plan around the closures.

September

$$$ PrimeWarm seasHarvest

The other star month. Summer warmth and swimmable seas linger while the crowds thin and prices ease after the first week. The grape harvest begins, food festivals multiply, and cities become comfortable again. A superb time for coast and culture combined.

October

$$ Best valueMild & goldenTruffles

Mellow, golden, and one of the best-value months of the year. Comfortable city weather, autumn color, truffle and olive-harvest season, and low crowds. Rain increases late in the month and coastal resorts start winding down, but the cities are at their calm best.

November

$ Low seasonCool & wetQuiet cities

Low season returns: cool, the wettest month in much of the country, and quiet. Venice sees occasional acqua alta flooding. But prices are low, museums empty, and Rome and Florence rewarding for travelers who don't mind a coat and an umbrella.

December

$ → $$$ SplitFestiveSki & markets

A month of two halves. Early December is quiet and cheap, with Christmas markets (especially in the Alto Adige) and ski resorts opening. The two weeks around Christmas and New Year are a sharp peak — festive and beautiful, but book early and expect higher rates.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Mid-November to March — with January and February the lowest

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSkip Christmas fortnight

Transatlantic airfares and hotel rates bottom out in deep winter. January and February are the cheapest weeks of the year for both, with mid-November and early December close behind — the one carve-out being the roughly two-week Christmas–New Year holiday, when prices briefly jump. You'll trade warmth and coastal life for the savings, but for city breaks and skiing the value is unbeatable.

Want warm weather and a deal? Target the edges of shoulder season: late March to mid-April (avoiding Easter) and late October. You get mild days and open sights at a fraction of the July–August cost. As a rule, midweek flights and stays beat weekends, and booking two to three months out lands the best fares.

When to Avoid the Crowds

The busy periods in Italy are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks to sidestep

July & AugustFerragostoEasterChristmas & NY

July and August are the busiest, hottest, and most expensive stretch, with August the peak. The days around Ferragosto (August 15) are the crunch point: beaches, lakes, and islands are at their fullest while many city businesses close for their own holidays. Easter week and the Christmas–New Year fortnight are shorter, sharper spikes, concentrated in the marquee cities.

To have the big sights closer to yourself, come in the shoulder months — late April, May, September, October — or in winter. Whenever you go, the crowd-beating tactics are the same: reserve timed-entry tickets for the Colosseum, Vatican, Uffizi, and Accademia in advance, and visit at opening or in the last hours of the day. Best Time by Region

Italy is long and varied — the ideal month depends on where you're headed.

The Art Cities — Rome, Florence, Venice

Best: Apr–May, Sep–OctValue: Nov–FebAvoid: Jul–Aug midday

Cities are about walking, queuing, and standing in stone piazzas — so heat is the enemy. Spring and autumn are ideal; winter is quiet and cheap if you can take the cold. Skip peak-summer midday sightseeing, when both the temperature and the lines peak together. See where to stay in Rome and Venice.

The Coast & Islands — Amalfi, Cinque Terre, Sicily, Sardinia

Best: late May–Jun, SepSeason: Easter–OctClosed: winter

Coastal Italy runs on a season — roughly Easter through October, with many hotels, ferries, and restaurants shuttered the rest of the year. The sweet spots are late May to June and September: warm water, open everything, and lighter crowds than the July–August crush. For the far south, Sicily and Sardinia stretch the swimming season into early October. Plan your base on the Amalfi Coast.

The Alps & Dolomites

Ski: Dec–MarHike: Jun–SepAvoid: Apr–May, Nov

The mountains have two seasons and two dead zones. December to March is for skiing, with the most reliable snow in January and February. June to September is for hiking, wildflower meadows, and via ferrata, with July and August warmest and busiest. The shoulder gaps — late spring and November — see many lifts, huts, and hotels closed, so aim for the core of each season.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing an Italian trip.

What is the best month to visit Italy?

May and September are the two strongest all-round months. You get warm, settled weather, long daylight, everything open, and noticeably thinner crowds and lower prices than July and August. May leans lush and green; September brings warm seas and the grape harvest. April and October are close behind and even cheaper.

What is the cheapest time to visit Italy?

Mid-November through March is the cheapest window, with January and February the lowest of all for both flights and hotels — the two weeks around Christmas and New Year excepted. If you want warm-weather value, the true bargains sit at the edges of shoulder season: late March to mid-April and late October.

When should I avoid Italy because of crowds?

July and August are the most crowded and expensive months, with August the peak. Around Ferragosto (August 15) many Italians take their own holidays, so coastal towns and lakes are jammed while cities can feel half-shuttered as family-run shops and restaurants close. Easter week and the Christmas–New Year period are also short, sharp peaks.

Is Italy worth visiting in winter?

Yes, for the right trip. Winter is the best time for crowd-free art cities — Rome, Florence, and Venice at their quietest and cheapest — plus Christmas markets, and world-class skiing in the Alps and Dolomites from December to March. The trade-off is cold, short days and that most coastal resorts, such as the Amalfi Coast and Cinque Terre, largely shut down until spring.

What is shoulder season in Italy and why is it best?

Shoulder season is spring (April–May) and fall (September–October), the stretches on either side of the summer peak. It's widely considered the best time to visit Italy because you get pleasant weather and full opening hours without the peak-season heat, queues, and prices — the sweet spot for combining cities with the coast or countryside.
