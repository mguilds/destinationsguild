Before you go

Yellowstone is big, high and seasonal. Plan around those three facts and the rest falls into place.

Yellowstone covers about 3,500 square miles, most of it on a high volcanic plateau around 7,000 to 8,000 feet. The main sights sit on a figure-eight of roads called the Grand Loop: the geyser basins and Old Faithful on the west side, the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone in the middle, Yellowstone Lake to the southeast, and Mammoth Hot Springs and the Lamar Valley in the north. The park says it is about 50 miles from Old Faithful to the canyon alone, and with traffic, bison jams and 45 mph limits, you will cover less ground in a day than you expect.

That is why we tell first-time visitors to give it at least three full days and to sleep in two different places, one on the west or south side and one on the north or east side. Doing the whole loop as a day trip from one gateway town means most of your time is spent behind the wheel.

The season matters more here than almost anywhere. Most park roads close to cars from early November until mid- to late April, and the higher passes open later still. In winter only the road from Gardiner through Mammoth and the Lamar Valley to Cooke City stays open to ordinary vehicles; the rest of the park is reached by guided snowcoach or snowmobile.

Two more things to know before you go. The ground in the geyser basins is thin and scalding, so stay on the boardwalks every step of the way. And give the animals room: bison and elk injure more visitors than bears do. Cell service is patchy, so download maps and your reservations before you leave town.

Yellowstone entrance fees, hours & reservations

The essentials at a glance. Fees and reservation rules change every year, so confirm on the park's official website before you travel.

Entrance fee$35 per private vehicle, good for 7 days; $30 motorcycle; $20 per person on foot or bicycle (16 and older). Yellowstone annual pass $70.
OpenAll year, but most roads are closed to cars from early November to mid-April. The North Entrance to Northeast Entrance road stays open all year, weather permitting.
Vehicle reservationsNone. No timed entry or vehicle reservation is needed to enter Yellowstone.
What does need bookingPark lodges and every campground (except Mammoth in the off-season). Fishing, boating and backcountry camping need permits.
Best timeLate May to mid-June and September, when the roads are open and the crowds are lighter. July and August are busiest.
Nearest airportsBozeman (MT), Jackson (WY), Cody (WY), Billings (MT) and Idaho Falls (ID). West Yellowstone has seasonal flights from Salt Lake City, early May to mid-October.
Size / elevationAbout 2.2 million acres in three states; most visitor areas sit between about 6,200 and 8,000 feet.

Right now (winter 2026–27): the West, South and East entrances and most park roads closed to cars on November 1, 2026. The road from the North Entrance at Gardiner to the Northeast Entrance at Cooke City stays open, weather permitting. Guided snowmobile and snowcoach travel on the interior roads begins December 15. In spring the park plows and reopens roads in stages, usually starting in mid-April with the west side; Dunraven Pass and the Beartooth Highway open last, around Memorial Day weekend.

Camping change for 2027: beginning with the 2027 summer season, every Yellowstone campground is booked through Recreation.gov, on a rolling window that opens about six months before your arrival date. Most sites are reserved well ahead, so book as early as you can.

Closures and construction: Biscuit Basin, north of Old Faithful, has been closed since a hydrothermal explosion in July 2024 and remains closed until further notice. Pebble Creek Campground stays closed for flood-recovery work. The park lists expected traffic delays for 2027 at the Firehole River bridge near Old Faithful, the Gardner River High Bridge near Mammoth and from chip sealing around the park. A full rebuild of the North Entrance Road between Gardiner and Mammoth was approved in September 2026; its construction schedule had not been announced when we checked.

Visitors from outside the U.S.: since January 1, 2026, Yellowstone is one of 11 national parks that charge non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older an extra $100 per person on top of the entrance fee. The America the Beautiful pass costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for nonresidents, and a pass covers that extra fee.

Sources: the National Park Service website (nps.gov/yell: fees, park roads, operating dates, campgrounds, lodging, fishing and accessibility pages, and 2026 park news releases), checked November 2026.

Best things to do in Yellowstone National Park

Six places to build a Yellowstone trip around.

Geysers

Old Faithful & the Upper Geyser Basin

Old Faithful erupts many times a day and the visitor center posts predicted times. Stay for an hour and walk the boardwalks beyond it, where the largest concentration of geysers in the world steams and gurgles.

Hot springs

Grand Prismatic Spring

The park's largest hot spring, ringed in orange, yellow and deep blue, sits in Midway Geyser Basin a few miles north of Old Faithful. The boardwalk is short but the parking lot fills by mid-morning in summer.

Canyon

Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone

The Lower Falls drop 308 feet into a canyon of yellow and rose rock. Drive both the North Rim and South Rim roads; several overlooks, including Artist Point, are a short walk from the car.

Wildlife

Lamar & Hayden Valleys

Bison herds, elk, pronghorn and, with luck and a spotting scope, wolves and grizzlies. Go at dawn or dusk, pull fully off the road and stay in or near your vehicle.

Terraces

Mammoth Hot Springs

Travertine terraces built by hot water stepping down the hillside, next to the old Army post of Fort Yellowstone. Elk often graze on the lawns. This is the one major area you can drive to all winter.

Lake

Yellowstone Lake

The largest high-elevation lake in North America, with the West Thumb Geyser Basin on its shore and the grand old Lake Yellowstone Hotel looking out over the water. A good place to slow down for an afternoon.

From the Guild

Get to the big-name stops early or late. The park itself advises arriving at an entrance before 7 a.m. or after noon in summer and avoiding the main attractions between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. We would add: eat a picnic lunch at a quiet pullout during those middle hours and save Old Faithful and Grand Prismatic for the long summer evening light.

When to visit Yellowstone

Summer (June to August) is when everything is open: all roads, all lodges and every campground. It is also when the park is busiest, and July and August bring afternoon thunderstorms and, some years, wildfire smoke. Average highs at Mammoth run in the 70s and low 80s; nights are cool everywhere, and frost can come any month at the higher elevations.

Late spring and fall are our favorite times for older travelers. From late May the roads are usually all open by Memorial Day weekend, the baby bison are out, and the waterfalls are full. September brings golden aspens, the elk rut and fewer people, though campgrounds begin closing during the month and snow is possible. Dunraven Pass and the Beartooth Highway typically close in mid-October, and most roads close for the season at the end of October.

Winter is a quiet, beautiful and serious season. From early November to mid-April most roads are closed to cars. You can drive the northern road from Gardiner to Cooke City all winter, stay at the Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel, or ride a guided snowcoach to the Old Faithful Snow Lodge once oversnow travel begins in mid-December. Temperatures below zero are normal, so bring real winter gear.

How to get to Yellowstone National Park

By car

Yellowstone has five entrances. The West Entrance at West Yellowstone, Montana, is the busiest and the quickest to the geyser basins. The North Entrance at Gardiner, Montana, is the only one open to cars all year. The East Entrance is reached from Cody, Wyoming, over Sylvan Pass; the South Entrance comes up from Jackson through Grand Teton National Park; and the Northeast Entrance at Cooke City is reached by the Beartooth Highway or the Chief Joseph Scenic Byway.

From Arizona, most travelers drive north through Utah on I-15 to Idaho Falls and then to West Yellowstone, or come up through Jackson and Grand Teton. The Beartooth Highway from Red Lodge is one of the great mountain drives in America, but it is high, steep and open only from about Memorial Day to mid-October.

By air / shuttle

The closest year-round airports are Bozeman, Jackson and Cody, with Billings and Idaho Falls a little farther out. West Yellowstone's small airport has seasonal flights from Salt Lake City from early May to mid-October.

There is no public transportation inside Yellowstone and no train service nearby. Private shuttles run from Bozeman to West Yellowstone all year and from Bozeman to Gardiner in summer and winter, and commercial tours operate from all the gateway towns. For most visitors a rental car is still the simplest way to see the park.

Lodges inside the park

All of Yellowstone's lodges are run by Yellowstone National Park Lodges, the park's authorized concessioner, and are booked through that company by phone or on its website. There are nine lodging areas: the Old Faithful Inn, the 1904 log landmark beside the geyser; the Old Faithful Snow Lodge & Cabins and Old Faithful Lodge Cabins nearby; the Lake Yellowstone Hotel & Cabins and the simpler Lake Lodge Cabins; Canyon Lodge & Cabins; Grant Village Lodge; Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel & Cabins; and the rustic Roosevelt Lodge Cabins.

The park's posted dates for 2027 open the Old Faithful Inn on April 30, Canyon Lodge and the Lake Yellowstone Hotel on May 14, and Roosevelt Lodge Cabins from June 4 to September 5. Only two lodges run in winter: the Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel, which you can drive to, and the Old Faithful Snow Lodge, open December 16 to March 6 this winter and reached only by snowcoach or snowmobile.

The park warns that its lodges fill quickly, and summer rooms at the Old Faithful Inn and the Lake Hotel often go a year ahead. Book as soon as you know your dates. If you need an elevator or a ground-floor room, ask when you book: the park notes the Lake Yellowstone Hotel has elevator access to select rooms, while Grant Village Lodge has none.

RV travel & camping at Yellowstone

Eleven campgrounds, one with full hookups, and all of them booked through Recreation.gov from 2027.

Fishing Bridge RV Park near Yellowstone Lake is the only campground in the park with full hookups. It has 310 sites, takes rigs up to 95 feet (RV plus tow vehicle), has showers and a dump station, and is scheduled for May 14 to October 16 in 2027. It is also the most expensive at $94 a night plus tax, and it books up early.

The big no-hookup campgrounds take large rigs too: Bridge Bay (431 sites) and Madison (276 sites) allow up to 60 feet, Grant Village (429 sites) up to 50 feet, and Canyon (272 sites) up to 40 feet. All four have flush toilets, dump stations and allow generators; Canyon and Grant Village have showers. The park's 2027 dates are Bridge Bay May 7 to September 12, Madison May 7 to October 16, Canyon May 28 to September 18 and Grant Village June 18 to September 25.

The small campgrounds are for shorter rigs: Lewis Lake 25 feet, Indian Creek 35 feet, and Slough Creek and Tower Fall 30 feet (the park warns of a hairpin curve into Tower Fall). Mammoth Campground is open all year, takes 45 feet from May to mid-October but only 30 feet in winter, and is reservation-only from April 15 to October 15 and first-come, first-served the rest of the year. Pebble Creek is closed for flood recovery.

Starting with the 2027 season, every campground is reserved on Recreation.gov, with dates opening on a rolling basis six months ahead. Camping or overnight parking anywhere outside a campground is not allowed. If you want full hookups and could not get Fishing Bridge, there are private RV parks in West Yellowstone, Gardiner and Cody.

Fishing in Yellowstone

Yellowstone has its own fishing rules. Anglers 16 and older need a Yellowstone National Park fishing permit, which you can buy online through Recreation.gov (print or download it, since cell service is poor) or on paper locally. A state licence is not needed inside the park, with one exception: on the Yellowstone River downstream of its meeting with the Gardner River at Gardiner, only a Montana licence is required. Children 15 and under may fish without a permit when supervised by a permitted adult, or get a free permit signed by an adult.

Most waters open the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend and close October 31, with some exceptions: the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon rivers open earlier, and Heart Lake and parts of the Yellowstone River not until July 1. Fishing is sunrise to sunset only. Use lead-free artificial lures or flies with barbless hooks, one rod and no bait. All native fish, including cutthroat trout, grayling and mountain whitefish, must be released, and every lake trout caught in Yellowstone Lake must be killed. Felt-soled boots are banned, and every boat, float tube and paddle board needs an inspection and a permit.

Hot summers can bring temporary closures on the Firehole, Madison and Gibbon to protect the fish, so check before you go. For more on the region's water, see our fly fishing library, Wyoming lake fishing and Montana, Idaho and Utah lake fishing.

Accessible travel at Yellowstone

What is available for visitors with limited mobility, and the passes that help.

Much of Yellowstone's best scenery is seen from the car or from wide boardwalks, and several overlooks at the canyon and the geyser basins are close to parking. The park cautions that not every feature marked accessible meets federal standards, so its Accessibility page and the official NPS app are the best guide to each boardwalk and trail. Two trails the park lists as accessible are the Lone Star Geyser Trail near Old Faithful and the Natural Bridge Trail near Bridge Bay.

The visitor centers at Canyon, Old Faithful and Grant show captioned films, Canyon and Old Faithful have assistive listening devices and audio-described films, and the Albright Visitor Center at Mammoth has an induction loop at the desk and an audio-described tour. The park map and guide comes in large print, Braille and audio. Service dogs are welcome on leash, but emotional-support and companion animals are not allowed on boardwalks, trails or in buildings.

For lodging, tell Yellowstone National Park Lodges exactly what you need when you book; the Lake Yellowstone Hotel has elevator access to select rooms, and Grant Village Lodge has no elevator.

Park passes that save you money. U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability can get the free lifetime Access Pass. Visitors 62 and over can buy the Senior Pass ($80 for life, or $20 a year). Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass. Each covers entrance fees at every national park.

Traveling on dialysis? There is no dialysis clinic inside the park; the nearest are in Bozeman, Montana, about 90 miles north of West Yellowstone, and in Cody, Wyoming, east of the park. Book your treatment at a clinic there several weeks before you go; the large dialysis companies run travel desks that will arrange it with your home unit.

Yellowstone National Park FAQ

Do I need a reservation to enter Yellowstone?

No. Yellowstone does not use timed entry or vehicle reservations. You only need to pay the entrance fee or show a valid pass. Lodges and campgrounds do need reservations.

How much is the Yellowstone entrance fee?

$35 per private vehicle for seven days, $30 per motorcycle, and $20 per person on foot or bicycle for visitors 16 and older. A Yellowstone annual pass is $70.

When do Yellowstone's roads open?

The park plows and opens roads in stages each spring, usually starting in mid-April on the west side. Most roads are open by Memorial Day weekend, weather permitting. Dunraven Pass and the Beartooth Highway open last.

Can you drive into Yellowstone in winter?

Only on the northern road from the North Entrance at Gardiner through Mammoth and the Lamar Valley to the Northeast Entrance at Cooke City. The rest of the park is reached by guided snowcoach or snowmobile from mid-December.

Which Yellowstone lodges are open in winter?

The Mammoth Hot Springs Hotel, which you can drive to, and the Old Faithful Snow Lodge, reached only by snowcoach or snowmobile from mid-December to early March.

Are there RV hookups in Yellowstone?

Only at Fishing Bridge RV Park near Yellowstone Lake, which has full hookups and takes rigs up to 95 feet. The other campgrounds have no hookups, though several have dump stations.

How do I book a campground in Yellowstone?

From the 2027 season, all Yellowstone campgrounds are reserved on Recreation.gov, with dates opening on a rolling basis six months ahead. Mammoth Campground is first-come, first-served in the off-season.

How many days do I need in Yellowstone?

At least three full days to see the main areas without rushing, ideally staying in two different places so you are not driving the whole loop every day.

Plan your Yellowstone trip

Book your rooms first, because the lodges and gateway towns fill months ahead for summer, then build your days around the roads that are open.
