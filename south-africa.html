Why visit South Africa

Few countries pack in so much — world-class wildlife, dramatic coasts, great wine and vibrant cities, all with excellent value for visitors.

The Big Five & the bush

Lion, leopard, elephant, rhino and buffalo in Kruger and the private reserves — some of the finest, most accessible safaris anywhere, from luxury lodges to self-drive parks.

Cape Town & Table Mountain

A city cradled between mountain and sea — the cableway, Cape Point, colourful Bo-Kaap, the V&A Waterfront and beaches at Camps Bay and Clifton.

The Winelands

Stellenbosch, Franschhoek and Paarl — Cape Dutch estates among the oak trees, world-class wines and some of the country's best restaurants, an hour from the city.

Coast & the Garden Route

The warm Indian Ocean at Durban, the forests, lagoons and beaches of the Garden Route, and whale-watching at Hermanus in season.

How the country divides up

Cape Town & the Western Cape

For many travellers the trip begins in Cape Town, and it should. Table Mountain rises straight above the city, the cableway or a hike delivers the view, and the Cape Peninsula runs south past Boulders' penguins to Cape Point. An hour inland, the Winelands of Stellenbosch and Franschhoek pour some of the world's finest wines among Cape Dutch estates.

Johannesburg, Pretoria & Kruger

Johannesburg is the country's biggest, most energetic city and its main air gateway, with the moving Apartheid Museum and nearby Soweto. Neighbouring Pretoria is greener and statelier, the seat of government. Both are the springboard to Kruger National Park and the private reserves — the classic Big Five safari.

Durban & the KwaZulu-Natal coast

Durban is South Africa's warm-water playground — the Golden Mile of surf beaches, the uShaka marine park, a strong Indian-South-African food culture, and glossy Umhlanga just up the coast. Inland rise the Drakensberg mountains and the historic Zulu battlefields.

The Eastern Cape & Garden Route

Port Elizabeth, now also called Gqeberha, anchors Nelson Mandela Bay with long city beaches and malaria-free game reserves close by. West of it, the Garden Route threads forests, lagoons and coastal towns like Knysna and Plettenberg Bay toward the Cape.

Top things to do in South Africa

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Safari in Kruger

Track the Big Five across the country's flagship park and the luxury private reserves alongside it — dawn and dusk game drives are the heart of any South African trip.

Table Mountain & the Cape

Ride the rotating cableway or hike Platteklip Gorge, then drive the Peninsula to Boulders' penguins and Cape Point's cliffs.

The Cape Winelands

Tastings among the oaks at Stellenbosch, Franschhoek and Paarl, paired with some of South Africa's finest farm-to-table dining.

Robben Island

The ferry from the V&A Waterfront to the island where Nelson Mandela was imprisoned — often led by former political prisoners.

The Garden Route

A classic self-drive from Mossel Bay to Storms River — forests, lagoons, Knysna's heads and clifftop whale-watching in season.

Durban's beaches

Warm Indian Ocean swimming on the Golden Mile, the uShaka marine park, and curry and bunny chow in South Africa's most tropical city.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Remember the seasons are reversed south of the equator. May to September — the dry winter — is prime safari season, with sparse bush and animals gathering at water.

November to March is warm summer, best for Cape Town, the beaches and the Garden Route, though the Cape can be windy. Spring and autumn are superb all-rounders.

See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit South Africa for weather, safari season, crowds and prices.

Getting there

O.R. Tambo International (JNB) in Johannesburg is the main long-haul gateway, with direct flights from across Europe, the Gulf, the US and beyond. Cape Town International (CPT) is the other major entry point.

Frequent, affordable domestic flights link Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban and Port Elizabeth (Gqeberha), saving long drives across a big country.

The currency is the South African rand. Cards work almost everywhere; carry some cash for markets and tips. Many visitors enter visa-free for up to 90 days — confirm the current rules with the official South African authority before booking.

South Africa travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit South Africa?

It depends on what you want, and remember the seasons are reversed south of the equator. For a Big Five safari in Kruger and the bushveld, the cool, dry winter of May to September is best, when thin vegetation and thirsty animals gathering at waterholes make wildlife easy to spot. For Cape Town, the Winelands and the Garden Route, the warm, dry summer of November to March brings long beach days and green landscapes. Spring (September to November) and autumn (March to May) are excellent all-rounders across most of the country.

What currency is used in South Africa?

The South African rand (ZAR, symbol R). Cards are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, shops and fuel stations across the cities and tourist areas, and ATMs are common. Carry some cash for markets, craft stalls, tips and rural areas. When a card machine or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, decline and pay in rand to avoid a poor built-in exchange rate.

Do I need a visa to visit South Africa?

Many visitors, including US, UK, Canadian and EU citizens, can enter South Africa visa-free for tourism for up to 90 days, receiving a visitor's stamp on arrival. Your passport should be valid for at least 30 days beyond your departure with two or more blank pages. Rules change and there are specific requirements for travelling with children, so always confirm the current entry rules with the official South African authority before you travel.

How many days do you need in South Africa?

For a first trip, ten to fourteen days is ideal: three or four days in Cape Town for Table Mountain, the Cape Peninsula and the Winelands, a few days on safari in or near Kruger, and time to add the Garden Route or the Durban coast. With a week you can pair Cape Town with a short safari and save the rest for next time. South Africa is large, so internal flights between Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban save a great deal of driving.

Is South Africa safe for travellers?

Millions of visitors travel South Africa happily every year, and the main tourist areas — Cape Town, the Winelands, the Garden Route, the safari lodges and the coastal resorts — are well set up for tourism. That said, South Africa has higher crime rates than many visitors are used to, so take sensible precautions: use trusted transport or ride-hailing at night, avoid displaying valuables, be cautious in quiet or unfamiliar city areas after dark, and follow local advice. Check your government's current travel advisory before you go.
