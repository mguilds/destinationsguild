Best Time to Visit Fiji

The Best Time to Visit Fiji

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Fiji has two seasons, and the honest version is that one of them is a great deal more reliable than the other. The dry season from May to October is cooler, breezier and far less wet, and it is when most visitors come. The wet season from November to April is hotter, greener, cheaper and emptier — and it is also the tropical cyclone season, which is the one thing about Fijian timing that is worth taking seriously rather than shrugging at. There is a second rule that matters almost as much as the calendar: the west of Viti Levu is dry and the southeast is not, in every month of the year.

Two Seasons, and Which Side of the Island

What each half of the Fijian year is actually like.

The dry season — May to October

The southeast trade winds settle in, humidity drops, the rain thins out and the days are warm rather than hot. This is Fiji at its most straightforward: good sailing, the best underwater visibility of the year as the river run-off drops away, and comfortable nights. It is also when the resorts are fullest and dearest, particularly through the Australian and New Zealand school holidays in late June and July.

The trade winds are the one catch. They are pleasant on a beach and less so on a small boat, and an exposed windward beach can be breezy enough to want a sheltered corner.

The wet season — November to April

Hotter, much more humid, and wetter — though rarely wet all day. The usual shape is a bright morning, a heavy afternoon downpour and a clear evening, and the islands are at their greenest. The sea is at its warmest, the rates are at their lowest, and you will have a good deal more of the place to yourself.

This is also the cyclone season, which has its own section below and is the reason a wet-season trip wants flexible bookings and proper travel insurance rather than optimism.

The west is dry, the southeast is not

Viti Levu has a mountain spine down the middle, and it divides the island’s weather completely. Nadi, Denarau, the Coral Coast and the islands offshore sit in the rain shadow on the dry, sunny western side. Suva and the southeast are on the windward side and get far more rain, in every month.

This is why almost every resort in Fiji is on the west and north, and it is the single most useful piece of Fijian geography a visitor can carry. If a forecast for Fiji looks bad, check which coast it is for.

What the water is doing

The sea is warm all year and never the reason to pick a month. Underwater visibility is generally at its best in the dry season, when less fresh water and sediment is coming off the land; the wet months are warmer but murkier after heavy rain.

Fiji’s southern reef passes pick up the big Southern Ocean swells through the middle of the year, which is why the serious surf season runs roughly April to October. Manta ray season in the Yasawa channels is also usually a dry-season affair — check with the operator for the year you are travelling rather than assuming.

Month by Month

What to expect, and what it costs you, through the Fijian year.

January

Hot, humid and the wettest stretch of the year, and inside the peak of the cyclone season. Everything is green, the sea is warm, the resorts are quiet after the New Year rush and the rates are among the lowest you will see. A gamble, and priced like one.

February

The other peak cyclone month, and much like January — hot, heavy afternoon rain, brilliant countryside, low prices and few other visitors. The month the Guild is least likely to recommend to someone with fixed dates and no insurance.

March

Still wet and still inside the season, but the back of it. Late March starts to feel like the turn, particularly on the west coast. Good value, and better odds than February.

April

The transition month and an underrated one. The rain is easing, the humidity is dropping, the islands are still green from the wet season and the crowds have not arrived. The cyclone season officially runs to the end of the month, so the risk has not gone, but it is falling.

May

The first dry-season month and one of the best of the year. Warm, much less humid, reliably sunny on the west coast, and ahead of the winter crowds and prices. If you want the dry season without the peak, this is the month to look at first.

June

Comfortably warm, dry and breezy. Excellent conditions and the start of the busy season — the back half of the month brings the first wave of Australian and New Zealand school holidays, and rates climb with it.

July

The coolest and one of the driest months, which in Fiji means pleasant rather than cold. It is also the busiest and most expensive, with school holidays across the Tasman. Book early or pick a different month.

August

Dry, sunny and breezy, with the trade winds at their steadiest. Peak conditions, easing crowds after the school holidays end, and a strong month for diving as visibility is at its best.

September

Many people’s favourite: dry, warm, clear water, and the winds starting to soften. A short school-holiday bump late in the month, but otherwise one of the best combinations of weather and price in the year.

October

The last dependable dry month and a very good one — warming up, still dry, and quieter before the wet season starts. Fiji Day on the tenth marks independence and is a national holiday, so expect celebrations and some closures.

November

The turn. Humidity climbs, the first serious afternoon rain returns, and the cyclone season formally begins. The first half of the month often still feels like the dry season; the second half rarely does. Prices start to fall.

December

Hot, humid and wet, and inside the cyclone season — but also the one wet-season month that is busy and expensive, because of Christmas and the southern summer holidays. The worst value in the Fijian year unless the dates are fixed for you.

The Cyclone Season, Properly Explained

The part of Fijian timing that is worth reading twice.

When it runs, and when it peaks

Fiji’s tropical cyclone season runs from November to April, and the peak is normally January and February. The Fiji Meteorological Service — which runs RSMC Nadi, the regional cyclone centre for the whole southwest Pacific, and which sits at the airport most visitors fly into — notes that storms have occasionally formed in October and May as well, so the dates are a guide and not a fence.

For the 2026–27 season, RSMC Nadi’s own published outlook forecasts above-average cyclone activity across its area of responsibility, which it links to a strong El Niño. That is this season’s outlook and not a permanent fact: check met.gov.fj close to your own dates rather than trusting any range printed months earlier.

What it means in practice

It does not mean a wet-season trip is a bad idea. Most wet-season holidays in Fiji pass without a named storm anywhere near them, and the trade-off — green islands, warm sea, low rates, few people — is a real one.

It does mean three things. Take travel insurance that actually covers weather disruption, and read what it says about named storms. Prefer bookings you can move. And if you are travelling between November and April, look at the forecast before you leave rather than after you land.

If a storm is coming while you are there

Fijian resorts and hotels have done this many times and have proper procedures; follow the staff, not the internet. Warnings and tracks come from the Fiji Meteorological Service, which is the official source for the region and the one local radio and television carry.

The practical advice is unglamorous and works: keep your phone charged, fill a water bottle, keep your passport and documents together and dry, and do not try to travel between islands on the day of a warning.

Best Time to Visit Fiji FAQ

The questions travellers ask most when they are choosing when to go.

What is the best month to visit Fiji?

September, for most people. It sits in the dry season, so the weather on the west coast and the islands is reliable, the water is clear, the trade winds have begun to soften, and the Australian and New Zealand school holidays that fill July have passed. May is the other strong answer — the first dry month, warm and quiet, and ahead of the winter prices.

When is the cyclone season in Fiji?

November to April, with the peak normally in January and February. The Fiji Meteorological Service, which runs RSMC Nadi — the regional cyclone centre at Nadi airport — notes that storms occasionally form in October and May too. For the 2026–27 season its published outlook forecasts above-average activity. Check met.gov.fj close to your own dates.

Is it worth going to Fiji in the wet season?

Often, yes. The wet season is hotter and wetter but rarely wet all day: the usual pattern is a bright morning, a heavy afternoon downpour and a clear evening. The islands are green, the sea is warm, the resorts are quiet and the rates are the lowest of the year. The trade is the cyclone risk, so take insurance that covers weather disruption and prefer bookings you can move.

Which part of Fiji has the best weather?

The west and north. Viti Levu's central mountains cast a rain shadow, so Nadi, Denarau, the Coral Coast and the Mamanuca and Yasawa islands offshore are markedly drier and sunnier than Suva and the southeast, in every month of the year. That is why almost all of the country's resorts are on that side of the island.

When is the cheapest time to go to Fiji?

January to March, and late April. Those are wet-season months outside the Christmas and southern-summer rush, so rates and airfares are at their lowest and the resorts are at their emptiest. December is the exception — it is wet season but priced like peak season, because of the holidays.

How far ahead should you book?

For June through September, and for Christmas, as far ahead as you reasonably can — those are the months that fill, particularly in the Australian and New Zealand school holidays. For the wet season, much less so; late availability is normal and often the point.
