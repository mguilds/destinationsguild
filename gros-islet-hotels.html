Gros Islet & Rodney Bay

Where to Stay in Gros Islet & Rodney Bay

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

The north of Saint Lucia is where most first visits are spent, and with reason. Rodney Bay has the best stretch of pale sand on the island at Reduit Beach, a marina full of yachts, more restaurants within walking distance than anywhere else in the country, and Pigeon Island National Landmark at the end of a causeway five minutes up the road. Gros Islet, the old fishing village next to it, supplies the Friday night street party that the whole north turns out for. Above both, Cap Estate climbs into quieter hills with villas and views. It is the easy end of the island: flat, walkable, well supplied and twenty-five minutes from Castries.

The areas, and which one suits you

Four distinct neighbourhoods sit within a couple of miles of each other here.

Reduit Beach and Rodney Bay Village

The centre of gravity: a long, calm, pale beach with hotels directly behind it, and a village of restaurants, bars, a mall and a supermarket a few minutes’ walk inland. If you want to be able to leave the car behind and choose dinner on foot, stay here. It is also the busiest and noisiest part, particularly at weekends.

The marina side

Across the main road from the beach, around Rodney Bay Marina: quieter water, chandleries, a boatyard, and restaurants looking over the pontoons. No beach on the doorstep, but a five-minute walk to one, and the best place to be if you sail or want the boat-watching evenings.

Gros Islet village

The working village north of the bay, with fishing boats, small guesthouses and local food at a fraction of resort prices. Friday night the main street closes for the street party — grills, speakers, dancing until late — which is wonderful if you want it and loud if you do not. Choose a room a street or two back if you plan to sleep.

Cap Estate and the north point

The hills at the top of the island: villas, apartments and a couple of larger properties, cooler air, long views to Martinique, and a golf course. You will need a car or a taxi for everything, which is the price of the quiet and the view.

Pigeon Island and the causeway

At the end of the causeway sits the national landmark — the ruins of Fort Rodney on a hill with a twenty-minute climb, two small beaches and the island’s best cheap view. A handful of places to stay line the causeway itself, with calm water on one side and the Atlantic on the other.

Getting about

Minibuses run constantly down the main road to Castries for very little, and taxi fares are set by zone — agree the figure first. Reduit Beach to Castries is about twenty-five minutes, to Soufrière an hour and a half, and to the international airport at Vieux Fort about ninety minutes.

What your money is worth in Rodney Bay
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Saint Lucia uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged at EC$2.70 to the US dollar, so EC$27 is about ten US dollars. This is the one part of the island where you have a real choice of price: resort restaurants on the beach charge resort prices, while the roti shops, bakeries and bars a few streets inland and in Gros Islet village charge what locals pay, and the Friday street party is the cheapest good dinner in the country. There is a large supermarket in Rodney Bay Village, which makes self-catering genuinely worthwhile here. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills — ask what is included when you book.

Bay Gardens Resorts

Rodney Bay VillageFour propertiesLocally run

Bay Gardens is a local group whose own website places it “in the heart of Rodney Bay Village” by the marina, and describes four distinct properties under one name, with on-site bars and restaurants between them. Being set in the village rather than on a private headland is the point of it: the beach, the mall, the supermarket and the restaurants are all within a short walk, and you are not dependent on a shuttle. The group does not publish a street address or a room count on its home page, and we have not repeated figures it does not state.

Travelling in your later years

This is the part of Saint Lucia we would send anyone who would rather not climb.

Rodney Bay is flat. The beach has an easy, shallow entry, the pavement between the hotels and the restaurants is level, there is a supermarket and a pharmacy within reach, and you can have a good week here without ever getting into a car. That is unusual on this island and it matters. Two things to ask before booking: whether the hotel is actually on the beach or across the road from it, since “Rodney Bay” covers both, and whether there is a lift, because several of the smaller properties are two or three storeys without one. If you are arriving at Hewanorra, the ninety-minute transfer north is the hardest part of the trip - consider breaking it with a first night in the south, or a regional flight into Castries.

Questions about staying in Gros Islet

Should I stay in Rodney Bay or Gros Islet village?

Rodney Bay for the beach, the restaurants and the comfort; Gros Islet village for lower prices, local food and the Friday street party on your doorstep. They are a ten-minute walk apart, so the choice is about what you want outside your door at nine in the evening rather than about what you can reach. Light sleepers should not take a room on the main street in Gros Islet on a Friday.

Is Reduit Beach the best beach in Saint Lucia?

It is the best long, calm, pale-sand beach, and the easiest to use: gentle entry, plenty of shade at the ends, loungers, water sports and somewhere to buy lunch. The dramatic beaches are in the south-west under the Pitons, where the sand is dark volcanic grey and the setting is extraordinary. If you want a beach to spend whole days on, Reduit; if you want a beach to photograph, Anse Chastanet.

What is the Friday night street party at Gros Islet?

The main street of the village closes, grills and speakers come out, and most of the north of the island eats, drinks and dances in the road until late. It has been going for decades and it is a genuine local night out that visitors are welcome at rather than a staged event. Go early for the food, keep your valuables close as you would in any crowd, and take a taxi back.

Is Pigeon Island worth the entry fee?

Yes, for the climb. It is a national landmark joined to the mainland by a causeway, with the ruins of Fort Rodney on a hill, a twenty-minute walk to the top and a view north over the sea to Martinique on a clear day. There are two small beaches, a lawn for a picnic and some interpretation of the military history. Go in the morning before the heat.

Can I manage without a car in the north?

Easily, and most people do. Minibuses run the main road to Castries all day for pocket change, taxis are plentiful with set zone fares, and within Rodney Bay everything is walkable. Hire a car for a day or two if you want to drive down to Soufriere at your own pace, and bear in mind you will need a temporary local driving permit, which the hire company arranges.

Is the north a good base for seeing the whole island?

For the north and the middle, yes. Soufriere and the Pitons are an hour and a half each way by road from here, which makes for a long day out - many people prefer to go by boat, which is calmer and more scenic, or to move south for two or three nights. If the Pitons are the reason you are coming, do not try to see them from a deck chair in Rodney Bay.
