Where to Stay in Lumbini

Where to Stay in Lumbini — The Monastic Zone & Town

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Lumbini is where the Buddha was born, and it is unlike anywhere else in Nepal: a flat, hot, quiet plain in the Terai laid out as a four-kilometre park, with the sacred garden and the Maya Devi Temple at one end and, along a canal running north, a monastic zone where some two dozen countries have each built a monastery in their own tradition. Stay near the garden to walk it early, in Lumbini Bazaar for the cheaper rooms and the restaurants, or in Bhairahawa if you are flying or crossing the border.

Choosing Your Area in Lumbini

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

Beside the sacred garden and the monastic zone

A handful of hotels sit close to the park entrances, and a few of the monasteries themselves keep simple guest rooms for pilgrims and visitors. This is the right choice if the point of your visit is the site: you can be at the Maya Devi Temple for the opening, walk the monastic zone in the cool of the morning, and come back in the late afternoon when the light is long and the day groups have gone.

Lumbini Bazaar

The small town just outside the park, a few minutes away, with most of the mid-range and budget rooms, places to eat and somewhere to hire a bicycle or a rickshaw for the day. Less atmospheric than sleeping by the garden, and considerably better supplied. Most independent travellers end up here and are perfectly happy.

Bhairahawa (Siddharthanagar), toward the border

The nearest real town, about 20 kilometres east, with the largest hotels in the area, the border crossing to India at Sunauli, and Gautam Buddha International Airport. Stay here if you are arriving or leaving by air or crossing into India, and accept a half-hour each way to the site. It is a working town rather than a destination.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Lumbini trip goes.

How the site is laid out, and how long it takes

The park is long and the distances are real: it is roughly four kilometres from the sacred garden at the southern end to the Peace Pagoda at the northern end, with the monasteries ranged along a central canal in two groups, Theravada on one side and Mahayana and Vajrayana on the other. Walking the whole thing in the heat is a serious undertaking. Hire a bicycle, an e-rickshaw or a cycle rickshaw for the day — it is cheap, and it is what makes a full day here pleasant rather than exhausting.

The heat, and the right months

This is the Terai, not the hills. From April to June the plain is very hot, and in the monsoon months it is humid and wet. October to March is the comfortable season, and December and January mornings can be genuinely cold and foggy, which occasionally delays flights into Bhairahawa. Whenever you come, do the walking early and late and sit out the middle of the day.

Getting there

Lumbini is in the far south-west of the country, a long way from both Kathmandu and Pokhara — most of a day by road from either. Gautam Buddha International Airport at Bhairahawa opened in 2022 to serve pilgrims from the Buddhist world, but regular international service has been thin since, so check what is actually flying rather than assuming a connection. Many visitors reach Lumbini overland from India through the Sunauli border, which is close by.

Lumbini Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Lumbini.

Where is the best place to stay in Lumbini?

Close to the sacred garden if you want the site early and late, which is when it is at its best. In Lumbini Bazaar for more choice, cheaper rooms and places to eat. In Bhairahawa only if you are flying in or out or crossing to India, since it is half an hour from the site.

How many days do you need in Lumbini?

One full day sees the sacred garden, the Maya Devi Temple, the Ashoka pillar and a good number of the monasteries. Two nights lets you do that without rushing and gives you an early morning and a late afternoon at the garden, which are the hours that justify the journey. It is a long way from anywhere else, so a one-night stop can feel like all travel and no visit.

Can you stay inside the monastic zone?

Some of the monasteries maintain simple guest accommodation, generally for pilgrims and generally basic — shared facilities, early hours, and in some cases an expectation that you join the daily routine. Arrangements vary from one monastery to another and are not usually bookable online, so enquire directly. Most visitors stay in a hotel nearby instead.

Is Lumbini worth the journey?

For anyone with an interest in Buddhism, yes, without qualification. For a general traveller it is a long detour to a flat, hot plain, and honesty requires saying so. What makes it is the monastic zone: two dozen countries each building a monastery in its own architecture along one quiet canal is a genuinely extraordinary thing to walk through, and nothing else in Nepal is remotely like it.
