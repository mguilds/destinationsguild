Where to Stay in Port of Spain

By the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Port of Spain is a working capital rather than a resort town, and that shapes every choice on this page. There is no hotel beach and no hotel strip. What there is, is a tight western quarter — St Clair, Woodbrook, Newtown and the green ring of the Queen’s Park Savannah — where almost every hotel in Trinidad sits within a few minutes of the restaurants, the Carnival route and the city centre. East and north of that the city becomes residential and then, in some districts, somewhere visitors have no reason to be. Stay west, take taxis after dark, and Port of Spain is an enjoyable and extremely well-fed few days.

The areas

St Clair and Maraval Road

The embassy-and-old-houses quarter on the west side of the Savannah, and the quietest central address. Leafy, safe to walk by day, a few minutes from Woodbrook’s restaurants and from the Savannah itself. Most of the independent hotels are here or just off it.

Woodbrook

The eating and drinking district: Ariapita Avenue is where Port of Spain goes out, and it runs hard from Thursday to Saturday and all through the Carnival season. Wonderful if you want to be in it, loud if your window faces the street — ask for a room at the back.

The Queen’s Park Savannah

A huge open park ringed by a road, with the Magnificent Seven houses along one side, the botanic gardens and the president’s house at the top. The Carnival grandstand stands here. Walking the perimeter in the early morning is what the city does for exercise.

The waterfront and downtown

Independence Square, the Brian Lara Promenade, the ferry to Tobago and the cruise berth. Busy and businesslike by day, largely shut after dark. A handful of larger hotels sit on or near the water here, convenient if you are sailing or catching the ferry.

Invaders Bay and the western highway

Reclaimed land just west of downtown along the Audrey Jeffers Highway, with the newer chain hotels, the big shopping and the Hasely Crawford stadium. Easy driving and parking, less atmosphere, a ten-minute taxi from the restaurants.

Maraval and the hills

North of St Clair the road climbs towards the Northern Range. Cooler, greener, residential, a few guest houses, and the start of the drive over to Maracas Bay. You will want a car or a reliable taxi number.

What your money is worth in Port of Spain
US $1 = about 6.80 TT$
TT$ 100 = about US $14.70
Trinidad and Tobago uses the TT dollar, which has sat near TT$6.80 to the US dollar for years — treat that as a guide rather than a fixed rate, and check before you change money. Hotels, car hire and the better restaurants will quote and take US dollars; everywhere else wants local cash. Port of Spain is cheap to eat in if you eat the way the city does: doubles from a street stand cost small change, a roti is a few dollars, and a good sit-down dinner on Ariapita Avenue is still well under what the same meal costs on a resort island. Taxis are the real expense, because you will take more of them than you expect. Value Added Tax and often a service charge are added to hotel and restaurant bills — ask what is included when you book. Carnival week is a different market entirely: expect multiples of the normal rate and minimum-stay rules.

Kapok calls itself an independent boutique hotel and gives its address as 16–18 Cotton Hill Road, St Clair — the quiet side of the Savannah, a short walk from the park and a few minutes from Woodbrook. Its own site describes a 95-room hotel with two restaurants and seven event spaces: Tiki Village, which it says serves Asian cooking with a panoramic view from the top of the building, and KAVA’s, for brick-oven pizza, burgers, pasta and steaks with indoor and patio seating. The site states no adults-only or all-inclusive policy.

Travelling in your later years

Port of Spain is hot, and the western quarter is flat but the hills behind it are not. The walk around the Savannah is level and shaded in parts, and is the pleasantest exercise in the city if you go early. Most other walking you will not want to do in the middle of the day.

Do not walk after dark, and do not hail a car in the street. Ask your hotel to call a taxi and agree the fare before you set off — fares here are by route rather than by meter. The same taxi number for your whole stay is worth more than saving a few dollars.

Piarco airport is about forty minutes east of the city in light traffic and considerably more in the rush, so allow for it. If you are catching the Tobago ferry, the terminal is downtown by the cruise berth and the crossing takes a few hours; the twenty-minute flight is kinder on a bad back, though it books up around holidays.

During Carnival the roads in the city centre close, taxis become hard to find and the noise runs through the night. If you are coming for it and want to see it comfortably, ask about a seated grandstand ticket at the Savannah rather than standing on the route.

Nearby

Maracas Bay is about an hour over the Northern Range, on a road that is beautiful and unrelentingly bendy. Go for the view from the lookout, the beach and a bake and shark, and take the front seat if you are prone to car sickness.

The Caroni Bird Sanctuary is around half an hour south. Boats go into the mangroves in the late afternoon to watch the scarlet ibis come in to roost; book ahead and bring something warm for the water.

Chaguaramas, twenty minutes west, has the marinas, the boardwalk, zip lines and boats out to Gasparee Island and its caves — the easiest half-day out of the city.

Questions travellers ask

Which part of Port of Spain should I stay in?

The western quarter: St Clair, Woodbrook, Newtown, or the roads around the Queen’s Park Savannah. That is where the hotels, the restaurants and the Carnival route are, and it is the part of the city visitors are comfortable in. Invaders Bay on the western highway is the alternative if you want easy parking and newer rooms and do not mind a taxi to dinner.

Is there a beach in Port of Spain?

No. The city sits on the Gulf of Paria, which is sheltered, shallow and silty rather than swimmable, and no hotel here has a beach. The nearest proper sand is Maracas Bay, about an hour over the mountains on the north coast. If a beach holiday is what you want, give Trinidad two or three nights and spend the rest in Tobago.

How do I get around without a car?

Route taxis and maxi-taxis cover the city cheaply and are how most people move, but they take learning and are not comfortable with luggage. For visitors the practical answer is a booked private taxi, arranged through your hotel, with the fare agreed before you set off. Within St Clair and Woodbrook you can walk in daylight.

Is it safe?

Trinidad has a serious violent-crime problem, and it is concentrated in particular districts of greater Port of Spain that no visitor has a reason to enter. The western quarter in daylight is a normal, friendly city. Our advice is plain: stay west, use booked taxis after dark rather than walking, do not wear jewellery you would mind losing, and read your own government’s current travel advice before you travel.

What should I eat, and where?

Doubles from a street stand before nine in the morning, for small change. Roti at lunch. Ariapita Avenue in Woodbrook for dinner, where the range runs from Creole to Chinese to Indian to modern Caribbean within a few hundred yards. And bake and shark at Maracas on the day you go over the mountain.

When is the city at its busiest?

Carnival, by a distance — 8 and 9 February in 2027, with the fetes running for weeks before. Rooms sell out many months ahead and rates multiply. Christmas and the parang season are the other busy stretch. Outside those, Port of Spain is a business city and rooms are easier midweek than you might expect.
