Nanyuki — The Equator, Mount Kenya and the Laikipia Plains

Where to Stay in Nanyuki — Town, Foothills and Plains

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Nanyuki is the market town on the north-western shoulder of Mount Kenya, a few minutes from the equator, and it is the practical base for three quite different trips: climbing or walking on the mountain, visiting the Laikipia conservancies, and simply spending a few days at altitude in cool air with the mountain in the window when the cloud lifts.

It is also a working Kenyan town rather than a resort, which is much of its appeal and the reason to be clear-eyed about what a room here is and is not.

Nanyuki town — the practical base

In town you get ordinary hotels and guest houses, banks, a good market, outfitters who know the mountain, and somewhere to eat that is not your own hotel. For anyone organising a climb, buying supplies or making day trips, this is the sensible place to be. Do not expect a view of Mount Kenya from the room; expect to be able to get things done.

The Mount Kenya foothills — cool air and the view

Spread around the town, on the slopes towards the forest, are the lodges and country hotels with gardens, log fires and the mountain in front of them. This is where to stay if the point is the setting rather than the errands: the air is cold at night at this height, the bird life is remarkable, and walking starts at the gate. It is also the right choice for a night of acclimatisation before a climb.

Towards Timau and Naro Moru — the trailhead towns

Naro Moru to the south and Timau to the north-east are the small places at the start of two of the mountain routes. Staying at one of them shortens the first morning of a climb and puts you in farming country with wide views. There is very little else there, which for some readers is the attraction.

The Laikipia conservancies — a different kind of stay

The plains north and west of Nanyuki are private and community conservancies, and the places to sleep in them are lodges and tented camps reached by a long drive or a short flight. They are not hotels in the sense this page has been using, the prices are of a different order and most are booked as a package including game drives. We name no property here because we have not been able to confirm one against its own official information, and the Guild does not recommend a hotel it has not checked. If a conservancy stay is what you want, browse what is actually available and read the lodge's own site before you book.

Before you book

Government travel insurance is now required

Since 5 October 2026 most visitors staying under twelve months must hold Kenyan government travel health insurance, bought with the electronic travel authorisation or afterwards through the eCitizen insurance portal. A policy from home is not accepted. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements, still current at 21 November 2026.

Altitude, and the mountain

Nanyuki is at about 1,950 metres. Mount Kenya goes far higher and altitude sickness is a documented risk on it. Build in acclimatisation days and use a reputable operator; this is not a mountain to hurry.

Security in the county, stated plainly

No advice against travel to Laikipia. But it is named among counties with continuing security operations against banditry and intercommunal violence, and restrictions can appear at short notice in remote areas. Ask locally before driving into open country.

Conservancy and park fees change

Mount Kenya National Park and the private conservancies each charge their own entry fees, set by the Kenya Wildlife Service and by the conservancies. We do not print the amounts because they are revised and we will not quote a figure we cannot source. Confirm the current rate when you book.

It gets genuinely cold

At this height the nights are cold all year and the rooms are not always heated. Bring a fleece even if the rest of your trip is the coast.

An Ebola case was confirmed in October

Kenya's Ministry of Health confirmed the country's first imported case of Ebola on 6 October 2026 and public health measures remain in place. Check your own government's travel health advice before you fly.

Questions readers actually ask

Why stay in Nanyuki rather than in a conservancy?


Cost and flexibility. Nanyuki is a real town with ordinary hotels and guest houses at ordinary prices, and it is the base from which you can do day trips into the conservancies, walk in the Mount Kenya foothills and stock up before a climb. The conservancy lodges are a different kind of holiday and a different price. Many people do a night or two in town at each end and the lodge in the middle.

How high is Nanyuki and will I feel it?


The town sits at roughly 1,950 metres, a little under 6,400 feet, which is higher than Nairobi. Most people notice it only as slightly shorter breath on the first day. If you are going on to climb Mount Kenya that matters a great deal more, and a night or two at Nanyuki or Naro Moru before you start is time well spent.

Is Laikipia safe to travel in?


The Foreign Office does not advise against travel to Laikipia. It does name Laikipia among the counties where security operations continue because of banditry, cattle rustling and intercommunal violence, and it notes that movement restrictions can be introduced during those operations. The named hotspots are elsewhere - the Turkana-West Pokot border, the Kerio Valley and remote parts of Samburu and Baringo. Nanyuki town and the main conservancies are not those places, but take local advice before heading into remote country and follow any restrictions in force.

Can I see rhinos from Nanyuki?


The Laikipia conservancies are among the most important rhino country in Kenya and Ol Pejeta, close to Nanyuki, is well known for its rhino conservation work including the last surviving northern white rhinos. Entry and conservancy fees apply and are set by the conservancies rather than by us - check the current rate when you book, because they change.

Is the equator signpost worth stopping for?


It is a signpost and a row of souvenir stalls just outside town, and somebody will show you water going down a plughole. Stop for five minutes, take the photograph, buy something from whoever gave you the demonstration, and do not build the day around it.
