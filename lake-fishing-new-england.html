Lake Fishing in New England — Six Great Lakes of the North-East and the Towns Beside Them

Moosehead and Sebago in Maine, Winnipesaukee in New Hampshire, Lake Champlain from the Vermont shore, Quabbin Reservoir in Massachusetts and Candlewood Lake in Connecticut, with a note on Rhode Island’s Worden Pond: what swims in each, the rules, the licence a visitor needs, where to launch, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the six New England state fishing agencies, 18 November 2026

New England is cold-water country. Its big lakes hold landlocked salmon and lake trout — Mainers call lake trout “togue” — in deep, clear water, with bass, pike and perch in the shallows. The fishing year has two high points: the weeks after the ice goes out in spring, when salmon chase smelt near the surface, and the depths of winter, when whole villages of ice shacks appear on the bays. Each state writes its own rules, and they differ more than you might expect from one lake to the next.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, ice fishers, and anyone who wants a guide for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 18 November 2026 from the agency that sets it — the Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife, New Hampshire Fish and Game, Vermont Fish and Wildlife, MassWildlife and the Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation, Connecticut DEEP and the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management — and is dated. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The best months, in plain words

Two seasons matter most in New England: the weeks after ice-out, and the hard-water months of winter. Summer sends the salmon and lake trout deep.

Moosehead Lake — Maine’s biggest lake

The Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife calls Moosehead “Maine’s largest lake”: about 40 miles long, 74,890 acres, and 246 feet at its deepest, with Greenville at its south end.

Moosehead Lake, from Greenville to Rockwood

What swims there. Wild brook trout, landlocked salmon (stocked and wild) and wild lake trout — togue — are the fish the state manages this lake for.

How people fish it. Trolling for salmon and togue after the ice goes out, casting for brook trout near the shore and the river mouths, and ice fishing in winter. It is a big, cold lake that can turn rough fast: small boats should stay close to shore and watch the weather.

Keeping a fish. Moosehead has its own special law, stricter than Maine’s general rules. As the department lists it: brook trout 1 a day, at least 14 inches, and any brook trout from 18 to 22 inches goes back; salmon 1 a day, at least 18 inches; togue 5 a day with no minimum length, but only one over 18 inches. Ice fishing is open from 1 January to 30 April; salmon may not be kept until 15 February, salmon caught through the ice before then and in April must be released without being taken out of the water, and so must brook trout in April. Several bays and coves are closed to fishing from 1 January to 31 March, and smelts may not be taken.

Confirmed with the Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife, 18 November 2026 — Moosehead region page (size, depth, species, launches) and the special fishing laws for Moosehead Lake. The special laws are long and detailed: read the current Maine fishing law book or the department’s special-laws page for the exact closed areas before you fish.

Boat ramps. The department lists public trailer launches on Lily Bay Road in Greenville, on Route 6 in Greenville and in Rockwood, on Hardwood Valley Road in Spencer Bay Township, on the Seboomook Dam Road, on Ross Farm Road at Northeast Carry, and at Lily Bay State Park. Lily Bay State Park, nine miles north of Greenville, has two trailer launches, docks, a swimming beach and about ninety campsites, and is open all year.

Nearest towns: Greenville, at the foot of the lake, and Rockwood, halfway up the west shore where the Moose River comes in.

Sebago Lake — deep water half an hour from Portland

The Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife gives Sebago 28,771 acres and a deepest point of 316 feet. It is also the water supply for the Portland area, and that shapes where you may go.

Sebago Lake, from Naples and Raymond to Standish

What swims there. Landlocked salmon (stocked and wild), wild lake trout, and smallmouth and largemouth bass.

How people fish it. Trolling for salmon and lake trout from a boat, bass fishing along the rocky shores and coves in summer, and ice fishing for lake trout in winter.

Keeping a fish. Sebago’s special law, as the department lists it: salmon at least 16 inches; lake trout under 26 inches have no bag or length limit, and only one lake trout of 26 inches or more may be kept. Every trout (other than lake trout) and salmon caught through the ice must be released at once, and so must those caught in open water from 1 October to 31 March. From 1 October to 31 December, artificial lures only. Smelts may not be taken, and there are closed areas at White’s Bridge, most tributaries and the Songo River mouth.

The water-supply zone. The department’s entry also carries the Portland Water District’s rule for the lower bay at Standish: no trespassing within 3,000 feet of the water intakes, and no bodily contact with the water out to two miles from them. Fish from a boat there, not from the water.

Confirmed with the Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife, 18 November 2026 — Sebago region page (size, depth, species, launches) and the special fishing laws for Sebago Lake, including the water-supply restrictions. The Sebago salmon daily limit is not spelled out in the entry we read; Maine’s general limit is two. Check the current law book.

Boat ramps. The department lists the state launch on Route 302, the launch at Sebago Lake State Park (fee) and a launch on Route 35 in Standish (fee), plus private marinas. Sebago Lake State Park, in Casco and Naples, has a 250-site campground, a trailer launch and sandy beaches, and is open all year.

Nearest towns: Naples and Raymond to the north and east, Windham and Standish to the south.

Lake Winnipesaukee — New Hampshire’s big lake

New Hampshire Fish and Game counts Winnipesaukee among the large lakes it manages for landlocked salmon, and the state’s rules list it as a lake trout and salmon water.

Lake Winnipesaukee, from Meredith and Laconia to Wolfeboro and Alton

What swims there. Landlocked salmon and rainbow trout (the department stocks its large lakes with both), lake trout in the deep water, and smallmouth and largemouth bass, with pickerel, perch and other panfish in the coves.

How people fish it. Trolling for salmon and trout from 1 April, when the open-water season begins; bass fishing from a boat or the shore in summer; and ice fishing for lake trout and perch in winter.

Keeping a fish. New Hampshire’s rule for lake trout and salmon waters: from 1 April to 30 September, 2 salmon and trout a day in total; trout, salmon and lake trout must be at least 15 inches. From 1 January to 31 March the lake is open to ice fishing only, with 2 trout a day and 2 lake trout, and no more than 2 ice-fishing devices plus up to 6 for cusk. Landlocked salmon may never be taken through the ice. From 1 October to 31 December lake trout, brook trout, salmon and lake whitefish may not be taken and trolling is not allowed, and from 16 October to 31 December there is no fishing within 500 feet of several river and brook mouths and of the Main Street bridge in Wolfeboro.

Confirmed with New Hampshire Fish and Game, 18 November 2026 — New Hampshire administrative rules Fis 406.04 (lake trout and salmon waters, effective 22 October 2025) and Fis 403; the department’s newsroom notice of 20 March 2026 on the 1 April salmon season; its large-lake fisheries page.

Boat ramps. Fish and Game runs a boat access site at Downing’s Landing in Alton. Marinas in Meredith, Wolfeboro and around the lake launch boats too; ask before you tow.

Nearest towns: Meredith, Laconia (with Weirs Beach), Wolfeboro and Alton.

Lake Champlain — the Vermont shore

The Lake Champlain Basin Program gives the lake 120 miles of length, 12 miles at its widest, about 455 square miles of water and 400 feet at its deepest, with more than 70 islands. Vermont Fish and Wildlife counts 81 species of fish in it.

Lake Champlain, from Burlington and the Islands to St. Albans and Vergennes

What swims there. Smallmouth and largemouth bass, northern pike, walleye, landlocked salmon, lake trout, steelhead and brown trout, yellow perch and panfish, with carp, drum and bowfin.

How people fish it. Bass and pike in the bays, trolling the deep water for salmon and lake trout, walleye in spring, and perch through the ice in the sheltered bays in winter.

Keeping a fish (Vermont waters). Lake trout and salmon have no closed season on the lake: at least 15 inches, 3 lake trout and 2 salmon a day. Northern pike at least 20 inches, 5 a day. Walleye from the first Saturday in May to 15 March, at least 18 inches, 3 a day. Yellow perch: no limit. Ice anglers may fish up to 15 lines on Lake Champlain. For bass, check the current Vermont fishing guide.

Confirmed with Vermont Fish and Wildlife, 18 November 2026 — the department’s fish management rule (§122) and its Lake Champlain and Grand Isle State Park pages; lake size from the Lake Champlain Basin Program. Licence reciprocity confirmed with Vermont Fish and Wildlife and the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation.

One licence or two? On most of the lake either a Vermont or a New York licence will do; a few bays are one state’s only. See the questions at the end of this page, and our New York lake fishing guide for the New York shore.

Boat ramps. Vermont’s fishing access areas on the lake include Malletts Bay and Colchester Point in Colchester, St. Albans Bay, Shelburne Bay, Keeler Bay in South Hero, Converse Bay in Charlotte and Chipmans Point in Orwell. The department lists concrete ramps at all but Keeler Bay (gravel) and Chipmans Point (not stated). Grand Isle State Park sells licences.

Nearest towns: Burlington, South Hero on the islands, St. Albans and Vergennes.

Quabbin Reservoir — Boston’s water, with its own rules

The Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation gives Quabbin about 25,000 acres when full, 18 miles of length and 181 miles of shoreline. It is a drinking-water reservoir, so fishing here runs on DCR’s own season and boat rules.

Quabbin Reservoir, from Gate 8 to Gate 43

What swims there. Lake trout and landlocked salmon in the deep water, with trout, smallmouth and largemouth bass and chain pickerel.

The season. DCR opens Quabbin on the third Saturday in April and closes boating on the third Saturday in October. In 2026 it opened on 18 April; boating closed on 17 October, and shore fishing runs to 31 December. The launches open daily at 6 a.m.

Keeping a fish. Lake trout 2 a day, at least 18 inches; landlocked salmon 2 a day, at least 15 inches; trout 3 a day in total; bass 5 a day, at least 12 inches; chain pickerel 5 a day, at least 15 inches. Smelt are the only bait fish allowed after 15 May; chumming is banned, and so are lead sinkers and jigs under one ounce.

Confirmed with the Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation and MassWildlife, 18 November 2026 — DCR Quabbin Reservoir fishing guide and its 2026 season handout (dated 20 January 2026); MassWildlife freshwater regulations.

Boats. Three launch areas: Gate 8 in Pelham off Route 202, Gate 31 in New Salem off Route 122, and Gate 43 in Hardwick. Rental aluminium boats with 10-horsepower motors were $14 for the boat or $40 with a motor in 2026, first come, first served, for renters 16 and older; parking is $6, or $8 with a trailer, and half price for people 62 and over. A private motorboat needs an intact Quabbin Boat Seal and Boater ID card and must keep within DCR’s horsepower limits. Private canoes and kayaks are not allowed, and pontoon boats, inflatables and sailboats are banned.

From the shore. Daytime shore fishing is allowed from Gates 8 to 16A and 22 to 44; night fishing needs a permit. The Swift River below Winsor Dam is catch-and-release fly fishing all year.

Nearest towns: Ware and Amherst. The Quabbin Visitor Center is at Winsor Dam in Belchertown (413-323-7221).

Candlewood Lake — Connecticut’s big lake, low this winter

Connecticut DEEP gives Candlewood 5,064 acres, shared by Danbury, New Fairfield, Sherman, New Milford and Brookfield.

Candlewood Lake, from Danbury to New Milford

What swims there. DEEP manages Candlewood as a Trout Management Lake, and bass anglers hold tournaments here too.

Keeping a fish. As a Trout Management Lake under rules in force since 1 January 2026, the trout limit is 1 a day, and trout may be kept all year. For bass and other species, check the current Connecticut Angler’s Guide.

This winter. DEEP’s launch pages say FirstLight planned a deep drawdown after 1 November 2026, with the lake back to normal before Memorial Day 2027. Expect shallow water at the ramps until then.

Confirmed with Connecticut DEEP, 18 November 2026 — Trout Management Lakes page (last updated December 2025) and the Lattins Cove and Squantz Cove boat-launch pages (last updated 15 September 2026).

Boat ramps. Two state launches: Lattins Cove in Danbury (concrete ramp, about 50 parking spaces, open all year) and Squantz Cove in New Fairfield (about 75 spaces). The speed limit on the lake is 45 mph by day and 25 mph at night.

Nearest towns: Danbury, New Milford and Brookfield.

Rhode Island — Worden Pond

Rhode Island is small and has no lake on the scale of the others on this page. Its biggest freshwater fishing water near the coast is Worden Pond in South Kingstown.

Worden Pond, South Kingstown

The Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management lists a state-owned concrete boat ramp on Worden’s Pond, reached from Route 138 and Route 110 by Worden’s Pond Road. Invasive hydrilla was found here in 2023, and the town has put a free, self-service boat-cleaning station at the ramp: clean, drain and dry your boat before and after. For the fish and the current rules, check with the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management.

Confirmed with the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management, 18 November 2026 — public boat launch list and its press release of 27 May 2025 on the Worden Pond boat-cleaning station. The two pages give different acreages (1,043 and 1,094 acres), so we give neither.

Nearest town: South Kingstown.

The licence, state by state

Six states, six licences. Each lake needs the licence of the state it is in — except Lake Champlain, where Vermont and New York share most of the water.

Maine

Who needs one. Anglers 16 and older. For a visitor: season $83, 15-day $66, 7-day $62, 3-day $30, 1-day $18. For a Mainer: season $30. Agent fees are extra. Where to buy: online, at the department in Augusta, or from agents such as sporting-goods stores and town clerks. Free fishing days in 2026 were 14–15 February and 30–31 May; 2027 dates were not yet published when we checked.

Confirmed with the Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife, 18 November 2026 — licence requirements page, 2026 fee table.

New Hampshire

Who needs one. Anglers 16 and older. Visitors can buy 1-, 3- and 7-day licences as well as a season licence, online or from an agent. Prices: we could not open the department’s 2026 price list, so we do not print prices — check with New Hampshire Fish and Game. Free fishing days are the third Saturday in January and the first Saturday in June (17 January and 6 June in 2026).

Confirmed with New Hampshire Fish and Game, 18 November 2026 — licence requirements page and newsroom notices of 19 December 2025 and 22 May 2026.

Vermont

Who needs one. Anglers 15 and older. For a visitor: season $54, 7-day $31, 3-day $23, 1-day $21; ages 15 to 17, $15. For a Vermonter: $28; ages 15 to 17, $8. Where to buy: online or from licence agents.

Confirmed with Vermont Fish and Wildlife, 18 November 2026 — the department’s licence information page, which shows no licence year. Check the price when you buy.

Massachusetts

Who needs one. Anglers 15 and older. For a visitor: season $50, 3-day $30.50. For a resident: $40; ages 65 to 69, $20; 70 and over and ages 15 to 17, free. A $5 Wildlands Conservation Stamp is added to every non-resident licence and the first resident licence of the year, plus a small admin fee. At Quabbin, a $5 one-day Quabbin licence is also sold. Where to buy: MassFishHunt online, licence vendors, town clerks or MassWildlife offices.

Confirmed with MassWildlife, 18 November 2026 — licence types and fees page (2026 fees) and who-needs-a-licence page; Quabbin one-day licence from DCR.

Connecticut

Who needs one. Anglers 16 and older; licences run to 31 December. For a visitor: season $55, 3-day $22. For a resident: ages 18 to 64, $28; 16 and 17, $14; 65 and over, free (renewed each year). Where to buy: DEEP’s Online Outdoor Licensing System, DEEP offices or vendors.

Confirmed with Connecticut DEEP, 18 November 2026 — fisheries licences and permits page (last updated May 2026).

Rhode Island

For a visitor: $38, or $18 for a three-day tourist licence. For a resident: $21; 65 and over, a free permanent licence. A $5.50 Trout Conservation Stamp is needed to keep or possess trout. Where to buy: online, from town clerks and vendors, or at DEM in Providence. For the age at which a licence is needed, check with the department.

Confirmed with the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management, 18 November 2026 — freshwater fishing licence fee page.

Eating what you keep

Every New England state has mercury advice for freshwater fish, and the strictest advice is for pregnant or nursing women and young children. Maine’s CDC advises those groups to eat no inland freshwater fish except brook trout and landlocked salmon, one meal a month. Massachusetts has a specific advisory for Quabbin: everyone should eat no smallmouth or largemouth bass and no lake trout over 24 inches. Vermont’s Department of Health advises that pregnant or nursing people and children under 15 eat no Lake Champlain lake trout, and others no more than one meal a month of lake trout over 25 inches, because of PCBs. New Hampshire’s advice comes from its Department of Environmental Services. Read the current advice for the water you fish before you keep fish to eat.

Questions

Which licence do I need on Lake Champlain — Vermont or New York?

Either, on most of the lake. Vermont Fish and Wildlife says a New York licence is good on most of the Vermont part of the lake, and a Vermont licence is good west to the New York shore. The exceptions matter: the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation lists the Inland Sea, Malletts Bay, Missisquoi Bay and the Gut as Vermont-licence-only, and South Bay as New York-only. There is no reciprocity with Massachusetts or Quebec, and you follow the rules of the state whose water you are on.

What does a fishing licence cost a visitor in New England?

As listed by each state on 18 November 2026. Maine: non-resident season $83, 15-day $66, 7-day $62, 3-day $30, 1-day $18. Vermont: non-resident season $54, 7-day $31, 3-day $23, 1-day $21. Massachusetts: non-resident season $50, 3-day $30.50, plus a $5 Wildlands stamp and fees; Quabbin also sells a $5 one-day Quabbin licence. Connecticut: non-resident season $55, 3-day $22. Rhode Island: non-resident $38, 3-day tourist $18. New Hampshire: we could not open its 2026 price list, so check with New Hampshire Fish and Game. Prices change; buy from the agency or a licensed agent.

Can I bring my own boat or kayak to Quabbin Reservoir?

A motorboat, yes, but only with an intact Quabbin Boat Seal and a Quabbin Boater ID card, which come through the Department of Conservation and Recreation’s hot-water decontamination or cold-weather quarantine programme, and within its engine limits. Private canoes and kayaks are not allowed, there were no canoe or kayak rentals in 2026, and pontoon boats, inflatables and sailboats are banned. The easy answer for a visitor is a rental boat at Gate 8, 31 or 43: $14 for the boat or $40 with a motor in 2026.

Can I ice fish on Moosehead, Sebago and Winnipesaukee?

Yes, with rules. Moosehead is in Maine’s North Zone, and its special law opens it to ice fishing from 1 January to 30 April, with salmon and brook trout released at certain times. Sebago is in the South Zone, open all year, but every trout (other than lake trout) and salmon caught through the ice must be released at once. On Winnipesaukee, New Hampshire’s rule for lake trout and salmon waters allows ice fishing from 1 January to 31 March, two devices plus up to six for cusk, and landlocked salmon may never be taken through the ice. Never go out without checking the ice yourself.

Is Candlewood Lake low this winter?

Yes. Connecticut DEEP’s launch pages say FirstLight, which runs the lake, planned a deep drawdown starting after 1 November 2026, with the lake back to its normal level before Memorial Day 2027. Expect shallow ramps and exposed shoreline until spring; check the launch pages before you tow a boat.

Maine’s special laws. The Moosehead and Sebago rules are long, with many closed areas and dates. We give the main limits as the department lists them; read the current law book for the full text, and note that the Sebago salmon daily limit is not spelled out in the entry we read.

Bass rules on Champlain and Candlewood. We did not confirm them today. Check the Vermont and Connecticut fishing guides.

Winnipesaukee’s size and launches. We could not open an official page giving its acreage or a full list of public launches, so we name only Downing’s Landing in Alton.

Vermont’s licence year. Vermont’s price page shows no year. Check the price when you buy.

Rhode Island. We give the Worden Pond ramp and the licence prices only; for the fish and the rules, ask the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management.

Hotel lists in small towns. Our hotel buttons are pinned to town numbers checked two ways with our own resolver. We do not open Trip.com’s hotel-list pages to count the hotels, so small towns may show few. Belchertown, beside Quabbin, would not resolve to the right place, so it has no button.
