Where to Stay in Crete

Where to Stay in Crete — Chania, Rethymno, Heraklion & the Elounda Coast

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Crete is not an island so much as a small country: about 160 miles end to end, with mountains down the spine and four quite different bases along the north coast. Chania in the west is the prettiest and the most walkable, built around a Venetian harbour, and it is the right first choice for most visitors. Rethymno is a smaller, gentler version of the same, with a long town beach and lower prices. Heraklion is a working city worth a night or two for the Archaeological Museum and Knossos rather than a week. And Elounda and Agios Nikolaos in the east are where the big resort hotels are: quieter, more expensive, and the right answer if you want to settle in one place with a spa and a sea view. Do you need a rental car?

If you are basing yourself in one town and taking the occasional taxi or day tour, no. If you want the Lasithi plateau, the south coast or the smaller beaches, yes. The modern north-coast highway is straightforward driving; the mountain roads are narrow, steep and best avoided after dark. Note that the drive from Chania in the west to Agios Nikolaos in the east is about 130 miles and a solid three hours — do not plan to do it twice in a day.

Which town suits which traveller

Chania for the prettiest old town and the best dinners, and it is flat once you are down at the harbour. Rethymno for the same at a gentler price, with a town beach attached. Heraklion for one or two nights of museums and Knossos. Elounda and Agios Nikolaos if you want a resort, a spa and a sea view and are happy to stay put.

Fly rather than sail, unless you enjoy the crossing

Athens to Heraklion or Chania takes under an hour in the air, with many daily departures. The ferry from Piraeus is roughly eight to twelve hours; the overnight sailings leave around nine in the evening and dock between six and seven in the morning, and a private cabin with beds is modestly priced. Take the overnight boat if you like a sea crossing and want to save a hotel night; take the plane if you would rather not be up at dawn hauling luggage down a car deck.

Which hotels stay open in winter

Most of the Elounda and beach resorts are seasonal and close roughly from November to April. The town hotels in Chania, Rethymno and Heraklion stay open all year, which makes Crete one of the few Greek islands that works for a quiet winter break. May, June, September and early October are the sweet spot: warm sea, manageable heat and hotels at around two-thirds of peak price.

Crete Hotels — common questions

Which part of Crete should I stay in?

Chania in the west is the prettiest and the most walkable, with a Venetian harbour and the island’s best dinners — the right first choice for most visitors. Rethymno is a smaller, gentler version of the same at lower prices, with a long town beach. Heraklion is a working city, worth one or two nights for the Archaeological Museum and Knossos rather than a week. Elounda and Agios Nikolaos in the east are the resort coast: quieter, more expensive, and the right answer if you want to settle in one place with a spa and a sea view.

Do I need to rent a car in Crete?

Only if you want to leave the north coast. If you are basing yourself in one town and taking the occasional taxi or day tour, you do not need one. If you want the Lasithi plateau, the south coast or the smaller beaches, you do. The modern north-coast highway is easy driving; the mountain roads are narrow, steep and best avoided after dark. Bear in mind that Crete is about 160 miles end to end, and Chania to Agios Nikolaos is a solid three hours each way.

Should I fly or take the ferry from Athens to Crete?

Flying to Heraklion or Chania takes under an hour and is usually the sensible choice. The ferry from Piraeus takes roughly eight to twelve hours depending on the vessel; overnight sailings leave around nine in the evening and arrive between six and seven the next morning, and a private cabin with proper beds costs relatively little. Take the overnight ferry if you enjoy a sea crossing and want to save a hotel night; take the plane if you would rather not be up at dawn hauling luggage down a car deck.

When is the best time to visit Crete?

May, June, September and early October are the sweet spot — warm sea, manageable heat and hotels at around two-thirds of peak price. August is the hottest and busiest month, with inland highs regularly in the nineties Fahrenheit, crowded beaches and top rates; if you must travel then, book a coastal hotel rather than an inland one and do your sightseeing early in the morning. Most Elounda and beach resorts close from about November to April, while the town hotels in Chania, Rethymno and Heraklion stay open all year.

Is Knossos worth visiting, and how do I get there?

Yes, if you have any interest in the ancient world — it is the great Minoan palace site and the reason many people come to Crete at all. It sits about three miles south of Heraklion and is reached by a frequent city bus, a short taxi ride or an organised half-day tour. Go early or late in the day in summer, since there is little shade and the ground is uneven. Pair it with the Heraklion Archaeological Museum, which holds the frescoes and objects found there, and the site makes far more sense.

Are the old towns of Chania and Rethymno easy to walk?

The harbour fronts of both are flat and pleasant. Behind them, the lanes are narrow, cobbled and occasionally stepped, and the historic buildings converted into hotels often have steep staircases and no lift. If walking is difficult, choose a hotel on the harbour front itself rather than up a lane, ask specifically about stairs when you book, and take a taxi to the edge of the pedestrian zone rather than expecting to be dropped at the door.
