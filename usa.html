The United States — a Canadian's Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Our nearest neighbour, and the easiest big trip we take. Written from this side of the border: what your dollar is actually worth down there, what you must not cross without, and where to spend a Canadian winter.

Why Canadians go south

Nowhere else gives us this much, this close, this often.

It's the closest sun

Toronto to Orlando is under three hours. When it's −20 in February, that is the whole argument, and it wins every year.

Everything is direct

Toronto, Montréal, Vancouver, Calgary, Ottawa, Halifax — the US network out of Canada is dense, frequent and competitive.

Drive if you want to

Buffalo, Detroit, Bellingham, Plattsburgh. Some of the best of America is a tank of gas and a border booth away.

Range

Theatre in New York, desert in Nevada, monuments in Washington, beaches in Florida. One country, all of it.

Before you cross

The four things that actually matter to a Canadian traveller — the rest is detail.

1. Travel medical insurance is not optional

This is the single most important line on this page. Your provincial plan — OHIP, RAMQ, MSP, AHCIP — covers only a small fraction of what care costs in the United States, and American medicine is staggeringly expensive. An ambulance ride and a night in a US hospital can cost more than the trip. Buy travel medical insurance for every crossing, even a day trip to Buffalo.

Declare pre-existing conditions honestly, and read the exclusions before you go — an undeclared condition is the most common reason a claim gets denied. If you take medication or have an ongoing condition, get the policy reviewed by a person, not a website.

2. The border: passport, NEXUS, and the six-month question

Canadian citizens travelling as tourists are generally visa-exempt and don't need an ESTA. To fly, you need a valid passport. At land and sea crossings, a passport, NEXUS card or enhanced driver's licence is normally accepted. If you cross often, NEXUS pays for itself in saved hours.

Visitors are commonly admitted for up to six months — but this is where snowbirds get into trouble. Spending long stretches in the US can trigger American tax-filing obligations under the IRS substantial presence test, and most provinces require you to be physically in your province for a minimum number of days each year to keep your health coverage. Count your days, keep records, and get professional advice.

Entry, tax and health-coverage rules change. Confirm the current position with U.S. Customs and Border Protection, the Government of Canada, the IRS and your provincial health plan before a long stay. Nothing on this page is legal or tax advice.

3. Your dollar buys less than the sticker says

The price tag is in US dollars, and then the exchange rate happens. A $200 hotel room is not a $200 hotel room. And American sales tax is added at the till, not shown on the shelf — so budget above the listed price, not at it. Use the converter below, and pay in the local currency when a terminal offers to "helpfully" charge you in Canadian dollars — that conversion is almost always a worse rate.

4. Coming home: what you can bring back

Canada Border Services Agency personal exemptions depend on how long you were away: under 24 hours — no exemption; 24 hours or more — generally up to CAD $200; 48 hours or more — generally up to CAD $800, which is also when the alcohol and tobacco allowances open up. Keep your receipts, and declare everything — the fine for a hidden bargain costs more than the bargain.

Allowances change. Check the CBSA site before a serious shopping run.

The great sites, and how a Canadian reaches them

Most of America's best landscapes are a flight plus a rental car. Here's the practical pairing.

Grand Canyon

Fly to Las Vegas (4½ hrs by road to the South Rim) or Phoenix. Doable as a long day trip from Vegas; far better with a night at the rim to catch sunrise.

Zion & Bryce

Also out of Las Vegas — Zion is under three hours. Pair the two with the Grand Canyon for the classic Southwest loop.

Monument Valley

The red buttes of every Western ever filmed. Remote — build it into a Southwest drive from Vegas or Phoenix, not a day trip.

Yellowstone

Geysers, bison and the Grand Prismatic. Fly to Bozeman, Jackson or Salt Lake City. Summer only, realistically — and book lodging very far ahead.

Yosemite

Granite walls and waterfalls, about four hours from San Francisco. Waterfalls are at their best in late spring.

The Everglades

Sawgrass, alligators and airboats, an hour from Miami — an easy add-on to a Florida winter, and best in the dry season.

Mount Rushmore

South Dakota's Black Hills, via Rapid City. Pair it with Badlands National Park, which is the better landscape of the two.

Niagara Falls, NY

The one we can drive to. Cross at the Rainbow Bridge and see our own falls from the American side — the Cave of the Winds decks put you right under them.

Two things that catch people out at the parks

Lodging inside the parks books absurdly early. Yellowstone and Yosemite in-park rooms open roughly a year ahead and go almost immediately. If you're planning inside six months, plan on a gateway town — that is not a downgrade, it's just how it works.

Several parks now require timed-entry or vehicle reservations in peak season, separate from your park pass, and the rules change year to year. Check the National Park Service page for your park before you drive to the gate — arriving without one can mean being turned away.

The trips worth taking

What Canadians actually go for.

A Florida winter

Two weeks, or two months. Orlando for the parks and the grandchildren; the Gulf coast for the quiet; the Atlantic side for the surf.

Vegas in January

Cheap flights, warm desert days, and a city that does not care what month it is. The Grand Canyon is a day trip.

New York at Christmas

An hour and a half from Toronto. The tree, the windows, the theatre — and a US dollar that hurts a bit less if you plan it.

Washington in spring

The cherry blossoms, the Smithsonian museums — free — and the monuments at night, which is when to see them.

The Falls from the other side

Cross at Rainbow Bridge and see our own waterfall the way Americans do: closer, wilder, and from the Cave of the Winds decks.

The Grand Canyon

Fly to Vegas or Phoenix and drive. It is the one American landscape that beggars every photograph you have seen of it.

When to go & getting there

When to go

January–March is snowbird season: peak demand, peak prices, and precisely when you want to be gone. Book Florida and Arizona months ahead.

Spring and fall are the value windows — Washington in April, New York in October, the desert in November.

Summer in Florida and the Southwest is brutally hot and, on the Atlantic and Gulf coasts, hurricane season runs roughly June to November.

Getting there

Flying: Toronto, Montréal and Vancouver have dense direct networks to every city on this page. Several Canadian airports offer US preclearance — you clear American customs before you board, and land as a domestic arrival. It is one of the great conveniences of travelling from Canada.

Driving: Buffalo, Detroit, Plattsburgh and Bellingham put a great deal of America within a day. Confirm your auto insurance covers US travel, and consider NEXUS if you cross more than twice a year.

Canadian traveller FAQ

Do Canadians need a visa to visit the United States?

Canadian citizens travelling for tourism are generally visa-exempt and don't need an ESTA. You'll need a valid passport to fly; at land and sea crossings a passport, NEXUS card or enhanced driver's licence is normally accepted. Rules change — confirm current requirements with U.S. Customs and Border Protection and the Government of Canada before you travel.

Do I really need travel medical insurance?

Yes. Treat it as non-negotiable, even for a day trip. Provincial plans cover only a small fraction of costs incurred outside Canada, and American medical care is extremely expensive — a short hospital stay can run into tens of thousands of dollars. Declare pre-existing conditions honestly and read the exclusions.

How long can a Canadian stay in the US?

Visitors are commonly admitted for up to six months. But long stays can create US tax-filing obligations under the IRS "substantial presence test," and most provinces require a minimum number of days physically in-province each year to keep your health coverage. Snowbirds should count days carefully and take professional advice — verify this, don't guess.

What can I bring back duty-free?

CBSA personal exemptions depend on time away: no exemption under 24 hours; generally up to CAD $200 after 24 hours; generally up to CAD $800 after 48 hours or more, which is also when alcohol and tobacco allowances become available. Keep receipts and declare everything. Check the CBSA site before a big shopping trip.

Should I pay in Canadian or US dollars?

Always choose to be charged in US dollars when a card terminal offers you the choice. "Dynamic currency conversion" — being billed in Canadian dollars at the till — is convenient and almost always a worse exchange rate than your own bank would give you.
