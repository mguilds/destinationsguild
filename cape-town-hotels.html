Cape Town’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Cape Town — Best Hotels by Style

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Cape Town wraps a working city around a mountain and two coastlines, so the kind of hotel you choose matters as much as the address. Do you want a grand old dame in the leafy City Bowl, a barefoot room above Camps Bay beach, or a family suite steps from the V&A Waterfront? We’ve grouped enduring, well-run Cape Town hotels by style and character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can just choose your dates.

Luxury

Cape Town’s finest hotels are experiences in their own right — historic gardens under Table Mountain, harbourside glamour and service that becomes part of the trip. Book here when you want the stay to be as memorable as the sightseeing.

$$$$ LuxuryCity Bowl / GardensHistoricNine-acre gardens

The rose-pink “Nellie” has been Cape Town’s grande dame since 1899, set in nine acres of gardens at the top of the City Bowl with Table Mountain rising behind it. Palm-lined lawns, heated pools, a fine spa and a famously lavish afternoon tea make it feel like a country estate minutes from the city. Best for a landmark stay where old-world grace is the whole point.

Boutique

For character over scale, Cape Town’s City Bowl hides a run of small, design-led hotels in converted Victorian and Cape Dutch houses — a dozen rooms, a plunge pool and staff who know your name. They suit independent travelers who prize personality and a quiet street over a big lobby.

Budget & Value

Cape Town rewards a careful budget: a tidy City Bowl room close to the sights costs a fraction of the Atlantic Seaboard rates, and puts you within walking distance of Company’s Garden, museums and the buzzing restaurant streets. You trade sea views for location — and spend the savings on wine country and Table Mountain.

Cape Town hotels — quick questions

What is the best area to stay in Cape Town?

For first-time visitors the V&A Waterfront is the easiest base — safe to walk at night, packed with restaurants, and the departure point for Robben Island and Table Mountain trips. The City Bowl neighbourhoods of Gardens and Kloof Street suit those who want cafés, galleries and a local feel, while Camps Bay and Bantry Bay along the Atlantic Seaboard are the pick for beach days and sunset views.

Is Cape Town a walkable city?

Parts of it are very walkable — the V&A Waterfront, the City Bowl around Bree and Kloof Streets, and the Camps Bay beachfront are all easy on foot by day. But Cape Town is spread out and hilly, and locals generally use a car, metered taxi or ride-hailing apps to move between areas and after dark, so plan on some driving between neighbourhoods.

When is the best time to visit Cape Town?

The warm, dry summer from November to March is peak season for beaches and Table Mountain, with long sunny days but higher prices and crowds. The shoulder months of March to May and September to November bring mild weather, thinner crowds and better value, while June to August is cooler and wetter but green, quiet and ideal for whale watching along the coast.
