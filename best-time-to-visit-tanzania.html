When to Go — Tanzania Month by Month

Best Time to Visit Tanzania — Month by Month

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Tanzania is two holidays sharing a country — the safari circuit inland and the coast with Zanzibar — and they do not keep the same calendar. A month that is perfect for the Serengeti can be the wettest week of the year on the east coast.

This page takes them separately, says plainly which months we would not book, and names the weeks where the price falls for no good reason.

Tanzania has four seasons, not two

Most of what you read flattens Tanzania into “dry” and “wet”, which costs people money. There are four distinct periods, they behave differently on the safari circuit and on the coast, and the cheapest one is not always the worst one.

The long dry: late June to October

The classic season. Grass is low, water is scarce so animals gather at it, and the great herds are in the northern Serengeti. Everything is open, everything is full, and everything costs the most it will cost all year. Book this a long way ahead.

The short rains: November into December

Afternoon storms rather than all-day rain, landscape turning green, dust gone, prices softer and camps far less crowded. For a second safari, or a first one on a budget, this is the Guild’s favourite window in the whole year.

The green season: January and February

A drier gap between the rains, and the one time the southern Serengeti and Ndutu are the place to be, because this is when the wildebeest calve in enormous numbers and the predators follow. Lush, photogenic, and quieter than the dry season.

The long rains: March to May

Genuine rain, roads that turn to mud, and a good many seasonal camps closed altogether. Rates fall to their lowest. April and May are the two months we would not book for a first visit, and the two we would consider for a cheap return trip with a tolerant attitude.

Month by month

January and February — calving on the southern plains

Warm, bright, with short sharp showers. The wildebeest are concentrated around Ndutu and the southern Serengeti dropping calves, and the predator action is the best of the year. Zanzibar is hot and the sea is calm. A strong and underrated time to come.

March, April and May — the long rains

The wettest stretch, April usually the worst of it. Some camps close, some roads become impassable, the grass is tall and the animals are dispersed because water is everywhere. The upside is real: the lowest prices of the year, the country at its greenest, and very few other vehicles. Not a first trip. Kilimanjaro is poor in these months.

June — the turn

The rain stops, the dust has not yet arrived, and the high-season rates have not quite begun. The migration is moving into the western and central Serengeti. A good value month that gets better as it goes on.

July and August — peak, and worth it

Dry, cool at night, superb game viewing, and the river crossings in the northern Serengeti if the herds have arrived. It is also the busiest and dearest time, and the Ngorongoro Crater floor can feel crowded by mid-morning. Book six to twelve months ahead for the better camps. The best window of the year for Kilimanjaro.

September and October — the quiet end of the dry

Our pick if you want the dry season without the full August crush. The herds are still north, the bush is thin so animals are easy to see, and by October the first clouds build without much falling. Zanzibar is excellent. October is the single best compromise month in the Tanzanian year.

November and December — the short rains

Afternoon downpours that clear, green country, migratory birds arriving, and prices well below the dry season until the Christmas fortnight, when the coast fills up and rates jump sharply for two weeks before falling again. Avoid 20 December to 3 January if you are paying attention to cost.

The coast runs on a different calendar

Zanzibar and the mainland coast are governed by the monsoon rather than the safari seasons. The north-east monsoon, from roughly December to March, brings the hottest, stillest weather and the clearest water — the best diving and snorkelling of the year. The south-east monsoon, from June to September, is cooler, breezier and better for sailing, with choppier water on the east coast. April and May are wet here too.

One practical note that decides more holidays than the weather does: much of Zanzibar’s east coast empties to shallow seagrass flats at low tide in any month, so if swimming from the beach matters, book the northern tip at Nungwi or Kendwa, where the channel stays deep.

If you are climbing Kilimanjaro

Two windows: the long dry from late June to October, and the shorter dry spell from January to early March. The long rains from March to May and the short rains in November make the forest sections wet and the summit views unreliable. In every month the summit night is below freezing. The number of days your route takes matters far more to your chances than the month does — official health advice names altitude sickness as a risk in parts of Tanzania, and on Kilimanjaro the extra acclimatisation day is the best money on the whole trip.

Before you book

Government travel insurance is compulsory

Every visitor to mainland Tanzania and to Zanzibar, residents aside, must hold inbound travel insurance provided by the Tanzanian government — and you must hold it even if you already have a policy of your own from home. You buy it online beforehand or at your port of entry, it covers the length of your stay up to 92 days, and you only buy it for your first port of entry. Arriving in Zanzibar first means buying from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation; arriving on the mainland first means the National Insurance Corporation. You may be refused entry without it. Health screening at the border

Tanzania’s Ministry of Health announced heightened screening on 17 October for travellers arriving from high-risk regions and affected countries, at every point of entry, and every traveller has to complete a Travellers Health Surveillance Form before departure or on arrival. You also pass through mpox screening — body temperature checks — at all points of entry. Check the current list of affected countries before you fly.

Questions readers actually ask

What is the best month to visit Tanzania?


October, if you want one answer. The dry season game viewing is still excellent, the herds are still in the northern Serengeti, the bush is thin enough to see through, the coast is at its best and the August crowds and prices have eased. July and August are better for the river crossings and worse for everything else. For a cheaper trip with green landscapes and few vehicles, take November instead.

When is the wildebeest migration in Tanzania?


It is a year-round circuit, not an event, so the question is where rather than when. Roughly: January and February in the southern Serengeti and Ndutu for the calving; March to May moving north and west; June and July through the western corridor; July to October in the northern Serengeti near the Mara River, which is where the crossings happen. If crossings are the reason you are going, aim at August and September and accept that no date can be promised, because the animals move with the rain.

What is the cheapest time to go to Tanzania?


April and May, during the long rains, when rates are at their lowest and a number of seasonal camps shut. You trade mud, tall grass, dispersed animals and unreliable roads for a fraction of the price and almost no other visitors. The better value compromise is November and early December in the short rains: much cheaper than the dry season, green rather than dusty, and everything still open. Avoid the Christmas fortnight, when coastal rates spike.

Is it worth going to Tanzania in the rainy season?


For a second visit, yes, and particularly in the short rains of November and December, which are our favourite weeks of the year here. The storms are short and in the afternoon, the country is green, birdlife is at its peak and the camps are quiet. The long rains of March to May are a different proposition and we would not book them for a first trip, when you have one chance at the Crater and the Serengeti.

Can I combine a safari and the beach in one trip?


Yes, and it is the standard shape of a Tanzanian holiday: the northern circuit from Arusha, then a short flight to Zanzibar. Weather-wise the two line up well from June to October and again in January and February. Allow a full travel day between them, and remember that the compulsory government travel insurance is bought for your first port of entry only, so entering via the mainland covers you for Zanzibar too.
