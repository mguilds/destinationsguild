Why visit Spain

Few countries pack this much history, art, coastline and sheer pleasure into a single trip — or make it feel this joyful.

Art and architecture

Gaudí's Sagrada Família and Park Güell, the Prado and Reina Sofía in Madrid, the Alhambra's Moorish palaces and Bilbao's Guggenheim — genius across the centuries.

The food and wine

Tapas and pintxos, Valencian paella, Basque grills, Iberian jamón and Manchego — paired with Rioja, cava and the fortified sherries of the south.

Sun, coast and islands

The Costa Brava and Costa del Sol, hidden coves and long city beaches, and the island worlds of the Balearics and the Canaries.

Fiesta and flamenco

Seville's Semana Santa and Feria, La Tomatina and Pamplona's bulls, late-night flamenco in Andalusia and a nightlife that runs till dawn.

How the country divides up

Madrid & central Spain

Many trips begin in Madrid, and it rewards the time — the Prado, Reina Sofía and Thyssen hold one of the world's great runs of art, and the city's plazas, markets and late dinners set the tempo. Within an hour lie the Castilian classics: Toledo, the old imperial city on its gorge; Segovia under its Roman aqueduct; and golden-stone Salamanca with Spain's oldest university.

Catalonia & the Mediterranean east

Barcelona is the great draw — Gaudí's Sagrada Família and Park Güell, the medieval Gothic Quarter and a city beach — and the gateway to the coves of the Costa Brava around Girona. South along the coast, Valencia is the birthplace of paella and home of the futuristic City of Arts and Sciences, while Tarragona keeps some of Spain's finest Roman ruins.

Andalusia & the south

The south is Spain at its most romantic. Seville has the Alcázar, a soaring cathedral and flamenco in the Triana barrio; Granada crowns its hill with the Alhambra, the loveliest Moorish palace on earth; and Córdoba keeps the Mezquita, its forest of red-and-white arches. From the beaches of the Costa del Sol and Picasso's Málaga, the whole region opens up.

The north & green Spain

The Atlantic north is cooler, greener and famously well fed. San Sebastián is a pintxos paradise on a perfect bay; Bilbao reinvented itself around Gehry's Guggenheim; and the pilgrim road, the Camino, ends at the great cathedral of Santiago de Compostela. Offshore, the Balearic and Canary islands add beaches and volcanic landscapes year-round.

Top things to do in Spain

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Gaudí's Barcelona

The Sagrada Família and Park Güell, Casa Batlló and La Pedrera, then the medieval lanes of the Gothic Quarter. Book timed tickets well ahead.

The Alhambra

Granada's Moorish palace-city — the Nasrid Palaces, the Generalife gardens and courtyards of fountains, framed by the Sierra Nevada. Tickets sell out; book early.

Madrid's art mile

The Prado's Velázquez and Goya, Guernica at the Reina Sofía and the Thyssen next door — three of Europe's finest museums within a short walk.

Moorish Andalusia

Seville's Alcázar and cathedral, Córdoba's Mezquita and white hill-towns strung across the hills — the legacy of eight Moorish centuries.

Eat and drink your way

Tapas crawls and Basque pintxos, paella in Valencia, jamón and Manchego, and a glass of Rioja, cava or Jerez sherry to go with it.

Beaches, islands & fiestas

The Costa Brava's coves, the Balearics and Canaries, and Spain's great festivals — Semana Santa, La Tomatina and Pamplona's bulls.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

April–June and September–October are the sweet spots: warm, bright and just before or after the peak crush, ideal for the cities, Andalusia and the coast alike.

July–August is hot, and inland cities like Madrid, Seville and Córdoba can be fierce — though the beaches and the cooler northern coast are in full swing.

November–March is low season: cooler, quieter and cheaper, perfect for museums, food and city breaks, with the Pyrenees and Sierra Nevada open for skiing.

Getting there

Madrid-Barajas (MAD) and Barcelona-El Prat (BCN) are the two main long-haul gateways, with direct flights from across the world.

Málaga (AGP), Valencia (VLC), Seville (SVQ) and Bilbao (BIO) also take international flights, and Spain's high-speed AVE trains link the major cities so smoothly that many visitors skip domestic flying altogether.

The currency is the euro. Cards work almost everywhere; carry a little cash for small bars and rural spots. Always check your current visa and passport-validity requirements before booking.

Spain travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Spain?

April to June and September to October are the sweet spots — warm, bright and just before or after the peak crush, ideal for the cities, Andalusia and the coast alike. July and August are hot, especially inland in Madrid, Seville and Córdoba, though the beaches and northern coast stay pleasant. November to March is low season: cool, quieter and cheaper, ideal for the cities, museums and food, with the Pyrenees and Sierra Nevada open for skiing.

What currency is used in Spain?

The euro (EUR, symbol €). Cards, including contactless and phone payments, are accepted almost everywhere — hotels, restaurants, shops and even many market stalls. Carry a little cash for small bars, tips and rural spots. ATMs (cajeros) are widespread and usually the best way to get euros, though watch for machines that offer to charge you in your home currency, which costs more.

Madrid, Barcelona or Seville — which should I choose?

Madrid is the grand, high-energy capital: world-class art at the Prado, elegant plazas, superb food and late nights. Barcelona pairs Gaudí's architecture and a medieval Gothic Quarter with a city beach and Mediterranean buzz. Seville is the soul of Andalusia — the Alcázar, the great cathedral, flamenco and orange-scented streets. On a first trip many travellers link Madrid and one other city by high-speed AVE train, which connects them in a few hours.

How many days do you need in Spain?

For a first trip, ten to fourteen days is ideal: a few days each in Madrid and Barcelona, linked by AVE train, with time added for Andalusia — Seville, Granada and Córdoba. With only a week, pick two bases rather than trying to see the whole country — Madrid and Seville, or Barcelona and Valencia, make a natural pair.

Is Spain safe for travellers?

Spain is one of the most-visited countries in the world and is generally very safe for travellers. The main thing to guard against is petty theft — pickpockets and bag-snatchers work crowded metros, ramblas and tourist sights in Barcelona and Madrid, so keep valuables secure. Use licensed taxis or official apps, keep an eye on your bags in busy squares, and check your government's current travel advisory before you go.
