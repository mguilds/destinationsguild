When to Go to Germany

The Best Time to Visit Germany

Short answer: late spring and early fall — roughly May–June and September — are the sweet spot. You get warm, settled weather and long days without the July–August school-holiday crush, and the shoulder edges in April and October are cheaper still. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the crowds, and the best timing for the cities, Bavaria, and the Alps — plus where Oktoberfest and the Christmas markets fall.

Germany is a year-round destination, but it is not the same destination year-round. The country you meet on a long June evening — beer gardens under chestnut trees, castles above green valleys, daylight past ten o'clock — is a very different place from a frost-bright December, when the air smells of mulled wine and roasting almonds and the market squares glow after dark. Choosing your dates well is the single biggest lever you have over the trip. Here is how to pull it.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$ ShoulderMild & bloomingBest for citiesFewer crowds

The country thaws and greens up, parks and palace gardens burst into blossom, and city sightseeing is a pleasure at 12–20°C. Late April and May are prime — everything reopens, beer gardens fill, and crowds haven't yet surged. March can still be cold, grey, and showery, but it's cheap. Watch Easter week, a short domestic peak, and the May 1 holiday.

Summer — June to August

$$$$ PeakWarm & busyFestivals & hikingBook early

High season, and it feels like it. June is the pick of the three — the longest days of the year, warm evenings, and open-air festivals — before July and August bring the summer school holidays, the biggest crowds, and occasional heat waves past 30°C. This is the time for the Alps and Black Forest for hiking, the beer gardens, and the river valleys. If summer is your only window, book flights and hotels well ahead.

Autumn — September to November

$$ ShoulderCrisp & goldenHarvest & OktoberfestGreat value

Many travelers' favorite season. September still has warm days and summer light with the peak crowds ebbing — and it opens Oktoberfest in Munich and the wine harvest along the Rhine and Moselle. October is mellow and golden, with vineyards turning and forests ablaze. Rain and chill build through November, but the cities are quiet and prices soft, right up until the Christmas markets arrive.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold & festiveMarkets & skiCheapest

The bargain season, and a genuinely magical one for the right trip: the world's finest Christmas markets from late November to December 24, top-tier skiing in the Bavarian Alps, and crowd-free art cities in Berlin, Munich, and Dresden. Days are short and cold, and some castles and river cruises pause until spring. Sidestep the two-week Christmas–New Year spike and January and February are the cheapest weeks in Germany.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ CheapestColdSki season

The quietest, cheapest month once the New Year holiday passes. Cold and often grey, sometimes crisp and snowy; ideal for near-empty museums in Berlin and Munich, winter sales, and prime skiing in the Bavarian Alps. Book cities and mountains, not the coast.

February

$ CheapColdCarnival

Still low season and low prices, with one big exception: Carnival (Karneval / Fasching), which fills Cologne, Düsseldorf, and Mainz for the days around Rose Monday. Skiing continues in the Alps. A great month for a quiet, affordable city break elsewhere.

March

$$ Value shoulderCool, variableSpring begins

Shoulder season opens. Weather is variable — mild days, cold nights, some rain and late snow — but crowds are thin and prices low. Cities shine and gardens begin to stir. If Easter falls in late March, expect a short surge around it.

April

$$ ShoulderMild & greenEaster peak

One of the loveliest months: blossom, fresh green, and comfortable sightseeing weather, though showers come and go. The catch is Easter and the school break around it, which bring domestic crowds and higher rates. Outside those dates, superb value and mood.

May

$$$ PrimeWarm & settledEverything open

Arguably the best all-round month. Warm, increasingly stable weather, long days, gardens at their peak, and the full summer machinery open before the school-holiday crowds and heat arrive. Beer gardens and river cruises hit their stride. Popular — book ahead — but worth it.

June

$$$$ Peak beginsWarm, long daysFestivals

Summer proper, and the most pleasant of the hot months. The longest days of the year, warm evenings, and a packed festival calendar, with crowds and prices climbing steadily toward the July–August peak. Excellent for the Alps, the Black Forest, and the Rhine.

July

$$$$ PeakWarm to hotBusy

Warm, busy, and pricey as the summer school holidays get underway. Great for hiking, lakes, and open-air concerts; the occasional heat wave makes midday city sightseeing hard work. Reserve accommodation, trains, and headline castles well in advance.

August

$$$$ PeakWarmestSchool holidays

The warmest, busiest month, with school holidays running across the country and families on the move. Popular spots — Neuschwanstein, the Romantic Road, the Alps — are at their fullest and dearest. Beautiful weather, but plan around the crowds and book everything early.

September

$$$ PrimeMild & goldenOktoberfest & harvest

The other star month. Summer warmth lingers while the crowds thin and prices ease after the holidays end. The wine harvest begins on the Rhine and Moselle, and Oktoberfest opens in Munich mid-month — superb everywhere except Munich itself, where rooms are scarce and steep during the festival.

October

$$ Best valueCool & goldenAutumn color

Mellow, golden, and one of the best-value months of the year. Comfortable city weather early on, spectacular autumn color, and low crowds once Oktoberfest closes in the first days of the month. Rain and chill build later, but the cities are at their calm best.

November

$ → $$ SplitCool & greyMarkets open late

The quietest, greyest, and often wettest stretch — and the cheapest, right up until the Christmas markets open in the last week of the month and rates begin to lift. Early November is a bargain for empty museums in Berlin, Munich, and Dresden if you don't mind a coat and short days.

December

$$ → $$$ FestiveChristmas marketsSki & snow

The festive centerpiece. The Christmas markets — Nuremberg, Dresden, Cologne, Munich — run through December 23 or 24 and fill the historic squares; ski resorts open in the Alps. Weekends in market cities are busy and pricier, and the Christmas–New Year fortnight is a sharp peak, so book early.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Mid-January to March — with January and February the lowest

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSkip Christmas fortnight

Transatlantic airfares and hotel rates bottom out in deep winter. January and February are the cheapest weeks of the year for both, once the New Year holiday passes. Early-to-mid November, before the Christmas markets open, is the other budget window — empty cities at low-season prices. The one carve-out is the roughly two-week Christmas–New Year holiday, when rates briefly jump. You'll trade warmth and long days for the savings, but for city breaks and skiing the value is unbeatable.

Want milder weather and a deal? Target the edges of shoulder season: late March to April (avoiding Easter) and late October. You get comfortable days and open sights at a fraction of the July–August cost. As a rule, midweek flights and stays beat weekends — a real difference in Christmas-market and Oktoberfest cities — and booking two to three months out lands the best fares.

When to Avoid the Crowds

The busy periods in Germany are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks to sidestep

July & AugustOktoberfestChristmas marketsEaster

July and August are the busiest and most expensive stretch, driven by warm weather and the summer school holidays. Munich is the standout: during Oktoberfest (mid-September to the first weekend of October) the city books out and hotel rates soar. In December, the Christmas markets pack the historic centers of Nuremberg, Dresden, Cologne, and Munich, especially on weekends, while Easter week is a shorter domestic spike.

To have the big sights closer to yourself, come in the shoulder months — late April, May, September, October — or in the quiet weeks of deep winter. Whenever you go, the crowd-beating tactics are the same: reserve timed tickets for Neuschwanstein and the headline museums in advance, and visit at opening or late in the day. Bavaria, Munich & the Alps

Ski: Dec–MarHike: Jun–SepOktoberfest: mid-Sep–early Oct

The south has two peaks. December to March is for skiing around Garmisch-Partenkirchen and the Zugspitze, with the most reliable snow in January and February. Munich's Oktoberfest (mid-September to the first weekend of October) is glorious but books out fast. Plan your base in Munich.

Winter is quiet, with many cruises and smaller attractions closed until spring, aside from riverside Christmas markets.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a German trip.

What is the best month to visit Germany?

May, June, and September are the three strongest all-round months. You get warm, settled weather, long daylight, gardens and beer gardens in full swing, and lighter crowds than the July–August school-holiday peak. May and June lean fresh and green; September brings warm days, the wine harvest, and the opening of Oktoberfest. April and October are close behind and cheaper.

What is the cheapest time to visit Germany?

Mid-January through March is the cheapest window, with January and February the lowest of all for both flights and hotels — the two weeks around Christmas and New Year excepted. Early-to-mid November, before the Christmas markets open, is also very cheap. If you want milder weather with a deal, target late March to April and late October, at the edges of shoulder season.

When should I avoid Germany because of crowds?

July and August are the busiest and most expensive months, driven by warm weather and the summer school holidays. Munich is a special case: Oktoberfest (mid-September to the first weekend of October) books out the city and sends hotel rates soaring. December Christmas markets pack the historic centers of Nuremberg, Dresden, Cologne, and Munich, and Easter week is a shorter domestic peak.

Is Germany worth visiting in winter?

Yes, for the right trip. The Christmas markets from late November to December 24 are among the best in the world, the Bavarian Alps offer reliable skiing from December to March, and the art cities — Berlin, Munich, Dresden — are at their quietest and cheapest outside the holiday fortnight. The trade-off is cold, short days and that some castles, gardens, and river cruises run reduced schedules or close until spring.

What is shoulder season in Germany, and when are the Christmas markets?

Shoulder season is spring (April–May) and fall (late September–October), the stretches on either side of the summer peak — pleasant weather and full opening hours without the crowds and prices. The Christmas markets are a separate winter draw: most open in the last week of November and run through December 23 or 24, with Nuremberg, Dresden, Cologne, and Munich the headline names.
