Lake Fishing in Nevada & New Mexico — Desert Lakes, Big Trout and the Towns Beside Them

Pyramid Lake, Lake Mead, Lake Tahoe, Elephant Butte and Navajo Lake: what swims in each, the rules from the agencies that set them, the licence or permit a visitor needs, which ramps are open this season, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe, the National Park Service, the New Mexico Department of Wildlife and New Mexico State Parks, 21 November 2026

Nevada and New Mexico are desert states, and almost every big fishing lake in them is either very old or very new. Pyramid Lake, north-east of Reno, is what is left of an Ice Age inland sea; it belongs to the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe and is famous for very large Lahontan cutthroat trout. Lake Tahoe, high on the California line, is cold, clear and deep, with mackinaw (lake trout) far down. The rest are dams: Lake Mead on the Colorado River at Las Vegas, a striped-bass lake; Elephant Butte on the Rio Grande in southern New Mexico, for white bass, striped bass, largemouth and catfish; and Navajo Lake on the San Juan River in the north-west corner of New Mexico, for kokanee salmon, northern pike and smallmouth bass — with one of the best-known trout rivers in the West running out from under its dam.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, and anyone who wants a guide for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 21 November 2026 from the body that sets it, and is dated. Three things to know before you plan: Pyramid Lake needs a tribal permit, not a Nevada licence; Lake Mead and Elephant Butte are low, so ramps open and close with the water; and the Nevada Department of Wildlife’s website would not open for us today, so for Nevada’s own bag limits we send you to the department rather than guess. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

Desert summers are hot on Lake Mead and Elephant Butte; winters are cold at Pyramid, Tahoe and Navajo, which sit high.

Pyramid Lake — the tribe’s lake, and the giant cutthroat

Pyramid Lake lies entirely inside the Pyramid Lake Paiute Reservation. The tribe makes the rules and sells the permits; Nevada does not.

Pyramid Lake, Sutcliffe and Pelican Point

What swims there. Lahontan cutthroat trout, the fish people travel for, and Tahoe suckers. The lake is also the only home of the cui-ui, an endangered sucker; federal law bars anyone who is not a tribal member from fishing for it, and any sucker you hook must go straight back, because the two look alike.

How people fish it. From the beaches with fly rods and spinning rods, many anglers standing on a small step-ladder in the water so they can cast past the shallow shelf to the drop-off; and from boats, trolling. Bait of any kind is banned and every hook must be barbless.

The season. Trout season runs 1 October to 30 June. From 1 July to 30 September the lake is open only to shore fishing for species other than trout and cui-ui — wading is allowed, but no boats, ladders or float tubes. Fishing hours are from one hour before sunrise to one hour after sunset. The tribe’s fishing page gives 1 October 2026 as this season’s opener.

Keeping a fish. Two cutthroat a day, measured fork length, and only fish from 17 to 20 inches or 24 inches and longer may be kept — everything else goes back. Only one of the two may be 24 inches or longer. Possession is four, and only with proof you have fished more than one day. Fish may not be filleted on the reservation.

Ladders. Allowed, but they must be occupied or closely attended (a ladder left alone for more than an hour counts as littering), carry a permanent tag with the owner’s name, address and phone number, and go in the water only during fishing hours.

Places you may not fish. Within 1,000 feet of the Marble Bluff fishway, the Sutcliffe spawning channel and the mouth of the Truckee River; within 250 feet of docks, and never from a dock. Boats keep 1,000 feet from Anaho Island, the Needles and the Pyramid and Stone Mother rocks, which visitors may not enter, and 250 feet from anglers on the shore.

Boat ramps. The tribe names the Pelican Point ramp and Sutcliffe Marina. Every boat is inspected at the station where Sutcliffe Drive meets State Route 445, and needs current registration from its home state. Popcorn Rock is closed to launching because of the threat of golden mussels.

Confirmed with the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe, 21 November 2026 — the tribe’s permits and fishing pages and its regulations brochure (effective 1 October 2025, with the 2026–27 brochure linked from the permits page). Permit prices are in the licence section below.

Nearest towns: Sutcliffe, on the west shore, has the ranger station, and Nixon, at the south end, has the tribe’s museum and visitor centre; our partner shows no hotels in either, so they have no button. Most visitors sleep in Reno or Sparks, or in Fernley to the south-east, and drive up for the day.

Lake Mead — stripers on the Colorado River, beside Las Vegas

Lake Mead National Recreation Area, run by the National Park Service. The lake is shared with Arizona; this guide covers the Nevada side.

Lake Mead, Nevada side: Hemenway Harbor to Echo Bay

What swims there. The Park Service names striped bass, largemouth bass, rainbow trout, channel catfish, bluegill and black crappie, with smallmouth bass and sunfish as well.

How people fish it. By boat, for stripers under the gulls when they push shad to the top, and with anchovy bait or lures in deeper water; from the shore at the coves for bass and catfish. Our guidance: early morning, every season.

Keeping a fish. On Nevada water, Nevada’s rules apply. The Nevada Department of Wildlife’s website would not open for us today, so we cannot print its Lake Mead limits; read them at ndow.org before you keep a fish. The Park Service says the rules of whichever state you are in apply, so on the Arizona side check with the Arizona Game and Fish Department too.

The water and the ramps. The Bureau of Reclamation’s weekly report of 15 November 2026 put Lake Mead at 1,041.14 feet. The Park Service’s ramp page (updated 5 October 2026) lists Hemenway Harbor open with four lanes; Echo Bay open to motorboats but not houseboats, with a minimum safe level of 1,035 feet; South Cove a primitive ramp only, also 1,035 feet; and Callville Bay with a minimum safe level of 1,065 feet — above today’s water, so expect Callville’s ramp to be out of reach until the lake rises. Boulder Harbor, Las Vegas Bay and Overton Beach are closed; Government Wash is for boats without motors. With the lake this close to the minimums, check the Park Service’s page the day before you tow.

Quagga mussels. Lake Mead has them. Pull your drain plugs for the drive, and boats kept in a slip must be inspected and hot-washed before they leave. The Nevada Department of Wildlife runs free wash stations at Hemenway and Callville (9:30 am to 8 pm daily) and at Cottonwood Cove on Lake Mohave (Wednesday to Sunday). Motorboats not registered in Arizona, and most paddle craft, need a Nevada aquatic invasive species decal; paddleboards and float tubes do not.

Park fees. Seven days: $25 a vehicle, $20 a motorcycle, $15 a person on foot or bicycle; a Lake Mead annual pass is $45. Boats pay $16 for seven days or $50 for an annual sticker.

Confirmed with the National Park Service and the Bureau of Reclamation, 21 November 2026 — the Lake Mead fishing page (updated 16 September 2025), fees page (25 June 2026), changing-lake-levels ramp page (5 October 2026) and Clean, Drain, Dry page (23 December 2025), and Reclamation’s weekly report of 15 November 2026. Nevada’s bag limits: check with the Nevada Department of Wildlife.

Nearest towns: Boulder City for Hemenway Harbor and the south end; Henderson and Las Vegas for Callville Bay and Las Vegas Bay; Mesquite, a long drive north-east, is the nearest town with a pinned button for the Overton Arm and Echo Bay. Overton itself sits at the top of the arm, but our partner’s search sends its name to a village in England, so it has no button. For the city itself, see our Las Vegas guide.

Lake Tahoe — the Nevada shore

One lake, two states. Either state’s fishing licence covers the whole of it.

Lake Tahoe: Incline Village, Sand Harbor, Cave Rock and Zephyr Cove

What swims there. The charter boats on the lake fish for mackinaw (lake trout), rainbow and brown trout and kokanee salmon.

How people fish it. Mostly from charter and private boats, trolling or jigging deep for mackinaw; from the shore at the Nevada state park beaches where fishing is allowed. The water is very clear and very cold — dress for it.

The licence. California’s 2026 freshwater regulations say: “Anyone 16 years or older fishing anywhere in Lake Tahoe or Topaz Lake must possess either a California sport fishing license or a Nevada sport fishing license.” The same booklet adds that Nevada also requires a trout stamp; we could not confirm on the Nevada Department of Wildlife’s own site whether that is still true, so check with the department. California’s rules also close the streams that feed the lake, and 300 feet around their mouths, except from the Saturday before Memorial Day to 30 September, with barbless artificial lures only and no trout kept.

Keeping a fish. The Nevada Department of Wildlife publishes a 2026 Lake Tahoe Angler Information Guide. Its website would not open for us today, so we do not print its limits; read the guide before you keep a fish.

Boat inspection is mandatory. Every motorboat must be inspected and sealed before it launches at Tahoe. The 2026 stickers (good for the calendar year): Tahoe In & Out $70 for boats up to 17 feet and personal watercraft, $115 for longer boats; Tahoe-Only $50; a single inspection $65 or $95. Decontamination costs extra, and arriving clean, drained and dry halves it. Hand-launched boats without motors are inspected free. In winter the only station is at Meyers, on the California side, on select Wednesdays by appointment.

Boat ramps, Nevada side. Sand Harbor’s ramp in Lake Tahoe Nevada State Park is closed for the season. Cave Rock has a double ramp, open 6 am to 4 pm outside summer (6 am to 8 pm from 25 May to 4 September); entry is $10 and launching $20 for Nevada vehicles ($15 and $25 for others). There is no fishing on the beach or in the ramp area at Cave Rock.

Confirmed with the California Department of Fish and Wildlife, the Tahoe boat inspection program and Nevada State Parks, 21 November 2026 — California’s 2026 Freshwater Sport Fishing Regulations (updated 10 July 2026), tahoeboatinspections.com stickers, fees and locations pages, and the Sand Harbor, Cave Rock and park fee pages. Nevada’s Tahoe limits: check with the Nevada Department of Wildlife.

Nearest towns: Incline Village on the north shore, near Sand Harbor; Zephyr Cove and Stateline on the south-east shore, near Cave Rock; Carson City, over the hill, for cheaper rooms. The California side is in our Northern California lake fishing guide.

Elephant Butte — a Rio Grande lake, rising again

A Rio Grande reservoir near Truth or Consequences, inside Elephant Butte Lake State Park. Caballo Lake is just downstream.

Elephant Butte Lake and Caballo Lake

What swims there. New Mexico State Parks names largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, white bass, striped bass, walleye, crappie, flathead and channel catfish and bluegill. Caballo Lake is stocked with wipers (hybrid striped bass).

How people fish it. By boat along the rocky points and the old river channel; from the shore for catfish. Live and dead baitfish are allowed here — fathead minnows, red shiners, shad and golden shiners only — and trotlines are allowed. The same bait and trotline rules apply at Caballo.

Keeping a fish. New Mexico’s statewide limits apply; the 2026–27 rules booklet lists no special limits for Elephant Butte. Largemouth 5 a day at 14 inches or more; smallmouth 12 inches or more within the same 5; white bass and wipers 25; striped bass 1; walleye 5; crappie 20; catfish 15.

The water and the ramps. The lake was nearly empty this summer: on 3 August 2026 State Parks closed the main ramp with the reservoir at about 1.4 per cent of capacity. It is coming back. The Bureau of Reclamation’s daily figures show 258,154 acre-feet on 20 November 2026, up from 143,256 on 1 November. On 6 November State Parks reported the Marina del Sur and Rock Canyon ramps accessible again without four-wheel drive (watch for submerged debris), and on 9 November listed the Main Ramp and Rock Canyon open and South Monticello closed. The Long Point area is closed from 9 November 2026 to 1 March 2027, and the Cliffs area is closed indefinitely. At Caballo, the West Ramp is open and the East Ramp closed (9 November).

Park fees. Day use at any New Mexico state park is $5 for residents and $10 for visitors; residents pay no day-use fee from 1 October to 30 April. An annual day-use pass is $5 for residents or $10 for visitors. Day-use hours at Elephant Butte are 6 am to 9 pm.

Eating the fish. New Mexico’s fish consumption advisories cover several species here, mostly for mercury, and are set by length — for example only one meal a month of striped bass 18 inches and longer. Read the advisory table in the 2026–27 rules booklet before you plan a fish fry.

Confirmed with the New Mexico Department of Wildlife, New Mexico State Parks and the Bureau of Reclamation, 21 November 2026 — the 2026–2027 New Mexico Fishing Rules & Info, the Elephant Butte boating page, park alerts posted 6–9 November 2026, the 5 August 2026 low-water notice, the fees page, and Reclamation’s daily storage data to 20 November 2026.

Nearest towns: Elephant Butte, right by the lake, and Truth or Consequences, just down the road, which has more rooms.

Navajo Lake — kokanee, pike, and the San Juan below the dam

A San Juan River reservoir in the far north-west of New Mexico. Its upper end crosses into Colorado.

Navajo Lake: the Pine and Sims Mesa sides

What swims there. Kokanee salmon (stocked), northern pike, smallmouth and largemouth bass, crappie, rainbow trout, channel catfish, yellow perch and bluegill.

How people fish it. By boat, trolling for kokanee in summer and snagging them in autumn; casting the rocky banks for smallmouth and the coves for pike.

Keeping a fish. Statewide limits: trout and kokanee together 5 a day; northern pike 10; black bass 5 (largemouth 14 inches, smallmouth 12 inches); crappie 20; catfish 15. Kokanee snagging is open 1 October to 31 December, 12 a day and 24 in possession — kokanee are the only fish that may be snagged, and the buoyed no-wake areas and the shoreline at the Pine River and Sims ramps are closed to snagging all season.

Getting there and launching. State Parks lists a marina at Pine, with rentals, guides, a restaurant and slips, and Sims Marina, with rentals, fuel and a store. On 9 November it rated the lake “Good”, at about 1.5 million acre-feet of a 1.7 million acre-foot reservoir. Road warning: since 25 October 2026 NM 539 has been closed from mile marker 0 to 5 after flood damage, with no reopening date; use NM 511 near Blanco or NM 173 from Aztec. Camping is by reservation only, and Stage I fire restrictions were posted on 13 October. The park says its boat decontamination unit is out of service. Ramp-by-ramp status was not posted when we checked.

The Colorado end. The upper lake lies in Colorado, where Colorado’s licence and rules apply. Check with Colorado Parks and Wildlife before you fish north of the line; we could not find the exact rule stated on a New Mexico page.

The San Juan River below the dam. The famous trout water. For 3.75 miles from Navajo Dam down to the Crusher Hole day-use area it is Special Trout Water: “artificial fly or lure with a single, barbless hook. Catch and release only,” and no more than two flies or lures on a line. Bait fish, chumming and disturbing the bottom are banned in all Special Trout Waters. Outfitters at Navajo Dam reported flooding on the river in early November 2026; check a current report before you go.

Confirmed with the New Mexico Department of Wildlife, New Mexico State Parks and the Bureau of Reclamation, 21 November 2026 — the 2026–2027 New Mexico Fishing Rules & Info, the Navajo Lake State Park page (updated 6 October 2026), the park alerts and boating conditions pages (9 November 2026), and Reclamation’s Navajo Dam page.

Nearest towns: Aztec, on NM 173 towards the Pine side; Bloomfield; and Farmington, the biggest town in the corner of the state. The village of Navajo Dam has the fly shops and lodges, but our partner’s search sends its name to Navajo County in Arizona, so it has no button.

The licence and the limits, plainly

Three different papers: a tribal permit for Pyramid Lake, a Nevada licence for Lake Mead (or Nevada or California for Tahoe), and a New Mexico licence for Elephant Butte and Navajo.

Pyramid Lake — Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe permit

Who needs one. Everyone who is not a tribal member, 12 and older. Children under 12 fish free with a permitted adult. No Nevada licence is needed.

Prices. Fishing, adult: $27 for one day, $70 for three. Youth 12 to 17: $15 and $39. Second rod: $27 and $70. Boating: $30 and $70. Day use: $25 and $65. Camping: $35 and $90. The tribe sells no season fishing permit; there is a $125 season day-use pass for those 65 and older.

Where to buy. Online at pyramidlake.us; at the Ranger Station, 2500 Lakeview Drive, Sutcliffe (daily 6 am to 6 pm, phone 775-476-1155); at the museum and visitor centre in Nixon (weekdays); or at the Nixon Store. Vendors may add up to $1.

Confirmed with the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe, 21 November 2026. The tribe’s permits page says permits are sold 1 October to 30 June, while a footnote on the same page reads differently; the regulations brochure gives trout season as 1 October to 30 June.

Nevada — Lake Mead and Lake Tahoe

Who needs one. Anyone 12 and older. Nevada residents under 12 need no licence; visiting children under 12 need none either, but may keep only half the limit.

What Nevada law sets. The fee schedule in Nevada’s statutes (NRS 502.240): resident annual fishing $40; resident one day $9, plus $3 for each extra day in a row; non-resident annual $80; non-resident one day $18, plus $7 for each extra day in a row. A Nevada licence runs for a year from the day you buy it.

Buy it from the Nevada Department of Wildlife (ndow.org) or a licence agent, and check the price there: the department’s own pages would not open for us today, so we could not confirm the retail price, any fees, or whether a trout stamp is still required. On Lake Tahoe a California licence also works (see the Tahoe section).

Checked against the Nevada Revised Statutes, 21 November 2026. Always confirm with the Nevada Department of Wildlife before you fish.

New Mexico (licence year 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027)

Who needs one. Anyone 12 and older. No New Mexico licence is needed on tribal land, where the tribe’s own permit applies.

For a visitor. Non-resident annual $90; five days $30; one day $12; junior (12 to 17) $20. A second-rod validation is $10.

For a New Mexican. Resident annual $35; five days $30; one day $12; junior $5; 65 to 69 $8; 70 and older free.

Two add-ons. The Habitat Management & Access Validation, $4, is needed once a licence year by everyone 18 and older and is added to your basket automatically. A Habitat Stamp, $10, is needed only when you fish on U.S. Forest Service or Bureau of Land Management land.

Boats. Stop at any boat inspection station that is open; boats from out of state must be inspected. Every boat in New Mexico travels with its plug pulled and its water drained.

Confirmed with the New Mexico Department of Wildlife, 21 November 2026 — the 2026–2027 New Mexico Fishing Rules & Info, the licences page and the aquatic invasive species page. The department was called the Department of Game and Fish until recently. Buy at onlinesales.wildlife.state.nm.us, on 1-888-248-6866, or from a licence vendor; a vendor fee applies.

Questions

Do I need a Nevada fishing licence at Pyramid Lake?

No. Pyramid Lake lies inside the Pyramid Lake Paiute Reservation, and the tribe sells its own permits: an adult fishing permit is $27 for one day or $70 for three, and youth 12 to 17 pay $15 or $39. Children under 12 fish free with a permitted adult. Trout season runs 1 October to 30 June; you may keep two cutthroat a day, only between 17 and 20 inches or 24 inches and longer, with barbless hooks and no bait. Checked with the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe on 21 November 2026.

Which licence do I need for Lake Tahoe?

Either one. California's 2026 freshwater regulations say anyone 16 or older fishing anywhere in Lake Tahoe must have either a California or a Nevada sport fishing licence. Bag limits differ by state, and the Nevada Department of Wildlife publishes a 2026 Lake Tahoe Angler Information Guide; its website would not open for us, so read the guide there before you keep a fish. Every motorboat must also pass the Tahoe boat inspection before launching.

Can I launch a boat on Lake Mead right now?

Yes, at some ramps. On 15 November 2026 the Bureau of Reclamation put the lake at 1,041.14 feet. The National Park Service lists Hemenway Harbor open with four lanes, Echo Bay open to motorboats and South Cove as a primitive ramp, both with a minimum safe level of 1,035 feet. Callville Bay's minimum is 1,065 feet, above today's water. Boulder Harbor, Las Vegas Bay and Overton Beach are closed. Check the Park Service's ramp page the day before you tow.

What does a New Mexico fishing licence cost a visitor?

For the licence year 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027: non-resident annual $90, five days $30, one day $12, junior (12 to 17) $20, plus the $4 Habitat Management and Access Validation for anyone 18 or older, and a $10 Habitat Stamp only on Forest Service or Bureau of Land Management land. Children under 12 need no licence. Checked with the New Mexico Department of Wildlife on 21 November 2026; a vendor fee applies.

Is the San Juan River below Navajo Dam catch and release?

Yes, for the first 3.75 miles. From Navajo Dam down to the Crusher Hole day-use area the San Juan is Special Trout Water: artificial flies or lures with a single barbless hook, catch and release only, and no more than two flies or lures on a line. Checked with the New Mexico Department of Wildlife's 2026-2027 rules booklet on 21 November 2026.

Arizona on Lake Mead. Whether a Nevada licence covers you on the Arizona side of Lake Mead was not stated on any page we could open. Ask the Nevada Department of Wildlife or the Arizona Game and Fish Department.

Best months. None of the agencies publishes a month-by-month calendar for these lakes. The months table is our guidance, and says so.

Ramps. Lake Mead is close to the minimum safe level at several ramps and below it at Callville Bay, and Elephant Butte is refilling from a near-empty summer. We give what the agencies said in October and November 2026; check again the day before you tow.

Navajo Lake ramps and the Colorado line. New Mexico State Parks did not post ramp-by-ramp status when we checked, and we found the licence rule for the Colorado end only by inference. Ask the park and Colorado Parks and Wildlife.

Elephant Butte businesses. We could not open a working, current website of their own for any marina or guide at Elephant Butte, so none is listed. That is not a judgement on them.

Towns with no button. Sutcliffe and Nixon (no rooms shown by our partner), Overton (sent to England) and Navajo Dam (sent to Arizona) are real lake towns without a booking button. Use the neighbouring towns.

Hotel lists in small towns. Our hotel buttons are pinned to town numbers checked two ways with our own resolver. We do not open Trip.com’s hotel-list pages to count the hotels, so small towns may show few.
