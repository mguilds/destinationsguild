When to Go to France

The Best Time to Visit France

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Short answer: the shoulder seasons — roughly April–June and September–October — are the sweet spot. You get warm, settled weather and full opening hours without the summer heat, queues, and peak prices. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the crowds, and the best timing for Paris, the coast, the vineyards, and the Alps.

France is a year-round destination, but it is not the same destination year-round. The country you meet on a golden October afternoon — harvest in the vineyards, warm light on limestone, room to breathe in the Louvre — is a very different place from mid-August, when the mercury climbs, the Riviera is elbow-to-elbow, and half of Paris has locked up and left for the coast. Choosing your dates well is the single biggest lever you have over the trip. Here is how to pull it.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$ ShoulderMild & bloomingBest for citiesFewer crowds

The countryside greens up, orchards and gardens come alive, and city sightseeing is a pleasure at 15–22°C. Late April and May are prime — everything is open, parks are at their peak, and the summer crowds haven't yet arrived. Watch the clusters of spring public holidays: Easter, plus May 1, May 8, and Ascension, when French travelers take long weekends and popular spots fill. March can still be cool and showery, but it's cheap.

Summer — June to August

$$$$ PeakWarm to hotCoast & festivalsBook early

High season, and it feels like it. June is the most pleasant of the three — the longest days of the year, warm seas, and lavender beginning in Provence — before July and August bring real heat and the biggest crowds and prices of the year. This is the time for the Riviera, the Atlantic beaches, and the Alps for hiking, not for marathon Paris sightseeing in the midday sun. If summer is your only window, book flights and hotels months ahead and start your days early.

Autumn — September to November

$$ ShoulderWarm & mellowHarvest & foodGreat value

Many travelers' favorite season. September still has warm water on the Mediterranean and summer light with the peak crowds ebbing; October is mellow, golden, and one of the best-value months of the year, with the grape harvest (les vendanges) across Burgundy, Bordeaux, and Champagne. Rain increases as November arrives, but the cities are quiet and prices soft — ideal for Paris, Lyon, and the châteaux of the Loire.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold & quietSki & marketsCheapest

The bargain season, and a genuinely lovely one for the right trip: crowd-free Paris, the Christmas markets of Alsace, and top-tier skiing in the French Alps and Pyrenees. Days are short and cold, and most of the Mediterranean coast — the Riviera resort towns and the smaller islands — largely shuts down. Sidestep the two-week Christmas–New Year spike and the peak ski weeks, and January and February are the cheapest weeks in France.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ CheapestColdSki season

The quietest, cheapest month once the New Year holiday passes. Cold and often grey; ideal for near-empty museums in Paris, the winter sales (les soldes), and prime skiing in the Alps. Book cities and the mountains, not the coast.

February

$ CheapColdCarnival

Still low season and low prices, with two bright exceptions: the Nice Carnival and Menton's Lemon Festival on the Riviera, and the busiest weeks of the ski season, when Alpine resorts and their rates peak. Almond blossom starts in the far south. A great month for a quiet, affordable city break.

March

$$ Value shoulderCool, variableSpring begins

Shoulder season opens. Weather is variable — mild days, cool evenings, some rain — but crowds are thin and prices low. Cities shine; the coast is only just stirring. If Easter falls in late March, expect a short surge around it.

April

$$ ShoulderMild & greenEaster peak

One of the loveliest months: green countryside, blossom, comfortable sightseeing weather, and gardens such as Giverny reopening. The catch is Easter and the run of spring holidays, which bring domestic crowds and higher rates. Outside those dates, superb value and mood.

May

$$$ PrimeWarm & settledEverything open

Arguably one of the best all-round months. Warm, stable weather, long days, gardens at their peak, and the full summer machinery open before the crowds and heat arrive. Note a busy events calendar — the Cannes Film Festival, the Monaco Grand Prix, and the French Open all fall in May, along with several public holidays. Popular, so book ahead.

June

$$$$ Peak beginsWarm, long daysCoast & lavender

Summer proper, and one of the most rewarding months of the year. The longest days, warm seas, and the lavender beginning to bloom in Provence, with crowds and prices climbing steadily toward the July–August peak. Excellent for the Riviera, the Atlantic coast, and Alpine hiking.

July

$$$$ PeakHotBusy

Hot, crowded, and pricey, especially on the coast. This is the month of Bastille Day (July 14), the Tour de France, and the Avignon and Aix festivals, with the Provence lavender at its peak on the Valensole plateau. Great for beaches and mountain trails; hard work for midday city sightseeing. Reserve accommodation, trains, and headline sights well in advance.

August

$$$$ PeakHottestGrandes vacances

The hottest, busiest month — and the trickiest. This is when France takes its own holidays: the Riviera, Provence, and the Atlantic beaches are packed and expensive, while in Paris and other cities many family-run shops and restaurants close for a few weeks. Doable, and the capital can feel unusually calm, but plan around the closures and the heat.

September

$$$ PrimeWarm seasHarvest

The other star month. Summer warmth and swimmable Mediterranean seas linger while the crowds thin and prices ease after the first week. The grape harvest begins in the wine regions, food festivals multiply, and the cities become comfortable again. A superb time for coast and culture combined.

October

$$ Best valueMild & goldenVineyards

Mellow, golden, and one of the best-value months of the year. Comfortable city weather, autumn color, the tail of the wine harvest, and low crowds. Rain increases late in the month and the Mediterranean coast starts winding down, but Paris and the châteaux country are at their calm best.

November

$ Low seasonCool & wetQuiet cities

Low season returns: cool, damp, and quiet. Days are short and skies often grey. But prices are low, museums empty, and Paris and Lyon rewarding for travelers who don't mind a coat and an umbrella — and the third Thursday brings the release of Beaujolais Nouveau.

December

$ → $$$ SplitFestiveSki & markets

A month of two halves. Early December is quiet and cheap, with the famous Christmas markets of Alsace (Strasbourg and Colmar) and the ski resorts opening. The two weeks around Christmas and New Year are a sharp peak — festive and beautiful, but book early and expect higher rates, especially in the mountains.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Mid-November to March — with January and February the lowest

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSkip Christmas fortnight

Transatlantic airfares and city hotel rates bottom out in deep winter. January and February are the cheapest weeks of the year for both, with mid-November and early December close behind — the carve-outs being the roughly two-week Christmas–New Year holiday and, in the mountains, the peak Alpine ski weeks, when prices jump. You'll trade warmth and coastal life for the savings, but for Paris city breaks the value is unbeatable.

Want warm weather and a deal? Target the edges of shoulder season: late March to mid-April (avoiding Easter) and late October. You get mild days and open sights at a fraction of the July–August cost. As a rule, midweek flights and stays beat weekends, and booking two to three months out lands the best fares.

When to Avoid the Crowds

The busy periods in France are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks to sidestep

July & AugustGrandes vacancesEasterChristmas & NY

July and August are the busiest, hottest, and most expensive stretch, with August the peak. During the French summer holidays the coast is at its fullest and priciest, while in the cities many businesses close for their own holidays. Easter week and the Christmas–New Year fortnight are shorter, sharper spikes, and the spring public-holiday long weekends (May 1, May 8, Ascension) briefly fill popular towns.

To have the big sights closer to yourself, come in the shoulder months — late April, May, June, September, October — or in winter. Whenever you go, the crowd-beating tactics are the same: reserve timed-entry tickets for the Louvre, the Eiffel Tower, Versailles, and the Musée d'Orsay in advance, and visit at opening or in the last hours of the day. Best Time by Region

France is large and varied — the ideal month depends on where you're headed.

Paris & the Cities — Paris, Lyon, Bordeaux

Best: Apr–Jun, Sep–OctValue: Nov–FebAvoid: Aug closures

Cities are about walking, queuing, and lingering in cafés and gardens — so mild weather is your friend. Spring and autumn are ideal; winter is quiet and cheap if you can take the cold and grey. August is hot and sees many shops and bistros shuttered for the holidays, though the capital itself thins out. See where to stay in Paris.

The Riviera, Provence & the Coast

Best: late May–Jun, SepSeason: Easter–OctLavender: late Jun–mid-Jul

The Mediterranean south runs on a season — roughly Easter through October, with many beach hotels and restaurants quiet the rest of the year. The sweet spots are late May to June and September: warm water, open everything, and lighter crowds than the July–August crush. For the Provence lavender, aim for late June to mid-July, when the Valensole plateau is in full bloom. Plan your base in Nice.

The French Alps & Pyrenees

Ski: Dec–AprHike: Jun–SepAvoid: May, Nov

The mountains have two seasons and two dead zones. December to April is for skiing, with the most reliable snow in January, February, and early March. June to September is for hiking, wildflower meadows, and via ferrata, with July and August warmest and busiest. The shoulder gaps — late spring and November — see many lifts, huts, and hotels closed, so aim for the core of each season.

Wine Country & the Loire — Burgundy, Bordeaux, Champagne

Best: May–Jun, Sep–OctHarvest: Sep–OctAvoid: deep winter

The vineyards and châteaux are at their finest in the shoulder seasons. Late spring brings green vines and open gardens along the Loire; early autumn brings the grape harvest across Burgundy, Bordeaux, and Champagne, with tastings, festivals, and golden light. Winter is quiet and many smaller estates close, so spring and fall are the times to plan a tasting tour.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a French trip.

What is the best month to visit France?

May, June, and September are the strongest all-round months. You get warm, settled weather, long daylight, everything open, and noticeably thinner crowds and lower prices than July and August. May and June lean lush, with long days and gardens at their peak; September brings warm seas on the coast and the start of the wine harvest. April and October are close behind and even cheaper.

What is the cheapest time to visit France?

Mid-November through March is the cheapest window, with January and February the lowest of all for both flights and hotels — the two weeks around Christmas and New Year, and the peak Alpine ski weeks, excepted. If you want warm-weather value, the true bargains sit at the edges of shoulder season: late March to mid-April and late October.

When should I avoid France because of crowds?

July and August are the most crowded and expensive months, with August the peak. During the French summer holidays the Riviera, Provence, and the Atlantic beaches are jammed and pricey, while in Paris and other cities many family-run shops and restaurants close for a few weeks. Easter week and the Christmas–New Year period are also short, sharp peaks.

Is France worth visiting in winter?

Yes, for the right trip. Winter is the best time for crowd-free Paris and the art cities at their quietest and cheapest, plus the Christmas markets of Alsace and world-class skiing in the French Alps from December to April. The trade-off is cold, short days and that most of the Mediterranean coast, from the Riviera to the smaller resort towns, largely shuts down until spring.

What is shoulder season in France and why is it best?

Shoulder season is spring (April–June) and fall (September–October), the stretches on either side of the summer peak. It's widely considered the best time to visit France because you get pleasant weather and full opening hours without the peak-season heat, queues, and prices — the sweet spot for combining Paris and the cities with the coast, the vineyards, or the countryside.
