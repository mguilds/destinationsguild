Sharjah — the cultural emirate

Where to Stay in Sharjah — Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Sharjah begins where Dubai ends — the two cities have effectively grown into one another — and it is the emirate people stay in by accident and visit on purpose. On purpose, because it holds the best museums in the country and a beautifully restored old quarter. By accident, because a cheap room advertised as being near Dubai is often here. Both are good reasons to read the one rule that matters first: Sharjah is dry. No alcohol is sold or served anywhere in the emirate, in any hotel, and bringing it in is an offence. If that is fine with you, this is the best value on this stretch of coast.

The Heart of Sharjah & the museum quarter

The restored old town along the creek — coral-stone houses, wind towers, the Al Noor mosque across the water and a cluster of genuinely good museums, including the Sharjah Art Museum and the Museum of Islamic Civilization in the old souk building. This is the reason to come to Sharjah rather than pass through it, and it is a short walk end to end.

The Chedi Al Bait, Sharjah

$$$$ LuxuryInside the restored old townBuilt into historic housesCourtyard pools

Built into and around restored merchant houses in the Heart of Sharjah, a few steps from the creek and the museums, and the most characterful hotel anywhere in the Emirates. Rooms are arranged around quiet courtyards with low buildings, palms and water; the restoration work is serious and the service is calm. There is no bar, as there is nowhere in the emirate. Best for a traveller who wants the old Gulf rather than the new one, and will pay for it.

Al Majaz & the Khalid Lagoon

The modern centre, built around a lagoon with a landscaped waterfront park, a musical fountain and a line of cafés. It is the pleasantest place in the emirate to spend an evening on foot, it is safe and busy with families until late, and it is ten minutes from the old town.

The Act Hotel Sharjah

$$$ UpscaleOn the Al Majaz waterfrontModern roomsLagoon views

A contemporary hotel right on the Al Majaz waterfront, with the park, the fountain and the restaurant strip immediately outside and lagoon views from the better rooms. Comfortable, well-finished and considerably cheaper than an equivalent room across the border in Dubai. Best for a city stay with somewhere to walk in the evening.

Al Khan & the beach road

Sharjah’s Gulf coast runs along Al Khan and Al Taawun, which is the stretch closest to Dubai — close enough that the border is a few minutes away and the traffic between the two is the single biggest practical issue of staying here. Allow for it generously at rush hour. The beaches are decent and much quieter than Dubai’s.

Sheraton Sharjah Beach Resort & Spa

$$$ UpscaleOn the beachFamily facilitiesSpa and pools

The most complete beach resort in the emirate: its own stretch of sand, several pools, a spa and the family facilities you would expect, at a rate well below the comparable Dubai product. Rooms are large and sea-facing on the right side. Remember there is no bar and no wine with dinner. Best for a beach holiday where the saving matters more than the drink.

The east coast — Khor Fakkan

Sharjah owns an exclave on the other side of the country, on the Gulf of Oman, and it is one of the most scenic places in the Emirates: a curved bay backed by the Hajar mountains, a landscaped corniche, a waterfall amphitheatre and genuinely clear water for snorkelling. It is about a ninety-minute drive from Sharjah city through the mountains and well worth a night or two.

Oceanic Khorfakkan Resort & Spa

$$$ UpscaleOn Khor Fakkan bayDive centreMountain backdrop

The long-established hotel on the Khor Fakkan corniche, with the bay in front and the mountains behind, and a dive centre that is the reason many guests come — the east-coast water is clearer and cooler than the Gulf side. The building is not new and makes no pretence otherwise; the position is exceptional. Best for divers and snorkellers.

Where to stay in Sharjah — questions

Is Sharjah really completely dry?

Yes. Sharjah is the only emirate in which alcohol is not sold or served at all — there are no licensed bars, no wine lists in restaurants and no minibars, including in the international beach resorts. It is also an offence to bring alcohol into the emirate or to transport it through, which matters if you are driving between Dubai and the east coast with duty-free in the boot. None of this affects anything else about a stay, and many visitors never notice, but it should be a deliberate choice rather than a surprise on the first evening.

Is it a good idea to stay in Sharjah to save money on a Dubai trip?

It can be, with two conditions. The first is the alcohol rule above. The second is the traffic: the Dubai-Sharjah road carries one of the heaviest commutes in the region and a journey that takes twenty minutes at midday can take well over an hour at the start and end of the working day. If you are staying in Al Khan or Al Taawun, right at the border, and you plan your crossings outside the peaks, the saving is real. If your hotel is deeper into Sharjah, the time will cost you more than the money saves.

What is actually worth seeing in Sharjah?

More than most visitors expect. The emirate has invested heavily in culture for decades and holds the best museum collection in the country: the Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization in the old souk building, the Sharjah Art Museum, and the restored Heart of Sharjah quarter around them, which is a genuine piece of conservation rather than a reconstruction. Add the Al Noor mosque, which runs tours for non-Muslim visitors, the Al Majaz waterfront in the evening, and the Khor Fakkan coast on the other side of the mountains.

How do I get to Khor Fakkan?

By road, over the Hajar mountains, in about ninety minutes from Sharjah city or a little over an hour from Fujairah. The mountain road is good and the drive is one of the better ones in the country. There is no rail link and no useful public transport for a visitor, so this means a hire car or a long taxi; most people make it part of an east-coast trip rather than a day return.

Is Sharjah airport a good way to arrive?

It is cheap, and for low-cost flights it is often the only option. It is small, straightforward and quick to clear. The thing to check before booking is where you are actually staying: Sharjah airport is reasonable for Sharjah and for the northern end of Dubai, but if your hotel is in the Dubai Marina or on the Palm you are looking at a long and expensive transfer, and a cheaper fare into Dubai International may work out better overall.

What should I wear?

Sharjah applies a published decency code more visibly than the other emirates, and it is a reasonable request rather than an onerous one: shoulders and knees covered in public places, nothing tight or transparent, and swimwear confined to the beach and the pool. Hotel beaches and pools operate normally. Dressing modestly costs a visitor nothing and is noticed and appreciated here.
