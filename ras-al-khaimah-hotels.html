Ras Al Khaimah — mountains and sand

Where to Stay in Ras Al Khaimah — Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Ras Al Khaimah is the northernmost emirate and the one with the landscape. Behind a flat coastal strip of beaches and resorts, the Hajar mountains rise to Jebel Jais, the highest point in the country, with the longest zipline in the world running off it. An hour and a quarter from Dubai’s airport, it is where people go for the same winter sun at a lower price and with somewhere to walk. The choice is between the resort island at Al Marjan, the old town on the creek, and the desert and mountains inland.

Al Marjan Island & the resort coast

Al Marjan is a set of reclaimed coral-shaped islands joined to the mainland by a causeway, and it holds most of the emirate’s beach hotels. The beaches are good, the water is calm, and the whole strip is quieter and cheaper than its equivalent in Dubai. There is little to walk to beyond your own resort, so expect to eat where you sleep or take a taxi.

Ras Al Khaimah town & the creek

The working emirate: a creek with dhows, the old fort and the national museum, a corniche, and the ruined pearling village of Al Jazirah Al Hamra a short way down the coast, which is the most atmospheric thing in the emirate and free to walk through. Hotels here are plainer and much cheaper than the island, and you are closer to the mountain road.

Built down a hillside around its own small bay between the town and Al Marjan, in a village of low domed buildings rather than a tower, with a quiet private beach and a marina. It has been there longer than most of the competition and is the better for it: mature planting, grown-up service and a genuine sense of place. Best for a calm beach stay with somewhere to walk.

The desert & the mountains

The reason to choose Ras Al Khaimah over the other emirates. Jebel Jais rises to nearly two thousand metres about an hour inland on an excellent mountain road, with viewing platforms, a via ferrata, a toboggan run and the longest zipline in the world; it is several degrees cooler than the coast and can be genuinely cold on a winter night. Between the coast and the mountains lies a protected desert reserve.

The Ritz-Carlton Ras Al Khaimah, Al Wadi Desert

$$$$ LuxuryDesert reservePrivate-pool villasFalconry and horses

Tented villas, most with their own pool, spread across a private desert reserve inland from the coast, with oryx and gazelle outside and falconry, horse riding and night-sky sessions on the programme. Guests can use a sister beach resort on the coast, which solves the one thing a desert hotel cannot. Best for a desert night done properly, and the best in the country that is within an easy drive of Dubai.

Where to stay in Ras Al Khaimah — questions

How far is Ras Al Khaimah from Dubai?

About an hour and a quarter from Dubai International by road in normal traffic, and rather less from the northern parts of Dubai. The emirate also has its own small airport at Ras Al Khaimah, which takes some low-cost and charter traffic. Most visitors arrive through Dubai and drive or take a transfer; a hire car is worth having here because the mountains, the desert and the old town are all spread out.

Is Ras Al Khaimah cheaper than Dubai?

Generally yes, for a comparable standard of beach resort, and the gap is widest in the winter peak when Dubai's rates climb hardest. You are paying less for the same sunshine and better beaches than you would find in central Dubai. What you give up is choice: the restaurant and nightlife options outside the resorts are limited, and most guests end up eating in their own hotel most evenings.

Is alcohol served in Ras Al Khaimah?

Yes. Licensed hotels and restaurants serve alcohol in Ras Al Khaimah, unlike neighbouring Sharjah, which is entirely dry. The usual rules about not drinking in public and not being drunk in public apply, as they do across the Emirates.

Is Jebel Jais worth the trip?

Yes, and it is the thing that distinguishes this emirate from its neighbours. The road up is a feat of engineering and a pleasure to drive, the viewing platforms look down into a deep gorge, and the temperature at the top is several degrees cooler than the coast — which is why Emirati families drive up on summer evenings. The zipline is the longest in the world and needs booking ahead; the via ferrata and the toboggan run are there for those who would rather not. Take a jacket even in spring.

What is the ruined village at Al Jazirah Al Hamra?

An abandoned pearling and fishing settlement of coral-block houses, mosques and a fort, largely left as it was when its inhabitants moved out in the middle of the last century. It is one of the very few places in the Emirates where an entire traditional village survives rather than a restored fragment, it is free to walk around, and it is best in the late afternoon. It sits just south of Al Marjan, so it is an easy walk or a very short drive from most of the resort hotels.

When should I come?

November to March, as everywhere in the country, with the mountains adding a reason to prefer the cooler months rather than endure them — a winter day on Jebel Jais is genuinely crisp. Summer here is as severe as the rest of the Gulf coast, though the mountain is the local escape from it. Our best time to visit page sets out the whole year.
