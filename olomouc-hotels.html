Where to Stay in Olomouc

Where to Stay in Olomouc — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Olomouc is the one we would push hardest. It has the second-largest historic reservation in the Czech Republic after Prague, a UNESCO-listed Holy Trinity Column that is the largest baroque sculpture group in Central Europe, six baroque fountains, a cathedral, a university that has been going since 1573 — and almost no tourists. It also has something the rest of this book cannot offer: the historic centre is flat. Genuinely, reliably flat, with the squares and the streets between them level underfoot. For an unhurried traveller who has found Prague’s cobbled hills hard going, Olomouc is the answer. Choosing Your Area

The historic centre is small, level and where you want to be. That is very nearly the whole of the advice.

The two squares, the cathedral quarter, or out by the congress hotels?

OrientationFlatSmallQuiet

The historic centre runs between Horní náměstí (the Upper Square, with the town hall, the astronomical clock and the Holy Trinity Column) and Dolní náměstí (the Lower Square) — a fifteen-minute walk end to end, level the whole way, with the baroque fountains scattered between them. Stay here. The cathedral quarter around St Wenceslas, a few minutes east, is quieter still and equally flat, and holds the archbishop’s palace and the university buildings. The congress and business hotels sit a little outside the centre; they are larger and cheaper and perfectly comfortable, and Olomouc’s trams make the distance trivial. Unlike almost everywhere else in this book, there is no hill to factor in and no bad choice to make.

Luxury & the larger hotels

No adults-only hotels here — but plenty of quiet

Honest noteVerifiedNo invented listingsBrowse instead

Olomouc is a small university city, not a resort, and we could confirm no formally adults-only property on Trip.com. What we can tell you is where the quiet is: the cathedral quarter east of the Upper Square is genuinely still after dark, and the small historic hotels there — Arigone above — are peaceful. The student bars cluster around the lower end of the centre and can be lively at weekends in term time, so book towards the cathedral rather than towards Dolní náměstí if you are a light sleeper.

Olomouc Hotel FAQ

Why should I go to Olomouc at all?

Because it is the second-largest historic reservation in the country, it has a UNESCO-listed baroque column, six extraordinary fountains, a cathedral and a 1573 university — and almost nobody visits it. You will hear Czech spoken around you rather than English, pay half what Prague charges, and walk a beautiful historic centre without queueing for anything.

How do I get there from Prague?

By train, in about two hours, on frequent fast services run by both the state railway and the private operators. Olomouc is on the main line towards Ostrava and Krakow, so services are plentiful and cheap. The station is a short tram ride or a twenty-minute flat walk from the centre.

Is Olomouc really flat?

Yes — and it is the main practical reason we recommend it to unhurried travellers. The historic centre between the Upper and Lower Squares is level, the cathedral quarter is level, and the ring of parks around the old walls is level. There is no equivalent of Prague’s castle hill or Český Krumlov’s slopes. The cobbles are still cobbles, so bring proper shoes, but the climbing is gone.

What is the Holy Trinity Column?

A thirty-five-metre baroque sculpture group on the Upper Square, finished in 1754, and the largest of its kind in Central Europe. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in its own right, has a chapel inside its base, and is the single most impressive object in the city. It is free, it is outdoors, and it is right in front of the town hall.

Is the Christmas market worth it?

Very much so. Olomouc’s market fills the Upper Square around the column and the town hall through Advent, and it is the loveliest of the three big Moravian and Bohemian markets and by far the least besieged. Hot honey wine, wooden stalls, and room to move — which is more than can be said for Prague’s.

What should I eat here?

Olomoucké tvarůžky — a small, pungent, ripened Olomouc cheese with protected origin status and a formidable smell. It is a genuine local speciality and something of a test of nerve. Beyond that, Moravian wine rather than beer, and the courtyard restaurants around the two squares.

How many nights?

Two. One is enough to see the squares and the column; two lets you add the cathedral, the museum, the park ring and an evening in a wine room without hurrying. It also works well as a base for Kroměříž and the Moravian countryside.
