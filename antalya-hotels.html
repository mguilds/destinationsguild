Antalya — Old Walls, Cliffs and a Turquoise Sea

Where to Stay in Antalya — Old Town, Beaches & Resorts

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Antalya is three decisions in one. Kaleiçi, the walled Ottoman quarter above the Roman harbour, is where the character is — restored mansion hotels, courtyards and orange trees, and cobbles underfoot. Konyaaltı and Lara, the city’s own beaches to either side, are where the lifts, the balconies and the full-sized pools are. And the all-inclusive strips at Belek, Side and Kemer are a different holiday altogether. Below is each one plainly, with hotels we can stand behind.

Kaleiçi — inside the old walls

The walled Ottoman quarter above the Roman harbour: lanes of restored wooden houses, orange trees, Hadrian’s Gate at the top and the marina at the bottom. This is where to stay if you want Antalya rather than a beach holiday.

Kaleiçi is small, walkable and genuinely lovely in the evening when the day visitors have gone. Most of its hotels are converted mansions with a dozen rooms, a courtyard and a pool the size of a plunge bath. Two honest warnings. The lanes are cobbled and steep in places, and cars mostly cannot reach the doors, so expect to walk the last stretch with your bags. And the bars around the harbour end can be loud in summer — ask for a room on a courtyard rather than a street.

A long-established old-town hotel built into a historic house on Hesapçı Sokak, the lane that runs down from Hadrian’s Gate towards the sea. Rooms in the original building have the beams and the character; the newer wing is plainer and easier. There is a small pool and a courtyard restaurant. Good value for the location, and a sensible first choice inside the walls.

Murat paşa, Konyaaltı and Lara — the city and the beaches

Outside the walls, modern Antalya runs for miles along the top of the cliffs. Konyaaltı is the long pebble beach to the west under the mountains; Lara is the sand to the east. Between them sits the ordinary, likeable working city.

This is where you stay for a lift, a level floor, a proper pool and a sea view from a balcony — the things the old town cannot give you. The beachfront promenades on both sides are flat, long and pleasant to walk, and the tram will take you to the old town in a few minutes. It is less charming than Kaleiçi and a great deal easier, and for many travellers that is the right trade.

Belek, Side and Kemer — a different holiday

The all-inclusive strips east and west of the city are what many people mean when they say “Antalya”. They are good at what they do. They are not a base for seeing the region, and we would rather say so plainly.

These are large resorts, often with their own beach, several restaurants and a golf course, placed thirty minutes to an hour outside the city along the coast road. If your holiday is a week beside a pool with the children occupied, they are excellent value and we have no argument with them. If you want to walk out in the evening, eat where local people eat, or make day trips to Aspendos, Perge, Termessos and the Lycian coast without a hire car or an excursion coach, stay in the city instead. What to know before you book

Old town or sea view

You cannot really have both. Kaleiçi gives you atmosphere, courtyards and a short walk to dinner; the cliff-top and beach hotels give you a balcony over the water, a lift and a full-sized pool. Decide which matters more before you look at rooms.

Steps and cobbles

Kaleiçi is cobbled, sloping and largely closed to cars; the mansion hotels have stairs and rarely lifts. The modern city is flat, with long level promenades. If walking is difficult, Konyaaltı or Lara is the comfortable answer.

The beaches differ

Konyaaltı is pebble, with the Tünektepe mountains behind it and clear cold water. Lara is sand, flatter and warmer, with the big hotels along it. Neither is in the old town — the old harbour is a harbour, not a beach.

Day trips worth the hire car

Aspendos, Perge, Termessos and the Düden waterfalls are all within an hour. West along the coast the Lycian sites and the Lycian Way footpath begin. A car makes all of this easy; coaches and tours cover most of it as well.

When to come

April to early June and September to October are the comfortable months. July and August are very hot — fine for a pool, hard work on an archaeological site. Winters are mild and wet; several seafront hotels close or run quietly.

The airport

Antalya (AYT) is about half an hour east of the centre and is one of the busiest holiday airports in the Mediterranean. Transfers are plentiful; the tram line runs into the city from near the terminal area.

Questions

Should I stay in Antalya's old town or on the beach?

Kaleiçi, the walled old town, if you want to walk out in the evening among restored mansions and courtyards and eat somewhere with character — the trade is cobbles, stairs, small pools and no sea-view balcony. The cliff-top and beach hotels at Konyaaltı and Lara if you want a lift, a level floor, a full-sized pool and the water in front of you — the trade is a tram ride to anything charming.

Is Antalya a beach holiday or a sightseeing base?

It can be either, which is unusual and is the city's real strength. The old town, the Antalya Museum, the Düden waterfalls and the Roman sites at Perge, Aspendos and Termessos are all within easy reach, and the beaches at Konyaaltı and Lara are in the city itself. What it is not is an all-inclusive resort: those are the separate strips at Belek, Side and Kemer, thirty minutes to an hour out of town.

Which beach is better, Konyaaltı or Lara?

Konyaaltı is pebble, with the mountains rising behind it and cold clear water — the better view and the better swim for many people. Lara is sand, flatter and warmer, and is where most of the big hotels sit. Both have long level promenades that are pleasant to walk in the evening. Neither is at the old harbour, which is a working marina rather than a beach.

When is the best time to visit Antalya?

April to early June and September to October. The sea is warm enough by late May and stays warm well into October, and the archaeological sites are bearable in a way they simply are not in midsummer. July and August are very hot and the coast is at its fullest. Winter is mild, green and wet, and some seafront hotels close or run at half pace.

Do I need a hire car?

Not for the city — the old town is walkable and the tram covers the rest. A car earns its keep if you intend to spend days at Termessos, Aspendos, Perge and the Lycian coast, where the alternative is an organised excursion to each. If you are staying in Kaleiçi, check with your hotel about parking before you book one: most of the old town is closed to vehicles.
