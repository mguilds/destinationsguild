Edinburgh’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Edinburgh — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Edinburgh is a city of two halves, and the one you choose sets the mood of the whole trip. The medieval Old Town tumbles down from the Castle in a maze of closes and crow-stepped gables; the Georgian New Town spreads out below in orderly, elegant terraces. Both are compact and walkable, so the real question is which Edinburgh you want to wake up in. We’ve grouped the city’s most enduring, well-loved hotels by area and character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can just choose your dates.

Old Town & the Royal Mile

The Old Town is postcard Edinburgh: the Castle on its crag, the Royal Mile running down to Holyrood, and a warren of stone closes and stairs on either side. Stay here and you are in the thick of the history and, come August, the Festival crowds. Expect atmosphere, a few hills, and the sound of bagpipes drifting up the cobbles.

The Witchery by the Castle

$$$$ LuxuryTop of the Royal MileGothic & theatricalIconic restaurant

A cluster of lavish, candlelit suites steps from the Castle gates, decked out in tapestries, oak panelling, and Gothic drama. It is more indulgent stage set than conventional hotel, and its restaurant has been a special-occasion legend for decades. Best for couples after a romantic, once-in-a-lifetime splurge at the very top of the Old Town.

New Town — George Street & Princes Street

Laid out in the 18th century as a model of Georgian planning, the New Town is all wide streets, symmetrical terraces, and elegant garden squares. This is Edinburgh’s address for shopping, smart dining, and grand hotels, with Princes Street Gardens and Castle views on its southern edge. Choose it for refinement, flat walking, and easy access to both halves of the city.

The Balmoral

$$$$ LuxuryPrinces StreetLandmark clock towerRocco Forte

Edinburgh’s grande dame, crowned by the clock tower that presides over Princes Street and the entrance to Waverley station. Behind its imposing facade lie polished Rocco Forte service, a Michelin-starred restaurant, and old-world grandeur throughout. The definitive landmark splurge for travellers who want the hotel to be part of the occasion.

West End & Haymarket

The West End blends the New Town’s Georgian elegance with a quieter, more villagey feel — independent boutiques, cafes, and theatres along leafy streets. Haymarket station and tram links make arrivals and airport runs painless. A smart base for travellers who want easy transport and a calmer evening while staying within a short walk of the centre.

Grand public rooms, a spa, and Waldorf Astoria service uphold its old-money reputation. Perfect for travellers who want landmark luxury with the Castle framed from their window.

Stockbridge & Dean Village

A short walk downhill from the New Town, Stockbridge is Edinburgh’s most beloved “village” — indie shops, a Sunday market, and cafes along the Water of Leith. Neighbouring Dean Village is a storybook pocket of former mills and cobbled lanes by the river. Choose this area for a residential, local feel with the centre still an easy stroll away.

It has the feel of a smart local institution rather than a chain. Ideal for travellers who want village charm, good food, and a stroll into town along the water.

Leith & the Waterfront

Once a working port and now one of Edinburgh’s most exciting quarters, Leith mixes historic docks with a celebrated food-and-drink scene and the Royal Yacht Britannia at its shore. A tram and buses connect it quickly to the centre. Choose Leith for waterfront atmosphere, great restaurants, and a bit more space for your money.

Grassmarket & Southside — Budget & Value

Just below the Castle, the Grassmarket and the Southside offer character and central location without the marquee price tags. The Grassmarket is a lively square of pubs and independent shops, while the Southside around the University keeps rooms sensible and transport easy. Choose this area to spend on whisky and museums rather than the nightly rate.
