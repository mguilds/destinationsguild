Where to Stay in Nadi

Where to Stay in Nadi — Airport, Wailoaloa & Momi Bay

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Nearly every Fiji holiday begins here, and a surprising number of them are spent somewhere else within the hour. Nadi is the country’s front door — the international airport, the boat marina twenty minutes away, the sugar-cane town and the dry western side of Viti Levu. Most visitors give it one night at each end of a trip and never see the town at all. That is a fair plan, and this page is mostly about doing it well: a bed near the runway for a 5 a.m. departure, a beach at Wailoaloa for a first afternoon, or a proper resort south at Momi Bay if you would rather start the holiday on arrival than wait for a boat.

Choosing Your Area in Nadi

Where you sleep decides how your first and last days in Fiji actually go.

Near the airport, for the flight

If you land late or leave at dawn — and many long-haul services do both — the sensible choice is a hotel within a few minutes of the terminal. It is not scenic, and it is not meant to be. It is a shower, a bed, a breakfast that starts early enough, and a transfer measured in minutes rather than hours.

The Tanoa International Hotel sits on Votualevu Road by the airport, and Fiji Gateway Hotel is on Queens Road just outside it. Tanoa International Hotel Fiji Gateway Hotel

Wailoaloa Beach, for a first afternoon

Wailoaloa is the strip of grey-sand beach on Nadi Bay, ten minutes from the terminal and the closest thing to a holiday that is reachable before you have unpacked. The sand is volcanic rather than postcard-white and the swimming is shallow, which is worth knowing before you arrive expecting the brochure. What it has is a sunset over the Mamanucas, a row of places to eat along the beach road, and no transfer at all.

Crowne Plaza Fiji Nadi Bay Resort & Spa Wyndham Garden Wailoaloa Beach Fiji

South to Momi Bay, for an actual resort

Half an hour south of the airport the coast opens out at Momi Bay, and this is where people stay who want the holiday to start on landing rather than after a boat ride. It is a full resort setting with a lagoon and over-water rooms, far enough from town to be quiet and close enough to the airport for a civilised last morning.

Fiji Marriott Resort Momi Bay

Nadi town itself

The town is a working one — a main street of duty-free shops and curry houses, a produce market, and the Sri Siva Subramaniya temple at the southern end, whose carved and painted gopuram is the one genuinely striking building in Nadi. Most visitors see it in an hour between other things, and that is about right.

Staying in town is cheap and convenient for the market and the temple, and inconvenient for everything else, since the beach and the marina are both a drive away.

Know Before You Book

The handful of Nadi details that change how a trip goes.

Port Denarau, not Nadi, is where the boats leave from

The catamarans to the Mamanuca and Yasawa islands go from the marina at Port Denarau, about twenty minutes from Nadi airport by road. If an island resort is your real destination, check the departure time before you book your first night — a morning boat and a late-evening arrival into Nadi do not fit together without a night somewhere first.

Denarau has its own page in this chapter if you would rather stay out there on the first night and be on the water early.

The western side is the dry side

Nadi sits in the rain shadow of Viti Levu’s central range, which is why the hills behind the airport are brown and the sugar cane grows here. The southeast of the island around Suva is a different climate entirely and gets several times the rain. If you are choosing between coasts for weather, the west wins almost every month of the year.

Sabeto, for the half-day nobody plans

Twenty minutes inland, the Sabeto valley holds hot springs and mud pools and the orchid gardens at the foot of the Sleeping Giant ridge. It is the standard way to fill a last day when you have checked out and your flight is not until midnight, and it is better than it sounds.

Nadi Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best place to stay in Nadi?

It depends on what the night is for. By the airport if you are flying at dawn or landing late — the transfer is minutes and nothing else about those hotels matters. Wailoaloa Beach if you want a sunset and a swim on your first afternoon without a transfer. Momi Bay, half an hour south, if you want a real resort rather than a staging post. Nadi town itself only if the market and the temple are what you came for.

Is Nadi worth staying in, or should you go straight to an island?

Both, in the right order. Almost everyone spends at least one night in or near Nadi because the long-haul flights and the island boats do not connect, and the boats to the Mamanucas and Yasawas leave from Port Denarau in the morning. One night on arrival and one before departure is the normal shape of a Fiji trip; the islands get the middle of it.

How far is Nadi airport from Port Denarau?

About twenty minutes by road in ordinary traffic. Port Denarau is the marina on Denarau Island, just west of Nadi town, and it is where the island catamarans, day cruises and most resort transfers depart from. Allow more than twenty minutes if you are catching a scheduled boat.

Is the beach at Nadi any good?

Honestly, no — not by Fijian standards. Wailoaloa is grey volcanic sand with shallow, often cloudy water, and it is a pleasant place for a sunset and a drink rather than a swimming beach. The beaches people picture when they picture Fiji are on the islands offshore and along the Coral Coast. Nadi's job is to get you to them.

Do you need a car in Nadi?

No. Taxis are plentiful and cheap for short runs, hotels run airport transfers, and the town, the beach and the marina are all short drives apart. A hire car only earns its keep if you are driving the Queens Road around to the Coral Coast or Suva under your own steam.
