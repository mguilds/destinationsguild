Lake Fishing in Minnesota — Six Great Walleye Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Mille Lacs, Lake of the Woods, Leech Lake, Lake Vermilion, Upper Red Lake and Lake Winnibigoshish: what swims in each, how people fish them summer and winter, the 2026 rules, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Minnesota Department of Natural Resources, 5 November 2026

Minnesota calls itself the land of lakes, and for anglers the big northern lakes are the heart of it. They are walleye lakes first — the state fish — with northern pike, perch, crappie, muskie and smallmouth bass besides. They have two seasons: open water from the May opener to freeze-up, and then ice, when whole villages of fish houses appear on the larger lakes. Every one of them has a small town on its shore with resorts, motels and cabins.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers, ice anglers and anyone who wants a lake, a cabin and perhaps a walleye for supper. Minnesota’s big lakes each carry their own special rules, and some of them change every season, so we have quoted the Minnesota DNR wherever a number is involved.

Mille Lacs — the famous one, and the most carefully managed

Minnesota’s second-largest inland lake, an hour and a half to two hours north of Minneapolis–St. Paul.

Mille Lacs Lake, from Garrison, Isle and Onamia

What swims there. Walleye above all — the DNR says the lake is best known for its catch rates — with trophy smallmouth bass, muskellunge and northern pike, yellow perch and tullibee (cisco). The DNR’s survey records also list black crappie, bluegill, rock bass and burbot.

How people fish it. From boats in summer, on the mud flats and reefs; from launch boats run by the resorts; and through the ice in winter, when the lake fills with fish houses and the walleye bite is fast. The lake is 132,516 acres — 207 square miles — so wind matters: watch the forecast before you head out in a small boat.

Keeping a walleye in 2026. Three, each at least 17 inches, and only one over 20 inches, from the opener on 9 May through 30 November. State-licensed anglers share the lake’s harvest with the Ojibwe Tribes, who keep treaty fishing rights, and the DNR may tighten the rule during the season — even to catch-and-release — if the state’s share is being used up.

Confirmed with the Minnesota DNR, 5 November 2026 — news release, 5 March 2026: “Anglers on Mille Lacs Lake will have the opportunity to harvest three walleyes starting on the fishing opener on Saturday, May 9. Harvested walleyes must be 17 inches or greater, with only one over 20 inches.” “The open-water walleye regulation for Mille Lacs Lake will be in place through Monday, Nov. 30.” Lake page: “This 132,516 acre-lake covers 207 square miles and is Minnesota's second-largest inland lake.”

Other species. Bass, pike, perch, tullibee and burbot on Mille Lacs also have their own special limits, and the lake has had a summer night closure in recent seasons. When we read the DNR’s Mille Lacs regulations page on 5 November 2026 it still printed the dates of the 2025–26 season, so we are not giving you numbers we cannot date. Read the DNR page, or the signs posted at every public access, before you fish.

Boat launches. The DNR runs public water accesses around the lake, and a boat decontamination station is open around the clock at the Shaw-Bosh-Kung Bay access on the west side, about eight miles south of Garrison.

Nearest towns: Garrison on the north-west shore, Isle on the south-east shore, and Onamia to the south-west. Brainerd is the nearest larger town, to the north.

Lake of the Woods — the border lake, and a long ice season

A huge lake shared with Ontario and Manitoba, at the very top of Minnesota, with Baudette and the Rainy River at its south-east corner.

Lake of the Woods and the Rainy River, from Baudette and Warroad

What swims there. Walleye and sauger, northern pike, yellow perch and lake sturgeon, with smallmouth bass, crappie, muskie and burbot — the state record burbot, 19 pounds 10 ounces, came from this lake.

How people fish it. By boat in summer, often from a resort’s charter or launch, and for long months on the ice — the walleye season here runs on to mid-April, after the inland season has closed. Resorts at Baudette rent “sleeper” fish houses for overnight stays on the ice. In spring, anglers fish the Rainy River for walleye and sturgeon.

Keeping a fish. Walleye and sauger, six together, no more than four of them walleye; every walleye from 19½ to 28 inches goes straight back, and you may keep one over 28. Northern pike: three, and every pike from 30 to 40 inches goes back. Lake sturgeon may be kept only in two short windows, with a sturgeon tag. Because it is a border water, you may fish two lines through the ice but only one in open water.

Confirmed with the Minnesota DNR, 5 November 2026 — 2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations booklet (effective 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027), Canada–Minnesota border waters: walleye and sauger “May 9, 2026 – April 14, 2027”, “6 (not more than 4 can be walleye; only 1 walleye over 28″; walleye 19½–28″ must be immediately released)”; Four Mile Bay and the Rainy River “March 1 – April 14 … Catch-and-release only”; northern pike “Continuous … 3 (All from 30–40″ must be immediately released. Only 1 over 40″ allowed in possession.)”; lake sturgeon 1 per calendar year, 45–50″ or over 75″, from 24 April to 7 May and 1 July to 30 September, tag $5; muskellunge 20 June–30 November, 1, minimum 50″; “1 line per angler is allowed, except 2 lines may be used when ice fishing.”

The Canadian side. Most of the lake lies in Ontario and Manitoba, which set their own rules and sell their own licences. A Minnesota licence covers Minnesota’s waters only. Check with the province before you cross the line.

Nearest towns: Baudette, on the Rainy River near the lake, and Warroad on the lake’s south-west shore.

Leech Lake — walleye, perch and muskie at Walker

One of Minnesota’s largest lakes, in the Chippewa National Forest country of Cass County, with the town of Walker on Walker Bay.

Leech Lake, from Walker

What swims there. Walleye, yellow perch, northern pike and muskellunge, with largemouth bass, crappie, bluegill, rock bass, tullibee and whitefish, according to the DNR’s survey records.

How people fish it. Boats across the big open lake and its many bays, shore fishing around Walker, launch trips from resorts, and ice fishing in winter. Walker is the place to stay: it has hotels, resorts, bait shops and the city park on the bay.

Keeping a fish. Four walleye, only one over 20 inches. Crappie five; sunfish five. Leech Lake is in the DNR’s north-central zone for northern pike: ten, not more than two over 26 inches, and every pike from 22 to 26 inches goes back.

Confirmed with the Minnesota DNR, 5 November 2026 — 2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations booklet (effective 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027): “LEECH LAKE (Cass) Crappie: daily limit 5. Sunfish: daily limit 5. Walleye: possession limit 4, only 1 over 20″.”

Nearest town: Walker.

Lake Vermilion — islands, muskie and walleye near Tower

A long, rocky lake of many islands and bays in the north woods of St. Louis County, between Tower and Cook.

Lake Vermilion, from Tower and Cook

What swims there. Walleye, muskellunge, smallmouth and largemouth bass, northern pike, crappie, yellow perch, bluegill and rock bass, according to the DNR’s survey records.

How people fish it. By boat among the islands and reefs, casting for bass and muskie in summer, trolling or jigging for walleye, and on the ice in winter. It is a lake of resorts and cabins rather than big hotels.

Keeping a fish. Four walleye; every walleye from 20 to 26 inches goes back, and only one over 26 may be kept. Most of the lake lies north and east of US Highway 53, the line the DNR uses for its Northeast zone, where the pike rule is two, with every pike from 30 to 40 inches released — check the zone map in the booklet for the bay you fish.

Confirmed with the Minnesota DNR, 5 November 2026 — 2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations booklet (effective 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027): “VERMILION LAKE (St. Louis) Walleye: all from 20–26″ must be immediately released. Possession limit 4, only 1 over 26″.” Northeast pike zone: “north and east of U.S. Highway 53 from Duluth to International Falls”; limit 2, 30–40″ released.

Nearest towns: Tower at the east end and Cook near the west end. Ely lies further east.

Upper Red Lake — the walleye comeback lake

A vast, shallow lake in Beltrami County, shared with the Red Lake Nation. Its state waters are reached from Waskish on the east shore.

Upper Red Lake, from Waskish and Kelliher

What swims there. Walleye, northern pike, black crappie, yellow perch and whitefish, with rock bass and other species, according to the DNR’s survey records.

How people fish it. Shallow, open water from boats in summer and a very busy ice fishery in winter, with resorts renting fish houses. Much of the lake lies within the Red Lake Indian Reservation, and those waters are closed to people who are not Band members unless the tribal council allows otherwise. Fish only the state waters unless you have that permission.

Keeping a walleye in 2026. During the 2026 open-water season, from 9 May, the possession limit is five walleye, only one longer than 17 inches. The DNR and the Red Lake Nation manage the walleye together, and the winter rule for 2026–27 is decided after the summer and the autumn netting — check the DNR before you go on the ice.

Confirmed with the Minnesota DNR, 5 November 2026 — news release, 16 March 2026: “Anglers fishing on Upper Red Lake during the 2026 open-water season that starts Saturday, May 9, will have a five-walleye possession limit, with only one walleye longer than 17 inches allowed.” “Winter harvest regulations for 2026-2027 will be determined after the summer fishing season and fall assessment netting.” Booklet: reservation waters “closed to non-band members except by special authorization of the tribal council”; northern pike 30–40″ released, possession 3, only 1 over 40″.

Boat launches. The DNR names the Tamarac River Big Bog Public Water Access — Homestead Park, locally — where there is a boat decontamination station.

Nearest towns: Waskish, a very small place on the lake, and Kelliher to the south. Bemidji, to the south-west, is the nearest city, with far more places to stay.

Lake Winnibigoshish — “Big Winnie” in the Chippewa National Forest

A big, wind-swept lake on the Mississippi River, with Cut Foot Sioux and other connected lakes, north-west of Grand Rapids.

Lake Winnibigoshish and connected waters, from Deer River

What swims there. Walleye, yellow perch and northern pike, with black crappie, tullibee and burbot, according to the DNR’s survey records.

How people fish it. Boats on the big lake and its bays, the sheltered connected lakes when the wind is up, and ice fishing for walleye and perch in winter. Family resorts sit around the shore.

Keeping a fish. Every walleye from 18 to 23 inches goes back, and only one over 23 may be kept, within the statewide walleye limit. Sunfish five. Winnie is in the north-central zone for pike: ten, not more than two over 26 inches, and every pike from 22 to 26 inches goes back. The rule covers the connected waters too — Cut Foot Sioux, Little Cut Foot Sioux, Egg Lake, Sugar Lake and stretches of the Mississippi and other rivers.

Confirmed with the Minnesota DNR, 5 November 2026 — 2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations booklet (effective 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027): “WINNIBIGOSHISH Lake and connected waters … Sunfish: daily limit 5. Walleye: all from 18–23″ must be immediately released, only 1 over 23″ allowed in possession.”

Nearest towns: Deer River, south-east of the lake, and the city of Grand Rapids a little further on. Bena is a small community on the south shore.

The licence and the limits, plainly

One Minnesota licence covers every lake on this page, in Minnesota’s waters. The licence year runs 1 March to the last day of February.

Who needs one, and what it costs in 2026–27

From sixteen. Minnesota residents younger than 16 or aged 90 and over fish without a licence. Visiting children under 16 need none if a parent or guardian is licensed. Residents aged 16 and 17, and visitors aged 16 and 17, pay $5 for a youth licence.

A $1 issuing fee is added, and buying online adds a convenience fee. Buy from the DNR online or from a licence agent — most bait shops and resorts in these towns sell them.

Leaving a fish house on the ice? A portable shelter you stay with needs no licence. A permanent house, or a portable one left unattended overnight or more than 200 feet away from you, needs a shelter licence and the owner’s name or number on the outside.

Bringing a boat? Clean off plants and mud, pull every drain plug before you leave the access and keep them out on the road, and put unwanted bait in the rubbish — never in the lake. That is Minnesota law, not just advice.

On the ice

The DNR does not measure the ice on any lake, and says your safety is your own responsibility. Its guide for new, clear ice: under four inches, stay off; four inches for walking and fishing; five to seven for a snowmobile or small ATV; nine to ten for a small car. White, snowy ice is about half as strong — double those figures. On the big lakes, ask the resort that runs the ice road what it has measured that morning.

Questions

Do I need a fishing licence to fish a lake in Minnesota, and what does it cost?

Residents need one from age 16 and visitors from age 16. Minnesota residents younger than 16 or aged 90 and older fish free, and visiting children under 16 need no licence if a parent or guardian is licensed. The Minnesota DNR’s 2026 booklet lists a non-resident 24-hour licence at $14, 72 hours at $36, 7 days at $43 and a year at $51; for residents, a year is $25 and 24 hours $12. A $1 issuing fee is added. The licence year runs 1 March to the last day of February.

When does walleye season open in Minnesota in 2026 and 2027?

The inland walleye and northern pike opener was Saturday 9 May 2026, and the season runs to 28 February 2027. The DNR lists the 2027 opener as 15 May. On Lake of the Woods and the Canadian border waters the 2026 season runs from 9 May 2026 to 14 April 2027.

How many walleye can I keep on Mille Lacs in 2026?

Three, during the 2026 open-water season from 9 May to 30 November. Every walleye kept must be 17 inches or longer, and only one may be over 20 inches. That is the Minnesota DNR’s regulation announced on 5 March 2026. The winter rules from 1 December are set separately, so check the DNR’s Mille Lacs page before you go.

What are the walleye rules on Lake of the Woods?

Six walleye and sauger together, but no more than four of them walleye. Every walleye from 19½ to 28 inches must go straight back, and only one walleye over 28 inches may be kept. In Four Mile Bay and on the Rainy River, walleye and sauger are catch-and-release only from 1 March to 14 April. That is the 2026 Minnesota DNR booklet. The Canadian side of the lake has its own provincial rules and licence.

Is Minnesota cutting the walleye limit?

It has proposed to. The Minnesota DNR has proposed lowering the statewide walleye limit from six to four, with only one over 20 inches, and says that if the rule is enacted it would take effect on 1 March 2027. It would not change existing special regulations or border-water limits. When we checked on 5 November 2026 the DNR page still described it as a proposal, so check before the 2027 season.
