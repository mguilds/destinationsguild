Lake Fishing in New York — Six Great Waters and the Towns Beside Them

Lake Champlain, Oneida Lake, the Finger Lakes, the Thousand Islands, Lake George and Chautauqua Lake: what swims in each, the 2026 rules from the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation, the licence a visitor needs, where to launch a boat, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation, 15 November 2026

New York has more good lake water than most people expect from a state they know for one city. In the north, Lake Champlain runs about 120 miles along the Vermont line, with lake trout and salmon in its deep middle and bass and pike in its bays. In the middle of the state, Oneida Lake is the walleye lake — DEC calls it the largest lake lying wholly inside New York — and just south of it the long, deep Finger Lakes hold lake trout, rainbows and smallmouth. On the St. Lawrence River the Thousand Islands are bass and muskie country. Lake George is clear Adirondack water, and in the far west Chautauqua Lake is where New York raises its muskellunge.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, ice fishermen, and anyone who wants a guide for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 15 November 2026 from the agency that sets it — mainly the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) and its 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide (in effect from 1 April 2026) — and is dated. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

Where these facts come from. Every rule, limit and price on this page was read on 15 November 2026 from the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation: its 2026 New York State Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide (regulations in effect 1 April 2026), its fishing-licence page, its lake pages and its boat-launch lists. Lake Champlain licence rules were also read from Vermont Fish & Wildlife; Lake Champlain facts from the Lake Champlain Basin Program; Lake George boat rules from the Lake George Park Commission; boating rules and state park launches from NY State Parks; fish-eating advice from the NYS Department of Health. Not from a lodge, a marina or a booking site. Rules change and differ lake by lake — always check the agency before you fish.

The best months, in plain words

Northern lakes: a short warm season, long cold winters, and hard ice on most of these waters in a normal winter. Seasons matter here — bass, walleye, pike and muskie all have closed times.

Lake Champlain — the big lake on the Vermont line

About 120 miles long and up to about 400 feet deep (Lake Champlain Basin Program), shared by New York, Vermont and Quebec. This guide covers the New York shore, from Plattsburgh south to Ticonderoga and Whitehall.

Lake Champlain, from Plattsburgh to Ticonderoga

What swims there. The Lake Champlain Basin Program counts about 20 fish that anglers seek, among them lake trout, landlocked Atlantic salmon, rainbow and brown trout, smallmouth and largemouth bass, walleye, northern pike, chain pickerel, yellow perch, bullhead and smelt. DEC lists Champlain among Essex County’s top waters for lake trout, salmon, both basses, pike and pickerel.

How people fish it. DEC says the main lake trout water runs about 35 miles, from around Westport north to Cumberland Head at Plattsburgh, and that slow trolling is the preferred method — with downriggers or lead core in summer. In spring light tackle works. Bass and pike anglers work the bays and weed edges. In winter people ice fish the protected bays; in a DEC creel survey yellow perch were the main target.

Keeping a fish. Lake Champlain has its own rules: walleye 18 inches, 3 a day (open first Saturday in May to 15 March); lake trout 15 inches, 3 a day; landlocked salmon 15 inches, 2 a day; northern pike 20 inches, 5 a day; bass 12 inches, 5 a day from the second Saturday in June to 30 November (catch and release with artificial lures the rest of the year); muskie 40 inches, 1 a day. Sauger may not be taken at all. Two lines each in open water.

Confirmed with the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation, 15 November 2026 — 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide, Lake Champlain special regulations: the limits above; no more than 2 lines per angler; ice fishing permitted with up to 15 lines from 15 November to 30 April. Licence reciprocity with Vermont: see the licence section below.

Boat ramps. DEC lists state launches at Peru Dock (south of Plattsburgh, 50 car-and-trailer spaces), Port Douglas, Willsboro Bay, Westport, Port Henry (Moriah), Ticonderoga and South Bay near Whitehall, with town ramps in Plattsburgh and launches at Point Au Roche State Park. DEC posts a spiny waterflea alert at every Champlain launch: clean, drain and dry your boat and gear.

Nearest towns: Plattsburgh for the north and the most hotels; Westport and Port Henry for the middle of the lake; Ticonderoga for the south end (and the north end of Lake George).

Oneida Lake — New York’s walleye lake

About 51,000 acres, between Syracuse and Rome. DEC calls it the largest lake lying entirely within New York, and the state’s third most-fished lake after Ontario and Erie.

Oneida Lake, from Brewerton to Sylvan Beach

What swims there. DEC’s lake map lists walleye, smallmouth and largemouth bass, northern pike, yellow perch, white perch, bullhead, crappie and sunfish. Cornell University, which has studied the lake every year since the 1950s, estimated about 603,000 adult walleye in 2025 — close to the long-term average — and well over three million adult yellow perch.

How people fish it. Mostly from boats for walleye, by drifting, jigging and trolling the shoals; bass along the rocks; and through the ice in the bays for perch and walleye in winter, which DEC singles out.

Keeping a fish. We found no special Oneida Lake rule in DEC’s 2026 guide, so the statewide rules apply: walleye open 1 May to 15 March, 15 inches, 5 a day; bass 15 June to 30 November, 12 inches, 5 a day; northern pike 18 inches, 5 a day; yellow perch 50 a day.

Confirmed with the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation, 15 November 2026 — 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide, statewide regulations (no Oneida Lake entry among the special regulations; ice fishing permitted). Population figures: Cornell University’s report on the 2025 season, published April 2026.

Boat ramps. DEC launches at Godfrey Point (Town of Vienna, north-east shore), the South Shore launch in the Town of Sullivan (100 car-and-trailer spaces) and Cove Road at Verona on the Barge Canal; Oneida Shores Park at Brewerton is a county ramp (fee). Verona Beach State Park and several Oswego County spots are hand launches for small boats only.

Nearest towns: Sylvan Beach at the east end; Brewerton and Cicero at the west end, near Syracuse.

The Finger Lakes — deep water, lake trout and wine country

Eleven long glacial lakes south of Syracuse and Rochester. DEC’s figures for the big ones: Seneca 43,343 acres and 618 feet deep; Cayuga 42,956 acres and 435 feet; Keuka 11,584 acres; Canandaigua 10,558 acres; Skaneateles about 8,960 acres.

Seneca Lake, from Geneva to Watkins Glen

What swims there. Lake trout, rainbow and brown trout, landlocked salmon, smallmouth and largemouth bass, northern pike, yellow perch and panfish (DEC). DEC calls lake trout, smallmouth and perch its traditional mainstays. The local tourism office calls Seneca “the lake trout capital of the world” — a nickname, not a DEC title.

Boat ramps. Sampson State Park (concrete ramps and a marina, about 12 miles south of Geneva), Seneca Lake State Park at Geneva, Lodi Point State Marine Park and a launch at Watkins Glen (fee), among others on DEC’s list.

Cayuga Lake, from Ithaca to Seneca Falls

What swims there. DEC calls Cayuga a mix of cold-water and warm-water fishing: lake trout, Atlantic salmon, rainbow and brown trout in the deep water, and largemouth bass, pike, pickerel and panfish in the shallow, weedy north end, which DEC says is a favourite with tournament bass anglers.

How people fish it. DEC describes spring trolling at the south end for salmon, rainbows, browns and pike; summer trolling and vertical jigging for lake trout; and winter shore fishing at Milliken Station.

Boat ramps. Allan H. Treman State Marine Park in Ithaca has an eight-lane ramp ($8 launch fee, April to November, NY State Parks); also Taughannock Falls, Long Point and Cayuga Lake state parks.

Canandaigua, Keuka and Skaneateles

Canandaigua. Lake, brown and rainbow trout and both basses (DEC). Launch at Canandaigua Lake State Marine Park in the city ($7 vehicle fee in season) or free at Woodville, three miles north of Naples.

Keuka. DEC says smallmouth now dominate and the lake trout are self-sustaining. Launch at Keuka Lake State Park near Branchport (park fee) or the Village of Penn Yan (launch fee).

Skaneateles. One of the cleanest Finger Lakes and a drinking-water supply: rainbows and salmon trolled near the surface in spring and autumn, lake trout and perch through the ice. DEC launch two miles south of the village on Route 41A.

Keeping a fish. The Finger Lakes have their own rules. On Seneca, Keuka and Canandaigua: trout and salmon open all year, 15 inches, 5 a day together with no more than 1 rainbow and 3 salmon; bass open 15 June to 15 March, 12 inches, 5 a day. On Cayuga: trout and salmon 15 inches (salmon 18), 5 together with no more than 3 of any one. On all the Finger Lakes: northern pike 22 inches, 5 a day; walleye 18 inches, 3 a day — except Skaneateles, where walleye are open all year at 12 inches with no limit, and keeping alewives is prohibited.

Confirmed with the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation, 15 November 2026 — 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide, Finger Lakes special regulations, and DEC’s Finger Lakes page: the limits above. Tributary streams have different seasons and tackle rules — read the guide before you fish a creek.

Nearest towns: Geneva and Watkins Glen (Seneca); Ithaca (Cayuga); Canandaigua; Penn Yan and Hammondsport (Keuka); Skaneateles.

The Thousand Islands — bass and muskie on the St. Lawrence

A river, not a lake, but fished like one: DEC defines the Thousand Islands stretch as running from Tibbetts Point near Cape Vincent to about Morristown. The far shore is Canada.

The St. Lawrence, from Cape Vincent to Alexandria Bay

What swims there. Smallmouth and largemouth bass, northern pike, walleye, yellow perch, bullhead, panfish and muskellunge (DEC). DEC says smallmouth are the most commonly caught fish, and that the New York record muskie — 69 pounds 15 ounces — was caught near Clayton in 1957. The river’s muskie breed on their own.

How people fish it. From docks and piers; by boat in the bays and around the islands; trolling the shoal edges for muskie; and through the ice with tip-ups for pike and jigging for perch. Clayton and Alexandria Bay have long-standing guides’ associations, and the traditional island “shore dinner” is still part of a guided day.

Keeping a fish. The river has its own rules: muskie 15 June to 15 December, 54 inches, 1 a day; walleye 18 inches, 3 a day; northern pike 22 inches. Bass follow the statewide limits, but all bass fishing — even catch and release — is prohibited from 1 December to 14 June. Lake sturgeon are closed.

Confirmed with the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation, 15 November 2026 — 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide, Lake Ontario and St. Lawrence River regulations: the limits above. Border waters table: a New York licence is not valid in the Ontario or Quebec waters of the river. Stay on the U.S. side of the line unless you hold the Canadian licence.

Boat ramps. NY State Parks launches at Wellesley Island State Park (a full-service marina and four launches), Cedar Point near Clayton, Keewaydin and Kring Point near Alexandria Bay, and Burnham Point near Cape Vincent; village launches in Alexandria Bay and Cape Vincent. DEC’s Cape Vincent Fisheries Station offers free public dockage (48 hours at most).

Nearest towns: Clayton, Alexandria Bay and Cape Vincent.

Lake George — clear Adirondack water

About 32 miles long and close to 200 feet at its deepest (DEC and NY Department of State figures), with 28,479 acres on DEC’s maps and dozens of state-owned islands.

Lake George, from Lake George village to Hague

What swims there. Lake trout, landlocked salmon, northern pike, smallmouth and largemouth bass, chain pickerel, rainbow and brown trout, crappie, perch and smelt (DEC lake maps). DEC names it a top water for lake trout, salmon, both basses, pike and pickerel.

How people fish it. Trolling deep for lake trout and salmon, casting the shorelines and islands for bass, and through the ice in winter — DEC lists ice access at Million Dollar Beach, Hearthstone Point, Rogers Rock and elsewhere.

Keeping a fish. Lake trout open all year, 23 inches, 2 a day; landlocked salmon open all year, 18 inches, 2 a day; smelt 25 a day from 16 May to 31 March. Other fish follow the statewide rules. Ice fishing is permitted.

Confirmed with the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation and the Lake George Park Commission, 15 November 2026 — DEC 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide, Warren County special regulations: the limits above. Lake George Park Commission: all trailered boats must be inspected before launching from 1 May to 31 October (free; cartop boats exempt); boats with motors of 10 hp or more need a Park Commission registration ($12 a day, $20 a week).

Boat ramps. DEC launches at Million Dollar Beach in Lake George village (in season) and Mossy Point at Ticonderoga (100 spaces), and at Rogers Rock Campground in Hague (day-use fee in season). Northwest Bay Brook at Bolton is a hand launch only. Inspection stations are at Million Dollar Beach, Mossy Point, Rogers Rock and Norowal Marina in Bolton Landing.

Nearest towns: Lake George village at the south end; Bolton Landing in the middle; Hague and Ticonderoga at the north end.

Chautauqua Lake — where New York raises its muskie

13,156 acres in the far south-west of the state. DEC calls it the largest inland lake in western New York. Bemus Point splits it into a deeper north basin (up to 75 feet) and a shallow south basin (up to 19 feet).

Chautauqua Lake, from Mayville to Jamestown

What swims there. Muskellunge, walleye, largemouth and smallmouth bass, yellow perch, crappie, bluegill, white bass and more (DEC). DEC calls it a world-class muskie fishery and one of New York’s top bass lakes. The DEC hatchery at Prendergast Point stocks about 13,000 young muskie a year, and the lake is the egg source for the state’s muskie programme.

How people fish it. For muskie: trolling, drifting and casting the weed edges, and trolling for suspended fish in the north basin. For walleye: casting or trolling from dusk to dawn, and jigging the drop-offs in autumn. Through the ice for panfish and walleye, mostly in the north basin.

Keeping a fish. Muskie and tiger muskie open 1 June to 30 November, 40 inches, 1 a day. We found no special walleye rule for Chautauqua in the 2026 guide, so the statewide rule applies: 1 May to 15 March, 15 inches, 5 a day.

Confirmed with the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation, 15 November 2026 — 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide, Chautauqua County special regulations: the muskie rule above, which also covers the tributaries to the first road bridge and the Chadakoin River to Warner Dam.

Boat ramps. Long Point State Park at Bemus Point (open 24 hours, $7 vehicle fee, NY State Parks); DEC launches at Prendergast Point and Bemus Point; town and city ramps at Mayville, Lakewood and Jamestown.

Nearest towns: Mayville at the north end; Bemus Point in the middle; Lakewood and Jamestown at the south end.

The licence and the limits, plainly

One licence for the whole state, a cheap one-day licence for visitors, and children under 16 fish free.

New York fishing licences, 2026 prices

Who needs one. Everyone 16 and older. Children under 16 need no licence. To buy at the resident price you must have lived in New York for more than 30 days.

For a visitor. Non-resident annual licence $50; seven-day $28; one-day $10.

For a New Yorker. Annual licence $25 (ages 16 to 69) or $5 (70 and over); seven-day $12; one-day $5. An annual licence runs 365 days from the day you buy it.

Where to buy. Online through DEC’s DECALS licensing system, by phone on 1-866-933-2257, or from a licence agent (sporting-goods and many bait shops). Carry it while you fish.

Free Fishing Days. On these days anyone may fish New York’s fresh water without a licence; every other rule still applies. DEC’s 2026 guide lists 13–14 February 2027 as the next ones.

Confirmed with the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation, 15 November 2026 — 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide and DEC fishing-licence page: the prices above; licence required at 16; annual licence valid 365 days. Prices change. Buy from DEC or a licensed agent.

Lake Champlain and the border waters

On Lake Champlain a New York or a Vermont licence covers the Main Lake and South Lake, and the shore along them. You need that state’s own licence in South Bay (New York) and in the Inland Sea, Malletts Bay and Missisquoi Bay (Vermont), and on the rivers flowing in. Vermont Fish & Wildlife adds that when you fish in New York you follow New York’s rules. There is no reciprocity with Quebec. On the St. Lawrence, a New York licence does not cover the Canadian side of the river.

Bait fish — read this before you buy minnows

New York is strict about bait, to keep diseases and invasive fish out of its lakes. Only the species on DEC’s approved list (golden shiner, fathead minnow, white sucker, creek chub and others) may be bought and used. Bait bought from a dealer is “certified” only while you carry the receipt, for 10 days. Bait you catch yourself may be used only in the water you caught it in. Alewives and smelt are allowed as bait only on the waters DEC names. Never use carp, goldfish or round goby as bait.

Ice fishing

Statewide, up to 7 ice-fishing lines, attended (Lake Champlain allows 15 from 15 November to 30 April). Ice shanties must carry the owner’s name and address and be off the ice by 15 March. In some northern counties ice fishing is banned on trout waters unless DEC says otherwise — Lake George and Lake Champlain are both open to it. No ice is ever certified safe: check locally.

Boats and life jackets

From 1 November to 1 May everyone aboard a pleasure boat under 21 feet — including canoes and kayaks — must wear a life jacket (NY State Parks). Children under 12 must wear one on boats of 65 feet or less. Since 1 January 2025 everyone driving a motorboat in New York needs a boating safety certificate, at any age (Brianna’s Law); renters 18 and over may drive a rented boat without one. On Lake George, see the inspection and registration rules above.

Eating what you keep

The NYS Department of Health publishes fish-eating advice. Women under 50 and children under 15 are told to follow the stricter advice. Lake Champlain and Lake George follow the Department’s Adirondack regional advice, which tells women under 50 and children not to eat larger fish such as bass, pike, walleye and lake trout; on Champlain, the bay from Cumberland Head to the Peru boat launch has a “don’t eat” advisory for yellow perch, pike, rock bass and eel. On Lake George, rainbow smelt carry a once-a-month advisory. Oneida Lake walleye and smallmouth: up to 4 meals a month (1 for women under 50 and children). Seneca Lake yellow perch: 1 meal a month. On the St. Lawrence, check the Department’s river advice by species. Advice changes — read health.ny.gov before you keep fish to eat.

Questions

Do I need a New York fishing licence, and what does it cost a visitor?

Everyone 16 and older needs one; children under 16 fish free. In the NYS Department of Environmental Conservation's 2026 Freshwater Fishing Regulations Guide a non-resident pays $50 for an annual licence, $28 for seven days or $10 for one day. New York residents pay $25 a year ($5 at 70 and over), $12 for seven days or $5 for one day. Buy online through DEC's DECALS licensing system, by phone on 1-866-933-2257, or from a licence agent.

Can I fish the Vermont side of Lake Champlain with a New York licence?

Yes, in most of the lake. DEC says anglers with either a New York or a Vermont licence may fish the Main Lake and South Lake portions of Lake Champlain, and from the shore along them. The exceptions are South Bay in New York and the Inland Sea, Malletts Bay and Missisquoi Bay in Vermont, where you need that state's licence, and the rivers that flow into the lake. A New York licence is not valid in the Quebec end of the lake.

When can I fish for bass in New York?

The statewide bass season is 15 June to 30 November, with a 12-inch minimum and five a day. From 1 December to 14 June bass fishing is catch and release with artificial lures only. Some waters differ: on Lake Champlain the open season starts on the second Saturday in June, and in the St. Lawrence River and the Jefferson County waters of Lake Ontario all bass fishing, even catch and release, is prohibited from 1 December to 14 June.

Do I need to have my boat inspected before launching on Lake George?

Yes, if you trailer it. The Lake George Park Commission requires every trailered boat to be inspected at one of its regional inspection stations before launching during the inspection season (1 May to 31 October). Inspection and, if needed, hot-water decontamination are free, and the boat is sealed so it can launch. Canoes, kayaks and other cartop boats are not inspected. Boats with motors of 10 horsepower or more also need a Lake George Park Commission registration.

What are the walleye limits on Oneida Lake and the Finger Lakes?

The 2026 DEC guide lists no special walleye rule for Oneida Lake, so the statewide rule applies there: open 1 May to 15 March, 15-inch minimum, five a day. On the Finger Lakes the walleye rule is 18 inches and three a day, open 1 May to 15 March, except Skaneateles, where walleye are open all year with a 12-inch minimum and no daily limit.

The part of a fishing page most sites leave out.

Six honest gaps

Oneida and Chautauqua walleye. We give the statewide walleye rule for both because DEC’s 2026 guide lists no special rule for either lake. DEC does not print that as a line about these lakes; if you plan to keep walleye, check with DEC.

Launch fees and ramp hours. We give fees only where the agency prints them. Several state parks charge a vehicle fee whose amount we could not confirm today, and low water or ice closes ramps.

Lake George inspection hours. The Lake George Park Commission lists its stations but no 2026 hours, and its own pages disagree on how many stations there are. Check its website before you tow.

Canadian waters. DEC says only that a New York licence is not valid on the Ontario and Quebec side. Which Canadian licence you need is for the Canadian authorities to say; we have not checked it.

Cayuga Lake guides. We could not find a Cayuga guide with a working website of their own today, so we list none rather than send you to a booking directory.

Free Fishing Days 2027. The February 2027 dates come from DEC’s 2026 guide; DEC had not yet announced its 2027 days when we checked.
