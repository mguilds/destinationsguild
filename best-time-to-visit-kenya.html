Kenya Month by Month — Dry Plains, Long Rains and a Warm Sea

Best Time to Visit Kenya — Month by Month

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Kenya does not have a summer and a winter. It has two rainy seasons and the dry spells between them, and because the country runs from sea level to the snow on Mount Kenya, the same month can mean very different things in different places. This page sets out what each part of the year is actually like, for the parks, for the highlands and for the coast.

The short answer

For wildlife: late June to October. Dry, short grass, animals gathered near water, good roads. July and August are the busiest and dearest weeks of the year; September and early October are the quiet end of the same season.

For a second-best, cheaper option: January and February. Hot and dry, very good game viewing, far fewer visitors than the mid-year peak, and the coast at its clearest.

To avoid: April and May. The long rains at their heaviest. Green and cheap, but tracks are difficult, some camps shut and the coast is wet.

Shoulder: November and early December. The short rains are usually lighter and more scattered than the long ones, the country is green, the birding is at its best and prices are low. A reasonable gamble.

Month by month

January and February — hot, dry and underrated

The dry spell between the two rainy seasons. Game viewing is very good because the bush is thin and water is scarce, the highlands are warm by day, and the coast is about as clear as it gets. It is hot in the lowlands and on the coast, and the light is hard in the middle of the day. Of the two main dry periods this is the one fewer people book, which is its advantage.

March to May — the long rains

March is the hinge: often still dry at the start, often properly wet by the end. April and May are the wettest weeks of the year across most of the country and on the coast. Rain here usually comes as heavy afternoon storms rather than all-day drizzle, so a trip is not washed out, but dirt tracks become difficult, some camps close for refurbishment, and photography gets harder. What you get in exchange is an empty, brilliantly green country at the lowest prices of the year.

June to October — the main season

The long dry. Cool, clear mornings in the highlands, cold nights from June to August, short grass on the plains and the best wildlife viewing of the year. This is also when the wildebeest herds are usually in the Maasai Mara, generally somewhere between July and October. July and August are peak season for price and for vehicles at a sighting; September and early October give you the same conditions with less company.

November and early December — the short rains

The second, lesser rainy season. It is normally patchier and less disruptive than April and May — a storm in the afternoon and a clear morning after it — and it brings the migrant birds, which makes this the finest birding window in the Kenyan year. Prices are low, the country is green and the risk is that you get a wetter fortnight than you hoped. Late December turns busy and expensive again over the holidays.

The same month in different places

The Rift Valley and the highlands — Nairobi, Naivasha, Nanyuki — are temperate rather than tropical because of the altitude. Expect warm days and cold nights all year, and the coldest nights between June and August. See Naivasha and Nanyuki.

The Maasai Mara and the southern plains follow the two rains closely. Dry from late June to October and again in January and February; difficult in April. The nearest town with ordinary hotel rooms is Narok, on the road in from Nairobi.

Mount Kenya has its own weather and its own two climbing windows, roughly January to February and July to October, matching the dry spells. The mountain makes cloud in the afternoon in almost any month, so the view comes early in the morning.

The coast is hot and humid year round with a warm sea. Wettest in April and May; clearest in January and February and from July to October. See Mombasa and Diani Beach.

Choosing your dates

If you are combining a safari and the coast — which is what most two-week Kenya trips are — the windows that suit both are July to October and January to February. A May trip will give you a wet beach and heavy tracks at the same time, which is the one combination to avoid.

If you are going for the Mara specifically, book for the season and not for the river crossings. The herds move with the rain, the dates shift every year, and an operator who guarantees you a crossing is selling you something nobody owns. Any good camp will tell you the same thing.

If you are travelling in your later years, the mid-year dry season is the kinder choice: cooler in the highlands, less humid, firmer roads and shorter drives between sightings. The altitude is the thing to plan around rather than the heat — give yourself an easy first day in Nairobi before any long drive. The Kenya travel guide goes into that in more detail.

One practical point that applies whatever month you choose: since 5 October 2026 most visitors must hold Kenyan government travel health insurance, bought as part of the electronic travel authorisation or afterwards through the eCitizen portal, and a policy from home does not replace it. Apply at least two weeks before you fly.

Questions readers actually ask

What is the single best month to visit Kenya?


If you are coming once and mainly for wildlife, choose a month between late June and October. The country is dry, the grass is short, animals concentrate around water and the plains are easy to drive. Prices and crowds peak in July and August, so September and early October give you most of the same conditions with a little more room.

Which months should I avoid?


April and May are the heart of the long rains and the weakest weeks of the year for most visitors: tracks turn, some camps close for refurbishment and the coast is at its wettest. The compensation is low prices and green landscapes, so it is not a wasted trip, just a harder one.

When does the wildebeest migration reach the Maasai Mara?


The herds are usually in the Mara somewhere between July and October, with the Mara River crossings most often seen from late July into September. Nobody can promise you a crossing on a given date. The timing follows the rain, it shifts from year to year, and a crossing can be hours of waiting and then nothing. Book the season, not the spectacle, and treat seeing one as luck rather than an itinerary item.

Is the coast on the same calendar as the safari parks?


Not quite. Mombasa and Diani are hot and humid all year and the sea stays warm all year. The coast's wettest weeks are the same long rains of April and May, and its clearest spells are roughly January to February and July to October. So a combined safari and beach trip works well in those windows and badly in May.

How cold does it get in the highlands?


Colder than people expect. Nairobi sits at about 1,800 metres and Nanyuki at about 1,950, and June through August nights are genuinely cold, with rooms that are often unheated. If your trip combines the highlands and the coast you need both a fleece and swimming things.
