Perth’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Perth — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Perth is a compact city on a very wide river, and almost everything a visitor wants sits in a strip about two kilometres long. The CBD holds Hay and Murray Street malls, the heritage arcades and St Georges Terrace; Elizabeth Quay and Barrack Square put you on the Swan River itself, with the ferry to South Perth and the bell tower at the water’s edge. Northbridge, a short walk over the railway, is the eating and gallery quarter. East Perth is quieter and greener along the river, and Burswood, across the water, is the stadium and casino precinct. Kings Park — one of the largest inner-city parks in the world, with the best view in Perth — rises directly behind the western end of town. Luxury

Perth’s two best hotels could hardly be less alike: one is a restored nineteenth-century government building, the other a resort tower across the river. Choose by whether you want to walk to the city or be driven to it.

Como the Treasury

$$$$ LuxuryCBD1 Cathedral AvenueIn the old State Buildings

Perth’s finest hotel, set inside the restored State Buildings on Cathedral Avenue, with high ceilings, deep window reveals and an unhurried, almost club-like calm. There is a rooftop pool, a serious spa, and some of the best eating in the city in the same block of buildings. St Georges Terrace, the Perth Mint, the cathedral and the Hay Street arcades are all within a block or two, and the free city buses stop at the door. If you want one grown-up address in Perth, this is it.

Crown Towers Perth

$$$$ LuxuryBurswoodGreat Eastern HighwayLarge resort pool

A full resort across the river at Burswood — very large rooms, a long palm-lined pool, a spa, and a whole precinct of restaurants, bars and the casino downstairs. It suits travellers who want everything under one roof and a short, level walk to dinner, and it is the obvious base for a night at Perth Stadium next door. The trade-off is honest: you are a train ride or a short drive from the city centre rather than in it.

Boutique

Perth kept more of its old buildings than it is given credit for, and the best small rooms in town are inside them.

The Melbourne Hotel

$$$ UpscaleCBD west33 Milligan StreetHeritage corner building

A handsome old corner hotel on Milligan Street, confusingly named for the other city, carefully brought back with a modern wing behind the original facade. Rooms in the heritage half have the character and the odd angles; the new rooms are quieter and more regular. It is a short flat walk to Hay Street, King Street’s galleries and boutiques, and the Perth Arena, and five minutes from Kings Park’s eastern stairs. Good value for a central address with some personality.

DoubleTree by Hilton Perth Waterfront

$$$ UpscaleElizabeth Quay1 Barrack SquareOn the river

The best-placed modern hotel in Perth, standing on Barrack Square at the water’s edge, with the Bell Tower, the Elizabeth Quay promenade and the South Perth ferry jetty outside the door. The CBD begins across the road, so you are a few minutes from St Georges Terrace on foot, and the free CAT buses stop nearby.

Budget & Value

Perth is not a cheap city, but it has a long-standing habit of good four-star rooms at fair rates on the eastern edge of the centre.

Pan Pacific Perth

$$ Mid-rangeEast Perth207 Adelaide TerraceOutdoor pool

A big, dependable hotel at the eastern end of Adelaide Terrace, with an outdoor pool, generous rooms and some of the best city-and-river views in Perth from the upper floors. It is about fifteen minutes on foot to the Hay Street malls, less to Elizabeth Quay, and the CAT bus runs along the terrace. Rooms here are regularly the most room for the money in the central strip, which is why conference visitors fill it.

Family-friendly

Perth is an easy city with children — a very safe centre, free city buses, big parks and a beach day in every direction — and the hotel question is mostly about the pool.

The Westin Perth

$$$ UpscaleCBD east480 Hay StreetIndoor pool and spa

A large, modern hotel on Hay Street with an indoor pool, a proper gym and a spa, and the most consistently comfortable beds in the city. Rooms are big enough for a rollaway, connecting rooms are easy to request, and the Hibernian Place courtyard downstairs means you can eat without crossing a road at night. The Hay Street mall, the Perth Mint and Elizabeth Quay are all within a flat ten-minute walk.

Adults-only

Perth has no significant hotel that enforces a strict adults-only age policy, and we would rather say so than name one that does not exist. For a quiet, grown-up few days, the heritage rooms at Como the Treasury and a high river-facing floor at the DoubleTree are the two best choices in the city. Ask for a floor away from the pool deck and the function rooms when you book.

Spa & pool

Perth’s swimming is really at the beaches — Cottesloe, City Beach and Scarborough are all a short train or bus ride west, and all are patrolled in season. For hotel water, the indoor pool and spa at The Westin, the rooftop pool at Como the Treasury and the large outdoor pool at Crown Towers across the river are the three best in the city, and all three are described above.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask us most when choosing where to stay in Perth.

What is the best area to stay in Perth for first-time visitors?

The CBD between Elizabeth Quay and Hay Street, because almost everything a visitor wants is inside a twenty-minute walk of it: the river, the arcades, the malls, the Mint, Northbridge’s museums and the eastern stairs up into Kings Park. Elizabeth Quay and Barrack Square are the prettiest corner of that area and put the South Perth ferry at your feet. Choose Burswood only if you want the resort and the stadium, and East Perth if you want quiet and river views for less.

How much do Perth hotels cost?

Expect roughly 140 to 220 Australian dollars a night for clean value rooms, about 220 to 380 for comfortable four-star and apartment stays, and from around 500 upward for the best suites. Perth has a resources-driven business market, so midweek rates in the CBD can be higher than weekends — the opposite of most tourist cities. Rates spike for big events at Perth Stadium and RAC Arena.

Is Kings Park worth staying near?

It is worth walking into every morning, but you do not need to sleep beside it. Kings Park sits on an escarpment directly above the western end of the city, and the walk up from the CBD takes fifteen to twenty minutes, with the best free view in Perth at the top. Stay in the centre and treat the park as a daily outing rather than paying for a room on its edge.

How do I get to Rottnest Island and Fremantle?

Ferries to Rottnest Island leave from Barrack Square at Elizabeth Quay in the city and from Fremantle and Hillarys, and the Fremantle crossing is the shortest. Fremantle itself is about half an hour by train from Perth Underground and is worth a full day for the markets, the prison and the harbour. Book Rottnest ferries ahead in the summer school holidays and check the current timetable, because sailings change by season.

Which Perth hotels take dogs?

We will not name one until we have read the policy on the hotel’s own site. Fees, weight limits and room-type conditions vary too much for a general answer to be any help. Shortlist from the city list on this page and telephone to confirm before you pay.

Do I need a car in Perth?

Not in the city. Trains and buses reach the beaches, Fremantle and the stadium, and the free CAT buses loop the central area. Hire a car for the day trips that make Western Australia — the Swan Valley, Rottnest’s ferry ports, Margaret River or the Pinnacles — and collect it on the morning you leave town.
