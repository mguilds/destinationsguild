Phuket Travel Guide

Thailand's biggest island and its busiest beach base — Andaman sand for every mood, the Big Buddha on the hill, the Old Town's shophouse streets, and the boats out to Phi Phi and Phang Nga.

Phuket is the island where most people meet the Andaman Sea for the first time. It hangs off the southwest coast of Thailand, connected to the mainland by a bridge, and it packs an improbable amount into one place: a west coast strung with beaches, a hilltop marble Buddha visible for miles, a compact old town of Sino-Portuguese shophouses, and a working harbour of long-tail boats that fan out each morning to some of the most photographed islands on earth. It is loud in places and serene in others. Patong throbs with neon and nightlife; Kata and Karon roll out long family sands; Kamala and Surin keep things quiet and expensive; and inland the rubber plantations and shrines feel a world away from the beach bars. More than anything, Phuket is a base — the launch pad for Phi Phi, Phang Nga Bay and the Similans — as much as a destination in itself.

The action is nearly all on the west coast, where the beaches face the Andaman Sea. Patong is the epicentre — the biggest town beach, the shopping, and the neon strip of Bangla Road after dark. South of it, Kata and Karon are the softer, family-minded beaches, while Kamala and Surin further north are calmer and more upmarket. Away from the sand, Phuket Old Town in the island's southeast is the cultural heart: pastel Sino-Portuguese shophouses, café-lined lanes and a genuinely local feel that the beaches lack.

One hard truth to file before you go, because it costs lives every year: the Andaman can turn dangerous in the monsoon. From roughly May to October the sea off Patong, Kata and the other west-coast beaches builds powerful rip currents, and drownings are sadly common when people ignore the warning flags. Red flags mean do not enter the water — take them seriously, whatever the weather looks like from the sand.

Thailand runs on cash more than you might expect — carry baht for markets, long-tail boats and street food, even though hotels and bigger restaurants take cards. Phuket is pricier than the Thai mainland, especially on the beach and in high season, but by international resort standards it still stretches a budget a long way. The island keeps Indochina Time (UTC+7) year-round, with no daylight saving to track.

Why visit Phuket

Four reasons Thailand's big island keeps drawing the crowds.

Andaman beaches for every taste

From the roar of Patong to the family sands of Kata and Karon and the hush of Surin, the west coast has a beach to match any mood.

Thailand's best island-hopping base

Phi Phi, Phang Nga Bay, the Similans and dozens of smaller islands are all a boat ride away — nowhere in the country launches you faster.

Patong's famous nightlife

Bangla Road is Southeast Asia's most notorious strip — bars, clubs, cabaret and street food that runs until dawn, love it or leave it.

Sino-Portuguese Old Town charm

The pastel shophouse streets of the old town are the island's cultural counterweight — cafés, galleries and a slower, more local Phuket.

Top things to do in Phuket

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Patong Beach & Bangla Road

The island's headline beach by day and its wildest street by night — come for the swim, stay for the neon and the cabaret.

● The Big Buddha viewpoint

A 45-metre marble Buddha on the Nakkerd Hills, with sweeping views over Chalong Bay and the west coast. Go early to beat the heat.

● Phuket Old Town

Wander the Sino-Portuguese streets of Thalang and Dibuk Roads — pastel shophouses, coffee, street art and a Sunday walking market.

● Phi Phi Islands day trip

The most famous boat trip of all: limestone cliffs, Maya Bay and snorkelling stops in impossibly clear water. Book ahead in high season.

● Phang Nga Bay & James Bond Island

Sea-kayak among the karst towers rising from emerald water, and see the leaning rock made famous by the 1974 film.

● Promthep Cape sunset

The southern tip of the island is the classic spot to watch the sun drop into the Andaman, an easy hop from Kata and Karon beaches.

Where to stay in Phuket

Choose by the trip you want. Patong is for people who want the busiest beach, the nightlife and the shopping on their doorstep — big, lively and never quiet. Kata and Karon are the family choice: broad, sandy beaches with a calmer, gentler pace just south of the chaos. Kamala and Surin, further north, are the upscale, quieter enclaves — smaller beaches, better restaurants and higher prices. Old Town suits travellers who want culture over sand: it's cheaper and full of character, though you'll ride out to the beach. And Bang Tao and Laguna in the northwest are the luxury resort belt — a self-contained cluster of five-stars around a long beach and a lagoon. First-timers who want life outside the hotel should look at Patong or Kata; couples and families after calm should head north.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

November–April is the dry season and the best time to visit: calm Andaman seas, blue skies and reliable sun. May–October is the southwest monsoon — rain, rougher water, and a lush, cheap, quieter island. In the monsoon months heed the red-flag rip-current warnings on the west-coast beaches; the swimming can be genuinely dangerous.

Getting there

Phuket International (HKT) sits at the north of the island, roughly 45 minutes to an hour from the west-coast beaches. It has direct flights across Asia plus easy Bangkok connections. On the island, Grab and taxis get you around, and scooters are popular — though island taxis are notoriously pricey, so use Grab where you can.

Phuket travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Phuket?

November to April is the dry season and the best time to go: calm Andaman seas, blue skies and reliable sun. May to October is the southwest monsoon, with rain, rougher water and lush, cheap, quieter conditions. In the monsoon months take the red-flag rip-current warnings seriously — the swimming can be genuinely dangerous.

Is Phuket good for families?

Yes, especially the calmer west-coast beaches. Kata and Karon are the classic family choices — big, sandy and gentler than Patong — while Kamala and Surin are quieter and more upscale. Patong is fun but loud and centred on nightlife. Away from the beach there are elephant sanctuaries, aquariums and easy boat trips that suit children.

How do you get around Phuket?

The Grab app is the cheapest and simplest way to get between beaches and town. Phuket's metered taxis and tuk-tuks are notoriously expensive and limited, so pre-agree fares or use Grab where you can. Many visitors rent scooters, which are convenient but account for a high share of tourist accidents — helmets and caution are essential.

Which airport do you fly into for Phuket?

Phuket International Airport (HKT) sits at the north of the island and handles direct flights from across Asia plus easy connections through Bangkok. It's roughly 45 minutes to an hour from the main west-coast beaches such as Patong, Kata and Karon, depending on traffic.
