Lake Fishing in Missouri — Five Ozark Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Table Rock, Lake Taneycomo, Lake of the Ozarks, Truman and Stockton: what swims in each, the 2026 rules from the Missouri Department of Conservation, the permits a visitor needs, where to put a boat in, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Missouri Department of Conservation, 12 November 2026

Missouri has no natural lakes worth the name. What it has instead is a string of big reservoirs in the Ozark hills, built across the White and Osage rivers and their branches, and they are some of the best fishing water in the middle of the country: largemouth and spotted bass on the rocky points of Table Rock, crappie by the bucket on Lake of the Ozarks and Truman, walleye on Stockton, and — below Table Rock Dam — Lake Taneycomo, a cold river-lake full of rainbow and brown trout, right on the edge of Branson.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, bass anglers towing a boat, crappie fishermen and fly anglers heading to Taneycomo. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 12 November 2026 from the agency that sets it, the Missouri Department of Conservation (MDC), and is dated. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The best months, in plain words

These are southern-Midwest lakes: hot summers, mild winters, and spring is the big time everywhere except Taneycomo, which fishes all year.

LakeBest monthsWhy
Table Rock LakeMarch to May; again October and NovemberOur guidance: bass move up onto the points and into the creek arms in spring, and crappie with them. Summer bass fishing is deep, early and late in the day.
Lake TaneycomoAll yearOur guidance: the water comes from the bottom of Table Rock Lake and stays cold, so the trout bite in every month. Winter and early spring are the quiet, uncrowded time.
Lake of the OzarksMarch to May; September to NovemberOur guidance: crappie in spring and autumn; MDC expects crappie fishing to be “excellent in 2026”. Summer weekends bring heavy boat traffic — fish early or midweek.

Table Rock Lake — clear water and bass on the points

The White River, dammed just west of Branson by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers; long arms reach west to Shell Knob and up the James River.

Table Rock Lake, from Branson to Shell Knob

What swims there. Largemouth, smallmouth and spotted bass above all — MDC’s 2026 prospect report says “Largemouth bass are plentiful throughout the lake.” Crappie, white bass, catfish and walleye too; in MDC’s James River arm survey every walleye caught was over 15 inches and more than half were over 20.

How people fish it. From a boat, mostly: bass on rocky points, bluff walls and brush piles; crappie on brush in the creek arms; walleye up the James River arm in spring. The water is very clear, so light line helps. There is bank fishing at the state park and Corps parks.

Keeping a fish. Black bass: 6 a day in total; largemouth and smallmouth 15 inches; spotted bass 12 inches since 30 April 2026. Crappie: 15 a day, 10 inches. Walleye and sauger: 18 inches, 4 a day. Paddlefish: 34 inches.

Confirmed with the Missouri Department of Conservation, 12 November 2026 — 2026 Summary, Table Rock Lake: “10″ minimum length limit; 15 fish daily limit” (crappie); walleye and sauger “18″ minimum length limit”; paddlefish “34″ minimum body length”. MDC news release, 19 February 2026: the spotted bass minimum “will lower from 15 inches to 12 inches”, effective 30 April 2026. 2026 prospect report: “Largemouth bass are plentiful throughout the lake”; James River arm walleye “100% of walleye were > 15″ and 56% were > 20″”.

Boat ramps and hire. Table Rock State Park, by the dam, has a free concrete ramp and State Park Marina; Kings River Marina and Campbell Point Marina at Shell Knob rent boats (details below).

Nearest towns: Branson and Branson West at the dam end; Kimberling City in the middle of the lake; Shell Knob at the west end.

Lake Taneycomo — a trout river in Branson’s back yard

The narrow lake between Table Rock Dam and Powersite Dam at Forsyth. Its water comes cold from the depths of Table Rock, which is why trout live here and bass mostly do not.

Lake Taneycomo, from Table Rock Dam to Branson and Hollister

What swims there. Rainbow and brown trout. MDC’s 2026 report says brown trout numbers “have increased compared to previous years and quality size fish are abundant”, and that quality rainbows are abundant in the upper lake “as a result of the slot limit.”

How people fish it. Drifting from a rented jon boat or pontoon with the current; wading and fly fishing in the upper lake when the generators at Table Rock Dam are off. The water rises fast when they start — wade carefully and get out when it rises.

The upper lake has its own rules. No fishing within 760 feet below Table Rock Dam. From there down to the mouth of Fall Creek: flies and artificial lures only (no soft plastics, no natural or scented baits, for any species); pole and line only; rainbow trout between 12 and 20 inches must go back. On the whole lake brown trout must be 20 inches, and of your 4 trout a day only one may be a brown. Waders with felt or other porous soles are banned.

The trout permit. Upstream of the U.S. Highway 65 bridge you need a trout permit for any fishing, as well as a fishing permit unless you are exempt.

Confirmed with the Missouri Department of Conservation, 12 November 2026 — Lake Taneycomo regulations page: “Brown trout: 20 inch minimum length limit”; “the daily limit of 4 trout, combined total of both species, may include only 1 brown trout”; within 760 feet below Table Rock Dam “No fishing is allowed”; to the mouth of Fall Creek, rainbow trout “12 to 20 inches protected slot length limit” and “Only flies and artificial lures may be used”. Trout permit page: required “for all fishing year-round in Lake Taneycomo upstream from U.S. Highway 65 bridge.”

Boat hire. Lilleys’ Landing and Scotty’s Trout Dock in Branson rent boats and have guides (details below).

Nearest towns: Branson and Hollister, on either bank.

Lake of the Ozarks — crappie, bass and a long, busy shoreline

The Osage River, dammed at Bagnell Dam in 1931; a winding lake with arms up the Niangua, Gravois and Grand Glaize.

Lake of the Ozarks, from Bagnell Dam to Laurie and Camdenton

What swims there. Crappie and largemouth bass, mainly; catfish, white bass and paddlefish too. MDC: “Crappie fishing will be excellent in 2026”, and “Catch rates for largemouth bass remain high.”

How people fish it. Crappie on the many boat docks and brush piles; bass along docks and in the coves; catfish in the old river channel. Summer weekends are crowded with pleasure boats — the quiet fishing is early morning, midweek, and spring and autumn.

Keeping a fish. Crappie: 15 a day, 9 inches. Black bass: 6 a day; largemouth and smallmouth 15 inches, spotted bass 12 inches. Walleye: the statewide rule (4 a day, 15 inches). Paddlefish: 34 inches.

Confirmed with the Missouri Department of Conservation, 12 November 2026 — 2026 Summary and crappie special-area page, Lake of the Ozarks: crappie “9″ minimum length limit; 15 fish daily limit”; black bass “15″ minimum length limit on largemouth and smallmouth bass; 12″ minimum length limit on spotted bass”; paddlefish 34-inch minimum. 2026 prospect report quoted above.

Boat ramps and hire. Lake of the Ozarks State Park has ramps at Grand Glaize Beach (four lanes, all year), Pa He Tsi (March to October) and Public Beach 1; launching is $5 a day for boats up to 30 feet. The park’s marina at Osage Beach rents fishing boats.

Nearest towns: Lake Ozark and Osage Beach at the dam end; Camdenton on the Niangua arm; Laurie and Sunrise Beach farther west.

Truman Lake — timber, crappie and big catfish

The Osage River again, upstream of Lake of the Ozarks, dammed at Warsaw by the Corps of Engineers. Much of the old forest was left standing when the valley flooded.

Truman Lake, from Warsaw to Clinton

What swims there. Crappie and largemouth bass in the timber; blue catfish — MDC says “Blue catfish in particular are abundant” — and hybrid striped bass, with “several successful year classes since 2019” now in the lake. Paddlefish in the river arms in spring.

How people fish it. Crappie and bass among the standing trees — go slowly, the timber is everywhere and much of it is just under the surface. Catfish in the river channels; hybrids chasing shad in the open lake.

Keeping a fish. Crappie: 15 a day, 9 inches. Black bass: 6 a day; largemouth and smallmouth 15 inches, spotted bass 12 inches. Hybrids and white bass: 15 a day together, no more than 4 over 18 inches (statewide). Paddlefish: 15 March to 30 April, 2 a day, 34 inches here.

Confirmed with the Missouri Department of Conservation, 12 November 2026 — 2026 Summary, Truman Lake: crappie 9-inch minimum and 15 a day; black bass as Lake of the Ozarks; paddlefish 34-inch minimum. Statewide: paddlefish season “March 15–April 30”, 2 a day. 2026 prospect report: “Blue catfish in particular are abundant.”

Boat ramps and hire. Harry S Truman State Park, west of Warsaw, has a free four-lane public ramp open all year. Long Shoal Marina at Warsaw and Bucksaw Resort & Marina near Clinton rent boats.

Nearest towns: Warsaw at the dam end; Clinton at the north end.

Stockton Lake — Missouri’s walleye lake

A 24,900-acre Corps of Engineers lake on the Sac River in Cedar, Dade and Polk counties, north-west of Springfield. Clear water and few houses on the shore.

Stockton Lake, from Stockton to Greenfield

What swims there. Walleye — in MDC’s latest survey about 89% of the walleye caught were over the 15-inch minimum. Crappie, largemouth and smallmouth bass and white bass too.

How people fish it. Walleye trolled or drifted along the points and flats with nightcrawlers or crankbaits; crappie on brush; bass on rock. Wind on the open water can be strong.

Keeping a fish. Walleye: the statewide rule, 4 a day at 15 inches. Crappie: 15 a day, 10 inches. Black bass: 6 a day; largemouth and smallmouth 15 inches, spotted bass 12 inches.

Confirmed with the Missouri Department of Conservation, 12 November 2026 — 2026 prospect report: Stockton is a 24,900-acre U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reservoir; walleye “approximately 89% of the fish captured over the 15-inch minimum length limit”. 2026 Summary and crappie special-area page: Stockton crappie 10-inch minimum, 15 a day.

Boat ramps and hire. Stockton State Park has free ramps at the marina and at Hartley Cove, and its marina rents pontoons, fishing boats and kayaks and sells bait. Orleans Trail Marina near Stockton rents fishing boats.

Nearest towns: Stockton on the north side; Greenfield to the south.

The permits and the limits, plainly

One state, one agency, and simpler than most: children fish free, and so do Missouri residents 65 and over.

Missouri fishing permits, 2026 prices

Who needs one. Everyone 16 and over, except Missouri residents aged 65 and older. Anglers 15 and under, resident or not, need no fishing permit. Exempt anglers still need a trout permit where one is required.

For a visitor. Nonresident annual fishing permit $57; daily fishing permit $9 (the same price for residents and visitors). Trout permit $24 for nonresidents.

For a Missourian. Annual fishing permit $14; trout permit $12. Youth trout permit (15 and under) $6.

Where to buy. From any permit vendor, online at mdc.mo.gov/buypermits, or by phone on 800-392-4115 ($1 extra). Carry it printed or in MDC’s MO Fishing app. Permits bought now run to the end of February 2027.

Free Fishing Days. The Saturday and Sunday after the first Monday in June (6–7 June in 2026). Size and number limits still apply. MDC had not announced the 2027 dates when we checked.

Confirmed with the Missouri Department of Conservation, 12 November 2026 — permit pages (prices in effect since 1 January 2026): resident annual $14, nonresident annual $57, daily $9, trout permit resident $12, nonresident $24, youth $6. Fishing permits page: “Missouri residents 65 and older are exempt from needing a fishing permit.” The October 2025 Conservation Commission actions confirm “a new $24 nonresident trout permit” (an earlier proposal of $40 was not adopted). Free Fishing Days release, 13 May 2026: “other fishing regulations remain in effect, such as limits on size and number.” Prices change. Buy from MDC or a licensed vendor.

Eating what you keep

MDC’s advice is to “Eat smaller, legal-size, younger fish because they tend to have lower levels of contamination.” Lake-by-lake advice, with extra care for pregnant women and children under 13, is published by the Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services — look up your lake there before you plan a fish fry.

Questions

Do I need a trout permit to fish Lake Taneycomo?

Yes, on the upper lake, even if you are not fishing for trout. The Missouri Department of Conservation requires a trout permit, as well as a fishing permit unless you are exempt, for all fishing year-round in Lake Taneycomo upstream from the U.S. Highway 65 bridge. Downstream of that bridge you need the trout permit only to keep trout. In 2026 the trout permit is $12 for residents, $24 for nonresidents and $6 for anglers aged 15 and under.

What does a Missouri fishing permit cost a visitor in 2026?

The Missouri Department of Conservation's prices in effect since 1 January 2026 are $57 for a nonresident annual fishing permit and $9 for a daily fishing permit, which residents and nonresidents both use. Missouri residents pay $14 a year. Anglers 15 and younger need no fishing permit, and Missouri residents 65 and older are exempt, but both still need a trout permit where one is required.

What size crappie can I keep on Lake of the Ozarks and Truman Lake?

On both lakes crappie must be at least 9 inches long and the daily limit is 15. On Table Rock Lake and Stockton Lake the minimum is 10 inches, also 15 a day. Elsewhere in Missouri the statewide crappie limit is 30 a day with no length limit. These are the Missouri Department of Conservation's 2026 special-area rules.

Did the spotted bass rule on Table Rock Lake change?

Yes. The Missouri Department of Conservation announced on 19 February 2026 that the minimum length for spotted bass on Table Rock Lake would drop from 15 inches to 12 inches from 30 April 2026. Largemouth and smallmouth bass stay at 15 inches, and the daily limit is still six black bass in total. Some printed copies of the 2026 regulations summary went to press before the change.

Can I use live bait below Table Rock Dam on Lake Taneycomo?

No. From the closed zone 760 feet below Table Rock Dam down to the mouth of Fall Creek, the Missouri Department of Conservation allows only flies and artificial lures, bans soft plastics and natural or scented baits for every species, and protects rainbow trout between 12 and 20 inches. Brown trout must be 20 inches to keep, and only one of your four trout a day may be a brown. Below Fall Creek, bait is allowed.
