Why go

The tallest thing in the capital, and the best view of it.

The Washington Monument stands at the center of the National Mall, a plain white marble obelisk 555 feet tall honoring George Washington, who led the Continental Army through the Revolution and became the nation's first president. It rises over everything around it and can be seen from much of the city.

From the outside it is free and always there to admire. Going inside takes a little planning: an elevator carries you to an observation level 500 feet up, with windows on all four sides looking out over the Capitol, the White House, the Lincoln Memorial and across the river into Virginia. The trick is the ticket, and this guide tells you how to get one.

How to get Washington Monument tickets

Tickets are free or nearly free, but they go fast. Here is how it works.

Reserve ahead (best)

Book at Recreation.gov or by phone at (877) 444-6777. Tickets open 30 days before the date and are released every day at 10:00 a.m. (Washington time). Each advance ticket carries a non-refundable $1.00 service charge.

In spring, summer and fall they often sell out right after release. Create your Recreation.gov account and sign in before 10:00 a.m., then book the moment they appear. A second batch is released one day before each date, so check again the day before you want to go.

Walk up on the day

A limited number of free same-day tickets are handed out first come, first served from the ticket window at the back of the Washington Monument Lodge, on 15th Street between Madison and Jefferson Drives. The window opens at 8:45 a.m., the line forms well before that, and tickets usually run out.

One person aged 16 or older may collect up to six tickets for an available time.

On the day: arrive up to 30 minutes before the time on your ticket. Recreation.gov tickets can be shown printed, on your phone, or in the National Park Service app. If weather closes the monument, tickets for the affected times are voided, so keep an eye on the forecast. Groups can reserve up to 55 tickets online, up to 30 days ahead.

What to see at the Washington Monument

Six things not to miss.

The top

The 500-foot view

The observation level sits at the base of the pointed top, with two windows on each side. On a clear day you look out over Washington, Maryland and Virginia: the Capitol, the White House, the Lincoln Memorial, the Tidal Basin and the Pentagon.

One floor down

The small museum

On the 490-foot level, just below the windows, a small exhibit tells the story of the monument and its long construction.

On the way down

The commemorative stones

The inside walls are lined with carved stones given by states, cities, countries, groups and individuals. Some can be seen through the elevator on the ride down.

The lobby

George Washington's statue

A statue of Washington greets visitors in the ground-floor lobby before the elevator ride up.

Look closely outside

The color line

About a third of the way up, the marble changes shade. It marks where construction stopped in 1854 and later restarted with stone from a different quarry.

Around the base

The ring of flags

A circle of American flags surrounds the monument on its grassy knoll, a fine spot for photographs.

A short history

The monument was designed by architect Robert Mills, and its cornerstone was laid on July 4, 1848. A private society raised the money, but by 1854 the funds had run out with the shaft only about 156 feet high. The Civil War came, and the stump stood unfinished for more than 20 years.

In 1876 the federal government took over, and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers under Thomas Casey finished the job. Rather than the 600 feet Mills had planned, Casey made the height ten times the width of the base, which is how it came to be 555 feet. The capstone was set on December 6, 1884, and for a few years the monument was the tallest building in the world.

President Chester Arthur dedicated it on February 21, 1885, and it opened to the public on October 9, 1888. Visitors could once climb its 896 steps, but the stairs have been closed since 1976. Today everyone rides the elevator.

Best time to visit the Washington Monument

Winter

Outside the busy seasons, so tickets are easier to get. Dress warmly for the line at the Lodge.

Spring

Cherry blossom season around the Tidal Basin, and the hardest time to get a ticket. Book on the release morning, 30 days out.

Summer

Hot, humid and busy. Tickets sell out quickly, and afternoon storms can close the monument.

Fall

Comfortable weather and crisp views. Still busy, so reserve ahead.

How to get to the Washington Monument

By Metro or train

Public transportation is the best way to reach the Mall. Smithsonian station (Blue, Orange and Silver lines) is the closest Metro stop, a short walk west along the Mall. Amtrak, MARC and VRE trains serve Union Station and L'Enfant Plaza. The monument's address is 2 15th Street NW, at 15th Street and Madison Drive.

By car

Parking is limited. The National Park Service points drivers to the Paddle Boat parking lot on Maine Avenue SW and to the spaces along Ohio Drive SW, between the Lincoln and Jefferson Memorials.

Washington Monument accessibility

Elevator. The elevator to the observation level is accessible for visitors in wheelchairs, so the top is open to everyone with a ticket.

Parking. Accessible parking is available at the Paddle Boat parking lot and at the World War II Memorial.

Phones. Mobile service can be unreliable at the top and at the base, so download your ticket before you arrive.

Questions or help planning: call the park at (202) 426-6841.

Washington Monument FAQ

Is the Washington Monument free?

Yes. There is no admission fee. Same-day walk-up tickets are free, and advance tickets reserved online carry a non-refundable $1.00 service charge per ticket.

Do I need a ticket to go inside the Washington Monument?

Yes. Everyone aged two and older needs a timed ticket to enter. Reserve up to 30 days ahead at Recreation.gov or by calling (877) 444-6777, or try for a free same-day ticket at the Washington Monument Lodge from 8:45 a.m.

What time are Washington Monument tickets released?

Advance tickets are released daily at 10:00 a.m. for the date 30 days ahead, and more tickets are released one day before each date. In spring, summer and fall they often sell out right after release.

What are the Washington Monument's hours?

The Washington Monument is open daily from 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. It is closed on Independence Day and Christmas Day, and one day a month for maintenance.

How tall is the Washington Monument?

555 feet, 5 1/8 inches, and 55 feet wide at the base. When it was finished in 1884 it was the tallest building in the world.

Can you climb the stairs of the Washington Monument?

No. There are 896 steps to the 500-foot observation level, but the stairs have been closed to the public since 1976. Visitors ride the elevator.

Why is the Washington Monument two colors?

Construction stopped in 1854 when the money ran out. When the government finished the upper two-thirds by 1884, it used marble from a different quarry, and the two sections have weathered differently.

Is the Washington Monument wheelchair accessible?

Yes. The elevator to the observation level is accessible for visitors in wheelchairs, and accessible parking is available at the Paddle Boat parking lot and at the World War II Memorial.
