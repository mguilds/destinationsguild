Where to Stay in Pokhara

Where to Stay in Pokhara — Lakeside, Sarangkot & Town

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Pokhara is where Nepal lets you sit down. A long lake with wooded hills behind it, a wall of eight-thousanders on the skyline when the air is clear, and a town that exists mostly to feed and rest people who are about to walk into the Annapurnas or have just walked out. Stay on Lakeside for the water and the restaurants, up at Sarangkot if the mountain view at dawn is what you came for, and in the old town or Damside if you would rather be somewhere that is not mainly about visitors.

Choosing Your Area in Pokhara

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

Lakeside (Baidam)

The strip along the eastern shore of Phewa Tal, and where most visitors stay. A couple of kilometres of guest houses, bakeries, bookshops, gear shops and restaurants with gardens running down to the water. The northern end is quieter and greener; the middle is the busiest; the southern end thins out toward Damside. Rooms on the lake side of the road are worth the small premium, and upper floors sometimes see the mountains over the rooftops.

Sarangkot and the ridges above

The ridge north-west of town, about an hour up by road, is the classic sunrise viewpoint: Machhapuchhre and the Annapurnas lit before the valley has woken, with cloud lying in the lake basin below. A handful of lodges sit along the ridge, which saves you the pre-dawn drive and gives you the view twice. It is cold up there at night in winter, and you are a long way from a restaurant that is not your own.

The old town and Damside

Pokhara existed long before the lakeside strip. The old bazaar in the north of town has Newar shopfronts and temples and almost no visitors, and Damside, at the southern end of the lake, is quieter and cheaper than Lakeside with the same water in front of it. Both are a short taxi ride from the restaurants. Choose these if you want a trip that is not spent entirely among other travellers.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Pokhara trip goes.

The view is seasonal, and that is the whole point

Pokhara’s mountains are a seasonal event, not a permanent fixture. From October to early December and again in March and April the air is usually clear enough to see the Annapurna wall and Machhapuchhre from the lakeside. Through the monsoon, roughly June to September, they are often invisible for days at a time, and in the spring haze they can wash out by mid-morning. If the mountains are the reason you are coming, give yourself several mornings rather than one, and look early.

Trekking starts here, and so do the permits

Pokhara is the gateway to the Annapurna trails, and the practical business of a trek — permits, guides, porters, hired gear, a bag left behind at the hotel — is all arranged in town. Nepal requires permits for its trekking areas, and the rules on whether a foreign trekker may walk without a licensed guide have changed more than once in recent years and differ between regions. Do not take the rule from a blog, this page included: confirm what is required for your route with the Nepal Tourism Board or a licensed agency in town before you set off.

Getting here, including the new airport

Most visitors arrive from Kathmandu, either on the short domestic flight or by road, which takes most of a day on a route that has improved but still depends on the season. Pokhara International Airport opened in 2023 and spent its first years handling almost nothing but domestic traffic; its first scheduled daily international service, a link to Dubai, began in September 2026. What is actually flying changes, so check live schedules rather than assuming an international connection exists.

Pokhara Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Pokhara.

Where is the best place to stay in Pokhara?

Lakeside for a first visit — the water, the food and the gear shops are all there. Sarangkot for one night if you want the sunrise without the pre-dawn drive. The old town or Damside if you would rather be somewhere quieter and more ordinary. Many people do several nights on Lakeside either side of a trek and one up on the ridge.

Can you see the Himalaya from Pokhara itself?

Yes, when the air is clear: the Annapurna range and the sharp fin of Machhapuchhre rise straight out of the skyline north of the lake, and on a good October morning they are reflected in the water. Clear air is the catch. October to early December and March to April are the reliable stretches; through the monsoon you may see nothing for a week.

How many nights do you need in Pokhara?

Two if it is a stop, four if it is a rest. There is a day in the lake, the World Peace Pagoda and the caves, a day going up to Sarangkot and perhaps paragliding off it, and a day doing nothing at all on a terrace — which is what Pokhara is actually for. If you are trekking, add a night on each side for arranging and recovering.

Is Pokhara a good base for people who are not trekking?

It is the best in Nepal. The lake, the boats, the pagoda, the sunrise ridge and the short walks in the hills behind town all ask very little of you, the food and hotels are good value, and the pace is gentle. Plenty of visitors come to Pokhara with no intention of walking anywhere and leave perfectly satisfied.
