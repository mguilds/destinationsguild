Where to Stay in Bali

Where to Stay in Bali — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Southern Bali is not one place but a string of very different coastlines, all within about an hour of the airport. Kuta and Legian are the old package-holiday heart — flat, busy and the island’s best value, with a long surf beach. Seminyak next door is quieter and more polished, full of boutique hotels, spas and good restaurants. Nusa Dua is a gated, manicured enclave of large resorts with calm swimming water and wide, level pavements, which makes it the easiest choice for anyone who values order and gentle ground underfoot; Jimbaran offers a sheltered bay and seafood grills, and Uluwatu, on the clifftops of the Bukit peninsula, trades easy beach access for dramatic ocean views and a good many steps. Below are southern Bali’s most enduring hotels by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com. Luxury

Bali’s finest run from a hillside village of private villas above Jimbaran Bay to the most formal beachfront address in Nusa Dua.

Four Seasons Resort Bali at Jimbaran Bay

$$$$ LuxuryJimbaranPrivate villasBay views

A hillside village of individual thatched villas stepping down to a sheltered fishing bay on Bali’s south-western coast, open since 1993 and still the island’s benchmark for polished service. Each villa has its own walled garden and plunge pool, so you can pass a whole day without seeing another guest, and the staff-to-guest ratio shows in small, unfussy attentions. Best for travellers who want genuine privacy and dependable standards, and who do not mind that the beach here is a working bay rather than a manicured strip.

Boutique

Family-friendly

Pet-friendly

An honest word first: Bali is a rabies-restricted province, and cats and dogs cannot clear customs at Denpasar airport — bringing a pet from overseas means importing through Jakarta with permits and quarantine, a process of months that may still be refused. The hotels below have real, published pet policies, but in practice they serve residents and long-stay visitors rather than holidaymakers. Always confirm the policy in writing before booking.

MAMAKA by Ovolo

$$ Mid-rangeKutaPets welcomeBeach opposite

A bright, design-forward hotel directly opposite Kuta beach, run by the Ovolo group, with a formal dog programme providing beds, bowls and treats. Dogs are accepted in designated garden-terrace rooms for a modest nightly fee; cats are not. Best for residents or long-stay visitors travelling with a dog who would rather have a proper hotel than a villa rental.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of Bali should I stay in?

For calm water, level ground and full-service resorts, choose Nusa Dua. For restaurants, small hotels and a lively but not rowdy beach, choose Seminyak. For the lowest prices and a long surf beach, choose Kuta or Legian. For clifftop views and sunsets, choose Uluwatu — and expect steps. For temples, rice terraces and cooler air, stay inland in Ubud instead.

Is the sea safe for swimming in southern Bali?

It depends entirely on where you are. Nusa Dua, Sanur and Tanjung Benoa sit behind a reef and are calm and gently shelving. Kuta, Legian, Seminyak and the west coast have strong rip currents that catch swimmers every year — swim only between the red-and-yellow flags where lifeguards patrol, and take the warnings seriously.

How far is Bali airport from the hotels?

Ngurah Rai International (DPS) sits in the middle of the southern strip. Expect roughly 15 minutes to Jimbaran, 20 to 30 minutes to Nusa Dua and Kuta, 30 to 45 minutes to Seminyak, and 45 minutes or more to Uluwatu — longer in the late-afternoon traffic. Arrange a hotel transfer or use a metered or app taxi rather than the touts at the door.

Do I need a visa for Bali?

Most visitors, including US, UK, Canadian, Australian and EU citizens, buy a visa on arrival, valid 30 days and extendable once. It is quicker to buy the electronic version online before you fly. Every arrival must also complete the free All Indonesia Arrival Card online within three days of travel, and Bali charges a one-off tourist levy of Rp150,000 per person. Rules change, so confirm with the official Indonesian immigration service before you travel.

When is the best time to visit Bali?

The dry season runs roughly from April to October, with June to September the sunniest and busiest. May, September and October are the sweet spot — dry weather without peak prices. See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Indonesia for the full picture.
