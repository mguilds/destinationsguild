Saint Vincent & the Grenadines

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines Travel Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is one country made of a steep volcanic main island and a scattered tail of smaller ones — usually counted as thirty-two islands and cays, nine of them inhabited — running south-west from Saint Vincent towards Grenada. About a hundred and fifty square miles of land in all, and something over a hundred thousand people, most of them on the big island. Saint Vincent is the working half: Kingstown, a market town built up the slope behind a working harbour, and behind that the green ridge of La Soufrière, which erupted explosively in April 2021 and is quiet again. The Grenadines are the half on the postcards — Bequia, Mustique, Canouan, Mayreau, Union Island and the Tobago Cays — reached by ferry and small plane rather than by road. The country has been independent since 27 October 1979 and it is English-speaking. One recent storm belongs in any honest guide: Hurricane Beryl crossed the Southern Grenadines on 1 July 2024, and the rebuilding there is not finished.

Why visit Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

Six reasons that hold up after the brochure is closed — and they are not the usual six.

It never went mass-market

There is no strip here, no row of thousand-room resorts, no fleet of jet skis. The international airport only opened in 2017, which is late by Caribbean standards, and the country never built the volume of rooms its neighbours did. The result is an archipelago that still feels like somewhere people live rather than somewhere people are processed.

The Tobago Cays

Five uninhabited islets inside a horseshoe reef in the middle of the Grenadines, run as a marine park. You get there by boat, you snorkel over turtle grass and reef, and you go home. It is the single best half-day in the country and it is the reason a lot of yachts are here.

Bequia

Seven square miles, a harbour at Port Elizabeth, a boatbuilding tradition it still practises, and a walk around the bay to a beach at the end of it. Small hotels and guest houses rather than resorts. People come for three days and rebook for a week; that is not a sales line, it is what the ferry crowd tells you.

A capital that works for a living

Kingstown is not pretty in the Caribbean-postcard sense and it is better than pretty. A covered market that feeds the islands, two cathedrals, an arched stone arcade down the main streets, and ships loading at the end of the road. Walk it on a market morning and you will understand the country in an hour.

The botanic gardens

Up the hill behind Kingstown, founded in 1765 and often described as the oldest botanical garden in the western hemisphere. Shaded, flat, quiet and a genuine relief after the heat of the town. There is a breadfruit tree there with a story attached to Captain Bligh that the guides will tell you with relish.

The volcano and the leeward coast

La Soufrière is a guided walk up through forest to a crater that was making news in April 2021 and is quiet now. The road up the leeward side to get near it — black sand bays, fishing villages, breadfruit and banana — is the drive that shows you what the island actually is.

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines at a glance

Where it is

The Windward Islands, in the eastern Caribbean, with Saint Lucia to the north and Grenada to the south. Saint Vincent is the main island; the Grenadines run south-west from it.

Getting there

Argyle International Airport (SVD), on the windward side of Saint Vincent about half an hour from Kingstown, opened in 2017 and replaced the old strip beside the capital. Routes change season by season, so check what is flying with the airline rather than with a guide.

Language and money

English. The Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged at EC$2.70 to the US dollar. US dollars are widely accepted; change often comes back in local money.

Driving

On the left. Visitors need a temporary local driving permit, which hire companies arrange when you collect the car. Roads on the main island are narrow, steep and busy near Kingstown; minibuses are cheap, frequent and quick to pull away.

Independence

27 October 1979. Independence Day is the national holiday and the flags go up in the week before it.

Hurricane season

1 June to 30 November. The country lies inside the belt, and Hurricane Beryl proved the point on 1 July 2024. If you travel in those months, take insurance that covers weather disruption.

The islands, and which one suits you

Where you sleep changes the holiday more here than almost anywhere in the Caribbean, because moving between islands takes a boat and half a day.

Kingstown and the leeward coast

The capital, the port, the cruise berth, the market and the botanic gardens, with fishing villages and black sand bays up the coast behind it. This is where the country works. Most visitors sleep nearby and walk through the town rather than living in it.

The south of Saint Vincent

Villa, Indian Bay, Calliaqua and the short crossing to Young Island: the main cluster of hotels, the dive shops, the restaurants a visitor will actually use, and the ferry jetty for the Grenadines. Close to the old airport side of the island and the usual base for a first trip.

Bequia

Nine miles south and an hour on the ferry. Port Elizabeth around Admiralty Bay, small hotels and guest houses, a working boatyard, and a coast path to Princess Margaret Beach and Lower Bay. The most straightforward of the Grenadines to visit independently.

Mustique

Privately managed and run on villa rentals rather than open hotel booking, with day visitors limited by how you arrive. Beautiful, expensive, and not somewhere you turn up and find a room. If it interests you, it is arranged in advance through the island's own company, not through a search box.

Canouan, Mayreau and Union Island

The Southern Grenadines: small, dry, beautiful and the part of the country Hurricane Beryl hit hardest on 1 July 2024. Union Island was still rebuilding homes and roofs in September 2026 and the tourism sector was still being consulted on in November 2026. Go with your eyes open and confirm everything direct.

The Tobago Cays

Uninhabited, inside the reef, and a marine park. You visit by boat on a day trip or from a yacht; you do not stay. Park fees apply and the boatmen collect them. Bring your own shade and water, because there is nothing there and that is the whole point.

Things to do in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

The things people are glad they did, and one or two they wish they had left off the list.

Walk Kingstown on a market morning

Start at the covered market, work up through the arcaded streets to the two cathedrals, and finish where the ships load. An hour and a half on foot, best before ten, and the single cheapest good thing to do in the country.

The botanic gardens

Flat, shaded paths behind the town, founded in 1765. Half an hour if you are pressed and a slow two hours if you are not. The easiest outing in the country for anyone who does not want a hill.

A day in the Tobago Cays

A boat out to the reef, a few hours of snorkelling over turtle grass, lunch on a beach that has no building on it. Book through a Bequia or Union Island operator, take more water than you think, and accept that the crossing can be bumpy.

Climb La Soufrière

A guided walk up through forest and then up a bare ridge to the crater rim, several hours up and down, and genuinely strenuous in the heat. Go early, go with a guide, and check the official advice on the volcano before you book.

The ferry to Bequia

An hour across open water to Admiralty Bay, and one of the better boat rides in the Caribbean on a calm morning. Do it as a day trip if you must, but it is a place that rewards a night or three.

The Falls of Baleine — or not

A waterfall at the northern end of Saint Vincent reached by boat, and a long day for what you get unless the sea is kind. If your week is short, the leeward coast drive and a beach are the better trade.

When to go

The dry season runs from about January to May and is the half of the year you can plan around: steady sun, a reliable trade wind, lower humidity and the highest room rates. June to November is hotter and wetter — heavy showers that pass rather than days of grey — and it carries the Atlantic hurricane season, 1 June to 30 November. Beryl came ashore on 1 July 2024, which is early in that season and a reminder that the calendar is not a shield. The two best-value windows are the first half of December, before Christmas rates begin, and late April into May. Vincy Mas, the national carnival, builds through the second half of June and finishes with two days of mas at the turn of the month; the dates move, so we print only the ones the carnival’s own organisers have published for the year you are asking about. Our best time to visit Saint Vincent and the Grenadines page goes through it month by month.

Arriving by ship

Saint Vincent is an easy port day for one good reason: the berth is in Kingstown, so you step off into a working town rather than onto an industrial pier outside one. The market, the arcaded shopping streets and the cathedrals are all within a short walk, and the botanic gardens are a cheap taxi up the hill. With a single day the sensible choices are the town and the gardens on foot, a drive up the leeward coast, or a boat out to one of the nearer Grenadines — one of those three, done properly, rather than two of them in a hurry. La Soufrière is a full day’s walk and does not fit a port call. Agree every taxi fare before you set off, in writing if the driver will, and be back aboard early on a day when the sea is up, because the tender and ferry timetables are the first things the weather changes. Travelling in your later years

This is a country that asks a little more of you than its neighbours, and it is worth knowing that before you book rather than after. Kingstown is steep and the pavements are uneven; the botanic gardens, by contrast, are flat and shaded and are the outing we would point anyone towards first. The real consideration is the water. Getting to the Grenadines means a ferry or a small plane, boarding is often by a short gangway or a step down into a boat, and the open crossings can be lively enough to be unpleasant if you are unsteady on your feet — ask for a seat low and amidships, and do not plan a crossing on the day you fly home. Staying on the south of Saint Vincent keeps you near the hotels, the restaurants and the ferry jetty without the hills of the capital. Medical facilities are concentrated near Kingstown and are limited in the Grenadines, which is worth a thought if you travel with a condition that needs watching. Minibuses are cheap but crowded and quick to pull away; taxis are plentiful, fares are negotiated rather than metered, and agreeing the figure first saves the argument later.

Getting in

For British passport holders the UK Foreign Office states that you do not need a visa to visit Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, that you will be granted a period of six months on entry, and that your passport must have an expiry date at least six months after the date you arrive. Staying longer than the period granted means applying and paying for an extension from the Immigration Department, and working without a work permit is illegal. Two rules catch people out: because of foot and mouth disease you need an import licence to bring any food into the country, and a yellow fever certificate is required if you are arriving from a country listed as a transmission risk. Anything prohibited or liable to tax or duty must be declared. We print no arrival form, departure tax or proof-of-funds requirement here because that page states none — and travellers on other passports should check their own government’s current advice, which is the only version that counts at the desk.

Fishing here

This is blue-water country rather than flats country. The shelf falls away fast off the leeward side of Saint Vincent and off the Grenadines, and the charter boats work close in for wahoo, dorado, tuna, kingfish and billfish; a half-day out of the south coast or out of Bequia is the usual way to do it. What the archipelago does not offer is the broad shallow sand flats that bonefishing is built on — for that you want the Bahamas or the Caribbean’s flats proper, which is the subject of our guide to fishing the flats. Inside the Tobago Cays Marine Park the rules are the park’s, not yours; ask the boatman before a line goes in the water. The Guild Fishing Library holds the whole shelf: lakes, rivers and salt.

Questions travellers ask about Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

When is the best time to visit Saint Vincent and the Grenadines?

January to May is the dry half of the year and the reliable one: steadier sun, lower humidity, a working trade wind and the dearest rooms. June to November is hotter and wetter, with heavy showers that clear rather than days of grey, and it carries the Atlantic hurricane season, which runs from 1 June to 30 November. Our own two picks are the edges of the dry season - the first half of December, before the Christmas rates begin, and late April into May - when the weather is good and a room costs noticeably less than in February. If you are coming for Vincy Mas, the national carnival, you are coming at the turn of June into July, and you should book early.

Is it one island or many?

One country, many islands. Saint Vincent is the big volcanic one at the north end and holds the capital, the airport and most of the population. South of it runs the tail of the Grenadines - Bequia, Mustique, Canouan, Mayreau, Union Island and a scatter of cays including the Tobago Cays - usually counted as thirty-two islands and cays in all, nine of them inhabited. There is no road between them. You move by ferry or by a small plane, and the hop itself becomes part of the holiday, which is either the charm of the place or the reason to go somewhere simpler.

Is the volcano a problem?

Not at the moment, and it is a fair question to ask. La Soufrière, at the northern end of Saint Vincent, erupted explosively in April 2021 and the north of the island was evacuated. When we checked in November 2026, the University of the West Indies Seismic Research Centre’s own site listed La Soufrière at alert level Green, which is its lowest. The Centre is the body that advises the government and is the page to read before you travel rather than any guide, including this one. The practical point for a visitor is that the volcano sits well north of Kingstown and of every hotel area, and that climbing it is a guided day's walk rather than a stroll.

What did Hurricane Beryl do, and does it still matter?

It matters, and we would rather say so than sell you a room we cannot stand behind. Beryl crossed the Southern Grenadines on 1 July 2024 and hit Union Island, Canouan, Mayreau and Petit Saint Vincent hardest. Rebuilding was still visibly under way more than two years later: in a report dated 8 September 2026 the St Vincent Times described a collaborative recovery effort on Union Island still rebuilding homes and roofs and replacing destroyed houses from scratch, and on 23 November 2026 NBC SVG reported that the Ministry of Tourism had held a public consultation on Union Island on how to rebuild the tourism sector after Beryl. Saint Vincent itself, Bequia and Kingstown were not in that path. We have published no where-to-stay guide for the Southern Grenadines because we could not confirm what is open; if you are headed there, write to the property itself and ask.

How do I get between the islands?

Ferry for the near ones, air for the far ones. The crossing from Kingstown to Bequia is the busy route and the one most visitors use; boats also work down the chain towards Canouan, Mayreau and Union Island on a less frequent timetable, and the Grenadines run on a schedule that changes with the season and the sea. Small aircraft serve several of the islands from Argyle. Two honest warnings: the open stretches between islands can be rough enough to spoil a morning, and a schedule you read online months ahead is not a promise. Confirm the sailing a day or two before, and leave slack on the day you fly home.

Do I need a visa?

For British passport holders the UK Foreign Office states that you do not need a visa to visit, that you will be granted a period of six months on entry, and that your passport must have an expiry date at least six months after the date you arrive. It also notes two things travellers trip over: because of foot and mouth disease you need an import licence to bring any food in, and a yellow fever certificate is required if you are arriving from a country listed as a transmission risk. That page states no arrival form, departure tax or proof-of-funds rule, so we print none. Travellers on other passports should check their own government’s current advice, which is the only version that counts at the desk.

What money do they use?

The Eastern Caribbean dollar, shared with several neighbouring islands and pegged to the US dollar at EC$2.70, so divide a local price by rather less than three. US dollars are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants and by taxi drivers, though change often comes back in Eastern Caribbean dollars and you will usually do a shade better paying in local money. Cards work in hotels, supermarkets and the larger restaurants; carry cash for minibuses, market stalls and the small bars. Expect tax and a service charge to be added to hotel and restaurant bills, so ask what is included when you book.

Is it a good island for a cruise day ashore?

Better than its reputation, because the berth is in Kingstown rather than miles outside it, and the town is a real working one rather than a parade of jewellery shops. With one day the honest choice is to stay on Saint Vincent - the market, the cathedrals, the botanic gardens up the hill, and a drive up the leeward coast - or to take a boat out to the nearer Grenadines and accept that you will see one of them properly rather than three badly.
