Where to Stay in The Hague — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

The Hague is the calmest and most dignified of the big Dutch cities — the seat of government and the world court, flat and easy underfoot, with one of the great small art collections on earth at the Mauritshuis and a proper North Sea beach at Scheveningen fifteen minutes away on the tram. Below are its best hotels arranged by style, in the centre and along the coast, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

The areas, the centre and the beach

The Hague’s historic core is small, flat and largely pedestrianised — the Binnenhof, the Mauritshuis, Escher in Het Paleis, the Passage arcade and Noordeinde Palace sit inside a compact rectangle you can cross in fifteen or twenty minutes on level paving, with no hills. The tram network fills in everything else and is the city’s real backbone: tram 9 runs from The Hague Centraal through the centre out to the seafront at Scheveningen, which is only about five kilometres away and roughly fifteen to twenty minutes on the tram. Kijkduin, the quieter second beach, has no tram and is reached by bus. It is a dignified, easy city to be old in.

City centre

Flat, walkable, all the major museums within ten minutes, and trams to the coast from your doorstep. Best for first-time visitors and anyone who wants to leave the car and the map behind.

Scheveningen

The big beach resort: boulevard, pier, casino, seafood and a long working promenade. Best if the sea is why you came, or you want a big hotel with a pool. Lively in summer, bracing and half-empty in winter.

Kijkduin

The quieter, smaller beach at the southern end, backed by dunes and woodland walks. Best for families with young children and anyone who wants calm sand — provided you accept bus-only transport.

Zeeheldenkwartier & Statenkwartier

Leafy residential districts west of the centre, with Art Nouveau facades, independent shops and good cafes, plus the Peace Palace nearby. Best for repeat visitors who prefer a neighbourhood to a hotel district.

Luxury

The city’s grande dame in the centre, and a genuine seaside palace on the Scheveningen boulevard.

Hotel Des Indes

$$$$ LuxuryLange Voorhout, city centre1858 mansionLift & spa

The Hague’s grande dame, a former nobleman’s mansion turned hotel in 1881, with a marble-columned lobby that still serves one of the country’s best-known high teas. It is formal and hushed rather than trendy — deep carpets, heavy drapes, a fair few diplomats at breakfast. There is a lift, a spa with sauna and steam bath, and the setting on the lime-tree avenue of Lange Voorhout is flat, paved and step-free, with the Mauritshuis and Binnenhof a five-minute stroll and trams a couple of minutes away — an easy address for older travellers who want to walk to the museums.

Grand Hotel Amrâth Kurhaus

$$$ UpscaleScheveningen seafront1885 monumentIndoor pool & spa

A vast seaside palace built around the domed Kurzaal concert hall, still the landmark of the Scheveningen boulevard and the only genuinely grand hotel on the Dutch coast. It is more faded-imperial than boutique-slick — enormous public rooms, a painted dome, a big terrace facing the North Sea — with sea-view and land-view room categories, so ask specifically for sea view. There is a lift, an indoor pool, sauna and treatment rooms; tram 9 from The Hague Centraal stops right outside, and the beach is across a flat promenade, which makes it one of the easiest seaside options for anyone who tires on foot.

Boutique

A design-led hotel in a traffic-free courtyard, and the city’s oldest four-star, standing next to the parliament.

The Collector

$$$ BoutiqueHaagsche Bluf courtyard57 rooms, vintage designLift

A design-led hotel built around a collector’s conceit — mid-century vintage pieces mixed with modern furniture and a commissioned artwork in every room. Downstairs, its cafe is a genuine neighbourhood coffee-and-cocktails room rather than a hotel lobby, so it feels sociable without being loud. There is a lift and a twenty-four-hour desk; it sits inside the traffic-free Haagsche Bluf courtyard, about five minutes’ flat walk to the Binnenhof and ten to twelve minutes to The Hague Centraal, with trams close by.

$$ Mid-rangeBuitenhof, by the BinnenhofThree 17th-century housesLift

Budget & Value

Two dependable central budget choices — one a straightforward chain with a lift, one in a much prettier street than its price suggests.

ibis Den Haag City Centre

$ BudgetJan Hendrikstraat, centreLift & air conditioningWheelchair accessible

The dependable central budget choice: small, clean, air-conditioned rooms, a restaurant and bar downstairs, and staff on the desk around the clock. Nothing here has any character, but it is quiet by budget standards and fully non-smoking. It has a lift and is flagged as wheelchair accessible; The Hague HS station is a few minutes away, The Hague Centraal about a fifteen-minute walk, and several tram and bus stops sit within a few minutes — ask for a room away from the street if noise bothers you.

Family-friendly

Two seaside resort hotels with indoor pools — one a block from the Scheveningen sand, one in the calmer dunes at Kijkduin.

$$ Mid-rangeScheveningenIndoor pool, sauna, gym198 steps to the beach

A well-run four-star whose own website measures the distance to the sand as “exactly 198 steps,” with an indoor pool, sauna and fitness room that earn their keep when the North Sea weather turns. Family rooms are a standard category, younger children get complimentary breakfast with an adult, and the bistro handles early dinners without fuss. Adults-only

Unusually for the Netherlands, The Hague has two genuine adults-only hotels — each publishes a written 18-and-over house rule on its own website. They appear to be sister properties.

Court Garden Hotel

$$ Adults-onlyWest of the centreStrictly 18+Walk-in showers, air-con

A seventy-room hotel that bills itself as the first eco-designed hotel in the Netherlands, with Green Key certification and organic provisioning — and a written house rule welcoming guests aged eighteen and above to keep the peaceful atmosphere. Rooms are plain and modern with air conditioning and walk-in showers, which are a real plus for anyone wary of climbing into a bath, and there is a lounge with free organic tea and coffee. No pets; the hotel does not publish lift details, so ring ahead if stairs matter. It sits on a main tram artery, about fifteen minutes’ walk or a few stops from the centre.

Hotel Sebel

$$ Adults-onlyZeeheldenkwartierStrictly 18+Quiet Art Nouveau square

A small hotel spread over three period houses on a leafy Art Nouveau square in the Zeeheldenkwartier — the district of Jugendstil facades, independent shops and cafe terraces that locals rate as the city’s most characterful. The same eighteen-and-over rule applies, which makes it genuinely quiet. Because it occupies three converted townhouses and publishes no lift information, and the desk is staffed only into the evening, it is the pick for adults-only atmosphere rather than step-free ease — ask for a low floor when booking. It is about fifteen minutes’ walk to the centre, with trams a couple of minutes away.

Pet-friendly

Two central hotels with clear, itemised dog policies — the first has thought its arrangement through unusually well.

The ALD Boutique Hotel

$$ Pet-friendlyCity centreDogs & cats to 25 kgGround-floor pet rooms · lift

A four-star boutique hotel in the former national government printing office, with quality beds, rain showers and a cocktail bar. Its pet policy is unusually specific and well thought out: one pet per room up to twenty-five kilos, a one-off cleaning fee, and dogs housed only in the ground-floor split-level studios — no lift or corridor to negotiate with a dog on a lead. There is a lift for the rest of the building and parking beneath it; The Hague Centraal is a seven-minute flat walk and the Binnenhof five.

Beachfront & seaside

For toes-in-the-sand mornings, base yourself on the Scheveningen boulevard. (The Grand Hotel Amrâth Kurhaus, under Luxury above, is also directly on this seafront and is the most seafront address in the city.)

ibis Styles Den Haag Scheveningen

$$ Mid-rangeScheveningen, opposite the beachAbout 50 m to the sandLift · accessible rooms

A cheerful, unfussy three-star directly opposite Scheveningen beach, roughly fifty metres from the sand, with a bar for breakfast and drinks and air-conditioned rooms. It is the practical seaside choice rather than the romantic one — bright, modern, and priced well below the Kurhaus along the boulevard. It has a lift and wheelchair-accessible rooms, and tram and bus stops are metres from the door, with tram 9 running straight through to The Hague Centraal — together the most step-free seafront option in Scheveningen; pets are welcome.

What to see — and one thing to check before you go

The Hague punches far above its size for art. One important current note, so you are not disappointed.

The Mauritshuis — check the dates

Home to Vermeer’s Girl with a Pearl Earring and one of the great small collections in the world. Two current caveats, both from the museum’s own site: the Mauritshuis is closed for maintenance from 24 August to 20 September 2026, and the Girl with a Pearl Earring is off display from 15 August to 4 October 2026. If you are travelling this autumn, check mauritshuis.nl before you build a day around the Vermeer.

Escher in Het Paleis

Over a hundred and twenty Escher prints in Queen Emma’s former winter palace on Lange Voorhout, open Tuesday to Sunday. The optical trickery is a delight for all ages and the building itself is worth the ticket.

The Peace Palace

The seat of the International Court of Justice. The visitors’ centre is free and open Tuesday to Sunday; guided tours of the palace and gardens are offered but tickets are released only about two weeks ahead and sell out, so book the moment they appear.

Madurodam

The famous miniature Netherlands between the centre and Scheveningen, open every day of the year and entirely outdoors and flat underfoot — an easy, delightful half-day for a mixed-generation group.

The Hague — common questions

Where should I stay in The Hague — the centre or the beach?

For a first visit and for most older travellers, the city centre is the better base: it is flat, walkable, and puts the Mauritshuis, Escher in Het Paleis, the Binnenhof and the shops within a ten-minute stroll, with trams to the beach from your door. Choose Scheveningen if the sea is the reason you came or you want a big hotel with a pool, and the quieter Kijkduin if you have young children. Because tram 9 links the centre to the seafront in fifteen to twenty minutes, you do not have to choose between museums and the beach.

How do I get from The Hague to Scheveningen beach?

By tram — the beach is only about five kilometres from the centre. Tram 9 runs from The Hague Centraal through the city out to the Scheveningen seafront, stopping right by the Kurhaus, and takes roughly fifteen to twenty minutes end to end. You tap a contactless card to ride. Kijkduin, the second and quieter beach, has no tram and is reached by bus, so budget a little more time for that one.

Is the Mauritshuis open, and can I see the Girl with a Pearl Earring?

Check the museum’s own website before you plan around it. As of this writing the Mauritshuis is closed for maintenance from 24 August to 20 September 2026, and the Girl with a Pearl Earring is off display from 15 August to 4 October 2026. Both are exactly the kind of date that shifts, so confirm the current position on mauritshuis.nl. When it is open, tickets are timed and best booked online in advance.

Does The Hague have adults-only hotels?

Yes, unusually for the Netherlands it has two genuine ones — Court Garden Hotel and Hotel Sebel, both of which publish a written eighteen-and-over house rule on their own websites. They appear to be sister properties with the same policy. Be wary of aggregator “adults only” filters for the city, which mostly tag apartments and B&Bs that simply do not take children rather than hotels with a real policy.

When is the best time to visit The Hague?

The city itself is a working government and diplomatic capital, so its hotel prices actually spike midweek in spring and autumn for conferences and often soften at weekends — the reverse of most city breaks. Scheveningen is the opposite, jammed on hot weekends from June to late August and on any sunny day when the region drives to the coast. The sweet spot for both is May to June and September into early October, when the weather is workable, the beach pavilions are open and the museums and trams are calm.
