Playa del Carmen Travel & Adventure Guide

The walkable heart of the Riviera Maya — a pedestrian avenue of restaurants and bars that runs a block from a Caribbean beach, with cenotes, Maya ruins and the Cozumel ferry all within reach.

Find Playa del Carmen Hotels Search Flights to CUN

Playa del Carmen is the Riviera Maya's most liveable town — the place travellers pick when they want the Caribbean without a wall of high-rise resorts. Thirty years ago it was a fishing village where you caught the boat to Cozumel; today it is a beach city of roughly 300,000, and its spine is Quinta Avenida, a pedestrian-only avenue running parallel to the sand, lined with taquerías, rooftop bars, dive shops and boutiques. What makes Playa work is scale: you can land at Cancún, drive an hour south, and then not touch a car again for a week. The whole coast opens up from here — cenotes and jungle parks inland, the Tulum ruins an hour south, and Cozumel's reefs a 45-minute ferry ride offshore. This guide covers where to stay, what to do, when to go, and how to book it.

Playa sits in the state of Quintana Roo, on the stretch of Caribbean coast marketed as the Riviera Maya. It grew from a Maya settlement and small fishing port — the old name, Xaman-Há, means "waters of the north" — into one of the fastest-growing towns in Latin America once the Cancún airport opened the coast to the world. That growth is the town's whole character: it is new, dense, and built for walking, which is why it feels more like a real town than a resort strip.

The currency is the Mexican peso. Cards work almost everywhere in town, and dollars are widely accepted along Quinta Avenida — though you will nearly always get a better rate paying in pesos. Spanish is the language, but English is spoken across the tourist trade. Quintana Roo keeps Eastern time (UTC−5) year-round with no daylight saving, so it can be an hour off from what you expect.

The town is laid out on a simple grid, which makes it hard to get lost: numbered avenidas run parallel to the sea and numbered calles run down to it. Quinta Avenida — Fifth Avenue — is the pedestrian one, and almost everything a visitor wants sits within a few blocks either side of it. South of the centre lies Playacar, a gated district of golf, big resorts and quieter beach; north of Calle 38 the streets get leafier and the restaurants more local.

Why visit Playa del Carmen

Four reasons Playa keeps pulling travellers back.

You can walk everywhere

Quinta Avenida runs for dozens of blocks a stone's throw from the sand. Hotel, dinner, drinks and beach are all on foot — no taxis, no shuttle schedules, no resort compound.

The best base on the coast

Cancún is an hour north, Tulum an hour south, Cozumel 45 minutes offshore and the great cenotes are inland. Playa sits in the middle of all of it.

Food beyond the resort buffet

Because it's a real town, it has real restaurants — Yucatecan cochinita, street tacos, Argentine grills and a serious rooftop-bar scene.

Beach clubs and reef

Day beds and music at Mamita's and Kool by day; the Mesoamerican Reef — the second-largest barrier reef on earth — a short boat ride out.

Top things to do in Playa del Carmen

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Walk Quinta Avenida

The pedestrian spine of town. Best after dark, when the heat drops and the whole avenue fills with music, mezcal and street performers.

● Ferry to Cozumel

Boats leave the town pier through the day for the 45-minute crossing. Go for world-class drift diving at Palancar, or just for a beach club day.

● Swim a cenote

Dos Ojos, Chikin Ha and Cristalino are all a short drive away — freshwater sinkholes in the jungle, cold, clear and unforgettable.

● Xcaret & Xplor

The big eco-parks sit just south of town: underground rivers, ziplines, snorkelling lagoons and a genuinely spectacular evening show.

● Day trip to Tulum

An hour south: the only Maya city built on the coast, its temples standing on a cliff straight above the Caribbean.

● Beach club afternoon

Claim a day bed at Mamita's or Kool Beach, order ceviche and a michelada, and do absolutely nothing until sunset.

Where to stay in Playa del Carmen

Three areas cover almost every trip. The Centro — the blocks around Quinta Avenida between Calle 4 and Calle 16 — puts you in the middle of the restaurants and nightlife; it's the best choice if you came to walk and eat, and the noisiest if you came to sleep. Playacar, the gated district just south, is where the big all-inclusive resorts and the golf course sit: quieter, greener, better beach, but you'll take a taxi into town. North of Calle 38 is the sweet spot for many travellers — boutique hotels and condos, calmer streets, still an easy walk to the avenue. Whichever you choose, note that the beach directly in the centre is narrower than the postcards suggest; the widest sand is north and in Playacar.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

December–April is the dry season: the most reliable weather, and the busiest and priciest, especially over the holidays and spring break. May and November are the value months — warm, quieter, cheaper. June–November is hurricane season, and sargassum seaweed tends to be heaviest in the warm months; trips can still be excellent value, but book flexibly.

Getting there

Fly into Cancún International (CUN) — Playa has no airport. It's roughly a 45–60 minute drive south. Private transfers, shared shuttles and the frequent ADO buses all run from the terminals; the ADO is the cheapest and drops you in the centre of town. From the US and Canada, CUN is one of the best-served leisure airports in the hemisphere.

Playa del Carmen travel FAQ

Is Playa del Carmen better than Cancún?

They suit different trips. Cancún has the bigger hotel zone, the airport, and the widest choice of large all-inclusive resorts. Playa del Carmen is smaller and far more walkable — you can stroll from your hotel to dinner, drinks and the beach along Quinta Avenida. Most travellers find Playa better for wandering and eating, and Cancún better for a big resort stay.

Which airport do you fly into for Playa del Carmen?

Cancún International (CUN). Playa has no airport of its own. The drive south takes roughly 45 to 60 minutes, and shared shuttles, private transfers and the ADO bus all run the route.

How do you get from Playa del Carmen to Cozumel?

By passenger ferry from the pier in the centre of town. The crossing takes about 45 minutes and boats run frequently through the day, which makes Cozumel an easy day trip for diving, snorkelling or a beach club.

When is the best time to visit Playa del Carmen?

December through April is the dry season, with the most reliable weather — and the biggest crowds and prices. May and November are excellent value. June to November is hurricane season, and sargassum seaweed can affect the beaches most in the warmer months.
