Aqaba — Jordan’s One Stretch of Coast

Where to Stay in Aqaba — the Best Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Aqaba is the whole of Jordan’s coastline, and it is about 26 kilometres long. It is a working port, a tax-free zone, a relaxed small city and a diving destination all at once, and it is the only place in the country that is pleasant in January. The choice here comes down to one question: do you want to be able to walk to dinner, or do you want to be able to walk into a reef? The two are at opposite ends of the shore.

The town and North Beach — walkable, local, better value

The northern end of the gulf, where the city actually is. Staying here puts you within walking distance of restaurants, coffee houses, the souk, the fort and the public beach, and it is noticeably cheaper than the resort coast. The water at this end is fine for a swim but the reefs are poor, so divers and snorkellers end up travelling south each day. For a night or two at the end of a trip, with an evening in a real town rather than a resort, this is the better half.

South Beach and the dive coast — where the reefs are

The shore running south towards the Saudi border holds Jordan’s serious reefs, the marine park, and most of the resort hotels and dive centres. If diving or snorkelling is the reason you came to Aqaba, this is where to sleep, because being able to walk into the water from your own hotel changes the trip. The cost is isolation: it is a 20 to 30 minute taxi into town, so dinner means either the hotel restaurant or a fare each way.

The marina and lagoon developments — new and self-contained

Between the two sit the newer marina and lagoon developments, built around man-made waterfronts with their own restaurants and beaches. They are comfortable, modern and easy, and they suit families and anyone who wants everything in one place. They are also entirely purpose-built, so if you were hoping for somewhere that felt like Jordan rather than like a resort anywhere on the Red Sea, look at the town end instead.

What to know before you book

Not every "beachfront" hotel has a reef

This is the most useful thing to know in Aqaba. Plenty of hotels sit on the water with sand and no coral worth the name. If snorkelling matters, ask whether there is house-reef access directly from the hotel, or whether you will be driving to a dive site.

Winter is the season

Aqaba is at its best from November to April, when it is mild and dry and the rest of Jordan is cold and wet. Midsummer is very hot. The sea is warmest in September and October.

Getting here

King Hussein International (AQJ) is minutes from the town and takes some international flights as well as the short hop from Amman. By road it is about four hours from Amman on the Desert Highway and about two from Petra.

The tax-free zone

Aqaba is a special economic zone, so some goods are cheaper here than elsewhere in Jordan. It also means the departure arrangements can differ from the rest of the country — check with your hotel if you are crossing a land border rather than flying.

Day trips

Wadi Rum is about an hour north and is very commonly done from Aqaba, either as a day trip or as a night in a camp on the way to or from Petra. It is the most popular pairing in southern Jordan.

A port city, not only a resort

Container ships and ferries are part of the view at the northern end, and the town has a working life of its own. Most visitors like that. If you want an unbroken resort horizon, the southern shore is the answer.

Aqaba — questions

Should I stay in Aqaba town or out on South Beach?

Town if you want to walk to dinner, see something of ordinary Jordanian life and pay less. South Beach if diving or snorkelling is the point, because house-reef access from your own hotel is worth more than anything else here. Doing both ends on one short stay means a lot of taxi fares.

Is Aqaba good for diving and snorkelling?

Yes, and it is the main reason people stay more than a night. The reefs are close to shore, the water is warm and clear, and there is a marine park along the southern shore. Conditions are generally calm. Ask any hotel directly about house-reef access rather than trusting the word beachfront.

When is the best time to visit Aqaba?

November to April, which is when the rest of Jordan is at its coldest. That is what makes Aqaba useful: it is the country's winter-sun destination. The sea holds its summer warmth into October, and midsummer is extremely hot.

How far is Aqaba from Petra and Wadi Rum?

Wadi Rum is about an hour by road and Petra about two, which makes Aqaba a natural last stop before flying home from AQJ, or a first stop if you fly in and work northwards.

How many nights does Aqaba deserve?

One or two on a standard week in Jordan, as the place to stop moving at the end. Three or four if you are diving, because the reefs are the reason to be here and one day underwater is not enough.
