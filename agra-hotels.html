Where to Stay in Agra

Where to Stay in Agra — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Agra is a one-monument town in the best sense, and the only question that really matters when booking is how far you will be from the Taj Mahal gate at half past five on a cold morning. Taj East Gate is the answer for most visitors: the quieter entrance, ten minutes on foot, no transfer at dawn. Fatehabad Road is the city’s hotel strip — wide roads, lifts, real pavements and proper hotel infrastructure a short drive away. Taj Ganj, the old quarter under the monument’s walls, is cheap and atmospheric and hard on the legs. The Cantonment is for the railway station. One thing to fix in your mind before you read further: the Taj Mahal is closed every Friday, all day, without exception. Build the trip around that first.

Choosing Your Neighbourhood

Four areas, and the distance to one gate decides between them.

Taj East Gate, Taj Ganj, Fatehabad Road or the Cantonment?

Taj East Gate is our default recommendation for anyone over about fifty-five. The East Gate is the quieter of the two entrances and clears faster, and this is the only quarter from which you can reach it on foot in ten minutes without a vehicle at all. That matters more than it sounds, because petrol and diesel vehicles are banned within five hundred metres of the Taj Mahal: from anywhere else your taxi stops at an outer car park and you continue by electric shuttle, golf cart or cycle-rickshaw, and at sunrise that queue can be long. The trade is that there is little to walk to in the evening beyond your own dining room. Fatehabad Road is the sensible compromise and where most of the good hotels are — wide roads, lifts, accessible rooms, dependable restaurants, easy taxis, and a drive of one and a half to four kilometres to the monument. Taj Ganj, the old lanes immediately south and west of the Taj, has the cheap rooftop guesthouses and the best atmosphere; it also has narrow, uneven, poorly lit lanes shared with rickshaws and animals, and very few lifts. We would not send anyone with knee, hip, balance or breathing trouble to sleep there, whatever the saving. The Cantonment and Sadar, five to seven kilometres out, hold the colonial-era town and Agra Cantt station — a good choice for one night if you are arriving and leaving by the fast train from Delhi, a poor one if you want a sunrise at the Taj, since the morning crawl can take forty-five minutes.

Luxury

Agra’s two grand hotels answer two different questions. One guarantees the view from every room; the other is the better-looking building, set in gardens, and does not see the Taj at all.

The Oberoi Amarvilas, Agra

$$$$ LuxuryTaj East GateEvery room sees the TajBuggy to the gate

The only hotel in Agra where the Taj Mahal is visible from every one of the hundred-odd rooms and suites, from the entry category upward — which is what you are paying for, and it is not a small thing at six in the morning with a cup of tea. Only some Premier Rooms have balconies, so ask for ‘Premier with balcony’ or a suite if you want to sit outside. The building is a Moorish-Mughal confection of terraced water gardens and colonnades, six hundred metres from the East Gate. Esphahan cooks from Mughal court recipes at dinner, Bellevue runs all day, and the pool is temperature-controlled. The detail that earns the price for an older traveller: the hotel runs its own electric buggy to the gate, so you skip the shuttle queue entirely. No pets, and no service animals.

ITC Mughal, a Luxury Collection Resort & Spa, Agra

$$$ LuxuryFatehabad RoadAga Khan AwardTwenty-three acres of garden

A low red-sandstone resort spread over twenty-three acres of Mughal-style gardens and watercourses, and the only hotel in Agra to have won the Aga Khan Award for Architecture. Two hundred and thirty-three rooms look over courtyards and lawns — and here is the point to be plain about, because the pictures mislead: no room sees the Taj Mahal. Book it for the building, the gardens and the Kaya Kalp spa, which is one of the largest in India, and drive the four kilometres to the monument. Peshawri is the celebrated Frontier kebab room; Taj Bano handles the buffet. The layout is flat and almost stair-free, which suits stiff knees, but the walks between wings are long — ask for a room near the lobby. Dogs are accepted with a vaccination certificate and no fee.

Boutique

Agra is thin on boutique hotels in the European sense. What it has instead is one surviving colonial hotel in the old commercial town, and a handful of very small family guesthouses by the East Gate.

The Grand Imperial

$ BoutiqueSadarBritish-era buildingFifty rooms

Agra’s only surviving hotel from the British period, standing on M. G. Road in the middle of the old commercial town: high ceilings, deep verandahs, period furniture, lawns, and fifty rooms including two suites grand enough to be called the Chamber of Prince. Shahenshah serves Mughlai and Awadhi cooking in a room that has clearly seen a century of it. There is a pool and a garden, and no view of the Taj — you are five or six kilometres away, which is the bargain you strike for real character at a very low rate. Sources disagree about the year it opened, so we will not print one. Recent guests divide sharply between charmed and disappointed, and the difference is the room: ask specifically for a refurbished one.

The Coral House Homestay by the Taj

$ BoutiqueTaj East GateSix roomsHome-cooked meals

Six rooms in a family house in a residential colony beside the Shilpgram car park, which puts you inside the East Gate approach — a genuine ten-minute walk to the entrance with no vehicle needed at any point. The courtyards are densely planted, there are several roof terraces, and dinner is home-cooked vegetarian food eaten together at one table, which is the reason solo and older guests rate it as highly as they do. It is simple: plain bathrooms, air-conditioning, no lift, no pool. Do not book it for a Taj view — guests report anything from a glimpse off the top terrace to nothing at all in winter haze. Six rooms sell out from October to March, so book direct and early.

Budget & Value

Agra rewards the middle of the market rather than the bottom of it. These two are the dependable choices: one a ten-minute walk from the East Gate, the other a garden hotel run to five-star standards at a third of five-star money.

Hotel Taj Resorts

$ ValueTaj East Gate550m to the gateRooftop pool

The best-known walk-to-the-Taj address in Agra: five hundred and fifty metres, about ten minutes, from the East Gate, with free station and airport pickup thrown in. Thirty-four rooms across four grades — and to be clear, no room has a Taj view; the view is from the roof, where there is a small pool, a marked viewing point and Taj Terrace, a rooftop restaurant with live music in the evening. BonAppetit is the indoor dining room. Two practical notes: avoid the basement rooms, which guests consistently describe as small, dark and without their own climate control, and expect a gentle nudge toward the in-house marble-inlay shop, which you may decline as often as you like.

Trident, Agra

$$ ValueFatehabad RoadAccessible roomsAlmost no stairs

The value-tier sister of the Amarvilas, run to Oberoi Group standards at roughly a third of the price, and the hotel we would put an older traveller in if the Amarvilas is beyond the budget. Low buildings stand around a walled garden with fountains and a poolside lawn, and there are almost no stairs anywhere on the property — accessible rooms and house wheelchairs are explicitly offered, which is still unusual in Agra. The dining is deliberately simple: one restaurant and one bar, both good. There is a spa, a fitness room, a children’s club and electric-car charging. No Taj view from anywhere on the site; about ten minutes by road to the East Gate, eight kilometres to Agra Cantt station.

Family-friendly

Agra with grandchildren is about space to run and something to do between monuments, since the sightseeing itself is a hot two hours followed by a long afternoon.

Jaypee Palace Hotel & Convention Centre

$$ FamilyFatehabad RoadTwenty-five acresBowling alley

The largest resort-style hotel in Agra: three hundred and forty-one rooms on twenty-five acres of walled grounds about a kilometre and a half from the Taj. Sheer space is the family argument — a jogging track, bicycles for hire, a bowling alley, billiards, a children’s activity area, a big outdoor pool and a spa, so nobody is confined to a bedroom in the heat of the afternoon. Paatra serves Indian food with live ghazal singing, C’est Chine the Chinese, and the Pavilion café runs all night. Children under five stay free and interconnecting rooms are available. The décor is dated in places and the complex is long — request a room near the lobby if walking distance is a consideration. Pets are accepted with three days’ notice.

Taj Hotel & Convention Centre, Agra

$$$ FamilyTaj East GateRooftop infinity poolChildren’s pool

Purpose-built and opened in 2019 on four and a half acres by the East Gate, with two hundred and twenty-six rooms and thirteen suites, a separate children’s pool, a children’s activity zone, cots, babysitting, and up to two children under twelve sharing their parents’ bed at no charge. The bedrooms look over gardens rather than the monument — the Taj view here is from the rooftop infinity pool and from Infini, the sky lounge, which is a very pleasant place to be at six in the evening. Daawat-e-Nawab does royal Indian cooking and Palato the large buffets that keep a mixed-age table happy. Ten minutes on foot to the East Gate. Some recent guests report the food service can be uneven; the rooms and the pool draw no such complaints.

Adults-only

There is no genuine adults-only hotel in Agra, and we will not name one to fill the space. The filter you may see on the booking sites is titled ‘adults only and adult friendly’, which is a marketing category rather than a policy: the seven or so Agra properties it returns are ordinary small hotels, and not one of them publishes a rule about children. Booking.com and Expedia return no adults-only Agra stock at all. If what you want is a hotel where you are unlikely to meet a family, the honest recommendation is The Oberoi Amarvilas — a hundred-odd rooms, an almost entirely adult clientele and no children’s facilities — while being clear that children are permitted there. Pet-friendly

Only two hotels in Agra publish a pet policy on their own pages, and both are already recommended above, so we have named them again here rather than padding the list with properties we could not verify. Be careful with the pet-listing sites: several Agra hotels they mark as welcoming animals in fact state the opposite, among them the Oberoi Amarvilas, Trident, Taj Agra, the Courtyard and the Grand Mercure.

ITC Mughal Agra — pet policy

$$$ Pet-friendlyFatehabad RoadNo pet feeDogs only

The clearest pet welcome in the city, and stated on the hotel’s own pages rather than inferred: dogs are accepted with a vaccination certificate and there is no additional pet charge, though animals are not permitted in the restaurants or other public rooms and owners are liable for any damage. The practical advantage is the one thing no Taj Ganj hotel can offer — twenty-three acres of walled garden with lawns and watercourses, which means there is somewhere civilised to walk a dog at six in the morning without meeting traffic. Agra Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Agra.

Do I need a hotel with a Taj Mahal view?

Only if you are willing to pay for a guaranteed one. The Oberoi Amarvilas is the single Agra hotel where every room faces the monument. Everywhere else the ‘Taj view’ is a rooftop — the infinity pool and sky lounge at Taj Agra, the roof terrace at Hotel Taj Resorts. Rooms at the ITC Mughal, the Trident, the Jaypee Palace and Taj Agra face gardens. And from late October to February the winter haze can erase the view altogether, whatever you paid for it.

How close can a taxi actually take me to the Taj Mahal?

Not to the gate. Petrol and diesel vehicles are banned within five hundred metres of the monument, so your car stops at an outer car park — Shilpgram for the East Gate — and you continue by free electric shuttle, golf cart, cycle-rickshaw or pony trap. Allow ten to fifteen minutes for that hop, and rather longer in the sunrise queue. It is included with a foreign visitor’s ticket. If making that transfer at half past five in the morning does not appeal, stay at Taj East Gate and walk.

Is one night in Agra enough?

Two is better, for one specific reason: the Taj Mahal is closed every Friday and the light is best at sunrise, so a single night gives you exactly one attempt, which a Friday or a foggy morning can spoil. Two nights let you see the Taj at dawn and still fit in Agra Fort, the small and exquisite Itmad-ud-Daulah — the ‘Baby Taj’, which hardly anyone visits — and Mehtab Bagh across the river at sunset.

What does a Taj Mahal ticket cost, and should I add the mausoleum?

A foreign visitor pays about eleven hundred rupees to enter, and a further two hundred to go up onto the marble platform and into the chamber holding the cenotaphs — around fifteen dollars in total, which includes a bottle of water, shoe covers, a map and the shuttle from the car park. Buy the mausoleum supplement at the same time: your ticket is single-entry, so you cannot step out and come back for it. Tickets are effectively digital, arriving as a QR code, and the windows at the monument no longer take cash. Book from your hotel wifi the night before rather than at the gate. Entry allows three hours inside, which is ample.

Which Agra hotels suit limited mobility?

Trident, Agra is the clearest answer: it publishes accessible rooms, keeps house wheelchairs, and its low garden layout means almost no steps. The ITC Mughal and the Jaypee Palace are also flat, but both are very spread out, so ask for a room near the lobby. Avoid the Taj Ganj guesthouses, where the lanes are narrow and uneven and lifts are rare. At the monument itself the Archaeological Survey provides wheelchair assistance, and the marble platform is reached by a ramp.
