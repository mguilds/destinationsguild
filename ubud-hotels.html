Where to Stay in Ubud

Where to Stay in Ubud — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Ubud is Bali’s inland cultural capital: a small town of temples, palaces, art museums and craft villages, ringed by terraced rice fields and two river gorges, and set high enough — around 600 feet — to stay a few degrees cooler than the coast. Central Ubud, along Jalan Bisma, Monkey Forest Road and Jalan Hanoman, is walkable and busy, good for dance performances, markets and restaurants, though the pavements are uneven and the traffic is slow. West of the centre, Campuhan, Penestanan, Sayan and Kedewatan drop into the Ayung River valley and hold most of the famous resorts — green and quiet, but taxi-dependent. Further north and east, Payangan, Tegallalang and Keliki are deeper countryside: dramatic views, cooler nights and twenty to forty minutes from town. A word worth saying plainly: Ubud is built on hillsides, and many of its loveliest hotels involve a great many steps. If stairs are a concern, ask for level access when you book. Luxury

Ubud’s grandest hotels sit above the Ayung River, west of town, where the valley does most of the work.

Four Seasons Resort Bali at Sayan

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I stay in Ubud town or out in the valley?

Stay in or near central Ubud if you want to walk to restaurants, the market, the Palace and evening dance performances. Stay in the Ayung valley at Sayan, Kedewatan or Payangan if the view and the quiet are the point, and you are content to be driven into town. Many visitors do a few nights of each.

How many days should I spend in Ubud?

Three to four nights is the comfortable minimum: a day for the town itself, a day for the temples and rice terraces, and a day with nothing planned. Travellers combining Ubud with the southern beaches usually give Ubud three or four nights and the coast the same.

Is Ubud difficult for anyone with mobility problems?

It can be. The town has narrow, uneven pavements and open drainage channels, and many hotels are built down steep valley sides with long flights of steps. This is entirely manageable with a little planning: choose a hotel on level ground near the centre, ask specifically about step-free rooms and lifts when booking, and hire a car and driver for the day rather than walking long distances.

How far is Ubud from the airport and the beaches?

Ngurah Rai International (DPS) is about 90 minutes from Ubud in normal traffic, and can be two hours or more in the late afternoon. Seminyak and Kuta are roughly the same. Arrange a hotel transfer for the first journey; it removes all the guesswork after a long flight.

When is the best time to visit Ubud?

The dry season, roughly April to October, is the most comfortable, and Ubud is a few degrees cooler than the coast all year. The wet season brings heavy afternoon downpours rather than all-day rain, and the rice terraces are at their greenest. See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Indonesia.
