Naivasha — A Freshwater Lake Under the Rift Valley Wall

Where to Stay at Lake Naivasha — Area by Area

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Naivasha is the freshwater lake an hour and a half below Nairobi, sitting on the floor of the Rift Valley with the escarpment behind it and two volcanoes within sight. It is the easiest first stop on a Kenyan trip: high, cool, full of birds, and close enough to the capital that it works as a weekend on its own.

It is also a lake with a great many hippos in it, and that is a practical matter rather than a charming detail. This page covers where to sleep and what to be careful about.

The south lake road — where most of the good places are

Moi South Lake Road runs along the southern shore and holds the greatest concentration of lakeside hotels, lodges and tented camps, along with the gates for Hell's Gate and the jetties for boat trips to Crescent Island. Gardens run down to the water, fever trees stand about in them, and the hippos come up at night. If you book one thing at Naivasha, book along here.

The northern and eastern shore — quieter, closer to town

The shore nearer the town and the main highway has a smaller number of places to stay and is handier if you are passing through rather than settling in. You are closer to shops and to the road north towards Nakuru, and further from the park gates. It is the practical half of the lake rather than the beautiful half.

Naivasha town — cheap, useful, not the point

The town itself has simple hotels and guest houses at low prices, a market and transport. It is a reasonable base for a very tight budget or an overnight stop between buses, and it has none of the reason you came. Stay here for the price, not the view.

Towards Hell's Gate and Longonot — for walkers and climbers

Properties at the western end of the south lake road put you close to the Hell's Gate gate, which matters if you want to be walking or cycling in the gorge early before the heat. For Mount Longonot the trailhead is off the main highway south-east of the lake, and an early start from the lakeside is perfectly manageable. Both are among the few places in Kenya where you explore on your own feet.

Before you book

Government travel insurance is now required

Since 5 October 2026 most visitors staying under twelve months must hold Kenyan government travel health insurance, bought with the electronic travel authorisation or afterwards through the eCitizen insurance portal. A policy from home is not accepted. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements, still current at 21 November 2026.

Hippos, seriously

They graze on lakeside lawns at night and they kill people in Kenya every year. Never walk between one and the water, never walk the shore after dark, and accept the escort to your room if one is offered.

Park fees are not ours to quote

Hell's Gate, Mount Longonot and Lake Nakuru charge Kenya Wildlife Service entry fees that are revised periodically. Confirm the current amount when you book rather than budgeting from any published figure.

High and cool, not hot

The lake sits at roughly 1,890 metres. Days are warm and evenings are cold, and the altitude makes the sun stronger than the temperature suggests. Bring a layer and a hat.

Malaria risk is lower up here

The Rift Valley highlands carry less malaria risk than the coast and the lowlands, but your trip will probably include both. Take travel health advice for the whole itinerary at least eight weeks before you fly.

An Ebola case was confirmed in October

Kenya's Ministry of Health confirmed the country's first imported case of Ebola on 6 October 2026 and public health measures remain in place. Check your own government's travel health advice before you fly.

Questions readers actually ask

Town or lakeside?


Lakeside, almost always. Naivasha town is a busy stop on the old Nairobi-Nakuru road and the reason to be here is the water, the birds and the escarpment. The hotels and camps along the south lake road put you on the shore with the hippos grunting at night. The town is where you buy things.

How close is it to Nairobi?


About ninety kilometres, and the drive down into the Rift Valley is one of the best short drives in the country. Allow much longer than the distance suggests, because the road out of Nairobi is slow and the escarpment traffic can be heavy. It is comfortably a weekend from the capital and that is largely who goes.

Are the hippos actually dangerous?


Yes, and this is the one thing we would press on anybody booking here. Hippos come out of Lake Naivasha to graze at night and they will be on the lawns of lakeside properties. They are responsible for human deaths in Kenya every year. Do not walk between a hippo and the water, do not walk the shore after dark, and if your camp offers an escort to your tent at night, take it rather than being polite about it.

What is there to do besides the lake?


Hell's Gate National Park is next door and is one of the few Kenyan parks where you are allowed to walk and cycle, which makes it a genuine change from sitting in a vehicle. Mount Longonot is a half-day climb to a crater rim. Crescent Island is a walk among plains game. Lake Elementaita and Lake Nakuru are an easy drive north. Park fees are set by the Kenya Wildlife Service and are revised from time to time, so confirm the current rate rather than trusting a figure you read anywhere, including here.

Is Naivasha a good first stop on a longer safari?


It is a very good one. It breaks the drive out of Nairobi, it is high and cool, it gives you birds and walking rather than another game drive, and it points you onwards to Nakuru or down towards the Mara. Two nights is the right length for most people.
