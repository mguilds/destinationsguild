Holetown

Where to Stay in Holetown

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Holetown is the middle of what the island calls the Platinum Coast: the leeward, west-facing shore in the parish of Saint James, where the Caribbean is usually as flat as a swimming pool and the sunsets go straight down into it. The English landed here first, in 1627, and the town still marks it with a festival every February. Today it is a small, tidy place with a church, a pair of shopping centres, a strip of good restaurants and, strung north and south of it, the most expensive hotels in Barbados. What you are buying is the water and the quiet. What you are not buying is nightlife, surf or value.

Where to base yourself in Holetown

Three stretches of the same calm coast, with an hour of difference in price and character between them.

Holetown itself

The best of the coast for anyone who would rather not depend on a taxi. You can walk to a supermarket, a bank, a dozen restaurants and the beach, which here is a narrow strip of pale sand with flat water over it. First Street and Second Street hold most of the eating and the only real evening life on the west coast, and it is still over by about eleven.

Paynes Bay and Fitts Village, just south

A wider beach, the best turtle snorkelling on the island, and the catamarans that come in to anchor off it in the afternoon. A string of hotels and villas along the coast road, ten minutes from Holetown by bus and twenty-five to thirty from Bridgetown. If swimming with turtles is on your list, stay here and walk out from the sand.

Porters and Mullins, just north

Quieter again, greener, and where several of the grander hotels sit in large gardens behind the road. Mullins Beach has a well-known beach bar and genuinely lovely swimming. This end of the coast needs a bus or a taxi for dinner, which most people here regard as a fair price for the hush.

Speightstown, further north still

The old northern port, six miles up the coast: a working town with a fish market, faded Georgian warehouses and hardly a tourist in it. Beds are few and far cheaper. Choose it if you want to live somewhere real rather than somewhere pretty, and do not mind a bus ride to dinner.

What your money is worth in Barbados
US $1 = 2.00 Bds$
Bds$ 1 = US 50¢
The Barbados dollar is pegged to the US dollar at two to one, so halving the local price gives you the dollar price. US dollars are accepted almost everywhere and change comes back in Barbados dollars. Hotel bills normally carry a nightly government room levy and a service charge on top of the room rate, and restaurants usually add service too — ask what is included when you book rather than at check-out. Keep some cash for buses, rum shops and the fish fry.

Holetown in your later years

This is the coast for calm water

The leeward west coast is the flattest sea in Barbados, often with no shore break at all, and the entry is usually gentle sand. If getting in and out of the water is the deciding question, this is the answer to it.

Ask whether the beach is reached on the level

Several hotels along this coast sit above the shore with steps or a slope down to the sand, and the listings rarely say so. Ask before booking: hotels a hundred yards apart differ completely on this.

Staying in Holetown saves you the taxi

North of Holetown you will take a bus or a taxi to dinner most evenings. In the town itself the restaurants are a short, level walk, which matters more after a day in the heat than it does when you are choosing the hotel.

The buses are frequent but lively

The coast road is served constantly by buses and minibuses, and they are cheap and easy to use. They are also driven briskly and the step up is a high one. A pre-agreed taxi for the evening is a small price for peace of mind.

Nearby, and the rest of the island

The whole west coast is one bus route, so Speightstown to the north and Paynes Bay to the south are both easy days out. Bridgetown and the Garrison are thirty to forty-five minutes south, and Oistins and the south coast, with the Friday fish fry, an hour. The island guide is here and the weather month by month here.

Holetown hotels — questions

Is Holetown the best place to stay in Barbados?

It is the best place to stay for calm, clear swimming water, quiet evenings and the island's grandest hotels, and it is the most expensive. If you want restaurants and bars within walking distance at a lower price, the south coast around St Lawrence Gap is better value. If you want the island's history and the easiest bus connections, stay nearer Bridgetown. Many people do a week here and wish only that they had booked longer, which is the truest thing we can say about it.

Why is it called the Platinum Coast?

It is a marketing name for the sheltered west coast, in use for decades, and it refers to the concentration of expensive hotels and villas rather than to anything about the sand. The older name for the same shore is the Gold Coast. The water really is calmer here, because the island shelters it from the trade wind and the Atlantic swell.

Can you swim with turtles from the beach?

Yes, most reliably at Paynes Bay, just south of Holetown, where green and hawksbill turtles feed close inshore and the catamarans come in each afternoon. You can swim out from the sand with a mask rather than paying for a boat. Choose operators who do not chase or hand-feed the animals.

Are the beaches private?

No. Every beach in Barbados is public to the high-water mark, including the sand in front of the grandest hotels on this coast. What the hotel owns is its loungers and its bar, not the shore. Access paths between the properties exist, though they are not always well signed.

How far is Holetown from the airport?

About forty to fifty minutes by taxi across the island from Grantley Adams, in Christ Church, depending on the traffic through Bridgetown. Agree the fare before you set off. It is the one disadvantage of this coast: the airport and the nightlife are both on the other side.

Is there anything to do in the evening?

Dinner, and a drink after it. Holetown's First and Second Streets have a good concentration of restaurants and a few bars, and there is a beach bar at Mullins up the coast. Beyond that the west coast is early to bed; the island's late evenings happen on the south coast.
