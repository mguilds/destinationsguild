Where to Stay in Almaty — the Centre, Dostyk, and the Mountain Road

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Almaty is the largest city in Kazakhstan and the one nearly every visitor arrives in: about two million people on a grid that tilts uphill toward a wall of the Tian Shan, close enough that you can be at a ski lift within an hour of leaving your room. It stopped being the capital in 1997 and never stopped being the centre of the country’s life. Navigating it is easy once you know that “up” is always south, toward the peaks. Where you stay comes down to three choices: the walkable centre, the smarter uphill avenues, or the mountains themselves.

The Golden Square and the central grid

The blocks between Abay, Dostyk, Nazarbayev and Gogol are what Almaty residents call the Golden Square, and it is the part of the city where you can leave the hotel and walk. The Green Bazaar, Panfilov Park with its nail-less wooden cathedral, the Central State Museum and most of the cafes and restaurants worth the trip are inside it or a short way outside. The single metro line runs beneath this area, which matters less than you would think, because most of it is walkable, and more than you would think in January. If this is a first visit and you want one address for the whole stay, take it here.

Dostyk Avenue, Al-Farabi and the uphill side

Dostyk Avenue climbs south out of the centre, and as it climbs the city gets quieter, greener and a degree or two cooler — which is the whole argument for staying up here in July. Al-Farabi Avenue runs across the slope near the top and carries the newer business district, the Esentai tower among it. You will use taxis or the ride-hailing apps more than you would downtown, and they are cheap and everywhere. Stay uphill if you have been to Almaty before, if you are here in high summer, or if you want the mountains out of the window rather than at the end of a drive.

Medeu, Shymbulak and the mountain road

One road leaves the top of the city and climbs the Medeu gorge to the high-altitude skating rink, then on by cable car to the Shymbulak ski area. Staying up the gorge or in the foothills above the city buys you cold clean air, a short walk to the lifts in winter and hiking from the door in summer; it costs you the evening in town, because getting back down for dinner is a committed trip. The ski season here usually runs from about December into March, but check conditions for the dates you want rather than trusting a calendar — and if you are coming for snow, book early, because Almaty skis at the weekend.

Almaty questions, answered

How many days is Almaty worth?

Four or five, and they fill themselves. Two days do the city — the Green Bazaar, Panfilov Park and the cathedral, the Central State Museum, Kok Tobe hill for the view. Then take a day up to Big Almaty Lake or Shymbulak, and another out to Charyn Canyon, which is a long day by road and worth every hour of it. The Kolsai Lakes and Kaindy need an overnight to do properly.

Which part of Almaty should a first-time visitor stay in?

The Golden Square, the blocks bounded roughly by Abay, Dostyk, Nazarbayev and Gogol. It is the walkable centre: the bazaar, the park, the museums and most of the good restaurants are either inside it or a few minutes away. Stay uphill on Dostyk or Al-Farabi on a second visit, or in high summer, when the extra height is cooler.

Do you need a car in Almaty?

Not in the city. The grid is walkable, there is a metro line under the middle of it, and ride-hailing apps work everywhere and cost little. A car or a driver earns its keep only for the days out — Charyn Canyon, the Kolsai Lakes, Altyn-Emel — where the distances are real and public transport is not the point.

How far is the airport from the centre?

Almaty’s airport (ALA) is northeast of the city, a drive of well under an hour in ordinary traffic rather than an expedition. Most visitors stay in the centre or uphill and simply ride out; there is no strong reason to sleep by the airport unless your flight is genuinely at dawn.

Can you ski from Almaty?

Yes, and it is the thing that surprises people most about the city. The road up the Medeu gorge reaches the skating rink and the Shymbulak lifts above it, close enough that a lot of people ski and sleep downtown in the same day. The season normally runs from about December into March.
