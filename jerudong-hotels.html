Where to Stay in Jerudong — the Empire and the Coast Road

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Jerudong is not a town so much as a stretch of coast fifteen miles west of the capital, and it exists on this page for one reason: The Empire, built as a royal guest house and now the only hotel in Brunei that people travel to rather than simply sleep in. Around it are the Jerudong Park Playground, a long quiet beach, and a handful of far plainer places to stay. Our booking partner files these hotels under Kampong Jerudong rather than under the capital, so this is a short page by design — three properties, each one opened on Trip.com’s own listing and its street address checked before we wrote the card.

Kampong Jerudong questions, answered

Should you stay in Jerudong or in Bandar Seri Begawan?

If you have two or more nights in Brunei, split them: the capital for the mosques, the museum and the water village, and one night out here. Jerudong is about fifteen miles west of the city — near enough for dinner in town, far enough that you will want a car or a hotel transfer. If you have one night only, the honest answer depends on what you came for: the centre if it is the country you want to see, Jerudong if it is the beach and the building.

Is The Empire worth it?

On architecture alone, yes, and it is the one address in Brunei with a reputation outside the country. It was built as a royal guest house on a scale that nothing else in the sultanate matches — marble halls, long sea frontage, gardens, a golf course — and then opened to the public as a hotel. Rates move a great deal by season and by room type, so check them on the booking page rather than assuming either a bargain or a fortune. We do not print prices here.

What is there to do in Jerudong?

The beach, the golf, and the Jerudong Park Playground — the amusement park Brunei built on a scale that had no equal in the region when it opened, and which has been through several changes since. The Jerudong night market is worth an evening for the food. Beyond that this is a quiet coast, which for a good number of visitors is the point.

Can you buy alcohol in Brunei?

No. Brunei does not sell alcohol — not in hotels, not in restaurants, not in shops. Non-Muslim visitors have long been allowed to bring in a personal quantity for their own consumption and must declare it on arrival; the allowance is set by the Royal Customs and Excise Department, and we are not printing a figure here because we could not read the current one off the department’s own site today. Check it before you fly. What is certain is that nothing is for sale once you are there, so a hotel bar is not a thing you will find in this country.

How do you get to Jerudong from the airport?

By road, in about half an hour from Brunei International Airport in Berakas, depending on traffic through the capital. There is no useful public transport on this route, so arrange a hotel transfer or hire a car. The Empire runs its own transfers; ask when you book rather than assuming.
