🚕Getting around — taxis, the cheap bus & no Uber

Aruba is one of the easiest walk‑off ports anywhere: your ship ties up right in Oranjestad, steps from the shops. For the famous beaches you have good options. Taxis use fixed island rates (no meters) from the official stand at the terminal — confirm the fare first. There's no Uber or ride‑share on Aruba, so don't count on it. The money‑saver is the public bus, Arubus, which runs a cheap, easy route from downtown out to the Eagle Beach and Palm Beach hotel strip. A free downtown trolley loops the shopping area.

⚓Stepping off the ship — downtown & the shops

You step straight into the colorful Dutch‑colonial capital — pastel buildings, the Renaissance Marketplace, and the Caya Betico Croes shopping street. It's more polished boutiques and duty‑free than pop‑up market stalls, but you'll find vendors, souvenirs and plenty of open‑air cafés right off the pier. Prices are generally fixed; USD is taken everywhere, so you don't need to change money for a day.

🏖️What to do with 6–8 hours ashore

  • Eagle Beach — wide, white and famous for its lone fofoti trees; consistently rated among the world's best. ~10–15 min by taxi or the bus.
  • Palm Beach — the lively resort strip with water sports, bars and calm swimming.
  • Renaissance Island — a private island with the celebrity flamingos (day passes when available).
  • Arikok National Park & the Natural Pool — rugged desert coast reached by 4x4 tour; a great half‑day.
  • Downtown — the trolley, the Marketplace, and easy shopping without leaving the pier zone.
An easy, foolproof day: hop the bus or a taxi to Eagle Beach for the morning, back to Oranjestad for lunch and a wander through the Marketplace, aboard by mid‑afternoon. Aruba's calm, safe and English‑speaking — a perfect do‑it‑yourself port.
New to cruising? Three quick things for Aruba: you walk straight off the ship into town (no transfer needed); bring your passport ashore — Aruba requires it; and note your ship's all‑aboard time and go by ship time, not island time.

👕What to wear & the weather

Aruba is hot, dry and breezy nearly year‑round (mid‑80s°F) and sits outside the hurricane belt, so rain rarely spoils a port day. Beach‑and‑resort casual all day; bring a hat, water and reef‑safe sunscreen — the constant trade winds hide a very strong sun.

💵Money, ATMs & tipping

No need to change money — US dollars are taken everywhere; downtown ATMs give florins or USD. Many restaurants already add ~10–15% service, so check the bill before adding more; round up taxis; a dollar or two for helpful hands.

📶Staying connected

Free wifi in the cruise terminal, the malls and most cafés. For all‑day data an eSIM (Airalo and similar) or a local SETAR SIM is cheap — but for one port day, hopping between wifi spots is usually plenty.

🛍️What to buy — and what to skip

Worth it: Aruba aloe skincare (grown on‑island), Dutch cheese and stroopwafels, a local Balashi beer. Skip: the generic "duty‑free" trinkets you'll see at every port. Prices downtown are fixed — no haggling here.

⚠️Scams & hassles to dodge

Aruba is low‑hassle and safe, but still: agree taxi fares up front, wave off "free gift"/timeshare touts near the resorts, and buy tour tickets from established operators, not beach hawkers.

🩹Health, water & sun

Tap water is safe — Aruba desalinates some of the best water anywhere. The real risks are sun and dehydration (the breeze fools you), so drink up and use reef‑safe sunscreen to protect both you and the reef.

♿Accessibility & families

Oranjestad and the terminal are flat, walkable and stroller/wheelchair‑friendly, and the downtown trolley is step‑free. A great family port: calm swimming at Palm Beach, the flamingos at Renaissance Island, and an easy De Palm Island day pass.

⭐Best excursions, ranked

  • Catamaran snorkel sail — to the Antilla shipwreck; the island's signature outing.
  • 4x4 Arikok & the Natural Pool — rugged desert coast and a swim in a hidden rock pool.
  • Renaissance flamingo island — the famous pink‑bird beach.

Only have 3 hours? Walk into Oranjestad, taxi or bus to Eagle Beach for a quick swim, and back — everything's close, so even a short call gets you sand and shops.

🍴Where to eat — the local tables

Aruba does "world‑class local" as well as anywhere in the Caribbean. Worth the taxi:

  • Zeerovers — the island legend: a no‑frills fishermen's shack in Savaneta where you point at the day's fresh catch and they fry it on the spot. Cash, plastic chairs, unforgettable. (taxi, ~25 min)
  • Old Cunucu House — traditional Aruban cooking — keshi yena, fresh fish — in a 100‑year‑old country home near Palm Beach.
  • Gasparito — Aruban cuisine and local art together; a warm, authentic favorite in Noord.
  • Madame Janette — consistently among the island's top‑rated tables when you want to splurge.

🎰Gaming, sports & a flutter

Yes — gambling is legal and Aruba is a genuine casino island, with around a dozen casinos.

  • Walk from the ship: the Crystal Casino at the Renaissance, right in downtown Oranjestad — steps from the pier and open by day.
  • The big resort floors (a short taxi or the cheap Arubus up to Palm Beach): Stellaris at the Marriott, The Casino at the Ritz‑Carlton, the Hyatt Regency, and Alhambra by Eagle Beach.
  • Will one pick you up from the boat? Don't count on a casino van waiting at the pier — the sure thing is to walk to the Crystal downtown, or hop the bus/a taxi to Palm Beach.

Sports: baseball is Aruba's national passion — this small island keeps sending players to the majors — but there's no reliable pro game to catch on a port day, and no active racetrack. Casinos, absolutely; spectator sports, another trip.

🧭Ship excursion or do‑it‑yourself?

Aruba is about as DIY‑friendly as the Caribbean gets — safe, walkable, English everywhere, and a cheap bus to the beaches. Save the cruise‑line tours for the things that need a guide and a vehicle: the 4x4 Arikok / Natural Pool run, catamaran snorkel sails, or a UTV adventure. Beach‑and‑shop days you can happily do on your own.

⏰Don't miss the ship

Go by SHIP time. Note your all‑aboard time (usually 30 minutes before sailing), set a phone alarm, and leave the beach with a cushion — buses and taxis can bunch up when everyone heads back at once. The ship leaves on schedule, with or without you.

🆘Emergency numbers & staying safe

In Aruba, dial 911 for ambulance or fire, and 100 for police — English is widely spoken.
Nearest care: Dr. Horacio Oduber Hospital, Oranjestad.
The one cruisers forget — your ship's Port Agent: your cruise line lists a local port agent in each day's program/app. Write that number down before you step off. If you're delayed and might miss the ship, call the port agent right away — they coordinate with the ship.
Also save: your cruise line's 24/7 emergency line (in your boarding documents), and keep a phone photo of your passport plus your ship and stateroom details.

❓Good to know — quick answers

Can you walk into town from the Aruba cruise port?
Yes. Ships dock right in Oranjestad, steps from the shops, restaurants and the downtown Crystal Casino.

Is there Uber in Aruba?
No. Aruba has no ride‑share. Use the fixed‑fare taxis at the terminal or the cheap Arubus bus to the beaches.

How do I get to Eagle Beach from the cruise port?
About 10 to 15 minutes by taxi, or hop the Arubus from downtown. Eagle and Palm Beach are both an easy ride.

Is gambling legal in Aruba?
Yes. The Crystal Casino is a short walk from the pier in Oranjestad, and the big resort casinos are a quick taxi up to Palm Beach.

Do I need a passport for Aruba?
Yes. Aruba requires a valid passport for entry, even on a cruise. Bring it ashore.
