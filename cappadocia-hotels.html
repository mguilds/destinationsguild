Cappadocia — Where to Stay in the Rock Valleys

Where to Stay in Cappadocia — Caves, Views & Towns

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Cappadocia is three towns rather than one, and the choice between them shapes the visit. Göreme sits in the bottom of the valley with the balloon fields, the walking paths and the restaurants all within fifteen minutes on foot. Uçhisar, ten minutes up the hill, is quieter, higher and has the long view. Ürgüp is a proper town with shops and a market and less of the feeling of sleeping inside an attraction. Below is each one, honest words about cave rooms and steps, and hotels we can stand behind.

Göreme — in the middle of everything

The village in the bottom of the valley, surrounded by the rock cones, with the Open-Air Museum a walk away and most of the balloon launch fields on its doorstep. If you have two nights and no car, stay here.

Göreme is small enough to cross on foot in fifteen minutes and holds most of the region’s cave hotels, its restaurants and its tour desks. The trade-offs are honest ones: it is the busiest of the three towns, the dawn chorus is hot-air balloon burners rather than birds, and the most photographed terraces are also the most crowded ones at sunrise. The walking paths into the Red, Rose and Love valleys start from the edge of the village, which is the real argument for being here.

A long-established family-run hotel spread over several terraced houses on the slope at the edge of the village, with a mix of cave rooms, arch rooms and ordinary rooms — which makes it one of the easier places to match a room to what you actually want. Uçhisar — the ridge and the long view

Ten minutes away and a good deal higher, built around a castle rock that looks out over the whole valley. Quieter, more residential, and the best views in Cappadocia from a hotel terrace.

Uçhisar is where we would stay on a second visit, or on a first if the view matters more than being able to walk to dinner. The hotels here are generally the most comfortable in the region and several of them are genuinely beautiful. The cost is the hill: this is a steep village, the lanes are cobbled and stepped, and you will want a taxi or a hotel shuttle down to Göreme in the evening rather than a walk back up it in the dark.

A whole restored section of the old village beneath the Uçhisar castle rock, built out of houses and tunnels that were abandoned for decades, with its own vineyard and one of the best restaurants in the region. Ürgüp — a proper town

The largest of the three, twenty minutes east, with shops, banks, a market and a life that does not depend entirely on visitors. Best if you want Cappadocia without feeling you are staying inside an attraction.

Ürgüp has the region’s most elegant restored mansion hotels, built into the hillside of the old quarter, and it is the usual base for the local wine. You are further from the balloon fields and the Open-Air Museum, so mornings start with a short drive — hotels arrange this as a matter of course. Flatter in the centre than either of the other two, which is worth knowing if steps are a problem.

What to know before you book

Cave rooms, honestly

A room cut into rock is cool, extremely quiet and genuinely memorable. It is also usually dim — a window is a hole in a cliff — and can feel damp in winter. If you want daylight, ask for an arch room or a terrace room rather than a deep cave.

Steps everywhere

These hotels are built up hillsides in terraces. Lifts are rare and the ground between buildings is often cobbled and stepped. If stairs are difficult, say so when booking and ask specifically for a room on the entrance level — the good hotels answer this question straight.

Balloons are weather-dependent

Flights are cancelled for wind more often than visitors expect, and nobody can promise you one. Book your flight for the first morning of your stay rather than the last, so a cancellation leaves you a second and third chance rather than none.

Getting here

Fly to Kayseri (ASR), about an hour away, or Nevşehir (NAV), about half an hour. Both have shuttle services that most hotels will book for you. The overnight coach from Istanbul is cheap, comfortable and long.

How long to stay

Two nights sees it. Three is better: it gives the balloons a second chance and leaves a whole day for the underground cities and the valley walks without rushing either.

Seasons

Spring and autumn are the best walking weather. Summer is hot and bright. Winter is cold and the snow over the rock is a genuine sight, but flights and rural roads are more often disrupted.

Questions

Should I stay in Göreme, Uçhisar or Ürgüp?

Göreme if it is your first visit and you have no car: it is in the middle of the valleys, the balloon fields and the restaurants, and you can walk to all of it. Uçhisar if the view from your own terrace matters most and you do not mind a taxi down to dinner — it is higher, quieter and generally more comfortable. Ürgüp if you would rather be in a working town with shops and a market than inside the attraction.

Is a cave room actually nice to sleep in?

Yes, with one caveat worth knowing beforehand. Cave rooms are cool in summer, remarkably quiet and genuinely memorable. They are also dim, because the only window is a hole cut in a cliff face, and in winter they can feel damp. If daylight matters to you, ask for an arch room or a terrace room rather than a deep cave — most hotels here offer both.

Will I definitely get a balloon flight?

No, and be wary of anyone who promises you will. Flights are grounded for wind fairly often, and it is nobody's fault when it happens. The practical answer is to book your flight for the first morning of your stay rather than the last, so that a cancellation leaves you a second and a third chance instead of none. Hotels are used to rebooking this and will do it for you.

How many nights do I need in Cappadocia?

Two nights covers the essentials — the Göreme Open-Air Museum, one underground city and a valley walk. Three is the number we would choose: it gives the balloons a second chance at the weather, and it means the underground cities and the walking do not have to be squeezed into the same day.

How do I get to Cappadocia?

By air into Kayseri (ASR), about an hour from the villages, or Nevşehir (NAV), about half an hour. Both airports have shuttle services and most hotels will book one for you as a matter of course. There is also an overnight coach from Istanbul, which is cheap and comfortable and takes a long time.
