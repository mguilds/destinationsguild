Where to Stay in San Antonio — River Walk, the Alamo, the Pearl & Beyond

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

San Antonio is built around a river and a mission, and most visitors want to sleep within a short walk of both. Downtown, the River Walk winds past the hotels, the Alamo stands on its plaza close by, and one hotel beside it calls itself the oldest continuously operating hotel west of the Mississippi. Up river, the Pearl has grown up around an old brewery. Families heading for the theme parks often stay further out. This page sorts the areas and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in San Antonio

Four bases, each suited to a different trip.

Downtown, the River Walk and the Alamo

The official visitors bureau, Visit San Antonio, describes downtown as the place to stroll the River Walk, see landmarks like the Alamo and find shops, restaurants and entertainment together. For a first visit this is the obvious choice: you can walk from your hotel to the river, the Alamo and the Rivercenter shops.

It is the busiest part of the city, and River Walk rooms with a view are the first to go. Book early for spring and the holidays.

The Pearl and the Museum Reach

The bureau sends visitors up river to the River Walk’s Museum Reach and the Pearl, which grew up around the old Pearl Brewery; Hotel Emma’s own website describes it as a culinary and cultural community. A good choice for food lovers and couples, a little apart from the downtown crowds but still on the river path.

Southtown

Just south of downtown. The bureau calls it a historic and eclectic neighbourhood with a vibrant arts scene, and it takes in the old King William district. For travellers who like galleries, old houses and local restaurants; there are fewer big hotels here.

Northwest and the theme parks

The bureau describes the La Cantera District, around Six Flags, as a place of upscale shops, family-friendly eateries and entertainment, and the Northwest side, home to SeaWorld, as a neighbourhood made for family fun. Stay out here if the parks are the point of the trip; you will need a car for downtown.

The Menger Hotel

Alamo PlazaHistoricPool

At 204 Alamo Plaza. Its own website calls it the oldest continuously operating hotel west of the Mississippi, opened in 1859 by William Menger, and places it directly on Alamo Plaza, next to the Rivercenter mall and the River Walk. It has 316 rooms and suites, some with a private balcony or a view of the Alamo, the pool or the Spanish courtyard garden on request, a swimming pool, and the Menger Bar, where it says Theodore Roosevelt recruited his Rough Riders. The history-lover’s address.

The Crockett Hotel

Alamo PlazaHistoricIndependent

At 320 Bonham Street, next to the Alamo and steps from the River Walk. Its own website says it is independently operated, a member of Historic Hotels of America and named for the Alamo defender Davy Crockett, with 138 non-smoking rooms and suites, an Alamo Plaza coffee bar and the Crockett Tavern, whose patio looks over Alamo Plaza. A smaller historic choice in the same square as the Menger.

Hotel Gibbs

AlamoHistoricPets welcome

At 105 North Alamo Street. Its own website says it was built in 1909 as San Antonio’s first high-rise, stands on the grounds of the original 1836 Alamo compound, and is about 100 feet from the main entrance to the River Walk. It serves a breakfast buffet with breakfast tacos, has a flower-wine tasting bar, and welcomes pets for a per-stay fee (checked 21 November 2026). As close to the Alamo as a hotel gets.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of San Antonio details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to San Antonio

San Antonio International Airport (SAT) is the city’s airport, and the visitors bureau describes it as just 10 minutes from downtown. If you will stay downtown and walk the River Walk, you may not need a car at all.

Getting around

Downtown is walkable, and the River Walk path links many of the hotels, the Alamo, the Rivercenter and, further up river, the Pearl. For the theme parks in the Northwest and the La Cantera District you will want a car. If you drive, ask your hotel about parking and what it costs before you book; some downtown hotels, such as the Drury Plaza, are valet only.

The missions

The visitors bureau notes that San Antonio Missions National Historical Park was named the first World Heritage Site in Texas, together with the Alamo. The park takes in the city’s four southernmost Spanish colonial missions: Concepción, San José, San Juan and Espada. They lie south of downtown, so plan a half day.

When to go

The bureau calls spring one of the most popular times to visit, with comfortable temperatures from March to May, and the season peaks with Fiesta San Antonio, an 11-day citywide celebration. Fall is another good time for food and the historic neighbourhoods. In summer the bureau advises planning outdoor activities early or late in the day, and at the holidays thousands of lights glow above the River Walk.

San Antonio Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in San Antonio?

Downtown on or near the River Walk for a first visit, within a walk of the Alamo, the river and the Rivercenter. The Pearl, up river on the Museum Reach, for food and a calmer evening. The Northwest or the La Cantera District if the trip is built around SeaWorld or Six Flags.

How far is the airport from downtown San Antonio?

The visitors bureau describes San Antonio International Airport as just 10 minutes from downtown.

Is there a San Antonio hotel with free breakfast on the River Walk?

Drury Plaza Hotel San Antonio Riverwalk lists a free hot breakfast every morning and a free evening 5:30 Kickback with hot food and drinks on its own website. It also has a rooftop pool and a microwave and refrigerator in every room.

Is there a pet-friendly hotel near the Alamo?

Hotel Gibbs, steps from the Alamo, says on its own website that it welcomes pets for a per-stay fee. Check the current pet rules and fee before you book.

When is the best time to visit San Antonio?

The visitors bureau calls spring one of the most popular times, with comfortable temperatures from March to May and Fiesta San Antonio, an 11-day citywide celebration. Fall is also good. Summer is hot, so plan outdoor sightseeing early or late in the day.
