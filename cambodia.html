Cambodia Travel Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

A small country with one enormous thing in it. Angkor is the reason almost everybody comes — a temple city of forty square miles, built when London was a town of timber — but Cambodia keeps giving after the temples: the Mekong capital at Phnom Penh, the colonial streets and rice fields of Battambang, pepper farms and a green river at Kampot, crab shacks on the shore at Kep. It is warm, inexpensive, easy to travel and, for a country with the history it has, startlingly generous to visitors. This is the Guild’s chapter on it.

Why visit Cambodia

Four reasons the Guild sends travellers here, in the order people tell us about them afterwards.

Angkor

The largest religious monument on earth, and around it a whole city of temples spread across forty square miles of forest — Angkor Wat at sunrise, the serene faces of the Bayon, and Ta Prohm with the silk-cotton roots pouring over its walls.

The welcome

Cambodians have every reason to be guarded with outsiders and are instead among the most open people in Asia. It is the thing visitors mention first when they get home, usually before the temples.

Value

One of the best-value countries in the region: a genuinely good hotel for the price of a mediocre one elsewhere, excellent food for a few dollars, and a car with a driver for the day that costs less than a taxi across most Western cities.

The slow south

Kampot, Kep and the islands run at their own pace — pepper farms, a crab market, a river under hills, and nothing whatever you are obliged to do.

How the country divides up

Siem Reap & Angkor

Siem Reap is a small, flat, walkable town whose whole purpose is the temple city a few miles north. Angkor is not one monument but hundreds, spread through forest over forty square miles: Angkor Wat itself, the face towers of the Bayon inside the walled city of Angkor Thom, and Ta Prohm in the grip of the trees. South of town the Tonlé Sap, Southeast Asia’s great lake, swells and shrinks with the seasons and carries floating villages on it.

Phnom Penh & the Mekong

The capital sits where the Mekong, the Bassac and the Tonlé Sap rivers meet: the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, the National Museum’s Khmer sculpture, a long riverfront promenade, and in the south of the city the two memorials — Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek — that no honest visit leaves out. Upriver, Kratie is where the last Irrawaddy dolphins of the Mekong surface at dusk.

Battambang & the northwest

Battambang is the country’s second city and the one visitors are most surprised by: the best street of French colonial shophouses in Cambodia, a serious arts scene built around the Phare Ponleu Selpak school, rice villages that start at the edge of town, and the bat cave at Phnom Sampeau that empties into the evening sky. Three hours from Siem Reap on a good road.

The south coast & islands

Kampot sits on a green river under the Bokor massif, surrounded by the pepper farms that made its name, with the old French hill station on the plateau above. Kep, half an hour on, is a faded 1960s seaside resort with the best crab in the country. Further west, the islands of Koh Rong and Koh Rong Sanloem hold the white sand, reached by boat from Sihanoukville.

Top things to do in Cambodia

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Sunrise at Angkor Wat

Be at the gate before five, cross the causeway in the dark, and wait by the northern pond as the five towers come out of the night and reappear upside down in the water.

The faces of the Bayon

Two hundred-odd serene stone faces looking out from fifty-odd towers in the middle of Angkor Thom. Go mid-morning when the side light picks them out, and climb to the upper terrace.

Ta Prohm

The temple left in the grip of the forest, where silk-cotton and strangler fig roots pour over the galleries. Early or late, because it is the most crowded ruin in Cambodia at mid-day.

Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek

A hard half day in Phnom Penh, and the one most visitors call the most important of the trip. Take the audio guide, and plan nothing demanding afterwards.

The bats of Phnom Sampeau

At dusk outside Battambang, millions of free-tailed bats pour out of a cliff cave in a single unbroken ribbon that takes half an hour to pass overhead.

Kampot pepper and the Kep crab market

A morning among the pepper vines and an afternoon at the crab shacks on the shore at Kep, with green peppercorns still on the stalk in the pan.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Cambodia has two seasons and no regional split to work around. November to February is the cool dry season and the best weather of the year. March to May is dry and very hot, with April the hottest month. June to October is the green season: heavy afternoon rain, full moats, brilliant countryside, few people and the lowest prices.

Two national festivals are worth knowing about before you fix dates. Khmer New Year falls in mid-April, when much of the country travels home. The Water Festival fills the Phnom Penh riverfront for three days around the November full moon. Both follow the lunar calendar and move from year to year.

Whatever month you choose, Angkor is a dawn activity. Be at the temples at first light and back at the pool by eleven. See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Cambodia for weather, crowds, prices and the festivals through the year.

Getting there

Two gateways, and both of them are new enough to catch travellers out. Siem Reap–Angkor International Airport (SAI) opened in 2023 roughly 40 kilometres east of Siem Reap, replacing the old airfield beside the town, so allow about an hour for the transfer rather than the ten minutes older guidebooks quote. Techo International Airport (KTI) opened in September 2025 about 20 kilometres south of Phnom Penh and has taken over the capital’s commercial flights from the old Phnom Penh International.

There are no direct flights from North America. Almost everyone connects through Bangkok, Singapore, Seoul, Taipei, Kuala Lumpur or Hong Kong. Many visitors arrive overland from Thailand or Vietnam, or combine Cambodia with one of them on the same trip.

Inside the country, distances are short and a car with a driver for the day costs remarkably little — Siem Reap to Battambang is about three hours, Phnom Penh to Kampot three to four. United States passport holders need a visa, applied for online through the official Cambodian government service, and air arrivals must also submit Cambodia’s electronic arrival card. Use only the official government portals; the lookalike sites that charge a fee for nothing are an unusually persistent nuisance here.

Cambodia travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Cambodia?

The cool dry season, November to February: warm days, genuinely cool mornings, very little rain, and Angkor comfortable to walk from first light. It is also the busiest and dearest stretch of the year. March to May is dry and very hot, with April the hottest month of all. The green season from June to October brings heavy afternoon downpours, full moats, brilliant countryside, thin crowds and the lowest prices of the year — a better bargain than its reputation suggests.

Do Americans need a visa for Cambodia?

Yes. United States passport holders need a tourist visa, which is issued online through the Cambodian government’s own e-Visa service before you travel, and normally allows a stay of up to 30 days. Travellers arriving by air are also required to submit Cambodia’s electronic arrival card shortly before arrival. Apply only through the official Cambodian government portals — Cambodia attracts an unusual number of convincing lookalike websites that charge a large fee for nothing. Your passport should have at least six months’ validity and a blank page, and the rules do change, so confirm the current requirements with the official Cambodian authority before you book.

What currency is used in Cambodia?

Both the Cambodian riel and the US dollar, side by side. In practice hotels, restaurants and tours price in dollars, you pay in dollars, and your change below a dollar comes back in riel. Bring notes in good condition, because torn, marked or heavily worn dollars are often refused, and withdraw from bank ATMs rather than street machines. Cards work at hotels and larger restaurants; markets, tuk-tuks and small cafes are cash. Decline any machine or terminal that offers to charge you in your home currency.

How many days do you need in Cambodia?

Ten days is the comfortable classic: three nights in Siem Reap for Angkor, two in Battambang, two in Phnom Penh, and three in Kampot and Kep at the end to slow down. With a week, do Siem Reap and Phnom Penh properly and leave the rest for next time. The country is small and the roads between these places are good, which makes it one of the easier itineraries in Southeast Asia to put together.

Is Cambodia a good country for older travellers?

Yes, with two things to plan around. The first is heat: Angkor is walked at dawn and left alone in the afternoon, in every month. The second is surfaces — temple steps are steep, worn and often without a handrail, and town pavements are uneven. Against that, hotels are excellent value, a private car and driver for the day costs very little and transforms the trip, and Cambodians are unfailingly helpful. Many of the finest things here — the river at Kampot, the bats at Phnom Sampeau, a crab lunch at Kep — ask nothing of your knees at all.
