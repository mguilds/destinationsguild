A short history

Washington State's Pacific coast and Puget Sound have been home to Coast Salish nations such as the Duwamish, Suquamish, and Lummi for thousands of years, sustained by salmon runs and cedar forests. British and American fur-trading interests overlapped here in the 19th century until an 1846 treaty fixed the border at the 49th parallel, leaving the region firmly American.

Washington became a U.S. territory in 1853 and the 42nd state in 1889, its growth driven by timber, fishing, and the transcontinental railway's arrival in Tacoma and Seattle. The Klondike Gold Rush turned Seattle into a boomtown supply hub, and the 20th century brought Boeing's aircraft industry and, later, Microsoft and Amazon, cementing the Puget Sound region as a global technology centre.

Beyond Seattle's skyline, the state remains defined by dramatic geography: volcanic Mount Rainier, the temperate rainforests of the Olympic Peninsula, and the dry wine country east of the Cascades around Yakima and Walla Walla. Washington's economy today spans aerospace, software, agriculture, and a maritime trade relationship anchored by its deep-water ports.

Bodhi

Mount Rainier is an active volcano, and its glaciers make it the most heavily glaciated peak in the lower 48, which is part of why those wildflower meadows below it are so lush come summer. Seattle's Pike Place Market opened in 1907 to let farmers sell straight to shoppers, cutting out the middlemen, and it's been going strong ever since.

Nolie

Go to Olympic National Park in shoulder season, spring or fall, when the rainforest trails are quiet and the coast fog rolls in just right. And for the San Juans, take the early ferry out of Anacortes, the orca sightings and the empty decks are worth the alarm clock.

When to go

July–September is driest; the rest of the year is famously wet.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Grey and wet in Seattle; snow piles up in the Cascades for skiing at Crystal Mountain and Stevens Pass.

Spring (Mar–May)

Rain gradually eases; tulip fields bloom in the Skagit Valley in April.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

The driest, sunniest stretch — ideal for the San Juans, national parks, and hiking.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

September is often gorgeous and dry, then rains return with fall colour in the mountains.
