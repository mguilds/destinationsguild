Where to Stay in Nashville — Downtown, Midtown, Germantown & Opryland

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Nashville is Music City, and the official visitors bureau puts it simply: the honky-tonks on Lower Broadway play live music 365 days a year. Where you sleep depends on how close to that music you want to be. Downtown puts you within a walk of Broadway, the Ryman and the big museums, and it is lively late into the night. Midtown and Germantown are a short ride away and calmer, and Opryland, out east of the city, is built around the Grand Ole Opry and family attractions. This page sorts the areas and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in Nashville

Five parts of the city, each suited to a different trip.

Downtown and Lower Broadway

The visitors bureau calls Lower Broadway the heart of downtown, with live music, food, sports and hotels all close together. Stay here if you want to walk out of the lobby and into the music.

The trade-off is noise and crowds, especially on weekends. Ask for a room on a high floor or away from Broadway if you are a light sleeper.

SoBro and The Gulch

Just south and west of Broadway. The bureau describes SoBro as a downtown neighbourhood with upscale hotels, and The Gulch as a walkable area of boutique hotels, high-rise condos and murals. Close to the action but a little calmer than Broadway itself.

Midtown and West End

The area beside Vanderbilt University, which the bureau notes is home to Centennial Park, with Music Row next door. A good base for a longer stay, for visitors to the university, and for anyone who wants restaurants and bars without the Broadway crowds.

Germantown

A historic neighbourhood north of downtown that the bureau describes as walkable, with baseball, brunch and bars. Smaller, quieter places to stay, popular with couples.

Opryland and Music Valley

The bureau places it ten miles east of downtown and seven miles north of the airport. Here are the Grand Ole Opry, the Opry Mills shopping centre, and the big Gaylord Opryland Resort with its SoundWaves water park. The choice for families and for a trip built around the Opry, though you will need a car or a ride to reach downtown.

At 231 Sixth Avenue North, on the corner of Union Street. Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Nashville details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Nashville

Nashville International Airport (BNA) is the city’s airport; the visitors bureau places Opryland seven miles north of it. The airport’s own website, flynashville.com, lists the buses, shuttles and other ground transport into town.

Getting around

Downtown, SoBro and The Gulch sit next to one another, and the bureau describes The Gulch as walkable. For Midtown, Germantown or Opryland you will want a car, a rideshare or a bus. If you drive, ask your hotel about parking and what it costs before you book.

When to go

Music City has music every night of the year, so there is no off season for the honky-tonks. The visitors bureau promotes winter as a time of festive events, live music and winter deals. Weekends and big event dates fill up fast downtown, so book early if your dates are fixed.

Nashville Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in Nashville?

Downtown if you want to walk to Lower Broadway, the honky-tonks and the big music venues. Midtown or Germantown for a calmer stay a short ride away. Opryland for families and for trips built around the Grand Ole Opry.

Is it noisy to stay near Lower Broadway?

It can be. The honky-tonks play live music every day of the year and the street is busy late, especially on weekends. Light sleepers should ask for a high floor or a room away from Broadway, or stay in Midtown or Germantown.

How far is Opryland from downtown Nashville?

The visitors bureau puts Opryland and Music Valley ten miles east of downtown and seven miles north of Nashville International Airport.

Are there pet-friendly hotels in downtown Nashville?

Yes. The Hermitage Hotel, Noelle and 1 Hotel Nashville each describe themselves as pet friendly on their own websites. Check each hotel’s pet rules and fees before you book.

Is there a budget hotel in Nashville with free parking?

Drake Inn on Murfreesboro Pike lists free parking, a microwave and refrigerator in every room, and a kitchenette studio on its own website. It does not allow pets.
