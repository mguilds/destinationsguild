When to Go to Egypt

The Best Time to Visit Egypt

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: the cool half of the year — roughly October to April — is the sweet spot. You get warm, dry days and comfortable evenings for the Pyramids, Luxor and the Nile, without the punishing summer heat of the south. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the heat and crowds, and the best timing for Cairo, the Nile and the Red Sea.

Egypt is a year-round destination, but the desert climate makes timing matter more than almost anywhere. The country you meet on a golden January morning — crisp air at the Pyramids, a sweater on the Nile at dusk — is a world away from Luxor in July, when the temperature climbs past 42°C by mid-afternoon and the tombs bake. Choose your dates well and everything is easier. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Winter — December to February

$$$$ PeakCool & dryBest for the NileBook early

The high season, and for good reason: warm, sunny days around 20–24°C in Cairo and Luxor, cool evenings, and near-perfect sightseeing weather. It's the prime time for the Pyramids, the temples and a Nile cruise, and reliable winter sun on the Red Sea. The trade-offs are the biggest crowds and highest prices of the year — sharpest over the Christmas–New Year fortnight — and chilly desert nights, so pack a layer.

Spring — March to May

$$$ ShoulderWarmGreat value earlyWatch the khamaseen

An excellent shoulder season, especially March and early April: warm, dry and noticeably less crowded than mid-winter, with the Red Sea at its best for diving. The one quirk is the khamaseen, a hot, dusty wind that can blow up on scattered days between March and May. By late May the south is heating up fast toward summer levels.

Summer — June to August

$ Low seasonVery hotCheapestRed Sea & early starts

The bargain season, if you can take the heat. Cairo sits in the mid-to-high 30s°C, and Luxor and Aswan regularly top 40°C, so temples are best at dawn and the middle of the day is for shade and pools. Prices for flights, cruises and hotels are at their lowest, and the temples are blissfully empty. The Red Sea stays popular, with the sea a welcome relief.

Autumn — September to November

$$$ ShoulderCooling downGreat valueWarm seas

The mirror of spring, and a superb time to go. The fierce summer heat eases through September and October into comfortable sightseeing weather, while the Red Sea keeps its warm summer water for diving and snorkelling. Crowds and prices climb again as November approaches the winter peak, so October is a real sweet spot for value and weather combined.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$$$$ PeakCoolestPrime Nile

The coolest, sunniest month and the heart of peak season. Ideal daytime weather for Cairo and the temples, chilly nights, and busy sites and cruises. Prices stay high after the New Year spike passes. Perfect for the classic Cairo–Nile itinerary if you don't mind the crowds.

February

$$$$ PeakCool & dryGreat sightseeing

More of January's excellent weather with days warming slightly. Still firmly peak season for the Nile and the Pyramids, and prime for the Red Sea. Book cruises and top hotels well ahead. One of the very best months to see Upper Egypt in comfort.

March

$$$ ShoulderWarmFewer crowds

Warm, pleasant and a little quieter as the winter peak eases — one of the best-value windows with near-perfect weather. Watch for the occasional khamaseen dust wind. Excellent for combining Cairo, a Nile cruise and a few days on the Red Sea.

April

$$$ ShoulderWarm & brightEaster spike

Lovely early-spring warmth and good value, though Easter and the Egyptian spring holiday of Sham El-Nessim bring a short domestic surge. The south starts heating up late in the month. Great for the Red Sea and still comfortable for the temples if you start early.

May

$$ ValueHot in the southQuiet temples

Prices drop as the heat builds. Cairo is warm, and Luxor and Aswan climb toward the high 30s°C, so plan the south for early mornings. The reward is thinner crowds and softer rates, plus superb Red Sea conditions before the summer peak of divers arrives.

June

$ Low seasonVery hotBargains

Summer proper. Hot in Cairo and searing in Upper Egypt, so temples are dawn affairs and the Nile cruise deck a midday refuge. In return, some of the year's lowest prices and emptiest sites. The Red Sea shines, with warm water and long, sunny days.

July

$ CheapestScorching southRed Sea

One of the hottest and cheapest months. Luxor and Aswan bake past 40°C; Cairo is hot but manageable with early starts. Best suited to a heat-tolerant traveler, or to a Red Sea beach-and-dive holiday where the sea keeps things bearable and prices are keen.

August

$ CheapestPeak heatEmpty temples

The other peak-heat, rock-bottom-price month. The same rules apply: sightsee at dawn, rest at midday, and lean on air-conditioned museums and the Red Sea. Those who brave it get the Pyramids and Karnak almost to themselves and the lowest rates of the year.

September

$$ ValueEasing heatWarm seas

The heat begins to loosen its grip, especially late in the month, while the Red Sea holds its warm summer water — prime diving conditions. Prices are still soft before the autumn climb. A smart-value month for beach-first trips and heat-tolerant temple-goers.

October

$$$ Prime valueWarm & comfortableSweet spot

Arguably the best all-round month: comfortable sightseeing warmth across the whole country, warm seas on the Red Sea, and crowds and prices not yet at winter-peak levels. Everything is open and pleasant. Book ahead — plenty of travelers have the same idea.

November

$$$ RisingCool & clearPeak begins

Beautiful, clear and comfortable, sliding into peak season as prices and crowds build toward the winter high. Ideal weather for the Nile and the Pyramids, with the Red Sea still warm. One of the loveliest months to travel the whole country before the December rush.

December

$$ → $$$$ SplitCoolChristmas peak

A month of two halves. Early December is cool, clear and still reasonable, with excellent weather. The two weeks around Christmas and New Year are the sharpest peak of the year — sublime weather but packed cruises and top rates — so book far ahead or aim for the calmer first fortnight.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Summer — June to August, with July and August the lowest

$ Rock bottomFlights, cruises & hotelsBeat the heat early

Nile cruises, Cairo hotels and long-haul airfares all bottom out in high summer, when the heat thins the crowds. July and August are the cheapest weeks of the year, with June and September close behind. The catch is the temperature: temples in Luxor and Aswan are only comfortable at dawn, so build early starts and midday breaks into the plan.

Want reasonable weather and a deal? Target the shoulder edges: late September to October and March to early April. You get comfortable sightseeing and warm seas at well below peak-winter prices. As a rule, midweek flights beat weekends, and booking a Nile cruise a couple of months out lands the best cabins and fares.

Beating the Heat & the Crowds

Egypt's busy and brutal periods are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the heat to sidestep

Christmas & NYEasterJun–Aug in the southKhamaseen

The Christmas–New Year fortnight is the crowd-and-price crunch, with Easter and Sham El-Nessim a smaller spring spike. On the weather side, June to August in Luxor, Aswan and the desert is genuinely punishing, and the khamaseen dust winds can blow up on odd days in spring. None of these need ruin a trip — they just reward planning.

To have the great sights closer to yourself, come in the shoulder months — October to November and March — and start early wherever you are: the Pyramids, Karnak and the Valley of the Kings are coolest and quietest at opening. For where to base yourself, see our guide to where to stay in Cairo.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to Egypt.

What is the best month to visit Egypt?

October, November, February and March are the strongest all-round months. Days are warm and dry rather than scorching, the evenings are pleasant, and sightseeing at the Pyramids and the temples of Luxor and Aswan is comfortable. December and January are the coolest and busiest peak, while these shoulder months give you similar weather with slightly thinner crowds.

What is the cheapest time to visit Egypt?

The summer months, roughly May to September, are the cheapest for flights, Nile cruises and hotels, because the heat in Cairo and especially Upper Egypt is intense. The Red Sea resorts stay busy but still offer strong summer deals. If you can handle early starts and midday breaks, summer delivers the lowest prices and the emptiest temples.

When should I avoid Egypt because of heat or crowds?

Avoid Luxor, Aswan and the desert in June, July and August, when daytime highs regularly top 40°C. For crowds and the highest prices, the Christmas–New Year fortnight and, to a lesser extent, the Easter period are the sharp peaks. Spring can also bring the khamaseen, a hot dusty wind, on scattered days between March and May.

Is the Red Sea good to visit year-round?

Largely yes. Sharm el-Sheikh, Hurghada and Marsa Alam enjoy warm, dry weather almost all year and are a reliable source of winter sun when Europe is cold. Spring and autumn are ideal for diving and snorkelling; summer is hot but the sea is a relief, and winter evenings can be breezy, so pack a layer.

What about visiting Egypt during Ramadan?

Egypt is welcoming during Ramadan, but the rhythm changes: some cafés and sites keep shorter daytime hours, and the atmosphere turns festive after sunset. The dates shift earlier each year on the Western calendar. Tourist sites, Nile cruises and Red Sea resorts continue to operate; just plan meals and timings with a little extra flexibility.
