Lake Fishing in the Great Plains — the Dakotas, Nebraska and Kansas, and the Towns Beside the Lakes

Lake Oahe, Lake Sakakawea, Devils Lake, Lake McConaughy and Milford Lake: what swims in each, the rules from four state fishing agencies, the licence a visitor needs, where to launch a boat this season, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against North Dakota Game and Fish, South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks, Nebraska Game and Parks and the Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks, 20 November 2026

The Great Plains have no mountains to catch the rain, so their big fishing lakes are mostly rivers held back by dams. The Missouri River makes two of the largest reservoirs in the country: Lake Sakakawea behind Garrison Dam in North Dakota, and Lake Oahe behind Oahe Dam at Pierre, which runs north all the way to Bismarck. Both are walleye water first, with northern pike, smallmouth bass and stocked chinook salmon on top. North Dakota adds Devils Lake, its largest natural lake, famous for walleye, white bass and winter ice fishing. Nebraska's Lake McConaughy is a long sand-beach reservoir on the North Platte River with big walleye and wipers, and trout in little Lake Ogallala below the dam. Kansas's Milford Lake, the largest lake in the state, is a walleye, white bass, crappie and blue catfish lake near Junction City.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, and anyone who wants a guide for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 20 November 2026 from the agency that sets it, and is dated. Two warnings before you plan: the water is low on the Missouri River lakes and very low on Lake McConaughy, and North Dakota found zebra mussels in Lake Sakakawea in October 2026. Both are covered lake by lake below. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The best months, in plain words

A hard continental climate: hot summers, real winters, and ice on the northern lakes from about Christmas to March.

Lake Sakakawea — North Dakota’s inland sea

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers calls it the third largest man-made lake in the United States: 178 miles long behind Garrison Dam, with more than 1,500 miles of shoreline. North Dakota Parks gives it 368,000 acres.

Lake Sakakawea, from Garrison Dam to Williston

What swims there. Walleye, sauger, northern pike, chinook salmon, smallmouth bass and channel catfish. North Dakota Game and Fish’s 2026 outlook puts walleye numbers above the long-term average, with plenty of fish from 11 to 20 inches and the bigger fish mainly in the middle and lower lake; pike are below their 2012 peak but fish over 15 pounds are still there; catfish are best at the upper end.

How people fish it. By boat, trolling and pulling bottom bouncers for walleye. Salmon anglers use downriggers in deep water in summer, and fish from the shore and with long lines when the salmon gather near the dam in the autumn. In winter the state park ramps are ploughed for ice access.

Keeping a fish. North Dakota’s statewide limits apply, with no walleye minimum length on this lake: walleye, sauger and saugeye together 5 a day, pike 5, salmon 5, bass 5, white bass 30, yellow perch 20. Hook and line is open all year, except the areas near the Garrison Dam tailrace that the Corps posts closed. New for 2026: snagging, bow fishing and spearing for chinook salmon are allowed from sunup to sundown, 25 September to 31 October, from Garrison Dam upstream to the Highway 23 bridge near New Town — but bows and spears are not allowed inside Lake Sakakawea and Fort Stevenson state parks.

Zebra mussels, October 2026. North Dakota Game and Fish confirmed zebra mussels in the far eastern part of the lake, near the Highway 83 embankment, on 27 October 2026, and says the rules will be refined before the 2027 boating season. Pull every drain plug, drain every drop and clean off every weed before you leave the ramp.

Low water, 2027. On 2 November 2026 the department warned that ongoing low water on Lake Sakakawea, Lake Oahe and the Missouri River is expected to make boat access hard through the 2027 season, with many ramps and parking lots “less than ideal”. Check the department’s ramp page before you tow.

Confirmed with North Dakota Game and Fish, North Dakota Parks and Recreation and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 20 November 2026 — the 2026–28 North Dakota Fishing Proclamation and Fishing Guide, the department’s news of 27 October and 2 November 2026, its 2026 fishing waters outlook, the three state park pages and the Corps’ Garrison Dam page.

Boat ramps. Lake Sakakawea State Park at Pick City has a full-service marina, two ramps and a fish-cleaning station by the dam. Fort Stevenson State Park, three miles south of Garrison, has the Garrison Bay and deTrobriand Bay marinas and boat rentals. Lewis and Clark State Park, 16 miles east of Williston, has a four-lane ramp and a marina when the water allows. The department also lists ramps at New Town Marina, Four Bears, Van Hook, Parshall Bay, Beulah Bay, Hazen Bay, Government Bay and Wolf Creek near Riverdale, and others.

Nearest towns: Riverdale and Pick City at the dam; Beulah and Hazen on the south shore; New Town in the middle of the lake; Williston at the top. Garrison, which calls itself the walleye capital, sits on the north shore, but our partner’s search sends its name to other towns, so it has no button — use Riverdale.

Devils Lake — walleye, white bass and the ice

North Dakota Tourism calls it the state’s largest natural lake. Its size has changed a great deal as the water has risen over the years, and we found no agency figure for it today.

Devils Lake, from Grahams Island to Creel Bay

What swims there. Walleye, northern pike, yellow perch and white bass. North Dakota Game and Fish’s 2026 outlook says walleye are doing well, with above-average numbers of 15- to 20-inch fish; pike are slightly below average, mostly 20 to 32 inches; white bass are above average at about 14 to 16 inches. Perch fishing is expected to be slower than usual in 2026, with a rebound expected from 2027.

How people fish it. From a boat in open water, and through the ice in winter, when the lake fills with fish houses. North Dakota Tourism says safe ice usually arrives in mid-December and lasts to late March. No ice is ever safe by the calendar: ask at a bait shop before you walk out.

Keeping a fish. North Dakota’s statewide limits, with no special rules here: walleye 5, pike 5, white bass 30 and yellow perch 20 a day. Two poles in open water, four through the ice, each within 150 feet of you. For bait south of US Highway 2 you may use fathead minnows, creek chubs, brook sticklebacks and white suckers. Fish houses must be off the ice from midnight on 15 March unless you are in them.

Confirmed with North Dakota Game and Fish and North Dakota Parks and Recreation, 20 November 2026 — the 2026–28 Fishing Proclamation and Fishing Guide, the 2026 fishing waters outlook and the Grahams Island State Park page; ice season from North Dakota Tourism.

Boat ramps. Grahams Island State Park, about 10 miles west of the town of Devils Lake on Highway 19 and then 6 miles south, has a four-lane ramp, a bait shop, a fish-cleaning station and ice access. The department also lists ramps at Minnewaukan, Round Lake Bay, Schwab Landing, Creel Bay, Henegar Landing, the Spirit Lake Casino, East Bay, Estenson Landing and Black Tiger Bay, and warns that some ramps can become marginal or unusable as the lake level changes.

Nearest town: Devils Lake.

Lake Oahe — 230 miles of Missouri River, two states

The Corps of Engineers gives Lake Oahe more than 300,000 acres and more than 2,250 miles of shoreline, stretching 230 miles from Pierre, South Dakota, to Bismarck, North Dakota.

Lake Oahe in South Dakota, from Oahe Dam to Mobridge

What swims there. Walleye, northern pike, smallmouth bass, channel catfish and chinook salmon, which cannot breed in the lake and are stocked by South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks. Each October the state collects salmon eggs at its Whitlock Bay station west of Gettysburg and stocks about 250,000 young salmon the following year.

How people fish it. South Dakota Tourism describes walleye fished with bottom bouncers, jigs tipped with minnows and spinners with crawlers, at about 25 feet in spring and often deeper than 45 feet in summer; chinook trolled with downriggers or planer boards at 50 to more than 100 feet in spring and summer, and cast for from shore with spoons and crankbaits in autumn, mostly from the face of Oahe Dam north to the Cheyenne River. Game, Fish and Parks asks anglers who plan to release walleye to fish shallower than about 30 feet, because fish pulled up from deep water often die.

Keeping a fish. South Dakota’s Missouri River rules for Oahe: walleye, sauger and saugeye together 4 a day and 8 in possession, with no minimum length but no more than one fish 20 inches or longer a day; northern pike 6; bass 5; trout and salmon together 5; yellow perch, crappie and sunfish 15 each; white bass and catfish no limit (but only one flathead 30 inches or longer). Muskellunge are catch-and-release only. Culling walleye — swapping a kept fish for a bigger one — is banned. Salmon snagging is open from Oahe Dam to the North Dakota border from 1 October to 30 November, with one line and one hook.

Tribal waters. Much of Oahe’s west and north shore borders the Cheyenne River and Standing Rock reservations. South Dakota says a state licence is all a non-member needs on the water itself, but fishing from tribal land may need a tribal licence or access permit. The Cheyenne River Sioux Tribe Game, Fish and Parks Department (Eagle Butte, 605-964-7812) and Standing Rock Sioux Tribe Game and Fish set their own rules.

Invasive species. South Dakota lists Lake Oahe as infested with zebra mussels, Eurasian watermilfoil, curly-leaf pondweed and rudd. Drain plugs, livewell plugs and valves must be out whenever a boat is trailered, except at the ramp while launching or loading, and you may not carry bait or fish away in lake water.

Confirmed with South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 20 November 2026 — the 2026 South Dakota Fishing Handbook (licence, Missouri River limits, salmon snagging, lines, invasive species, tribal waters), the department’s Whitlock Bay, park entrance licence, lakeside use area and recreation area pages, and the Corps’ Oahe Dam page. Fishing methods and depths from South Dakota Tourism.

Boat ramps. South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks runs recreation areas with ramps at Oahe Downstream, five miles north of Fort Pierre below the dam (its campgrounds 1 and 2 close from 15 November to late March for the roosting bald eagles; day use and campground 3 stay open); Spring Creek and Okobojo Point, north-west of Pierre; West Whitlock, 18 miles west of Gettysburg; Swan Creek, west of Akaska; Indian Creek at Mobridge; and West Pollock. Lakeside use areas include West Shore at the dam, East Shore, Bob’s Landing and Bush’s Landing. A park entrance licence is needed for every vehicle at state recreation areas and at some lakeside use areas, including their ramps.

Nearest towns: Pierre and Fort Pierre at the dam; Gettysburg for Whitlock Bay; Mobridge at the north end of the South Dakota water.

Lake Oahe in North Dakota, from Bismarck to the state line

What swims there. The same fish. North Dakota Game and Fish’s 2026 outlook says small walleye are plentiful but thin because the bait fish are short, and that there are chances at trophy fish.

Keeping a fish. North Dakota’s statewide limits apply on this end (walleye 5 a day, no minimum length), and you need a North Dakota licence. Darkhouse spearing and bow fishing for walleye are allowed here from 15 November to 15 March.

Confirmed with North Dakota Game and Fish and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 20 November 2026 — the 2026–28 Fishing Proclamation and Fishing Guide, the 2026 outlook, the 2 November 2026 low-water notice and the Corps’ recreation status page (updated 20 November 2026).

Boat ramps. The department lists ramps at Hazelton, south-east of Bismarck; Fort Rice, south of Mandan; Beaver Bay and Jennerville, west of Linton; Cattail Bay and Langeliers Bay, west of Strasburg; Fort Yates; and the Prairie Knights Marina. On 20 November 2026 the Corps listed its ramps at Beaver Creek, Cattail Bay and Hazelton as open, with the campgrounds closed for the season. Low water is expected to make access hard in 2027.

Nearest towns: Bismarck, about 30 miles from the Hazelton ramp, and Linton for the Beaver Bay area. The Prairie Knights Casino and Resort at Fort Yates has a lodge beside its marina.

Lake McConaughy — Nebraska’s big lake, low this year

The Central Nebraska Public Power and Irrigation District, which owns Kingsley Dam, gives Lake McConaughy 30,500 surface acres when full and 22 miles of length, on the North Platte River north of Ogallala.

Lake McConaughy and Lake Ogallala, from Kingsley Dam to the west end

The water this year. On 20 November 2026 the district read the lake at 3,205.8 feet, about 452,600 acre-feet — 26 per cent of capacity, against a summer maximum of 3,265 feet. Nebraska Game and Parks announced that the last public boat ramp at Lake McConaughy State Recreation Area closed on 15 August 2026 because of the low water. Since then, getting a boat on the lake means a private launch service; in August the commission named Van’s Lakeview, Ski Patrol and Big Mac Marina, and warned boaters not to go west of Van’s Lakeview, where boats were sinking in the mud flats. Launching from a beach is at your own risk.

What swims there. Walleye, wipers, white bass, smallmouth bass, northern pike and channel catfish. Nebraska Game and Parks recommends McConaughy for big walleye and manages wipers as a low-density trophy fishery; its 2024 survey found walleye catches slightly above the 15-year average and naturally breeding channel catfish, some over 28 inches. Lake Ogallala, the 650-acre lake below Kingsley Dam, is fed cold water from the bottom of McConaughy and is stocked with rainbow trout and cutbows.

How people fish it. By boat, while the launch services can get you out; from the long sand beaches; and on Lake Ogallala from the bank or a small boat, at no-wake speed (5 mph).

Keeping a fish. Nebraska’s 2026 statewide rules: walleye, sauger and saugeye together 4 a day and 8 in possession, at least 15 inches, and only one 22 inches or longer; white bass, striped bass and wipers together 15 a day, only one 18 inches or longer; channel catfish 5 a day in a lake, only one 30 inches or longer; rainbow, tiger and cutbow trout 5 a day, only one 16 inches or longer; crappie and yellow perch 15 a day together. Two lines with two hooks each in open water; five lines through the ice. Special rules on McConaughy: from 1 April to 30 June you may not keep walleye, white bass or wipers in the Lonergan Creek and Otter Creek sections; in April, boats fishing at night near the dam must follow the posted one-way pattern. On all Game and Parks property, every drain plug must be pulled when you arrive at or leave the water.

Confirmed with Nebraska Game and Parks and the Central Nebraska Public Power and Irrigation District, 20 November 2026 — the 2026 Nebraska Fishing Guide, the commission’s permit, park entry and Lake McConaughy pages, its news of the ramp closure, its August 2026 McConaughy updates and its 2024 survey summary, and the district’s lake data for 20 November 2026 and its Lake McConaughy and Lake Ogallala pages.

Getting there and camping. Every vehicle in the state recreation area needs a Nebraska park entry permit. Game and Parks runs campgrounds at Martin Bay, Little Thunder, Lone Eagle, Sandy Beach, Cedar View, Spring Park and Otter Creek, beach camping on the north shore, and campgrounds on both sides of Lake Ogallala. Fish-cleaning stations are at Martin Bay, Cedar View and Otter Creek. The Lake McConaughy Visitor and Water Interpretive Center is a quarter of a mile south of Kingsley Dam on Highway 61.

Nearest towns: Ogallala, nine miles south of the dam; Brule, east of the lake; North Platte, the nearest small city. Keystone and Lemoyne sit on the north shore; our partner’s search could not place Keystone or Lemoyne reliably, so they have no button.

Milford Lake — the biggest lake in Kansas

The Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks calls Milford the state’s largest lake. Its state park page gives about 15,700 surface acres and 163 miles of shoreline; its fishing page gives 16,200 acres. It lies on the Republican River about five miles north-west of Junction City.

Milford Lake, from the dam to School Creek

What swims there. Walleye, white bass, wipers, crappie, blue, channel and flathead catfish, largemouth, smallmouth and spotted bass, and bluegill.

How people fish it. The department’s Milford fishing report describes catfish on cut bait over wind-blown flats and channel ledges; crappie at 10 to 20 feet near points and brush, on jigs and minnows; walleye along rocky, wind-swept banks and the dam, on jigs, crankbaits or bottom bouncers with worms; and white bass and wipers in deeper water on shad, spoons and swimbaits. Below the dam, the Corps’ Outlet Park has fishing piers.

Keeping a fish. Milford has its own walleye rule: walleye, sauger and saugeye together 2 a day, at least 21 inches. Blue catfish from 28 to 40 inches must go back, and only one 40 inches or longer may be kept (the daily blue catfish limit differed between the department’s pages when we checked — confirm before you keep any). Channel catfish 10 a day; flathead 5; wipers and striped bass together 5; crappie 50; black bass 5 together at 15 inches; white bass no limit. Two rods each, or three with a $6 three-pole permit.

Zebra mussels and algae. Zebra mussels have been in Milford since 2009. Kansas requires you to drain livewells and bilges and pull every drain plug before a boat goes on a public road, and you may not leave a water with zebra mussels carrying live fish. Kansas Department of Health and Environment watches lakes for toxic blue-green algae from 1 April to 31 October; Milford was not on its warning list of 30 October 2026, but look at the current list before you swim, let a dog in the water or eat what you catch in summer.

Confirmed with the Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks, the Kansas Department of Health and Environment and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 20 November 2026 — the department’s Milford Reservoir and Milford fishing report pages, its fishing regulations and licence pages, its special length and creel table, its Milford State Park page and its aquatic invasive species rules; KDHE’s algae release of 30 October 2026; the Corps’ Milford Lake page and its parks on recreation.gov.

Boat ramps. Milford State Park, 1,084 acres on the south-east shore, has three ramps with courtesy docks (the Walnut Grove ramp was closed with damaged lanes when we checked), a full-service marina, cabins, campgrounds and an accessible fishing dock with a kayak launch; a state park vehicle permit is needed there. The Corps of Engineers runs Curtis Creek, Farnum Creek, School Creek and West Rolling Hills parks, with boat ramps booked through recreation.gov, and Outlet Park below the dam.

Nearest towns: Junction City by the dam; Milford on the east shore; Manhattan, home of Kansas State University, half an hour east; Abilene to the west.

The licence and the limits, plainly

Four states, four licences. On Lake Oahe, fish with the licence of the state you are in.

North Dakota (licence year 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027)

Who needs one. Everyone 16 and older. Everyone also buys a Fishing, Hunting and Furbearer Certificate once a year: $2 for residents, $5 for non-residents.

For a visitor. Non-resident season $68; 10-day $58; 3-day $48; married couple season $108. There is no non-resident senior licence.

For a North Dakotan. Resident $27; married couple $42; 65 and older $10.

Boats from other states. A motor boat not licensed in North Dakota needs an aquatic nuisance species sticker each year, sold only on the department’s website. No live bait or water plants may be brought into the state.

Confirmed with North Dakota Game and Fish, 20 November 2026 — the department’s resident and non-resident licence pages and the 2026–28 Fishing Guide. Prices change. Buy from gf.nd.gov, the department’s app or a licence vendor.

South Dakota (licences valid to 31 January 2027)

Who needs one. Anyone 18 and older. Under-18s need no licence and may keep their own limits. Anyone 18 or older buying an annual or 3-day licence also buys a habitat stamp.

For a visitor. Non-resident annual $80; 3-day $45; 1-day $26; habitat stamp $25 (not needed with the 1-day licence).

For a South Dakotan. Annual (18–64) $31; 1-day $10; senior annual (65+) $17; habitat stamp $10.

Park entrance licence. Needed for every vehicle at state parks, recreation areas and some lakeside use areas, ramps included: $10 a day for South Dakota vehicles and $15 for others; annual $40 and $60. Agents may add up to $4 to a licence.

Confirmed with South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks, 20 November 2026 — the 2026 South Dakota Fishing Handbook and the department’s licence and park entrance pages. Prices change. Buy from gfp.sd.gov, the app or a licence agent.

Nebraska (2026)

Who needs one. Anyone 16 and older. A non-resident child under 16 is covered only when fishing with a permit holder.

For a visitor. Non-resident annual $95.50; 3-day $40; 1-day $12. A motor boat registered in another state needs a Nebraska aquatic invasive species stamp, $15 plus a $3 fee, fixed to both sides before launching.

For a Nebraskan. Resident annual $41; 3-day $33; 1-day $12. Resident permits include the Aquatic Habitat Stamp. All prices include a $3 issue fee.

Park entry. Every vehicle needs a park entry permit: daily $7.35 for Nebraska vehicles and $14.35 for others; annual $36 and $71.

Confirmed with Nebraska Game and Parks, 20 November 2026 — the commission’s permit pricing, fishing permit, AIS stamp and park entry pages and the 2026 Nebraska Fishing Guide. The price page does not label its two columns; we read them in order. Buy from outdoornebraska.gov or a permit vendor.

Kansas (2026)

Who needs one. Residents 16 through 74 and all non-residents 16 and older.

For a visitor. Non-resident annual $75; 5-day $25; 1-day $10.

For a Kansan. Resident annual $25; 1-day $3.50; 5-year $100; senior (65–74) annual $15. Three-pole permit $6. Agent and transaction fees are added to every purchase.

Milford State Park. A state park vehicle permit is required — the department’s fee page showed $5 a day or $25 a year, labelled with 2025 prices, when we checked.

Confirmed with the Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks, 20 November 2026 — the department’s fishing licence fee, licence requirement and state park fee pages. Buy online at gooutdoorskansas.gov or from a licence vendor.

Eating what you keep

Each state publishes its own advice on eating fish: North Dakota’s Department of Environmental Quality (“A Guide to Safe Eating of Fish Caught in North Dakota”), South Dakota’s Department of Health, Nebraska’s Department of Water, Energy, and Environment with the Department of Health and Human Services, and the Kansas Department of Health and Environment. We found no lake-specific advisory for Lake Oahe, Lake McConaughy, Lake Ogallala or Milford Lake on 20 November 2026; North Dakota’s guide sets meal advice by species and size for all its waters. Children and anyone pregnant should read their state’s advice before eating large predators such as walleye and pike.

Questions

Do I need a different licence for each state on Lake Oahe?

Yes. Lake Oahe runs from Pierre, South Dakota, to Bismarck, North Dakota, and you follow the licence and the rules of the state you are standing or floating in. A South Dakota licence covers the South Dakota water; a North Dakota licence covers the North Dakota water. South Dakota’s handbook also warns that holding more than one day’s limit on the water is banned in both states. Where the lake borders the Cheyenne River and Standing Rock reservations, South Dakota says the state licence is enough on the water, but fishing from tribal land may need a tribal licence or access permit from the tribe. Checked with South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks and North Dakota Game and Fish on 20 November 2026.

How many walleye can I keep?

It depends on the state. North Dakota: walleye, sauger and saugeye together 5 a day and 10 in possession, with no minimum length on Lake Sakakawea, Devils Lake or Lake Oahe. South Dakota on Lake Oahe: 4 a day and 8 in possession, no minimum length, and no more than one fish 20 inches or longer in the daily limit. Nebraska, including Lake McConaughy: 4 a day and 8 in possession, at least 15 inches, and only one 22 inches or longer. Kansas on Milford Lake: 2 a day, at least 21 inches. Checked with the four state agencies on 20 November 2026.

Can I launch a boat on Lake McConaughy right now?

Not from a public ramp. Nebraska Game and Parks announced that the last public boat ramp at Lake McConaughy State Recreation Area closed on 15 August 2026 because the water is so low; private launch services are the way onto the lake until the water comes back. On 20 November 2026 the Central Nebraska Public Power and Irrigation District read the lake at 3,205.8 feet, about 26 per cent of capacity. Call the park or a launch service before you tow a boat to Ogallala.

What does a fishing licence cost a visitor?

North Dakota (licence year 1 April to 31 March): non-resident season $68, 10-day $58, 3-day $48, plus a one-time $5 certificate each year. South Dakota: non-resident annual $80, 3-day $45, 1-day $26, and a $25 habitat stamp with the 3-day and annual licences. Nebraska: non-resident annual $95.50, 3-day $40, 1-day $12. Kansas: non-resident annual $75, 5-day $25, 1-day $10, plus agent and transaction fees. Children are covered differently in each state: under 16 in North Dakota, Nebraska and Kansas, and under 18 in South Dakota, need no licence. Checked with each state’s agency on 20 November 2026; prices change, so buy from the agency or its agents.

When can I snag salmon on Lake Oahe and Lake Sakakawea?

South Dakota allows salmon snagging on Lake Oahe from Oahe Dam north to the North Dakota border from 1 October to 30 November, with one line and one hook. North Dakota reinstated snagging, bow fishing and spearing for chinook salmon on Lake Sakakawea from Garrison Dam upstream to the Highway 23 bridge near New Town, sunup to sundown, 25 September to 31 October. Any other fish that is snagged must be released. Checked with both agencies on 20 November 2026.

Seven honest gaps

The Kansas regulations booklet. The Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks’ 2026 regulations booklet would not open for us. Milford’s rules above come from the department’s web pages and its special length and creel table; the blue catfish daily limit differed between them. Check the booklet or call the Milford State Park office (785-238-3014).

Best months. None of the four agencies publishes a month-by-month calendar for these lakes. The months table is our guidance and South Dakota Tourism’s, and says so.

Devils Lake’s size. The lake has changed size a great deal as its water rose; we found no current agency figure.

Zebra mussel rules on Lake Sakakawea. North Dakota found the mussels in October 2026 and says rules will be refined before the 2027 season. Whether the lake is now formally listed as infested was not clear when we checked; treat it as infested.

Ramps next year. Low water on all three Missouri River and North Platte lakes means ramps open and close with the level. We give what the agencies said on 20 November 2026; check again before every trip.

Towns with no button. Garrison (North Dakota), Pick City, Akaska, Keystone and Lemoyne are real lake towns, but our partner’s search sends some of their names elsewhere or shows no rooms in the town itself, so they have no booking button. Use the neighbouring towns.

Hotel lists in small towns. Our hotel buttons are pinned to town numbers checked two ways with our own resolver. We do not open Trip.com’s hotel-list pages to count the hotels, so small towns may show few.
