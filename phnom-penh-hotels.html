Where to Stay in Phnom Penh

Where to Stay in Phnom Penh — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Phnom Penh is a river city that rewards picking a quarter and staying in it. The riverside along Sisowath Quay puts the Royal Palace, the National Museum and the evening promenade at your door, and puts traffic there too. BKK1, fifteen minutes south, is the leafy, low-rise embassy and café district where most visitors are happiest. Tonle Bassac has the towers and the river views, and the Russian Market quarter has the best value in the city. One practical change worth knowing before you book a transfer: the capital’s flights moved to the new Techo International Airport, about 20 kilometres south of the centre, when it opened in September 2025.

Choosing Your Area in Phnom Penh

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

Riverside & Sisowath Quay

The postcard address, and the right one for a short first visit: the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, the National Museum, the Foreign Correspondents’ Club and a mile of riverfront that fills up pleasantly at dusk. It is also the busiest stretch of road in the city, so ask for a room off the quay, and expect a certain amount of pavement commerce. Walkable, flat, and full of places to eat.

BKK1 (Boeung Keng Kang)

The quarter the Guild recommends most often. Wide, tree-lined streets of villas and small hotels, the best coffee and restaurants in Cambodia, embassies and clinics on every other corner, and a fifteen-minute tuk-tuk to the palace. Quieter at night than the riverside, safer-feeling for an evening walk, and better value than the tower hotels for the same money.

Tonle Bassac & Koh Pich

South of BKK1 where the Bassac meets the Mekong: the city’s tall buildings, the international hotels with the long views, shopping malls and a big new convention quarter on the Diamond Island peninsula. The choice if you want a high floor, a proper gym and a pool with a skyline, and do not mind that the street life around you is thin.

Daun Penh & the centre

The old administrative heart behind the riverside: Wat Phnom on its mound, the Post Office square, Central Market under its ochre dome. A handful of restored colonial buildings have become good small hotels. You are close to everything and in the thick of the traffic; choose a side street rather than a boulevard.

Toul Tom Poung (the Russian Market)

A residential grid of workshops, cafes and guesthouses around the market that everybody still calls Russian. The best value in the city and the most everyday Phnom Penh you will see from a hotel window, at the cost of fifteen minutes to the palace and fewer pavements you would call smooth.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Phnom Penh trip goes.

Check which airport your ticket says

Techo International Airport opened in September 2025 about 20 kilometres south of the city and has taken over the capital’s commercial flights from the old Phnom Penh International, which sat much closer in. Allow more time than an older guidebook suggests, agree the transfer with your hotel in advance, and if a booking confirmation still names the old airport, query it rather than assume.

Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek

The former school used as the S-21 prison and the killing field at Choeung Ek are not sights in the ordinary sense; they are the reason a great many Cambodian families have gaps in them. Go, take the audio guide, allow a half day and plan nothing demanding afterwards. Dress as you would for a memorial, photograph sparingly, and keep your voice down. Most visitors find it the most important few hours of the trip.

Everyday practicalities

Phnom Penh is a US dollar city in daily practice, with Cambodian riel used as small change below a dollar; carry clean, unmarked notes, because torn or heavily worn dollars are often refused. Tuk-tuks booked through an app cost a fraction of a street negotiation. The one common nuisance is bag-snatching from a passing motorbike — walk with your bag on the inside shoulder, keep your phone out of your hand near the kerb, and it stops being a worry.

Phnom Penh Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Phnom Penh.

What is the best area to stay in Phnom Penh?

BKK1 (Boeung Keng Kang) for most visitors — leafy, quiet, full of good places to eat, and a short ride from everything. Stay on the riverside if this is a short first visit and you want the Royal Palace and the promenade on your doorstep, in Tonle Bassac if you want a tower with a view, and around the Russian Market if value matters more than polish.

How far is Techo airport from central Phnom Penh?

About 20 kilometres south of the centre. The airport opened in September 2025 and replaced the old Phnom Penh International for commercial flights, so transfer times in older guidebooks are too short. Book the transfer through your hotel and allow for city traffic, which is heaviest in the late afternoon.

How many nights do you need in Phnom Penh?

Two full days is the usual answer and it works: one for the Royal Palace, the Silver Pagoda, the National Museum and the riverside, and one for Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek with a quiet afternoon afterwards. A third day buys you the markets, a Mekong sunset cruise and time to sit in a café, which is a large part of what the city is for.

Is Phnom Penh safe for visitors?

In the ordinary tourist areas, yes, with the normal caution of any large city and one local habit worth adopting: bags and phones are occasionally snatched from passing motorbikes, so keep them away from the kerb. Traffic is the real hazard — crossing a boulevard means walking at a steady, predictable pace and letting the scooters flow around you. Check your own government’s current travel advice before you go.

Do Phnom Penh hotels take US dollars?

Yes. Hotels, restaurants and shops price in dollars and give change in riel below a dollar, and cards are widely accepted at the hotel level. Bring notes in good condition, since worn or torn dollars are often turned away, and withdraw from bank ATMs rather than street machines. Decline any terminal that offers to charge you in your own home currency.
