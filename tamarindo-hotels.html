Where to Stay in Tamarindo

Where to Stay in Tamarindo & Guanacaste — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Guanacaste is the sunniest, driest and easiest corner of Costa Rica, and the only part of the country with a real cluster of all-inclusive resorts. Tamarindo itself is a surf town grown into a lively beach town — walkable, full of restaurants, and genuinely loud on the central strip until the small hours. North of it the coast quietens down through Playa Flamingo, Playa Conchal and the resort peninsula of Papagayo, all within an hour of Liberia airport on good paved road. Below are the hotels worth knowing across that whole stretch, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Luxury

Costa Rica’s two finest resorts are both on the Papagayo peninsula, about half an hour from Liberia airport.

Four Seasons Resort Peninsula Papagayo Costa Rica

$$$$ LuxuryPapagayoTwo baysGolf35 min from LIR

This is the property that taught North America that Costa Rica could do proper luxury, and it still wears the crown with an ease that never tips into stuffiness. It straddles a narrow isthmus with a calm bay on one side and a surf-facing beach on the other, so you can choose your water each morning without moving the car. Service is warm rather than performed, the grounds are alive with howler monkeys and coatis, and it is the shortest airport transfer of any property on this page — a real gift after a travel day.

Boutique

Two long-established small hotels at the quiet southern end of Tamarindo — the answer for anyone who wants lovely but not a resort.

Capitan Suizo Beachfront Boutique Hotel

$$$ UpscaleQuiet end of TamarindoBeachfront gardens40 roomsOpen-air spa

Swiss-founded and family-run for decades, Capitán Suizo is what long-time Costa Rica hands recommend when somebody says ‘somewhere lovely but not a resort’. Forty rooms sit in deep tropical gardens that open directly onto the quiet end of Tamarindo beach, with the spa doing open-air treatments under the trees. It is far enough from the bar strip to sleep well and close enough to walk into town in fifteen or twenty minutes along the sand — a combination almost nothing else in Tamarindo manages.

Budget & Value

Two modest, friendly places in Tamarindo town that have been quietly out-performing their price for years.

Villas Macondo

$ BudgetTamarindo townTwo minutes to the beachTropical gardenPool

A modest, well-tended little compound on a side street two minutes’ walk from both the beach and the town’s restaurants. Family-friendly

One right in the middle of Tamarindo, one in a much sleepier village half an hour north.

Tamarindo Diria Beach Resort

$$ Mid-rangeMid-town TamarindoDirectly on the beachSeveral poolsSurf lessons at the desk

The Diria has anchored the middle of Tamarindo for generations and remains the most practical choice for a family that wants the beach on one side and the whole town on the other. Several pools, mature gardens, surf lessons arranged at the desk, and a beachfront position that puts grandchildren in the water in under a minute. Be honest with yourself about the location: this is the liveliest stretch of Tamarindo, so ask for a room in the garden wing away from the road if anyone in the party is a light sleeper.

Adults-only

Guanacaste is the strongest region in Costa Rica for adults-only — one big resort on a calm bay, one tiny hilltop retreat.

Secrets Papagayo Costa Rica

$$$ UpscalePlaya Arenilla18 and overAll-inclusive40 min from LIR

An eighteen-and-over, all-inclusive resort on a sheltered bay where the water is calm enough for a genuine swim rather than a wrestle with surf. Two pools — a main one with a swim-up bar and a quieter relaxation pool — plus a full spa, and the plan here does include premium spirits, tips and taxes, which is not universal in Costa Rica. Pet-friendly

Two very different hotels with written dog policies. Confirm your dog’s weight in writing before you book — the limits here are strict.

Perched on the hillside at the edge of town with wide ocean views from most rooms and an infinity pool with a swim-up bar, and it takes two dogs up to a moderate weight for a modest nightly fee. All-inclusive

Read this before you book anything. Guanacaste is the only part of Costa Rica with a real all-inclusive cluster, and the plans here are narrower than what you may know from Cancún or Punta Cana. Included almost everywhere: all meals in the buffet and à-la-carte restaurants, standard beer, wine and house cocktails, room service at most properties, kayaks and paddleboards, the children’s club, nightly entertainment, taxes and tips. Not included, and this is the part that surprises Americans: every excursion — zip-lines, volcano trips, catamaran days — typically running well over a hundred dollars a head; all spa treatments, without exception; golf green fees; scuba diving, sport fishing and motorised watersports; premium spirits at several properties; and airport transfers. Put simply: the food and drink are covered, the Costa Rica is not. Budget for the excursions as a real line item, because in this country the excursions are the point.

The Westin Reserva Conchal an All-Inclusive Golf Resort & Spa

$$$$ LuxuryPlaya ConchalNine restaurantsGolf courseShuttle to the beach

The largest and most complete all-inclusive in the region, set on a great swathe of protected dry forest above the crushed-shell sand of Playa Conchal. Nine restaurants, round-the-clock room service, a serious spa and a championship golf course mean you genuinely need not leave, and the Family Club section handles grandchildren well — though the children’s club is capped and fills fast in high season. A shuttle carries you down to the beach, so factor in that the sand is not quite at your door.

Noise, walkability and the drive from Liberia

Before you book

Walkable to both restaurants and sand: Tamarindo Diria, Villas Macondo and Hotel Luna Llena in town, and Capitán Suizo at the quiet end with a fifteen-minute beach walk into town. Walkable to the beach but not to a dinner scene: Cala Luna, Las Tortugas, El Mangroove and Margaritaville. Car or shuttle essential: Wyndham Tamarindo, Los Altos de Eros, the JW Marriott, all the Papagayo resorts and both Reserva Conchal hotels. On noise, the straight version: Tamarindo is a surf town that grew into a party town, and the central beach strip has open-air bars running loud music until two in the morning, worst from Thursday to Sunday in the December-to-April high season. If you want Tamarindo’s restaurants without its soundtrack, stay at Capitán Suizo, in Playa Langosta, or up the hill at the Wyndham. Playa Grande, Flamingo, Conchal and Papagayo are quiet at night, full stop. As for the drive: Papagayo is thirty to forty minutes from Liberia airport, Flamingo and Conchal about an hour, and Tamarindo about ninety minutes on well-paved highway with no river crossings — this is by a wide margin the easiest corner of Costa Rica to reach. Most travellers over fifty-five are happier with a private transfer than a hire car, and nobody should be driving these unlit roads after dark.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travellers ask most about staying in Tamarindo & Guanacaste.

Are there real all-inclusive resorts in Costa Rica?

Yes, but almost only in Guanacaste. The Westin Reserva Conchal, Planet Hollywood Costa Rica by Royalton, Secrets Papagayo, Occidental Papagayo and Dreams Las Mareas are genuine all-inclusives, and Margaritaville Playa Flamingo and El Mangroove sell optional plans. Elsewhere in the country — Manuel Antonio, Arenal, Monteverde — ‘all-inclusive’ on a booking site usually just means breakfast is included.

What does all-inclusive actually cover in Costa Rica?

Meals, standard drinks, non-motorised watersports, the children’s club, entertainment, taxes and tips. It does not cover excursions — zip-lines, volcano trips, catamaran days — which are the whole reason most people come, nor spa treatments, golf fees, diving, fishing or airport transfers. Budget a real sum for excursions on top of the resort price.

Is Tamarindo too noisy for a quiet holiday?

The central beach strip genuinely is, with open-air bars until two most nights in high season. The fix is simple: stay at the quiet southern end (Capitán Suizo), in Playa Langosta (Cala Luna), or up the hill at the Wyndham with its shuttle into town. Playa Grande, Flamingo, Conchal and Papagayo are quiet, and all are within an easy drive of Tamarindo’s restaurants.

How far is Tamarindo from Liberia airport?

About ninety minutes on good paved highway with only a handful of turns — no dirt sections and no river crossings. Papagayo is thirty to forty minutes and Flamingo and Conchal about an hour. Private transfers are widely available and are what we would suggest for most travellers; shared shuttles cost less and take longer.
