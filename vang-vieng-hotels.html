Where to Stay in Vang Vieng

Where to Stay in Vang Vieng — Town & Riverside

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Vang Vieng has had two lives. For about a decade it was the byword for everything that can go wrong with backpacker tourism — river bars, rope swings and a genuine death toll — and in 2012 the Lao authorities closed that down. What was left turned out to be one of the most spectacular landscapes in Southeast Asia: a green river running under a wall of limestone towers, caves in every direction, and balloons going up at first light. Stay in the town to walk to dinner, on the west bank to wake up facing the karsts, or upriver to hear nothing at all.

Choosing Your Area in Vang Vieng

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

The town grid

A compact few blocks of guest houses, restaurants, bakeries, tour agencies and minimarts, with the old airstrip down the middle. Not beautiful, but it is where the food, the tour bookings and the transport are, and almost everything in it is a short flat walk.

Best for a short stay, for a first visit, and for anyone who wants to book activities on arrival rather than in advance. Rooms on the river side of town get the view without giving up the walk.

The west bank of the Nam Song

Cross the river and the town falls away: bungalows and small lodges strung along the bank, with the full wall of karst directly in front of them and rice fields behind. This is the view people come for, and waking up to it is a different trip from waking up in the grid.

You will cross a bridge to get to dinner, which is a pleasant ten minutes in the dry and less pleasant in a downpour. Some of the smaller places are genuinely basic — read what is actually included.

Upriver and out of town

Further north along the Nam Song the valley opens and the resorts get larger and quieter, with pools, gardens and road access to the caves and lagoons.

Choose this for calm and comfort over convenience. You are committed to a tuk-tuk or a hired scooter for everything, and the town's restaurants are no longer a stroll away.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Vang Vieng trip goes.

The party years are over, and that is the good news

If your impression of Vang Vieng dates from a decade ago, it is out of date. The riverside bars that lined the tubing route were shut in 2012 after a series of deaths, and the town rebuilt itself around the landscape instead: ballooning, kayaking, caving, climbing, cycling and the swimming lagoons.

It now draws families and couples as readily as backpackers. Travellers who skipped it on reputation are usually the ones who wish they had stayed an extra night.

It is still a real river, and the caves are real caves

Tubing and kayaking continue in a managed form, and the Nam Song remains a river with current that rises fast in the wet season. Wear the life jacket, go with an operator rather than alone, and do not go in after heavy rain or after drinking.

Several of the caves involve wading, scrambling or water tubing in the dark. Take a torch of your own, ask what a trip actually involves before you pay, and be honest about what you are up for.

Getting here is easy now, and the listings can confuse you

Vang Vieng has a station on the Laos–China Railway and sits on the Vientiane–Boten expressway opened in 2020, so what used to be a long grind up Route 13 from the capital is now short. Book train seats ahead; the station is out of the town centre.

One booking quirk worth knowing: Vang Vieng is in Vientiane Province, which is a different thing from Vientiane Capital. Listings and maps sometimes show the province name, which can read as though a hotel is in the capital 150 kilometres away. Check the town name, not the province.

Vang Vieng Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Vang Vieng.

Is Vang Vieng still a party town?

No. The riverside bars were closed by the authorities in 2012 after a run of deaths on the river, and the town rebuilt around its landscape instead — balloons, kayaking, caving, climbing and the lagoons. There are bars and there is nightlife, as in any travellers' town, but the organised free-for-all on the water is gone and has been for well over a decade. Most visitors now are couples, families and older travellers.

Where is the best place to stay in Vang Vieng?

In town if you want to walk to dinner and book trips on the spot, which suits a one or two night stop. On the west bank of the Nam Song if the view is the point — you wake up facing the karsts, and it is worth the walk back over the bridge. Upriver if you want quiet and a pool and are happy to ride in for everything.

How many nights do you need in Vang Vieng?

Two. One full day covers a balloon at dawn, a lagoon in the afternoon and a cave in between, and a second lets you kayak or cycle without rushing. It is also a natural break on the journey between Vientiane and Luang Prabang, which is how most people fit it in.

When is the best time for the balloons and the views?

November to February: clear air, cool mornings and reliable flying. Avoid late March and early April if the view is your reason for coming — the agricultural burning haze across northern Laos can reduce the karsts to grey outlines for weeks at a time. The green season from June to October is lush and dramatic but flights are more often cancelled by weather.
