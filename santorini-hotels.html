Where to Stay in Santorini

Where to Stay in Santorini — Villages, Hotels & the Truth About the Steps

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Santorini offers two entirely different holidays, and choosing the wrong one is the single most common mistake visitors make. The caldera villages — Oia, Imerovigli, Firostefani and Fira — are built down the face of a volcanic cliff. The views are the ones you have seen all your life, and the price is stairs: hotels there are terraced, almost none have a lift, and you may climb sixty steps between your room and breakfast. The south and east coast — Kamari, Perissa and Perivolos — is flat, sits on black volcanic sand, costs a great deal less, and lets you walk from your room to a lounger without a single step. Many of the happiest travellers we know sleep on the flat coast and visit the caldera by taxi. Below are our picks by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

The steps — said plainly

Oia, Imerovigli, Firostefani and Fira are built down a cliff. Hotels are stacked in terraces and very few have a lift; several of the best-known caldera hotels state outright that they are unsuitable for guests with limited mobility. The old path from Fira’s harbour up to town is about 588 steep, uneven steps — take the cable car instead. If stairs are a worry, sleep at Kamari or Perissa, which are flat, and see the caldera on day trips.

Ask one question before you book

Whatever cliff hotel you choose, e-mail them and ask exactly how many steps there are from the road or car drop-off to your room, and whether a porter carries luggage. Good hotels answer this precisely and without fuss. A hotel that will not answer it clearly is telling you something.

Fly rather than sail, if you can

The flight from Athens is about forty-five minutes; the ferry is five to eight hours depending on the boat. If you must sail, choose the large conventional Blue Star ferry over a high-speed catamaran — it is far steadier in a swell, and the summer meltemi wind cancels fast ferries regularly in July and August. Santorini’s ferry port sits below the cliffs and is genuinely chaotic on arrival; pre-book a hotel transfer rather than queueing for a taxi with luggage.

Work around the cruise ships

Day visitors flood up the cable car roughly between half past nine and noon, fill Fira and Oia through the middle of the day, and queue to go back down between half past three and six. Cable car waits reach thirty to sixty minutes in those windows. See Oia before half past nine or after six, when it becomes a different and far lovelier place, and keep the middle of the day for lunch, your hotel pool or the flat south coast.

Santorini Hotels — common questions

Should I stay in Oia, Fira or on the beach in Santorini?

It depends almost entirely on how you feel about stairs. Oia and Imerovigli give the famous caldera views and the sunsets, but they are built down a cliff and almost no hotel there has a lift. Fira has the same views with shops, buses and a pharmacy within a fairly level walk. Kamari, Perissa and Perivolos on the south and east coast are flat, on black volcanic sand, considerably cheaper, and let you reach a lounger without a step. Many travellers get the best of both by sleeping on the flat coast and visiting the caldera by taxi or bus.

How bad are the steps in Santorini really?

Worse than the photographs suggest. The caldera villages are terraced down a volcanic cliff, and a single hotel can have sixty or more steps between the road and your room, with more between your room and the pool. Several of the best-known cliff hotels state plainly that they are unsuitable for guests with limited mobility. The old donkey path from Fira’s harbour up to town is roughly 588 steep and uneven steps, so take the cable car instead. Before booking any cliff hotel, ask them in writing how many steps there are from the drop-off point to your room.

Is it better to fly or take the ferry from Athens to Santorini?

Fly if you can. The flight is about forty-five minutes in the air; the ferry is five to eight hours at sea depending on the vessel. If you do sail, choose the large conventional Blue Star ferry rather than a high-speed catamaran — it is much steadier in a swell, and the summer meltemi wind cancels fast ferries regularly in July and August. Santorini’s ferry port sits below the cliffs and is chaotic on arrival, so pre-book a hotel transfer instead of queueing for a taxi with your luggage.

When do the cruise ships crowd Oia and Fira?

Day visitors come up the cable car mostly between about half past nine in the morning and noon, spend the middle of the day in Fira and Oia, and queue to go back down between roughly half past three and six for evening sailings. Cable car waits of thirty to sixty minutes are normal in those windows. See Oia before half past nine or after six, when the lanes empty and the village becomes a genuinely different place, and use the middle of the day for lunch, a swim or the flat south coast.

When is the best time to visit Santorini?

Late April to early June and mid-September to late October are the sweet spots: highs in the seventies and eighties Fahrenheit, everything open, and crowds well below peak. July and August run into the nineties at close to full occupancy, with packed lanes and meltemi winds that cancel ferries. For anyone who finds heat and crowds hard work, late September and early October are the best two weeks of the year here — the sea is still warm, the light is beautiful and the steps feel half as steep.

How many days do you need in Santorini?

Three nights is enough to see the island properly: one day for Oia and the caldera villages, one for the beaches or a boat trip to the volcano and hot springs, and one for the Akrotiri excavations and a winery or two. Four or five nights lets you slow down and enjoy your hotel, which on this island is much of the point. Most travellers pair Santorini with Athens at one end of the trip and perhaps one more island at the other.
