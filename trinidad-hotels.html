Where to Stay in Trinidad

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

A sugar town that got rich, then stopped. Trinidad’s cobbled streets and pastel houses have barely changed since the 1850s, which is exactly why UNESCO listed it and exactly why walking it takes care. The choice here is between sleeping inside the picture and sleeping somewhere your ankles will thank you for.

American readers: U.S. law does not permit tourism to Cuba, and travel must fall within an OFAC licence category. Under the widely used Support for the Cuban People category you are expected to stay in privately owned casas particulares rather than state hotels. The State Department’s Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List separately bars U.S. persons from lodging at, paying for lodging at, or booking a listed property, wherever the booking is made — and it covers many of the best-known hotels. The neighbourhoods, one by one

Trinidad is small — the historic core is a few hundred yards across — but it sits on a slope, and the streets in the middle are laid with river cobbles the size of grapefruit. Where you sleep decides how much of that you cross each day.

The historic centrePLAZA MAYOR

The postcard: cobbled streets, one-storey pastel houses with iron window grilles, and the Plaza Mayor with its bell tower.

  • Atmosphere Complete and genuine
  • Everything on your doorstep Music, museums, restaurants
  • The cobbles Large, uneven, and on a slope
  • Noise until late The music runs past midnight
The streets just below the centreTHE EASY EDGE

A few blocks downhill and outward, where the paving turns to normal road, the houses are larger and the casas often have a garage.

  • Level, walkable streets Much kinder underfoot
  • Five minutes to the Plaza Uphill, but short
  • Quiet at night Music audible, not overhead
  • Postcard charm You walk into it
Playa Ancón & La BocaTHE COAST

Twenty-five minutes south to a genuine white-sand beach, with the fishing village of La Boca on the way.

  • The beach Good sand, calm water
  • Casas at La Boca Cheap, simple, by the water
  • Evening life Almost none — town is a taxi ride
  • Hotels at Ancón State-owned; check the U.S. list
Valle de los IngeniosTHE SUGAR VALLEY

The UNESCO-listed sugar-mill valley just inland, with a handful of rural casas among the cane.

  • Quiet and green Very
  • A day out Tower, ruins and the old steam train route
  • As a base You will need a driver

What kind of stay suits you

Casas particulares are the strength here

Trinidad has one of the highest concentrations of private guest houses in Cuba, many of them in colonial houses with interior courtyards, high beamed ceilings and a roof terrace. The best of them are lovely and cost a fraction of a hotel. As everywhere in Cuba, and particularly for American travellers under Support for the Cuban People, this is the stay to choose.

If the cobbles worry you, say so

This is the practical heart of the matter. The stones in the historic centre are large, rounded and uneven, and the streets slope. If you or anyone with you is unsteady, has a stick, or simply values a flat surface, book two or three blocks below the centre rather than on the Plaza. You will be five minutes’ walk from everything and considerably more comfortable.

Ask for a roof terrace

Trinidad is hot and still, and the town’s pleasure at the end of the day is a roof terrace looking over the tiles to the Escambray mountains. Many casas have one. It is the difference between a good stay and a memorable one, and it costs nothing extra.

State hotels and the Ancón resorts

The beach hotels at Playa Ancón and one property in the hills are state-owned. American travellers must check the Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List before considering any of them — a Trinidad-area property was among those added in the July 2025 update — and the international chains that used to manage Cuban hotels wound up their operations in mid-2026.

Getting about, and what is genuinely walkable

On foot

The historic centre is walkable in the sense that it is small — you can cross it in ten minutes — but the surface is the thing nobody warns you about. The streets are laid with rounded river stones, set unevenly, on a gradient. Wear proper shoes with a sole, look down rather than up while you are moving, and do your sightseeing in the morning and the evening rather than in the flat heat of two o’clock.

A few blocks out, the paving becomes ordinary road and walking is easy. This is why we suggest the lower streets for anyone who is not confident on their feet: you get the same town, five minutes further off.

The walk up to the Ermita ruined church for the view is short but steep. Worth it at sunset; take it slowly.

Taxis, and when to take one

Getting here. From Havana, four to five hours by tourist coach or shared colectivo taxi. From Cienfuegos it is only ninety minutes, which is why the two towns pair so naturally. Your casa will arrange the onward car with a phone call.

To the beach. Playa Ancón is twenty to twenty-five minutes by taxi; agree the fare and the pick-up time before you set off, because there is very little at the beach and even less passing traffic. A bicycle-taxi to the edge of town and a taxi from there is the local way, and cheaper.

Around town, nothing needs a vehicle except the beach and the valley. For the Valle de los Ingenios and the Escambray, hire a car with a driver for the day — the roads are poor and the fuel situation makes self-drive a gamble.

Things worth doing

Trinidad is a town for evenings. Do your walking early, sleep through the afternoon, and be out again at six.

The steps at the Casa de la Música

The great stone staircase beside the Plaza Mayor fills up after dark and a band plays to the whole square. Free, entirely unforced, and the best hour in town. Arrive before nine for a seat on the steps.

The Plaza Mayor in the early morning

Before eight the square is empty, the light is low and gold, and you can hear the town waking up. This is the photograph everyone else is queuing for at eleven.

Climb the bell tower

The old convent tower above the square gives the view of the tiled roofs and the mountains behind. The stairs are steep and wooden; take them one at a time.

Playa Ancón for a day

Real white sand and calm, shallow water on a peninsula south of town. Take water, take cash and take your own shade — facilities are thin.

The Valle de los Ingenios

The sugar valley inland, with the Manaca Iznaga tower you can climb and the ruins of the mills. A half-day with a driver, and a genuinely affecting piece of history.

A paladar dinner in a courtyard

Trinidad’s private restaurants often occupy the courtyards of colonial houses. Book in the morning for the evening; the good ones fill.

Practicalities

Power, water and the everyday

Power. Outages are common and can be long — Trinidad is not on the priority grid. Bring a torch; the cobbled streets are genuinely dark and genuinely uneven when the lights go out, and this is the one place in this chapter where we would ask you to be careful about walking after a blackout.

Heat. Trinidad sits in a bowl and holds the heat. The afternoons in summer are fierce and airless. Plan around them.

Water. Bottled or filtered only. Most casas keep a tank, so an interruption rarely reaches the guest.

Money, food and asking for help

Money. Cash. Since 6 June 2026 Visa and Mastercard have not been processed anywhere in Cuba, for any nationality, and the bank queues in Trinidad are long. Arrive with what you need.

Eating. Better than its size suggests, thanks to the private paladares. Book ahead in the evening, and if a place offers you lobster at a suspiciously low price, ask the question you are already thinking.

Hustling. More persistent here than in Havana, because the town is small and the visitors are concentrated. It is friendly rather than threatening; a clear, cheerful “no, gracias” and continuing to walk is all that is needed.

Trinidad — FAQ

Should I stay inside the historic centre?

If you are sure on your feet and want the atmosphere, yes — it is one of the loveliest places to wake up in the Caribbean. If uneven ground is a concern, or if late music under your window would spoil your sleep, book two or three blocks below the centre instead. You will be five minutes’ walk from the Plaza Mayor, on level paving, and considerably more comfortable.

How bad are the cobbles, really?

Worse than photographs suggest. They are large rounded river stones set unevenly, on a slope, and they are the single most common complaint we hear about Trinidad. They are not a reason to skip the town — they are a reason to wear proper shoes, walk in daylight where you can, and think carefully about which street you sleep on.

How many nights in Trinidad?

Two or three. Two gives you a morning in the town, a beach afternoon and two evenings of music. Three lets you add the Valle de los Ingenios or a day in the Escambray. Trinidad pairs beautifully with Cienfuegos ninety minutes away, and many people do both in the same stretch.

Is the beach worth it?

Playa Ancón is a genuine Caribbean beach — white sand, calm shallow water, twenty-five minutes from town. It is not Varadero and does not pretend to be, and facilities are basic. As a half-day from a Trinidad base it is excellent; as a place to stay, only if the beach is the whole point of the trip.

What about the state hotels?

The Playa Ancón hotels and one property in the hills above town are state-owned. American travellers must check the Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List first — a Trinidad-area property was among those added in the July 2025 update — and note that Meliá, Iberostar and Barceló all wound up their Cuban operations in mid-2026. For every reader, the casas here are better value and better company.
