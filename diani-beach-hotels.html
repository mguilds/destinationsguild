Diani Beach — White Sand, a Reef Offshore and Colobus Overhead

Where to Stay at Diani Beach — Area by Area

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Diani is the stretch of sand most Kenyans will name if you ask them where to go for a week by the sea: about ten kilometres of white beach in Kwale County, south of Mombasa, backed by coastal forest with colobus monkeys in it. It is the south coast's main resort and it has rooms at every level from simple guest houses to the large beachfront hotels.

Two things decide a good Diani booking: how you intend to arrive, and which end of a long beach you end up on. This page is about both, because the brochures treat the whole strip as one place and it is not.

The northern end — nearest the airstrip and the main road

The top of the strip, closest to Ukunda and the turn-off from the main coast road, holds several of the bigger hotels and the shortest transfer from the airstrip. If you are arriving with children, or late, or with more luggage than you want to think about, this is the easiest landing. The beach here is good and slightly busier than further south.

Diani centre — the only part you can really walk

The middle of the strip, around the main beach road, is where the shops, the dive outfits and most of the independent restaurants are. It is the one stretch where you can leave the hotel on foot in the evening and have a choice of somewhere to eat, which over a week matters more than most people expect. It is also the liveliest, with the noise that implies.

Galu and Kinondo — the quiet southern end

South of the centre the development thins out and the beach gets wider and emptier. Galu and Kinondo are where to go if the point of the holiday is to read a book and swim, and they have some of the better small hotels and villas on the coast. You will need a car or a tuk-tuk for every dinner you do not eat where you are staying. That is the whole trade: quieter beach, less choice at eight in the evening.

Ukunda village — inland, cheaper, not the beach

Ukunda sits inland at the junction, a working Kenyan town with the airstrip beside it. Rooms here are considerably cheaper and you are a short ride from the sand rather than on it. For a long stay on a budget it is a reasonable base; for a week by the sea it is a false economy.

Before you book

Government travel insurance is now required

Since 5 October 2026 most visitors staying under twelve months must hold Kenyan government travel health insurance, bought with the electronic travel authorisation or afterwards through the eCitizen insurance portal. A policy from home does not count. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements, still current at 21 November 2026.

Arriving: fly if you can

Ukunda airstrip puts you minutes from the beach and avoids the Likoni ferry, which official advice names among the higher-risk places around Mombasa. If you are driving, ask about the inland route.

Malaria: take it seriously here

This is a low-lying tropical coast and the malaria risk is real all year. Get travel health advice at least eight weeks ahead and finish the course you are given.

Tides decide your swimming

Behind a reef, the water is calm and the sea retreats a long way twice a day. Look up the tide times for your week; it changes when you will want to be in the water.

Dress for the coast away from the beach

The coast is mainly Muslim and official advice asks visitors to dress conservatively away from the resorts. Beachwear on the beach, something more covered for Ukunda and the villages.

An Ebola case was confirmed in October

Kenya's Ministry of Health confirmed the country's first imported case of Ebola on 6 October 2026 and public health measures remain in place. Check your own government's travel health advice before you fly.

Questions readers actually ask

How do I get to Diani without the ferry?


Fly. Ukunda airstrip, usually sold as Diani Airport, takes scheduled flights from Nairobi's Wilson Airport and from Mombasa and is ten or fifteen minutes from most of the beach. That avoids the Likoni ferry, which official advice names among the higher-risk spots around Mombasa. If you do drive, there is also an inland route that bypasses the crossing; ask your hotel to arrange it rather than assuming the ferry is the only way.

Which end of Diani should I stay at?


The middle if you want to walk to restaurants and shops, the southern end at Galu or Kinondo if you want quiet and a wider, emptier beach. The north end is closest to Ukunda and the turn-off and has some of the larger hotels. Diani is a long strip and 'Diani Beach' on a booking page can mean anywhere along ten kilometres of it, so check which end before you book.

Is the swimming good, or does the reef get in the way?


Both are true. The reef sits offshore, which keeps the water calm and clear and makes it one of the safer stretches of coast for weak swimmers and children. It also means the tide goes out a long way and at low water you may be walking across sand and seagrass rather than swimming. Most hotels have a pool for exactly that reason.

Are the monkeys a problem?


The colobus and vervet monkeys are one of the pleasures of Diani and they are also entirely capable of taking fruit off your balcony table. Close the doors when you go out. There are local conservation groups working on the colobus population and the road crossings, and it is worth asking your hotel about them.

What is there to do besides the beach?


Boats out to the Kisite-Mpunguti marine area from Shimoni and Wasini island to the south, which is the best snorkelling on this coast; the Shimba Hills inland, which is close enough for a morning; and dhow trips and diving along the reef. Kenya's wildlife parks are a long way from here, so treat Diani as the resting half of a trip rather than the whole of it.
