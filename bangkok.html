Bangkok Travel Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

A city of gilded temples and roadside woks, where longtail boats thread the canals and the Skytrain glides over a river that has always been the point of the place.

Find Bangkok Hotels Search Flights to BKK

Bangkok is a city that refuses to be seen all at once. It grew up on the Chao Phraya, the broad brown river that still cuts through its heart, and for a long time it moved on water — a lattice of canals, the khlongs, earned it the old flattery of "the Venice of the East." That city is still there, half-hidden, if you take a boat. But laid over it now is another Bangkok entirely: a vertical, air-conditioned capital of glass towers, elevated railways and shopping malls the size of small towns. The genius of the place is that neither cancels the other. You can stand in the hushed, gold-leafed courtyards of the Grand Palace in the morning, eat the best meal of your trip from a plastic stool on a Chinatown pavement at dusk, and finish the night on a rooftop bar sixty floors up. It is hot, loud, devout and endlessly hospitable, and it rewards travellers who let it come at them sideways.

Orient yourself around the river and the trains and the city shrinks to a manageable size. The old royal quarter, Rattanakosin, sits on the east bank inside a loop of the Chao Phraya: this is where the Grand Palace, Wat Pho and the temples are, along with the backpacker legend of Khao San Road. Downtown Bangkok — Sukhumvit, Silom and Siam — lies to the east and south, strung along the BTS Skytrain and MRT metro lines, and that is where the modern hotels, malls and nightlife cluster. The river ties it all together, and the express boats are as much a highlight as anything they carry you to.

One honest word before you go, because it trips up first-timers: the temples enforce a modest dress code, and the Grand Palace is strict about it — shoulders and knees covered, for men and women alike. Bring a light layer or a scarf and you'll sail through. And treat with deep suspicion any friendly stranger near the palace gates who tells you "the Grand Palace is closed today — but I know a special temple, take my tuk-tuk." It isn't closed; it's the oldest scam in the city, and the tuk-tuk will detour you through a gem shop and a tailor before it ever nears a temple.

Bangkok remains one of the great-value big cities: a bowl of noodles from a street stall costs a dollar or two, a taxi across town not much more, and even the smart rooftop bars charge a fraction of what their equivalents would in London or New York. Carry some cash — the stalls and markets that make the city are cash affairs — and note that Thailand keeps Indochina Time (UTC+7) year-round, with no daylight saving to juggle.

Why visit Bangkok

Four reasons the Thai capital gets under your skin.

The great temples & the Grand Palace

Gold spires, mirrored mosaic and the Emerald Buddha in its royal chapel. Bangkok's temples are among the most dazzling sacred spaces anywhere on earth.

The street-food capital

No city on the planet eats on the street like Bangkok. Boat noodles, grilled skewers, mango sticky rice, curries ladled from a wok — dinner costs a dollar and rivals the restaurants.

River & canal life

The Chao Phraya and its khlongs are the old city's arteries. A longtail boat down a back canal reveals a slower, stilted Bangkok the towers never touched.

Markets, malls & nightlife

From the vast maze of Chatuchak to gleaming mega-malls and rooftop bars in the clouds, Bangkok shops and stays up later, and with more style, than almost anywhere.

Top things to do in Bangkok

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew

The former royal seat and the temple of the Emerald Buddha — Thailand's most revered image, carved from a single block of jade-green stone. Go early, and dress modestly.

● Wat Pho

Just south of the palace, home to the colossal gilded Reclining Buddha, 46 metres of it, and the country's most famous school of Thai massage.

● Wat Arun

The Temple of Dawn, its porcelain-studded spire rising over the west bank. Cross by ferry and climb the steep steps; it is loveliest at sunset from across the water.

● Chatuchak Weekend Market

Thousands of stalls under one sprawling roof — clothes, antiques, plants, street food and more. A weekend institution best tackled with a plan and cold water.

● River boat & canal tour

Ride the Chao Phraya express boat past the temples, then hire a longtail to weave into the Thonburi khlongs and the older, waterborne city behind them.

● Rooftop bars & Khao San Road

Two versions of a Bangkok night: a cocktail high above the skyline, or the neon, buckets and buzz of backpacker Khao San. Many travellers do both.

Where to stay in Bangkok

Pick your neighbourhood by the trip you want. Sukhumvit is the modern, cosmopolitan choice — restaurants, bars and nightlife strung along the BTS Skytrain, which makes the rest of the city easy to reach. Silom and the Riverside is business-district by day and comes alive after dark, and it holds the grand old riverside hotels with their legendary service and sunset views over the Chao Phraya. Rattanakosin, the Old City, puts you within walking distance of the Grand Palace, Wat Pho and Khao San Road, in the atmospheric heart of the historic quarter, though it's quieter and less connected to the trains. Siam sits dead centre, wrapped around the great shopping malls and a hub of the Skytrain — unbeatable for convenience and shopping. First-timers who want the temples on their doorstep should look at Rattanakosin; everyone else usually does best on the Skytrain in Sukhumvit or Siam.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

The cool-dry season, November to February, is the best time: bright, warm days and the lowest humidity of the year. March to May is very hot, peaking around the Songkran water festival in mid-April, when the whole city erupts into a good-natured water fight. June to October is the rainy season — expect short, heavy afternoon downpours that clear as quickly as they come, and lower rates.

Getting there & around

Suvarnabhumi (BKK) is the main international gateway, with the Airport Rail Link running straight into town; Don Mueang (DMK) handles most budget and regional flights, so check which one your ticket uses. Once you're in the city, the BTS Skytrain, the MRT metro and the Chao Phraya express boats beat the traffic every time.

Bangkok travel FAQ

What is the best time to visit Bangkok?

The cool-dry season from November to February is the best time: warm, bright days and lower humidity. March to May is very hot, peaking around the Songkran water festival in mid-April, and June to October is the rainy season, with short, heavy afternoon downpours that clear as fast as they arrive.

How many days do you need in Bangkok?

Three or four days is enough to see the great temples and the Grand Palace, cruise the Chao Phraya, eat your way through the street-food stalls, and shop the markets. Add a day or two if you want to slow down or take a trip out to Ayutthaya or a floating market.

How do you get around Bangkok?

The BTS Skytrain and the MRT metro are fast, cheap and air-conditioned, and they skip the notorious traffic entirely. The Chao Phraya express boats run up and down the river past most of the big sights. For everything else there are taxis (insist on the meter) and ride-hailing apps — but at rush hour the trains and boats will always beat the road.

Which airport do you fly into for Bangkok?

Bangkok has two airports. Suvarnabhumi (BKK) is the main international gateway, with the Airport Rail Link running straight into the city. Don Mueang (DMK) is the older airport that now handles most budget and regional flights. Check which one your ticket uses — they are on opposite sides of the city.
