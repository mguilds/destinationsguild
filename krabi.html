Krabi Travel Guide

Where towers of jungle-topped limestone rise straight out of a turquoise sea — the climbers' cliffs of Railay, hidden lagoons, and longtail boats to a hundred islands.

Find Krabi Hotels Search Flights to KBV

Krabi is the Andaman Coast at its most dramatic. Down the west side of southern Thailand, where the mainland breaks apart into a scatter of islands, great blocks of limestone erupt from the water — sheer, undercut, hung with jungle and cave — and between them lie the beaches and hidden lagoons that made this coast famous. Railay is the postcard: a cliff-locked peninsula reachable only by boat, where rock climbers cling to the overhangs while longtails idle in the shallows below. But Krabi is more than one beach. It's the Four Islands strung out beyond Ao Nang, the lagoon at the heart of the Hong Islands, the day boats running west to Phi Phi, and, inland, a jungle temple with more than a thousand steps to a hilltop shrine. Quieter and greener than Phuket across the bay, Krabi is where a lot of travellers wish they'd based themselves all along.

Think of Krabi as a base and a set of spokes. Ao Nang is the hub: a beachfront strip of hotels, restaurants and dive shops with a boat pier at one end where the longtails and speedboats leave for everywhere else. Krabi Town, about twenty minutes inland on the river, is the workaday provincial capital — cheaper, more local, with a good night market and few tourists staying overnight. And Railay, just around the headland from Ao Nang, is the showpiece: four beaches wrapped in vertical cliffs, cut off from the mainland by the karst, and reachable only from the water.

One thing to plan around, because it shapes the whole trip: the sea sets the schedule here. Krabi's greatest days out are all on the water — the Four Islands, the Hong Islands lagoon, the run to Phi Phi — and in the dry months they're glorious. In the monsoon, the Andaman turns rough, tours get cancelled at short notice, and some island beaches close. Come in the right season and Krabi rarely disappoints; come at the wrong one and you may spend more time watching the rain than the reefs.

Krabi is easy on the wallet by Andaman standards — cheaper than Phuket, with street food, guesthouses and longtail rides that cost a fraction of what the same trip would run at a big resort island. Cash still rules for markets, boats and small vendors, so keep baht on hand. Thailand keeps a single time zone (UTC+7) year-round, with no daylight saving to trip you up.

Why visit Krabi

Four reasons the Andaman Coast is worth the flight.

Dramatic limestone karsts

Towers of jungle-topped rock rise straight from the sea and river. The scenery alone — Railay, the Hongs, Phi Phi — is reason enough to come.

Railay's climbing & longtails

World-famous rock climbing on the cliffs, and the picture-book beaches below, reached by wooden longtail boat.

A greener alternative to Phuket

Quieter, cheaper and less built-up than its famous neighbour across the bay — the same Andaman sea, at a slower pulse.

World-class island-hopping

The Four Islands, the Hong lagoon and a day trip to Phi Phi are all on the doorstep, and among the best in Thailand.

Top things to do in Krabi

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Railay Beach

Take a longtail from Ao Nang to the cliff-locked peninsula — watch the climbers, wade the shallows, and stay for sunset on West Railay.

● The Four Islands tour

The classic Krabi day out: Chicken Island, Tup, Poda and Phra Nang, with snorkelling stops and a sandbar you can walk between islands at low tide.

● A Phi Phi day trip

Speedboats run west to Maya Bay, Pileh Lagoon and the snorkelling reefs of the Phi Phi Islands — go early to beat the crowds.

● The Hong Islands lagoon

A ring of karst enclosing a shallow emerald lagoon you paddle or motor into — one of the most photographed spots on the coast.

● Tiger Cave Temple

Wat Tham Suea, inland from Krabi Town: climb the 1,200-plus steps to the hilltop shrine for a giant Buddha and a sweeping view over the plain.

● Emerald Pool & Hot Springs

Inland at Khlong Thom, a spring-fed jungle pool the colour of jade, paired with warm mineral cascades in the rainforest.

Where to stay in Krabi

Choose by the trip you want. Ao Nang is the main hub — a walkable beachfront of hotels, restaurants and dive shops, with the boat pier at one end for every island run; it suits most first-timers. Railay is the dramatic choice: four beaches sealed off by cliffs and reached only by boat, quieter after the day trippers leave, but with no cars, no roads and everything ferried in. Krabi Town is the local, cheap option — riverside, with a lively night market and few other tourists staying over. Koh Lanta is a long, laid-back island to the south for travellers who want to slow right down. And Tup Kaek / Klong Muang, north of Ao Nang, hold the quiet upscale beaches — calm, low-key resorts away from the crowds. Match the base to your pace, and let the boats do the rest.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

November–April is the dry season and the best time to come: calm seas, sunny days and reliable island-hopping. May–October is the southwest monsoon — frequent rain and a rough Andaman Sea, with boat tours cancelled at short notice and some island beaches closed. Low-season rates drop sharply if you'll gamble on the weather.

Getting there

Krabi International (KBV) is about 30 minutes from Ao Nang, roughly a 1.5-hour flight from Bangkok with frequent daily service. You can also arrive by ferry from Phuket or the Phi Phi Islands. Railay is boat-only — take a longtail from Ao Nang or Krabi Town for the final leg.

Krabi travel FAQ

Krabi or Phuket — which should I choose?

Choose Krabi if you want quieter, greener scenery and easy access to Railay's cliffs and the best island-hopping; choose Phuket if you want a bigger range of hotels, nightlife and direct international flights. Many travellers do both, since Krabi and Phuket are only a couple of hours apart by road or ferry and share the same Andaman islands in between.

How do you get to Railay Beach?

By boat only. Railay is a peninsula walled off by limestone cliffs, so there are no roads in. You take a longtail boat — about 10 to 15 minutes from Ao Nang, or a slightly longer ride from Krabi Town — and step off into the shallows onto the beach. Boats run through the day but wind down after dark.

When is the best time to visit Krabi?

November to April is the dry season and the best time — calm seas, sunny days and reliable island-hopping. May to October is the southwest monsoon, when rain is frequent and the Andaman Sea turns rough, so some boat tours are cancelled and a few islands close. Prices in the low season drop sharply.

Which airport do you fly into for Krabi?

Krabi International Airport (KBV), about 30 minutes from the resort strip at Ao Nang. It has frequent domestic flights from Bangkok — roughly a 1.5-hour hop — plus some regional international routes. You can also reach Krabi by ferry from Phuket or the Phi Phi Islands.
