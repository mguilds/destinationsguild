Uruguay — upside-down seasons and a two-month coast

Best Time to Visit Uruguay — Month by Month

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Uruguay has one climate story rather than several, which makes this an unusually simple country to time. It is southern-hemisphere temperate: summer runs December to February, winter June to August, and rain falls steadily all year with no dry season to chase. What actually decides your month is not the weather but the coast’s very short season — the Atlantic resorts live for about ten weeks and close for the rest. Below is the month-by-month picture for Montevideo, what each region does across the year, and the handful of dates that fill every bed in the country.

The short answer

Go in March, or between late October and mid December. Those are the weeks when the water is warm enough to swim, the weather is kind, and you are not paying January prices or sharing the beach with half of Buenos Aires. January and February are the hottest and liveliest months and the most expensive by a wide margin. June to August is cold, grey and wet on the coast; Montevideo carries on happily, and most of Punta del Este closes.

Uruguay is in the southern hemisphere, so everything is inverted from a North American or European calendar. The country is also small and low — nothing in it is high enough for altitude to matter — so there is one climate story rather than several, which is unusual on this continent and makes the planning simple.

Rain falls fairly evenly through the year in Uruguay — about 46 inches, or 1,165 mm, in Montevideo — with the heaviest stretch in autumn and the peak in April. There is no dry season to aim for, which is why temperature and crowds, not rainfall, should decide your month.

By region

Montevideo

Works in every month of the year, which is the main argument for it. Summer is hot but the Rambla and the city beaches take the edge off; winter is cool and damp and the museums, markets and grill rooms are all indoors. Spring and autumn are the pleasantest for walking the old quarter.

Punta del Este and the Atlantic coast

A short, intense season. It is effectively December 20 to the end of February, with March as a warm, emptying coda. Outside that window a good deal of the resort is closed — restaurants, beach clubs and some hotels — and that is not a failure of the place but how it is built. Come in January for the scene; come in March or November for the beach.

Colonia del Sacramento

Year-round, and better out of season than in it. The old quarter is at its best in spring and autumn light and when the day-trip ferries from Buenos Aires are not landing three at a time. Winter is quiet and some of it shuts, but the cobbles and the river do not.

The thermal north and the interior

The hot springs at Salto and Paysandú are a winter holiday, which is the point of them: the colder the air, the better the water. The interior is hot and shadeless in January and at its best in spring.

Dates that fill the country

Uruguay is small and its holiday coast is smaller, so a handful of dates move every price on it. Book months ahead for any of these, or avoid them deliberately.

  • The second half of January. The single busiest stretch of the Uruguayan year on the Atlantic coast, with Argentine and Brazilian holidaymakers in residence. Punta del Este is a different town.
  • New Year. The turn of the year on the coast is booked long in advance and often sold in multi-night minimums.
  • Carnival. Uruguay runs about forty days of it from late January, and the Montevideo parade nights and the drumming of Las Llamadas fill the capital’s hotels on specific evenings rather than for the whole season.
  • Semana de Turismo, the Easter week, which in Uruguay is a secular national holiday week. Domestic travel peaks, the coast refills briefly and the interior estancias fill with Uruguayan families.
  • Any long weekend between November and March. The coast is two hours from the capital, so a three-day weekend empties Montevideo onto it.

Carnival and Easter week move with the calendar every year. We do not print the dates, because a date printed here goes stale; check them for your own year before you book.

Uruguay seasons — questions

What is the best month to visit Uruguay?

March, or the stretch from late October to mid December. In those weeks the air is warm, the sea is swimmable, the Atlantic resorts are open and staffed, and the prices are a fraction of the January peak. If you want the full summer scene at Punta del Este rather than a quiet beach, the answer is instead the second half of January, and you should book months ahead and expect to pay for it.

When is the sea warm enough to swim in Uruguay?

Roughly December to March, with January and February the warmest at about 73 degrees Fahrenheit, or 23 Celsius, off Montevideo. By November it is about 63 Fahrenheit, which is bracing but swimmable for many people, and by April it is dropping away. In July and August the sea is around 53 to 54 Fahrenheit, or 12 Celsius, and nobody is swimming in it. The Atlantic beaches east of Punta del Este run a little cooler than the river shore to the west.

Is Uruguay rainy?

It rains a reasonable amount and it rains all year: about 46 inches, or 1,165 mm, a year in Montevideo, spread fairly evenly across the months with the heaviest stretch in autumn and a peak in April. There is no dry season and no wet season in the tropical sense, so there is no month to aim for on rainfall grounds. Showers tend to come through quickly rather than settling in for days. Punta del Este is the driest part of the coast at about 40 inches a year.

What is closed out of season on the coast?

A good deal of Punta del Este and the smaller beach towns east of it shut between about April and November, including restaurants, beach clubs and some hotels, and bus services thin out. This is normal for a resort coast with a two-month season and not a sign of decline. Montevideo and Colonia del Sacramento are year-round towns and are not affected in the same way, which is why a winter trip to Uruguay should be built around the cities.

How cold does Uruguayan winter get?

Mild by northern standards and colder than people expect for South America. Montevideo in July sits around 57 Fahrenheit, or 14 Celsius, by day and drops to the mid-forties Fahrenheit, around 7 or 8 Celsius, overnight. It does not snow. What makes winter feel raw is the damp and the wind off the water rather than the thermometer, and a good deal of older housing is not heated the way a northern visitor assumes. Bring a proper coat.
