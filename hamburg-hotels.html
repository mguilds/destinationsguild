Hamburg’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Hamburg — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated October 2026

Hamburg is a city of water, and where you sleep decides which water you wake up to. The lake called the Alster sits in the middle of town, ringed by parks and quiet, well-to-do streets; the Elbe runs along the south edge past the cranes, the ferry piers and the warehouses. Between them is a compact centre you can cross on foot. So the useful question is not which hotel first — it is which stretch of waterfront you want on your doorstep each morning. We have grouped Hamburg’s most enduring hotels by quarter and character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can simply choose your dates.

Altstadt, the Rathaus & the Binnenalster

The old city between the town hall square and the Inner Alster is the easiest base for a first visit. The arcades along the water, the shopping streets, the great churches and the main station are all within a short walk of one another, and everything else in this guide is a few minutes away by U-Bahn or S-Bahn. Expect the city’s highest room rates and its shortest walk home after dinner.

Fairmont Hotel Vier Jahreszeiten

$$$$ LuxuryOn the Inner AlsterGrande dameAfternoon tea

Hamburg’s great traditional hotel, standing on the Neuer Jungfernstieg with the Inner Alster directly in front of it. The public rooms are panelled and antique-filled rather than fashionable, the service is formal in the old sense, and the lake-facing rooms are among the finest addresses in northern Germany. The definitive splurge for travellers who want the hotel itself to be part of the occasion.

St. Georg & the Outer Alster

Just east of the main station the city opens onto the Outer Alster, a broad lake with a footpath all the way round it and sailing boats on it from spring to autumn. The hotels along An der Alster look straight across the water and are still only ten minutes’ walk from the centre. This is the quarter to choose if you want a morning walk by the lake rather than a shopfront outside your door.

HafenCity & the Speicherstadt

South of the old town the city runs out into the water on reclaimed quays: the red-brick warehouse district of the Speicherstadt, and beyond it the new quarter of HafenCity with the Elbphilharmonie on its tip. Staying here puts the concert hall, the harbour promenades and the warehouse canals on your doorstep, and the quarter goes quiet in the evening once the day visitors have left. It is the newest part of Hamburg and feels it.

St. Pauli, the Reeperbahn & the Landungsbrücken

West of the centre, St. Pauli has the best river views in Hamburg, the ferry piers at the Landungsbrücken, the Sunday fish market, and the Reeperbahn running through the middle of it. It is the liveliest quarter in the city and the best value for the view you get. It is also loud late into the night, particularly at weekends — a fair trade for some travellers and the wrong choice for others, which is worth deciding honestly before you book.

The Alster’s west bank & the Elbchaussee

The green, well-to-do side of Hamburg runs from Rotherbaum and Pöseldorf along the west shore of the Outer Alster, then west again down the Elbchaussee, the long road above the river that leads out past villas and parkland towards Nienstedten and Blankenese. These are residential quarters with the university, the museums and the lake on one side and the Elbe on the other. They suit repeat visitors, and anyone who would rather walk by water in the morning than step into a shopping street.

Budget & Value

Hamburg is not a cheap city, and it gets dearer in high summer, during the big trade fairs and when several cruise ships are in. The good news is that the value tier here is genuinely central: well-run design-budget hotels and smart hostels sit inside the ring, so choosing one costs you money, not location.

Hamburg — common questions

Which area should I stay in for a first visit to Hamburg?

The Altstadt and Neustadt, between the Rathaus and the Binnenalster. It is the compact centre of the city: the town hall square, the arcades along the water, the shopping streets and the main churches are all on foot from each other, and the U-Bahn and S-Bahn take you everywhere else in minutes. It is also the shortest walk back to your room at the end of a long day.

Where should I stay to see the Elbphilharmonie and the Speicherstadt?

HafenCity. The concert hall, the red-brick warehouse district and the harbour promenades are all within a short walk, and the quarter is quiet in the evening once the day visitors have gone. It is the newest part of Hamburg and feels it, so travellers who want old streets and corner bars may prefer the Altstadt or St. Pauli and visit HafenCity on foot.

Is it a good idea to stay on the Reeperbahn?

It depends entirely on how you sleep. St. Pauli has the best views over the Elbe, the Landungsbrücken ferry piers and the liveliest streets in the city, and it is excellent value for what you get. It is also genuinely noisy late into the night, especially at weekends. If you are a light sleeper, take a room facing away from the main streets, or base yourself by the Alster instead and come down for the evening.

Where should I stay in Hamburg for a quiet, green base?

The west side of the Outer Alster, around Rotherbaum and Pöseldorf, or further out along the Elbchaussee towards Nienstedten. Both are leafy, residential and well-to-do, with the lake or the river on your doorstep and the centre a short ride away. They suit repeat visitors, and anyone who would rather walk by water in the morning than step straight into a shopping street.

Do I need a car in Hamburg?

No. The U-Bahn, S-Bahn and buses reach everything in this guide, and the public harbour ferries are part of the same city transport network, so an ordinary ticket puts you on the water. Parking in the centre is expensive and the city is flat and walkable, so most visitors are better off without a car.
