Historic Hotels of Massachusetts

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

12 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 12 in Massachusetts, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1716

Concord's Colonial Inn

Concord, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2005

Captain James Minot put up the oldest of this inn's three buildings in 1716. Before the Revolution the middle building held the militia's arms and supplies, the stores British troops marched from Boston to seize in April 1775. After the fight at the North Bridge, Dr. Timothy Minot Jr. treated wounded militiamen in his house on the western side. In 1799 John Thoreau, grandfather of Henry David, bought the eastern house, and Henry David lived there while a Harvard student. It has taken guests since 1889.

Est. 1771

Publick House Historic Inn

Sturbridge, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2018

Colonel Ebenezer Crafts won this plot beside the Sturbridge green from Dr. Elisha Marcy in a game of cards, then built a large house that doubled as a roadside tavern, opening it in 1771 on the Boston Post Road. Hard times after the Revolution forced him to sell, and he left for Vermont, where Craftsbury bears his name as its founder. Lafayette and his son Georges stayed during their tour of the 1820s, and the big 18th-century kitchen fireplace still warms the inn.

  • Tantiusques nature trail and its old mines
  • American Antiquarian Society in Worcester

Est. 1773

The Red Lion Inn

Stockbridge, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 1989

Silas Pepoon built this Stockbridge tavern in 1773 for stagecoach travelers between Boston and Albany, and townspeople met here to protest acts of the British Parliament before the Revolution. Charles H. Plumb bought it in 1862, and his wife filled it with colonial furniture and china from local farmhouses, the start of the collection still on display. Rebuilt after damage in the 1890s, it reopened in 1897, the year its Bird Cage Elevator went in. Hawthorne, Longfellow and Presidents Cleveland, McKinley and Theodore Roosevelt all stayed.

Est. 1855

Omni Parker House

Boston, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2006

Harvey D. Parker left the farm at 20 and reached Boston in 1825, saved enough to open a restaurant, and with partner John F. Mills opened the Parker House in 1855. The Saturday Club of Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr., Emerson, Hawthorne and Longfellow met here, and Charles Dickens joined them; their talk helped give rise to The Atlantic Monthly. Architect G. Henri Desmond's rebuilt hotel opened in 1927. John F. Kennedy kicked off his 1946 campaign here and held his bachelor party in the Press Room in 1953.

Est. 1884

Deerfield Inn

Deerfield, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2025

In 1884 drought and pests ruined farms up and down the Connecticut River Valley, and brothers Edward and Frederick Everett answered by building a country inn on Old Main Street in their hometown, hoping visitors to Deerfield's surviving colonial houses would steady the local economy. It soon became the village's social center. Teachers visiting the nearby schools stayed here, and so did Eleanor Roosevelt. Its restaurant is named for Deerfield artist James Wells Champney. Since 1945 the inn has belonged to Historic Deerfield, the nonprofit that cares for the village.

Est. 1894

The Kendall Hotel

Cambridge, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2004

In the 1890s Kendall Square was a busy factory district, named for Edward Kendall of the Kendall Boiler and Tank Company, and Cambridge gave its new Engine 7 company a station of its own, designed by R.J. Fitzgerald and S.D. Mitchell. Few stations in the region then served firefighters alone. Engine 7 moved out in 1993. Preservationists Charlotte Forsythe, an artist, and Gerald Fandetti, her husband, bought the empty building in 1999, restored its cupola, added a seven-story tower and turned it into The Kendall Hotel.

  • Cambridge Antique Market in East Cambridge

Est. 1903

XV Beacon

Boston, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2016

Early in the 1900s the Beacon Hill Trust hired William Gibbons Preston, trained at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris, to raise a ten-story office building near the State House, with cast-iron ornament, Roman brick and a marble staircase. The Boston Transit Commission took offices here first and oversaw the building of the nation's first subway from here, and the building took its name. The Boston School Committee later occupied it for decades. Hoteliers bought it in 1998, and the early-1900s stairs and caged-glass lifts still serve guests.

  • Boston Common
  • The Massachusetts State House
  • Museum of African American History

Est. 1907

The Mansion at Ocean Edge

Brewster, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2022

Chicago financier Samuel Mayo Nickerson bought 48 acres outside Brewster and built Fieldstone Hall, a Victorian summer mansion meant largely for his son Roland, whose family began coming in 1890. After tragedy struck the family, Roland's wife Addie began a new house in 1907; architect Horace Frazer gave it stucco walls, Italian marble and a carved oak staircase. The Missionaries of La Salette bought it in 1945 for a seminary, and in the early 1980s Corcoran Jennison made it the centerpiece of the new Ocean Edge resort.

Est. 1912

Fairmont Copley Plaza Boston

Boston, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 1989

Henry Janeway Hardenbergh, architect of New York's Plaza and Washington's Willard, designed this hotel on the former site of Boston's Museum of Fine Arts, and it opened in 1912 under the name Copley Plaza, honoring the painter John Singleton Copley. John Singer Sargent nearly lived here in its early years and used elevator operator Thomas McKeller as his model for Apollo. Babe Ruth was feted here in 1935, Elizabeth Taylor and Richard Burton spent a second honeymoon here in 1975, and every president since Taft has visited.

Close by

  • Boston Public Library, across the street
  • Boston Common
  • The Mapparium at the Mary Baker Eddy Library

Est. 1925

Hawthorne Hotel

Salem, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 1991

In the 1920s Salem's business leaders formed the Salem Hotel Company, with Sylvania founder Frank Poor as its first president, and sold public stock to raise more than $500,000. Their hotel on Salem Common, named for Nathaniel Hawthorne, opened in July 1925 with 150 rooms and a two-story lobby. In the 1950s it was briefly the Hawthorne Motor Hotel. Elizabeth Montgomery and the Bewitched cast stayed here in 1970 while filming in Salem, and Bette Davis and Johnny Cash were also guests.

Est. 1925

The Eliot Hotel

Boston, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2024

By the 1920s Back Bay's Paris-style boulevards were drawing visiting scholars, and Charles William Eliot, Harvard's former president, joined his family in buying a lot beside the Harvard alumni club to house the university's guest lecturers. Architect Harold Field Kellogg began designing the nine-story Georgian Revival apartment hotel in 1925; Eliot died before it opened. Retired professors took rooms for months at a time. The Depression bankrupted the family, and Nathan Ullian's group bought and renovated it shortly before World War II.

Est. 1926

Inn on Boltwood

Amherst, Massachusetts · Historic Hotels member since 2011

Amherst College alumni had wanted a proper inn for their town since 1910. Selling stock in a new Amherst Inn Company, they bought land beside the Amherst Common, and architect Allen Cox of Putnam and Cox designed a three-story Colonial Revival building to suit the neighboring fraternity houses. It opened in June 1926, and its big lobby fireplace became a gathering place for faculty parties and alumni reunions. Robert Frost often stayed while lecturing at the college, and the inn was restored in 2011.

Close by

  • Emily Dickinson Museum
  • Beneski Museum of Natural History
  • The Eric Carle Museum of picture book art
