Stone Town — Carved Doors and a Plan That Defeats Maps

Where to Stay in Stone Town — Area by Area

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Stone Town is a small place and the choice inside it is narrower than the number of hotels suggests. What really divides them is not price but access: whether a taxi can reach the door, how many flights of stairs stand between you and the bed, and whether you have a window that opens onto a breeze or onto a lane a metre wide.

That is the practical question this page answers, because it is the one that spoils holidays here and the one the pictures never show.

Inside the old town — the reason you came

The lanes of Stone Town are the point: carved doors, Omani and Indian merchant houses, and a plan that defeats every map. Most of the atmospheric rooms are in converted merchant houses in here, which means stairs, small windows, uneven floors and, often, no vehicle access to the door. Take a small bag and expect to be walked the last hundred metres. For a first visit to Zanzibar this is where to stay, and two nights is enough.

The seafront — Shangani and Forodhani

The western edge, where the old town meets the water, has the larger historic hotels, the sunset terraces and the Forodhani night food market on the doorstep. You get a breeze, a view and a taxi that can actually reach you, at the cost of some of the maze. If anybody in your party is unsteady on narrow stairs, book here rather than deep inside.

Just outside the old town — Mbweni and the southern edge

South towards the airport the coast opens into gardens and a handful of quieter hotels with pools, ten or fifteen minutes from the old town by car. This suits a family, a long stay, or anyone who wants the history by day and a swim in the afternoon without moving to the north coast.

Before you book

Government travel insurance, and which one to buy

Every visitor to Zanzibar and to mainland Tanzania, residents aside, must hold inbound travel insurance provided by the government — and you must hold it even if you already have a policy of your own. The important detail for Zanzibar: you buy it for your FIRST port of entry only. Fly straight into Zanzibar and you buy from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation, online beforehand or on arrival. Come through Dar es Salaam or Kilimanjaro first and you buy from the National Insurance Corporation instead, and you do not buy again when you reach the island. It covers your stay up to 92 days, and you may be refused entry without it. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements for Tanzania, still current at 22 November 2026.

Get the e-visa before you fly

You need a tourist or business visa, and you apply for an e-visa online before you travel — it is no longer possible to get one from the Tanzanian High Commission in London. A single-entry visa is available on arrival at the main ports of entry if you meet the requirements, and you may be asked to show your return or onward ticket; a multiple-entry visa cannot be had on arrival. Your passport needs an expiry date at least six months after you arrive and at least one blank page.

Health screening at the border

Tanzania’s Ministry of Health announced heightened screening on 17 October for travellers arriving from high-risk regions and affected countries, at every point of entry, and every traveller has to complete a Travellers Health Surveillance Form before departure or on arrival. You also pass through mpox screening — body temperature checks — at all points of entry. Check the current list of affected countries before you fly.

What official advice says about Zanzibar specifically

There is a risk of armed crime across Tanzania, and on Zanzibar official advice records incidents in Stone Town and at hotels and on popular tourist beaches throughout the island — not only in the town. The Foreign Office added guidance on 30 October 2026 about using the security measures your accommodation provides to reduce the risk of burglary. Use the room safe, lock the balcony door, and do not leave a bag on a sunbed. Past elections in Zanzibar have also turned violent.

Dress, and Ramadan

Zanzibar is a mainly Muslim island and the beach resorts are an exception to it rather than the rule. Shoulders and knees covered away from the sand is the courtesy that costs nothing, and in Stone Town it will change how you are treated. During Ramadan many restaurants outside the hotels close during daylight and eating or drinking in the street is poor form. This is the Guild’s own advice, not a legal requirement.

Malaria, and where the hospitals are

Malaria is a risk across Tanzania, and dengue, cholera and mpox are all named in official health advice. Get travel health advice at least eight weeks before you fly and ask about your whole itinerary rather than one city. Medical facilities are limited, particularly outside Dar es Salaam, so insurance that covers medical evacuation is not a luxury here. The emergency number is 112.

Questions readers actually ask

Should I stay in Stone Town or on the beach?


Both, and in that order. Stone Town rewards two nights and then runs out of new lanes for most visitors, while the beaches are what the rest of the week is for. Doing the town first also means you arrive at the beach with the shopping and the history done. If you only have time for one, and you came for the sea, go north and give Stone Town a day trip.

What are the rooms in Stone Town actually like?


Mostly converted merchant houses: beautiful, characterful and genuinely awkward. Expect steep narrow stairs, rooms on the third floor with no lift, small windows, and a taxi that cannot reach the door because the lane is too narrow. Ask before booking how many flights of stairs there are and whether a vehicle can get close. If stairs are a problem, stay on the Shangani seafront instead, where the larger hotels are.

Is Stone Town safe to walk around?


By day, yes, and getting lost is part of it. Official advice does record incidents affecting visitors in Stone Town and at hotels and beaches across the island, so the ordinary rules apply: nothing valuable on show, a bag worn on the inside shoulder, and a taxi rather than the unlit lanes late at night. The Foreign Office also added advice in October 2026 about using the security measures your hotel provides against burglary, which in practice means the room safe.

Which insurance do I buy if I fly straight into Zanzibar?


The Zanzibar Insurance Corporation's inbound policy, bought online beforehand or on arrival, because you buy the compulsory government insurance for your first port of entry only. If you arrive on the mainland first, at Dar es Salaam or Kilimanjaro, you buy the National Insurance Corporation policy there and you do not buy again when you reach the island. You must hold it even if you already have your own travel insurance from home.

How do I get from Stone Town to the north coast?


By road, about an hour and a half to Nungwi at the northern tip. Hotels and taxi drivers run the transfer as a fixed-price job and it is worth arranging in advance rather than at the rank. The ferry from Dar es Salaam and the airport are both on the Stone Town side, so the usual shape of a week is town first, then north.
