A short history

The islands of St. Thomas, St. John and St. Croix were first home to the Taíno and Carib peoples, whose villages and petroglyphs still surface in national park sites. Danish colonists established sugar plantations worked by enslaved Africans from the late 1600s, and Danish rule left a lasting cultural fingerprint in architecture, place names and the islands' historic forts.

The United States purchased the territory from Denmark in 1917 for 25 million dollars, mainly for its strategic Caribbean position guarding approaches to the Panama Canal. Emancipation had come decades earlier under Danish rule, in 1848, and the islands' Afro-Caribbean culture, Crucian dialect and carnival traditions remain central to local identity today.

Modern the U.S. Virgin Islands runs on tourism, with cruise ships and resort travellers drawn to St. Thomas's harbour and duty-free shopping, while two-thirds of St. John is protected as Virgin Islands National Park. Rum distilling, historic Christiansted on St. Croix, and a laid-back island pace round out an economy still recovering and rebuilding after major 2017 hurricanes.

The U.S. bought St. Thomas, St. John, and St. Croix from Denmark for 25 million dollars in gold, mainly to keep a strategic Caribbean naval base out of German hands during World War One. St. John is two-thirds national park today because Laurance Rockefeller donated the land to the federal government in 1956, which is why so much of the island still looks untouched.

Skip crowded Magens Bay and take the ferry over to St. John, where Trunk Bay and the quieter Hawksnest Bay have the same turquoise water with half the crowd. And for dinner, follow the locals to a food truck on St. Croix for real johnnycakes and saltfish, better and cheaper than anything on the cruise-ship strip.

When to go

December–April is the dry, popular season.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Peak season with warm, dry, breezy weather — book flights and hotels well ahead.

Spring (Mar–May)

Still dry and pleasant with thinner crowds than the winter holiday rush.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Hot and increasingly humid, but with lower prices and fewer visitors.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Caribbean hurricane season peaks in September; watch storm forecasts closely.
