Sapporo’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Sapporo — Best Hotels by Style

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Sapporo is easy to love: a flat, numbered grid in the heart of Hokkaido, warmed by underground walkways, ringed by ramen counters and beer halls, and connected to New Chitose Airport by a quick train. Most of the good hotels cluster around JR Sapporo Station, leafy Odori Park and lively Susukino, so the real question is what kind of stay you want. We’ve grouped Sapporo’s most dependable, well-run hotels by style and character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can just choose your dates.

Luxury

Sapporo’s top hotels trade on two things — sweeping views over the city and the mountains beyond, and a location right on top of the transport that gets you to the airport, the ski slopes and the food. These are the places to book when you want the stay itself to feel like part of the trip.

Rising directly above JR Sapporo Station, this is the city’s most convenient luxury address, with large, high-floor rooms that look out over Hokkaido’s rooftops toward distant mountains. The 22nd-floor sky resort spa and a clutch of fine restaurants make it a destination in itself, and you can step straight onto the airport train without going outside. Best for travelers who want polish, panoramas and zero transfer fuss.

Sapporo hotels — quick questions

What is the best area to stay in Sapporo?

For a first visit, stay around JR Sapporo Station or Odori Park in the central Chuo ward. The station area keeps you dry and warm with underground passages, department stores and the airport train on the doorstep, while Odori and Susukino put you a short walk from dining, the TV Tower and nightlife. Both are compact, well connected by subway, and easy to reach from New Chitose Airport.

Is Sapporo walkable?

Yes. Central Sapporo is laid out on a flat, numbered grid that makes it one of the easiest Japanese cities to navigate on foot, and a long underground walkway links the station to Odori and Susukino so you can cross the center without going outside. In deep winter, sidewalks can be icy, so wear proper boots; the subway and that underground network make it comfortable year round.

When is the best time to visit Sapporo?

February is the headline month, when the Sapporo Snow Festival fills Odori Park with giant ice sculptures, but rooms sell out early and cost the most. Summer, from June to August, is cool, green and comfortable, and is the cheapest, calmest time to explore Hokkaido. Autumn brings vivid foliage, so pick the season by whether you are chasing snow, mild weather or fall color.
