Where to Stay in New Orleans — French Quarter, Marigny, CBD & Garden District

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

New Orleans is a city you walk, ride by streetcar and listen to, and the neighbourhood you sleep in shapes the whole trip. The French Quarter puts you inside the balconies, the jazz and the late nights. The Marigny, just down river, is where locals go for music on Frenchmen Street. The Central Business District is handy for the Superdome and the National WWII Museum, and the Garden District, out along the St. Charles streetcar, is green, grand and quiet. This page sorts the areas and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in New Orleans

Four parts of the city, each suited to a different trip.

The French Quarter

The city’s official visitors bureau, New Orleans & Company, sums up the Quarter as “bawdy, cultured, serene and brash”. This is the historic heart, with Royal Street’s galleries and antique shops, Jackson Square, the French Market and, a few blocks over, Bourbon Street.

Stay here for a first visit, or if you want to walk everywhere. The trade-off is noise: Bourbon Street and the blocks closest to it are loud late into the night. Light sleepers should ask for an inner courtyard room, or pick a hotel a few streets away from Bourbon.

Marigny and Bywater

The bureau describes a “bohemian spirit” in the Marigny and Bywater, just down river from the historic centre. Frenchmen Street, in the Marigny, is lined with small music clubs, and the area feels more like a neighbourhood than a stage set. A good choice for music lovers and repeat visitors who still want to walk into the Quarter.

The CBD and Warehouse District

Downtown, across Canal Street from the Quarter. The bureau calls the Central Business District “reborn” and the Warehouse Arts District a community “celebrating and reimagining its culture”. Here are the big hotels, the Caesars Superdome, the convention centre and the National WWII Museum. Handy for games, conventions and families who want a little more space.

The Garden District and Uptown

Grand old houses under live oaks, reached by the St. Charles streetcar. The bureau notes that the Garden District is known for its grand mansions, and that Uptown mixes mansions with modest homes along Mardi Gras parade routes. Quieter and greener than the Quarter, and the streetcar does the commuting for you.

Hotel Monteleone

French QuarterHistoricFamily-owned

At 214 Royal Street. Its own website says it has welcomed travellers since 1886 and calls itself a family-owned landmark in the French Quarter. Its Carousel Bar, a bar that slowly turns, has been spinning since 1949, and the hotel offers literary suites, the Criollo restaurant and a separate Iberville Tower. Its own neighbourhood map puts Bourbon Street 0.2 miles away, close but not on your doorstep.

Bourbon Orleans Hotel

At 717 Orleans Street. Its own website tells the building’s story: French aristocrats once held balls here, and later the Sisters of the Holy Family cared for orphans. Today it offers guest rooms, balcony rooms and two-level suites with balconies, each listed as sleeping two to four, plus a pool and its Bourbon “O” Bar. Getting There & When to Go

The handful of New Orleans details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to New Orleans

The city’s airport is Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport (MSY). The visitors bureau lists the ways into town: the RTA’s 202 Airport Express city bus to downtown, a taxi stand and a rideshare pick-up point on level 1, and shared airport shuttles that run every day of the year, about every 30 minutes from 5:55 AM to 10:00 PM.

Getting around

The streetcars are the visitor’s best friend. The bureau describes the St. Charles Avenue line as the oldest continuously operating streetcar line in the world, running from Canal Street through the Garden District and Uptown to Carrollton and Claiborne, 24 hours a day. The Riverfront line runs from the French Market to the convention centre. The Quarter and the Marigny are best on foot. If you bring a car, ask your hotel about parking and what it costs before you book.

When to go

Carnival is the big one. The visitors bureau gives Fat Tuesday, Mardi Gras Day, as 9 February 2027, with parades in the weeks before it. Hotels fill early for Carnival and for the spring festivals, so book as soon as your dates are fixed.

Summer is hot and humid. The US National Hurricane Center gives the Atlantic hurricane season as 1 June to 30 November, with most activity between mid-August and mid-October. If you travel then, choose a rate you can change and keep an eye on the forecast.

New Orleans Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in New Orleans?

The French Quarter for a first visit and for walking everywhere. The Marigny for live music on Frenchmen Street in a more local setting. The Central Business District for the Superdome, the convention centre and the National WWII Museum. The Garden District for a quieter, greener stay on the St. Charles streetcar.

Is it noisy to stay on Bourbon Street?

Yes, it can be. Bourbon Street is busy and loud late into the night, especially on weekends and during Carnival. Light sleepers should ask for a courtyard-side room, or stay a few blocks away on Royal or Chartres Street, in the Marigny or in the Garden District.

Do I need a car in New Orleans?

Usually not if you stay in the French Quarter, the Marigny, the CBD or along St. Charles Avenue. The St. Charles streetcar runs 24 hours a day from Canal Street through the Garden District, and the airport has an express city bus, taxis, rideshare and shared shuttles into town.

When is Mardi Gras in 2027?

The New Orleans visitors bureau gives Fat Tuesday, Mardi Gras Day, as 9 February 2027, with parades in the weeks before. Hotels fill early for Carnival, so book as soon as your dates are fixed.

When is hurricane season in New Orleans?

The US National Hurricane Center gives the Atlantic hurricane season as 1 June to 30 November, with most activity between mid-August and mid-October. Travellers in those months should book a rate they can change and watch the forecast.
