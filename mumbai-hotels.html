Where to Stay in Mumbai

Where to Stay in Mumbai — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Mumbai is a long, narrow city and the traffic on it is the single fact that should decide your hotel. Everything an American visitor came to see — the Gateway of India, the Elephanta ferry, the Victorian and Art Deco waterfront, the museums of Kala Ghoda, Marine Drive at dusk — sits inside a two-mile arc at the southern tip, and it is fifteen miles from the airport with anything from forty-five minutes to two hours of traffic in between. South Mumbai, meaning Colaba, Fort and Marine Drive, is therefore where a first visit belongs; it also has the only genuinely walkable pavements in the city. Bandra and Juhu are for beach days and grandchildren. The airport district is for the night before a dawn flight and nothing else. Below are the city’s most dependable hotels by style, each with a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Choosing Your Neighbourhood

Three parts of Mumbai, and the drive between them is longer than the map suggests.

South Mumbai, Bandra and Juhu, or the airport district?

South Mumbai — Colaba and Apollo Bunder, Fort, Nariman Point and Marine Drive — is the recommendation for a first visit, and not only for the sights. Colaba Causeway and the Marine Drive promenade are two of very few stretches in this city where you can walk comfortably and continuously: flat, wide and along the sea. Elsewhere the footpath is contested or simply absent. The cost of staying here is the airport run, and it is worth being blunt about it: route-planners quote twenty-odd minutes and you should ignore them. Allow forty-five to sixty minutes off-peak and ninety minutes to two hours in rush hour or in rain, and leave your hotel four hours before an international departure. There is now a genuine alternative, and it is the best news in Mumbai travel for years — the underground metro line completed in October 2025 runs from inside the airport terminals to Cuffe Parade in about forty-five to fifty minutes for the price of a cup of coffee, air-conditioned, step-free and untouched by traffic. Bandra and Juhu, in the north, are low-rise, leafy and full of cafes, with real beach and walkable seafront promenades, twenty to thirty-five minutes from the airport — but sixty to ninety minutes from the Gateway of India, which means four hours a day in a car if you sightsee in the old city from there. Choose them for a second visit or a family stay. Bandra Kurla Complex and the airport hotels are a planned office district with excellent hotels and nothing whatever to walk to; they are the right answer for a single night before an early flight and the wrong one for a holiday. For most readers over fifty-five: south Mumbai throughout, or three nights in Colaba and two at Juhu, with a final night near the airport if the flight home leaves before dawn.

Luxury

Mumbai has two great hotels and they are a mile apart: one is a monument in its own right, the other has the finest view in the city from every single room.

The Taj Mahal Palace, Mumbai

$$$$ LuxuryApollo BunderOpened 1903Faces the Gateway of India

Opened in 1903 by Jamsetji Tata and standing directly opposite the Gateway of India, which came twenty-one years later. One point of precision matters when you book, because the hotel is now sold as two: the domed Palace Wing is the 1903 original, with the great cantilevered staircase, the period rooms and the harbour outlook; the Tower Wing, added in 1973, has larger and more modern rooms at a noticeably lower price and full use of everything else. Book the Tower and you have stayed beside the Taj Mahal Palace rather than in it. Nine restaurants and bars include Wasabi by Morimoto, the Sichuan Golden Dragon, the Sea Lounge for afternoon tea, and the Harbour Bar, the oldest licensed bar in India. If you are paying Palace rates, specify a sea-facing Palace Wing room in writing. No pets.

The Oberoi, Mumbai

$$$$ LuxuryNariman PointEvery room faces the seaButler on each floor

Two hundred and thirty-seven rooms and suites at the southern end of Marine Drive, and every one of them looks out over Back Bay and the curve of lights the city calls the Queen’s Necklace — which after dark is the best hotel view in India after the Taj Mahal itself. Ziya serves modern Indian cooking mentored by Vineet Bhatia, Fenix runs all day, and the Eau Bar is an Art Deco room with live music in the evening. There is a temperature-controlled pool in a private courtyard and a butler on every floor. The quietest luxury in Mumbai, and the practical reason we recommend it to older guests is just outside the door: the Marine Drive promenade is flat, wide, lit and three kilometres long, which is the best evening walk in the city.

Boutique

Mumbai’s small hotels are design projects in old buildings rather than heritage houses. Both of these are delightful; check the stairs before you commit to either.

Abode Bombay

$$ BoutiqueColaba1910 buildingAround twenty rooms

India’s original design-led small hotel, in a 1910 building behind the Regal Cinema, a five-minute flat walk from the Gateway of India. Around twenty rooms — the hotel says twenty-five, the review sites twenty, so confirm the category you are booking — put together from recovered teak floorboards, hand-laid tiles and Art Deco pieces found in the local markets, with a small library, a wellness room and a Subko coffee counter downstairs. Breakfast is a genuine pleasure rather than a duty. Two things to settle before booking: the cheapest categories have shared bathrooms, so ask for an ensuite room; and reception and the rooms are on the first floor with no lift documented anywhere we could find. Email and ask if stairs are a difficulty.

Le Sutra — The Indian Art Hotel

$$ BoutiqueKhar WestSixteen roomsCarter Road nearby

Sixteen rooms in Union Park, Khar West, each built around an idea from Indian philosophy and each individually made with commissioned art and sculpture — no two are remotely alike, which is the charm and also the reason to ask for photographs of the actual room being held for you rather than the one in the brochure. Olive, one of the city’s long-standing restaurants, is next door, and the Carter Road seafront promenade is a few minutes’ walk, which is a pleasant flat stroll in the evening. There is a small spa, and breakfast is included. This is the low-rise, tree-lined, cafe-heavy side of Mumbai: excellent for a second stay or a slower week, poor as a base for sightseeing in the old city.

Budget & Value

Mumbai is the most expensive city in India for hotels, so value here means a modest, well-kept room in an expensive postcode. Both of these are in south Mumbai, which is precisely the point.

Residency Hotel Fort

$ ValueFortThirty-eight roomsBreakfast included

Thirty-eight rooms in the old fire-brigade building at the corner of D. N. Road, in the middle of the Victorian-Gothic and Art Deco quarter that UNESCO added to the World Heritage list — which is to say the architecture you came to Mumbai for is the street you step out onto. Buffet breakfast, air-conditioning throughout, a twenty-four-hour desk, and a heritage wing alongside newer renovated rooms, for the price of a chain motel at home. It ranks improbably high among the city’s hundreds of hotels and holds that ranking year after year. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Kala Ghoda and the Asiatic Society steps are all a short walk; the Gateway is about twenty-five flat minutes on foot or five by cab.

Hotel Suba Palace

$$ ValueApollo BunderForty-nine roomsSteps from the Gateway

Forty-nine rooms on Battery Street, roughly three hundred and fifty yards behind the Taj Mahal Palace — the same neighbourhood at a quarter of the money, which for an older traveller is the whole argument. The Elephanta ferry jetty, the Gateway, Colaba Causeway, Leopold Cafe and the Sassoon Docks are all reachable on foot, so you can spend a day in south Mumbai without once getting into a car. Breakfast is included, the front desk is staffed around the clock and transfers can be arranged. Guests praise the housekeeping and the staff consistently and are equally consistent that the breakfast is simple and the rooftop dated. Rooms are compact; ask for a higher floor away from the street. No pool.

Family-friendly

With children the calculation flips: Juhu, where the hotels stand on the sand and there is somewhere to swim, is worth the hour of traffic to the old city.

JW Marriott Mumbai Juhu

$$$ FamilyJuhuThree poolsOn the beach

The default Mumbai family hotel and, with three hundred and fifty-five rooms on Juhu Tara Road, the one with the most water: an infinity pool, a saltwater pool and a whirlpool, plus direct access to Juhu beach. In a city where outdoor time is limited by heat, traffic and monsoon, that is the practical argument. Six dining rooms cover every appetite at the table, from the all-day Lotus Cafe to Saffron for Indian and the Bombay Baking Company for the grandchildren, and there is an executive lounge with sea views. Rates swing hard with the season — roughly double in December what they are in July. One point to check directly if it matters: Marriott’s own pages currently show no accessible rooms at this hotel, which may be a data error. Airport twenty to thirty-five minutes; the Gateway sixty to ninety.

Novotel Mumbai Juhu Beach

$$$ FamilyJuhuBeachfront poolAlso pet-friendly

Two hundred and four rooms and suites directly on Juhu beach, from a brand built around families: connecting rooms, larger formats and suites big enough for three generations, so nobody is sleeping on a folding bed. The pool is open from seven in the morning until nine at night. Five separate restaurants solve the fussy-eater problem for good — The Square for all-day Indian, Sampan for pan-Asian, Bageecha for regional cooking with live ghazals, a French patisserie, and Gadda da Vida, a sea-facing cocktail lounge that is an institution in its own right. About twenty minutes to the airport. It is also officially pet-friendly, which makes it the rare hotel that works both for grandchildren and for a dog.

Adults-only

Mumbai has no adults-only hotel — not one property in the city enforces a minimum guest age — and we are not going to name one to fill the heading. The booking sites appear to offer them because the filter is titled ‘adults only and adult friendly’, a merged category that returns budget lodges and hostels, while another site applies its ‘adult only’ tag to large family hotels including The Leela and Trident BKC. India’s genuine age-restricted resorts are in Goa and Kerala, not in a working port city. For a stay in Mumbai where you are unlikely to meet a child, book one of the business-luxury houses — The Oberoi, the Four Seasons at Worli, the St. Regis — and know that children are permitted there. Pet-friendly

Genuine options are few but real, and all of them bar animals from the restaurants, bars, pool and gym and require that they are not left alone in the room, so plan the daytime before you travel. Note that the Taj Mahal Palace does not accept pets, whatever a listing site may say. Novotel Mumbai Juhu Beach, above, is a third option.

Sofitel Mumbai BKC

$$$ Pet-friendlyBandra Kurla ComplexOne pet up to 20kgMinutes from the airport

The published policy is refreshingly specific: one pet per room, dogs or cats, up to twenty kilograms, for a set nightly charge. Three hundred and two rooms in the business district, a mile and a half from the international terminal and now on the metro line — which makes it the easiest arrival in Mumbai with an animal in tow, and the sensible first or last night of a trip. The dining is much better than a business address deserves: Jyran does Frontier tandoor cooking, Tuskers is a rare vegetarian fine-dining room at this level, and there is a poolside bar. Pets are not permitted in the restaurants, bars, pool or gym. There is nothing to walk to outside, which is the one real drawback for a dog.

The Resort, Mumbai

$$ Pet-friendlyAksa BeachPets up to 25kgBed and bowl provided

The most generous published pet policy in the city: animals up to twenty-five kilograms for a single charge covering the whole stay, with a pet bed, a bowl and some basic food waiting on arrival, which is a thoughtful touch few hotels anywhere manage. Ninety-four rooms on Aksa beach on the far north-western coast, with sea-facing rooms, a pool, a spa and children staying free. Be clear about the geography before you book, because it is the catch: this is about seven miles from the airport but ninety minutes to two hours from Colaba. It is a weekend beach retreat, not a base for sightseeing. Guide dogs excepted, pets are kept out of the dining areas, health club and pool.

Mumbai Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Mumbai.

Is the Taj Mahal Palace’s Palace Wing worth the premium over the Tower?

Yes, if the building is why you are going. The Palace Wing is the 1903 original behind the dome, with the grand staircase, the period furniture and the harbour outlook the hotel is famous for. The Tower Wing, added in 1973, has rooms that are generally larger, more modern and noticeably cheaper, with full access to all nine restaurants and bars and the same pool and spa. Book the Tower and you have stayed next to the Taj Mahal Palace rather than in it. If you are paying Palace rates, put ‘sea-facing, Palace Wing’ in writing on the reservation.

How long does the airport really take to Colaba?

Assume forty-five to sixty minutes off-peak and ninety minutes to two hours in rush hour or heavy rain, for about fifteen miles. Any route-planner quoting twenty-two minutes is showing free-flowing conditions that essentially never occur. Since October 2025 there has been a reliable alternative: the underground metro line runs from inside the airport terminals to Cuffe Parade in roughly forty-five to fifty minutes for about fifty rupees, air-conditioned and step-free. For a departure, leave your hotel four hours before an international flight and do not be talked out of it.

Are there adults-only hotels in Mumbai?

No — not one Mumbai hotel enforces a minimum guest age. The booking sites appear to offer them because one large site’s filter is titled ‘adults only and adult friendly’, a merged category that returns budget lodges and hostels, while another applies an ‘adult only’ tag to ordinary five-star hotels that plainly accept children. India’s genuine age-restricted resorts are in Goa and Kerala. In Mumbai the nearest equivalent is a business-luxury hotel with a naturally adult clientele: The Oberoi, the Four Seasons at Worli, or the St. Regis.

Can I bring a dog to Mumbai?

Options are limited but genuine. Sofitel Mumbai BKC accepts one pet up to twenty kilograms, dog or cat, for a nightly charge. The Resort at Aksa beach accepts pets up to twenty-five kilograms for a single charge covering the stay and provides a bed, a bowl and food. Novotel Mumbai Juhu Beach is also listed as pet-friendly by its own group. The Taj Mahal Palace does not accept animals. At all three that do, pets are barred from the restaurants, bars, pool and gym and may not be left alone in the room, so arrange daytime cover before you travel.

When should we visit, and is the monsoon really that bad?

Go between November and February; January and February are the sweet spot, with average temperatures around the mid-seventies Fahrenheit, effectively no rain and nine hours of sunshine a day. The monsoon is genuinely disruptive rather than merely inconvenient: from June to September Mumbai takes between thirteen and twenty-eight inches of rain a month, streets flood, the Elephanta ferry stops, and July averages two and a half hours of sunshine a day. For a traveller over fifty-five the real difficulty is not the rain but the combination of heat and eighty-odd per cent humidity, which makes outdoor sightseeing exhausting long before it makes you wet. May is the hottest month and also worth avoiding.
