Before you go

Small, historic, and changing fast. Go for the food, and for the stories.

Washington's Chinatown sits a few blocks north of the National Mall, around H and I Streets between 5th and 8th Streets NW. Its great painted Friendship Archway is one of the most photographed sights in the city.

Be prepared for a surprise. Chinatown today is small, and it is busier with chain restaurants, bars and fans heading to Capital One Arena than with Chinese shops. Only around ten Asian-owned small businesses remain. The family restaurants below have held on for decades, and a meal at one of them is the best way to see what Chinatown was, and still is.

Where to eat in Chinatown

Long-running family restaurants and a few newer favorites. Restaurants open and close, so check before you go.

Roast duck · 746 6th St NW

Chinatown Express

A long-running storefront where you can watch noodles being pulled by hand in the window. Order the roast duck soup with hand-pulled noodles. It reopened after a renovation in 2025.

Cantonese, open late · 610 H St NW

New Big Wong

A Cantonese standby that stays open long after most of the city has gone to bed. Try the roast pork lo mein and the wonton soup.

Since 1986 · 619 H St NW

Tony Cheng's

A Chinatown institution serving classic Cantonese seafood. It came close to closing for good in 2024, and reopened in June 2025.

Since 1993 · 748 6th St NW

Joy Luck House

One of the neighborhood's longest-running family businesses, and a good sweet stop on a Chinatown food crawl.

Chinese burgers · 736 6th St NW

Bonker's Chinese Burger

A newer favorite. Order the rou jia mo, the Chinese "burger" of slow-cooked meat in flatbread, and the spicy pork wontons in chili oil.

Dinner with a story · 604 H St NW

Wok & Roll

Chinese food, sushi and karaoke inside the old Mary Surratt boarding house, where the plot to kill President Lincoln was hatched. See the history below.

From the Guild

Chinatown restaurants change hands often. Check the restaurant's hours online before you make a special trip, and if you can, choose one of the family-run places. Every meal helps keep Chinatown Chinese.

What to see in Chinatown

The Friendship Archway

Dedicated in 1986 to mark the friendship between Washington and its sister city, Beijing. It stands 60 feet high, with more than 7,000 glazed tiles and 280 painted dragons.

The Surratt boarding house

At 604 H Street, Mary Surratt's boarding house was where John Wilkes Booth and his fellow conspirators met. A historical marker stands outside.

Chinese street signs

Look up: street signs and many business names are written in both English and Chinese, a requirement for the neighborhood.

Lunar New Year parade

Each year around Lunar New Year, a parade with lion dancers, drums and firecrackers fills H Street. It is Chinatown's biggest day.

A short history

Washington's first Chinatown grew up in the 1880s along Pennsylvania Avenue. In the early 1930s, when the government cleared that area to build the Federal Triangle, the community moved north to H Street, where it remains today.

At its height Chinatown was home to thousands of Chinese families, with groceries, laundries, a Chinese school and a community church. Over the following decades many families moved to the suburbs, and new building projects, including the convention center and the arena, displaced many more. About 3,000 Chinese residents lived here in 2010; today the figure is closer to 350.

What remains is small but proud: the archway, the bilingual signs, the Lunar New Year parade, and the restaurants that have stayed.

Getting there

By Metro

Gallery Place–Chinatown station, on the Red, Green and Yellow lines, opens right into the neighborhood beside Capital One Arena.

On foot

Chinatown is about a 15-minute walk north of the National Mall, and right next to the National Portrait Gallery.

Chinatown, Washington, D.C. FAQ

Where is Chinatown in Washington, D.C.?

Chinatown is around H and I Streets NW, between 5th and 8th Streets, a few blocks north of the National Mall. The nearest Metro station is Gallery Place–Chinatown.

What are the best Chinese restaurants in DC's Chinatown?

Long-running favorites include Chinatown Express for roast duck and hand-pulled noodles, New Big Wong for late-night Cantonese food, Tony Cheng's for seafood, and Joy Luck House. Bonker's Chinese Burger is a popular newer spot.

What is the Friendship Archway?

The Friendship Archway spans H Street at 7th Street NW. Dedicated in 1986 to mark Washington's sister-city friendship with Beijing, it stands 60 feet high, with more than 7,000 glazed tiles and 280 painted dragons.

Is the Mary Surratt boarding house in Chinatown?

Yes. The boarding house where John Wilkes Booth and his fellow conspirators met stands at 604 H Street NW, and it is now a restaurant called Wok & Roll.

How far is Chinatown from Ford's Theatre?

Ford's Theatre, where President Lincoln was shot, is a few blocks west of Chinatown on 10th Street NW, an easy walk.

When is the Chinese New Year parade in Washington, D.C.?

Chinatown holds a parade each year around Lunar New Year, usually in late January or February. Check local listings for the date.
