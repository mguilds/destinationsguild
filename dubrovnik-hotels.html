Dubrovnik

Where to Stay in Dubrovnik

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Dubrovnik is a small city with a very famous middle, and where you sleep changes the trip more here than in almost any European town we know. The walled old town is magnificent and almost entirely pedestrian — which means no car reaches your door, the lanes are stepped, and the stone is polished slick. Ploče, the hill just east of the walls, holds most of the grand hotels and looks straight back at the city. Lapad, twenty minutes west, is where the beaches, the flat promenade and the sensible prices are. Gruž, around the harbour, is the practical choice for the ferry and the bus. This page goes through all four honestly, then names hotels by the kind of stay you want.

Where to base yourself in Dubrovnik

Four distinct places to sleep, and they suit quite different travellers.

Inside the walls (Stari Grad)

Unbeatable for atmosphere: you step out of your door into a marble-paved lane and the city is yours at seven in the morning, before the day-trippers land. The costs are real, though, and worth knowing before you book. No vehicle can reach your door, so the taxi stops at a gate and you carry your bags the last stretch over stepped lanes. Almost nothing has a lift. And in July the noise carries up the stone walls until very late. Wonderful for the sure-footed traveller with one small bag.

Ploče — the hill just east

The address for the grand hotels, and our pick for most first-time visitors who want comfort. Ten to fifteen minutes on foot to the Ploče Gate along a level road, and the view back at the walled city from this side is the one on the postcards. Hotels here have lifts, terraces and swimming platforms on the rock below. The walk back up from town is a gentle climb rather than a stair.

Lapad & Babin Kuk — west, by the beaches

The best-value part of Dubrovnik, and much the kindest underfoot. Uvala Lapad has a long, flat, traffic-free promenade of restaurants running down to a pebble bay, and the hotels here are largely modern with lifts, pools and parking. You are twenty to thirty minutes from the old town by bus or boat, which most people find a fair trade for paying a third less and being able to walk about in the evening.

Gruž — the working harbour

Where the ferries dock, the buses leave from and the morning market actually feeds people. Unglamorous, central to your travel arrangements, and the cheapest beds in the city. Good if you are catching a boat, arriving late or leaving early; less good if you came for the view.

Croatia uses the euro. Dubrovnik is the most expensive town in the country and prices in July and August are roughly double what they are in October. A nightly city tourist tax of one to two euros per person is normally collected at check-out and is not usually included in the room rate you booked. Cards are taken everywhere; decline the machine’s offer to charge you in dollars and pay in euros.

Dubrovnik in your later years

Dubrovnik rewards a little planning if stairs, heat or uneven ground are a consideration — and it punishes none at all.

Sleep on Ploče or in Lapad, not inside the walls

This is the single most useful decision. Hotels on Ploče and in Lapad have lifts, level approaches and a taxi that can reach the door. Inside the walls, no vehicle can reach you, the lanes are stepped, and most buildings have no lift — charming for a week at thirty, punishing at eighty.

The Lapad promenade is the flattest ground in the city

Uvala Lapad’s promenade is traffic-free, level, lined with restaurants and ends at a pebble bay with a gentle entry to the water. If walking is a consideration, basing yourself along it changes the whole holiday for the better.

Be realistic about the walls

The circuit is about two kilometres of uneven stone with a great many steps up and down, no handrail in places, and almost no shade. It is glorious and it is genuinely demanding. If in doubt, go at opening time, walk as far as the first stair you dislike, and turn back — nobody minds. The view from the Buža bar in the wall costs the price of a drink and asks nothing of your knees.

Take the cable car rather than the hill

The cable car up Mount Srđ is level to board and delivers the finest view in Croatia without a single step of climbing. It is the best-value outing in the city for anyone who would rather not walk uphill in the heat.

Go in May, late September or October

Dubrovnik in the mid-eighties with white stone throwing the glare back at you is hard work at any age. The shoulder months give you the same city at a temperature that lets you enjoy it, at half the price and with a fraction of the people.

Ask two questions before you book

Is there a lift, and can a car reach the entrance? Booking sites often answer neither. A short email to the hotel will, and it is the difference between a good week and a difficult one.

Dubrovnik hotels — questions

Should I stay inside the old town walls?

Only if you are steady on your feet and travelling light. Inside the walls is magical at seven in the morning and after eight at night, when the day-trippers have gone. But no vehicle can reach your door — taxis stop at a gate and you walk the rest over stepped marble lanes — almost nothing has a lift, and in high summer the noise rises off the stone until very late. For most visitors, and for nearly everyone with a suitcase or a knee, Ploče or Lapad is the better bet.

How far is Lapad from the old town, really?

About two and a half miles, which is twenty to thirty minutes by the frequent public bus, a similar time by the seasonal boat from Lapad bay, and ten to fifteen minutes by taxi. Most people staying in Lapad go into the old town once a day and are perfectly happy about it. In return you pay roughly a third less, you can walk to dinner on the level, and there is somewhere to swim that is not a queue.

Which hotels have lifts?

Every hotel we have named on this page has a lift, which is deliberate: it is the single most useful thing to know in a city built on a hillside. Inside the walls, by contrast, most guesthouses and apartments do not, and many involve a narrow stone stair. If a lift matters, ask before you book rather than after.

When is Dubrovnik at its worst for crowds?

A July or August day between about ten in the morning and four in the afternoon, especially when cruise ships are in. The city agreed to cap arrivals at two ships at a time under the arrangement it announced in 2018, but even so the old town can hold many thousands of people in a space you can cross in ten minutes. Staying the night rather than visiting for the day is the whole answer: be inside the walls before nine or after six and you will have a different, much better city.

Are there sandy beaches in Dubrovnik?

No. Banje beach below Ploče and the bay at the end of the Lapad promenade are the two main swimming spots and both are pebble, as is every other beach here. The water is exceptionally clear in exchange. Buy rubber swimming shoes at any supermarket for a few euros — every local owns a pair, and after one barefoot attempt on the pebbles you will understand why.

Do I need a car in Dubrovnik?

No, and it is a positive nuisance. The old town is entirely pedestrian, parking is scarce and expensive, and the buses run frequently between Gruž, Lapad, Ploče and the Pile Gate. Hire a car only for the day you drive up the coast or into the Konavle valley, and let the hotel park it the rest of the time.
