Before you go

Zion Canyon is narrow, and for most of the year you see it from a free shuttle bus rather than your own car.

Most of what people come to Zion for lies along the Zion Canyon Scenic Drive, a six-mile road up the floor of the canyon beside the Virgin River. From March through late November, and again over the Christmas holidays, that road is open only to the park's free shuttle buses. You park at the Zion Canyon Visitor Center or in the town of Springdale, ride in, and hop off at the lodge, the trailheads and the viewpoints. No ticket or reservation is needed for the shuttle; you just pay the park entrance fee.

The second thing to know is new for 2026. Since June 7, 2026, large vehicles may no longer drive the Zion–Mt. Carmel Highway between Canyon Junction and the East Entrance, the switchback road through the famous tunnel. The limits are 35 feet 9 inches long, 7 feet 10 inches wide, 11 feet 4 inches tall or 50,000 pounds for a single vehicle, and 50 feet overall (trailer no longer than 26 feet) for a combination, mirrors and racks included. If your rig is over any of those, plan to come and go by Springdale and the South Entrance.

Zion is one of the busiest parks in the country for its size. Campgrounds are full almost every night from mid-March to late November, and the visitor center lots often fill early in the morning. The answer is to sleep close by, start early and let the shuttle do the work. Springdale sits right at the South Entrance, and you can walk or ride the town shuttle to the entrance.

There is a quieter Zion too. The Kolob Canyons section, off Interstate 15 south of Cedar City, has its own visitor center and a five-mile scenic drive you can do in your own car any time it is open. It sees a fraction of the crowds.

Large vehicles (new June 7, 2026): vehicles over 35'9" long, 7'10" wide, 11'4" tall or 50,000 lbs, and combinations over 50 feet (or with a trailer over 26 feet), are barred from the whole Zion–Mt. Carmel Highway between Canyon Junction and the East Entrance, not just the tunnel. Oversize vehicles must use the South Entrance at Springdale. They may still enter there to camp at Watchman or South Campground, to stay at Zion Lodge with a reservation, to park in the large-vehicle lot by the visitor center if there is room, or to drive the Scenic Drive when the shuttle is not running.

Angels Landing: a permit is required to hike the chains section beyond Scout Lookout. Apply on recreation.gov through the seasonal lottery or the day-before lottery (apply before 3 p.m. Mountain Time for the next day). No permit is needed to hike as far as Scout Lookout.

Current trail closures: after a large rockfall, part of the East Rim Trail is closed, Hidden Canyon is closed, and Observation Point cannot be reached from Zion Canyon; it can only be hiked from the east. The Narrows closes to all travel when the Virgin River runs above 150 cubic feet per second or during a flash flood warning.

Winter roads: Kolob Terrace Road and Lava Point Road usually close for several months from fall to spring because of snow, and Kolob Canyons Road closes often in winter.

Visitors from outside the U.S.: since January 1, 2026, Zion is one of 11 national parks that charge non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older an extra $100 per person on top of the entrance fee. The America the Beautiful pass costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for nonresidents, and a pass covers that extra fee.

Sources: the National Park Service website (nps.gov/zion: fees, hours, shuttle schedule, current conditions, large vehicles, Angels Landing, camping, lodging and accessibility pages), checked November 2026.

Best things to do in Zion National Park

Six ways to spend your days in Zion.

The classic ride

Zion Canyon Scenic Drive

Ride the shuttle all the way to the Temple of Sinawava and back, getting off at Court of the Patriarchs and Zion Lodge. Buses come every few minutes in season.

Easy river walk

Riverside Walk & The Narrows

A paved, mostly level mile from the Temple of Sinawava along the river to where the canyon closes in. Wade on into The Narrows if you like; no permit is needed to go upstream as far as Big Spring.

Gentle and paved

Pa'rus Trail

A paved path from the Zion Canyon Visitor Center along the Virgin River, with the Watchman behind you. The one trail in the park where bicycles and leashed pets are welcome.

Short hike

Lower Emerald Pool

Starts across the road from Zion Lodge and leads to a seasonal waterfall and a small pool under red cliffs. Have lunch at the lodge afterward.

Permit required

Angels Landing

The famous chain-assisted climb along a narrow fin of rock, 1,500 feet above the canyon. Strenuous and exposed; for sure-footed hikers with a permit only.

The quiet side

Kolob Canyons

A five-mile scenic drive among finger canyons of red Navajo sandstone, reached from Interstate 15. Drive it in your own car, with pullouts and a viewpoint at the end.

If you are staying in Springdale, leave the car at your hotel and ride the free Springdale Line shuttle to Zion Canyon Village at the park entrance. The park lots often fill early in the day, and the town shuttle saves you the hunt and the paid parking.

When to visit Zion

Spring (late March to May) brings cool mornings, warm afternoons, running waterfalls and the cottonwoods leafing out. It is also busy, especially around Easter and Memorial Day. Summer is hot on the canyon floor, often over 100°F, with afternoon thunderstorms and flash-flood risk from July into September. Start early and be back in the shade by noon.

Fall (October and November) is many regulars' favorite: cooler days, golden cottonwoods and slightly smaller crowds. The 2026 shuttle runs through November 28, with reduced hours from late October; the last bus leaves the Visitor Center at 5 p.m.

Winter is quiet and often sunny, with a dusting of snow on the high walls now and then. From November 29 to December 25, and again after the holiday shuttle ends on January 2, you can drive the Scenic Drive in your own car, though the road may close to drivers if parking at the end fills up. Higher roads, such as Kolob Terrace, close for snow.

How to get to Zion National Park

By car

Most visitors arrive from Interstate 15. From the St. George area, follow Highway 9 east through Hurricane and La Verkin to Springdale and the South Entrance. Las Vegas is under three hours away.

From the east (Bryce Canyon, Kanab or Page), Highway 9 enters at the East Entrance and drops through the Zion–Mt. Carmel Tunnel to Canyon Junction. Cars and smaller RVs are fine; anything over the new size limits must go around and enter from Springdale, which the park says adds 10 to 45 minutes depending on your destination.

By air & shuttle

St. George Regional Airport (SGU) is the closest, about an hour from Springdale. Las Vegas (LAS) has far more flights and rental cars, and Salt Lake City (SLC) is about 4½ hours north.

Inside the park, the free Zion Canyon Line shuttle runs up the Scenic Drive, and the free Springdale Line links stops through town with Zion Canyon Village at the park entrance. In 2026 both run through November 28, pause until Christmas, then run December 26 to January 2.

Lodges inside the park

Zion Lodge is the only lodging inside the park. It sits halfway up Zion Canyon and has 76 hotel rooms, 6 suites and 40 historic cabins. The Red Rock Grill serves sit-down American and Southwestern meals all year; the Castle Dome Café and an outdoor beer garden are open spring to fall. Book through the lodge's own website or at 1-833-778-9290, as far ahead as you can.

During shuttle season, plan to reach the lodge by shuttle; if you need to drive in with luggage or a larger vehicle, ask the lodge about a driving pass when you book. The lodge also runs a narrated canyon tram, spring through fall, that starts and ends at the lodge.

RV travel & camping at Zion

Zion's two main campgrounds sit together near the South Entrance, a short walk from the visitor center and the shuttle.

Watchman Campground is open all year and takes reservations on recreation.gov up to six months ahead. It has 152 sites, including two loops with electric hookups, but no showers. Generators are not allowed. There is a dump station and water fill, which non-campers may use for $5. Some loops are closed to RVs or to rigs 19 feet and longer, so book a site that fits your length.

South Campground, next door, reopened on May 29, 2026, after construction, and now requires reservations, which open on recreation.gov only 14 days before arrival. It has no hookups and no showers, it takes RVs up to 40 feet, and it closes for the winter. Both campgrounds are full almost every night from mid-March to late November.

Lava Point Campground is a small, primitive campground with six sites high on the Kolob Terrace Road, about an hour from Zion Canyon. It has no water and closes in winter; reserve up to 14 days ahead.

If you need full hookups, Springdale and Hurricane both have private RV parks. And remember the new size limits: an oversize rig can still reach Watchman and South campgrounds from Springdale, but it cannot drive east through the tunnel toward Bryce Canyon.

Accessible travel at Zion

What is available for visitors with limited mobility, and the passes that help.

Shuttle buses: all Zion shuttle buses are wheelchair accessible. The ramp has a combined weight limit of 1,000 pounds and cannot take chairs longer than 48 inches or wider than 32 inches.

Driving the Scenic Drive with a disability: during shuttle season you may get a permit to drive your own vehicle up-canyon if you need vehicle-supported medical equipment, cannot ride the shuttle for medical reasons, or cannot be carried because of weight or size. Ask at the information desk at the Zion Canyon Visitor Center or the Zion Human History Museum.

Trails and buildings: the park says none of its trails meets the ADA standard, but three are considered wheelchair-friendly with some help: the Pa'rus Trail, the Riverside Walk and the Lower Emerald Pool Trail. The visitor centers, shuttles, restrooms, picnic areas and Zion Lodge are accessible, and several campsites are designated for visitors with accessibility needs. The park does not lend standard wheelchairs, but an all-terrain wheelchair can be borrowed at the Zion Canyon Visitor Center.

Service animals are welcome throughout the park, including on the shuttles, where they are the only animals allowed.

Park passes that save you money. U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability can get the free lifetime Access Pass. Visitors 62 and over can buy the Senior Pass ($80 for life, or $20 a year). Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass. Each covers entrance fees at every national park.

Traveling on dialysis? The nearest dialysis clinics are in St. George, Utah, about 40 miles southwest of Springdale. Book your treatment at a clinic there several weeks before you go; the large dialysis companies run travel desks that will arrange it with your home unit.

Zion National Park FAQ

Do I need a reservation to enter Zion National Park?

No. Zion has no entry reservation or timed-entry system, and the shuttle needs no ticket. You do need a permit for Angels Landing and reservations for the campgrounds.

How much is the Zion entrance fee in 2026?

$35 per private vehicle, $30 per motorcycle and $20 per person on foot or bicycle, each good for 7 days. Ages 15 and under are free. Cards are preferred, but cash is accepted.

Can I drive my car through Zion Canyon?

Not during shuttle season. In 2026 the Zion Canyon Scenic Drive is shuttle-only from March 7 to November 28 and from December 26 to January 2. Outside those dates you may drive it, unless it closes because parking is full. Visitors with a disability permit from the park may drive in during shuttle season.

How do I get an Angels Landing permit?

Apply on recreation.gov, either in the seasonal lottery a few months ahead or in the day-before lottery, which closes at 3 p.m. Mountain Time for the next day's hike. The permit covers the chains section beyond Scout Lookout.

Can my RV go through the Zion tunnel?

Since June 7, 2026, vehicles over 35'9" long, 7'10" wide, 11'4" tall or 50,000 lbs, and combinations over 50 feet, may not drive the Zion–Mt. Carmel Highway between Canyon Junction and the East Entrance at all. Smaller vehicles may use it as usual. Oversize rigs must enter and leave by Springdale.

Do I need a permit to hike The Narrows?

Not for a day hike from the bottom. You may walk upstream from the Temple of Sinawava as far as Big Spring without a permit. Hiking from the top down requires a wilderness permit. The Narrows closes when the river runs above 150 cubic feet per second or during a flash flood warning.

Where is the best place to stay near Zion?

Zion Lodge is the only lodging inside the park. Springdale, right at the South Entrance, is the most convenient town, and Hurricane, about 30 minutes west, is larger and usually less expensive.
