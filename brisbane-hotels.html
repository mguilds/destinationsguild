Brisbane’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Brisbane — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Brisbane is a river city, and in Brisbane the river decides the address. The CBD sits inside a tight bend of the Brisbane River with the Queen Street Mall, the Botanic Gardens and both main stations inside it. South Bank, one footbridge over the water, holds the parklands, the galleries, the museum and Streets Beach, and it is the easiest base in the city for anyone who would rather walk than ride. Fortitude Valley and James Street are the design, dining and shopping quarter a short ride north; New Farm and Kangaroo Point are quieter and greener, with the CityCat ferries doing the work. Luxury

Brisbane’s best rooms look at water rather than down at traffic. The two below are the city’s most interesting high-end houses, and neither is a tower full of identical rooms.

W Brisbane

$$$$ LuxuryCBD riverside81 North QuayRiverside pool deck

A deliberately loud, deliberately colourful hotel on North Quay, with the river on one side and the law courts and CBD behind it. Rooms are large and theatrical, the pool deck sits above the water looking across to South Bank, and the restaurants downstairs are among the busiest in the city. The CityCat terminal and the Queen Street Mall are both a few minutes on foot, and the Wheel of Brisbane is straight across the river. Ask for a river-facing room on a high floor — the city-side rooms are the same price and a fraction of the view.

Emporium Hotel South Bank

$$$$ LuxurySouth Bank267 Grey StreetRooftop pool

The best address in South Bank, a block from the parklands and the Cultural Centre, with a rooftop pool and bar that looks straight across the river at the CBD skyline. Rooms are generous, dark and quiet, the service is unusually attentive for Australia, and breakfast is a proper sit-down affair. Everything a first-time visitor comes to Brisbane for — the gallery, the museum, Streets Beach, the Saturday markets and the ferry terminal — is within a flat ten-minute walk, which matters in a subtropical summer.

Boutique

Brisbane’s small hotels cluster in Fortitude Valley, five minutes north of the centre, where the city keeps its restaurants and its best shopping.

The Calile Hotel

$$$$ LuxuryFortitude Valley48 James StreetResort-style pool

An unusually good-looking hotel on James Street, built around a long ground-floor pool and a colonnade of boutiques, and far more relaxed than its design-magazine reputation suggests. Rooms have deep balconies and tiled bathrooms, the day spa is genuinely good, and some of Brisbane’s best restaurants are in the building or on the block. It is a fifteen-minute walk or a short ride from the CBD, so take it if you would rather eat and shop locally than sightsee from the door.

Budget & Value

Brisbane is the cheapest of Australia’s three big east-coast cities, and a central room here costs noticeably less than the same room in Sydney.

Rydges South Bank Brisbane

$$ Mid-rangeSouth Bank9 Glenelg StreetSteps from the parklands

A straightforward, well-kept four-star on Glenelg Street, right beside the Brisbane Convention Centre and one block back from the South Bank parklands and the Cultural Centre. Rooms are plain and quiet rather than stylish, the upper floors look over the river to the city, and South Bank railway station and the busway are at the corner. Family-friendly

Families in Brisbane want three things: a pool, a room that is not a shoebox, and a short walk to dinner. The parklands supply the rest free of charge.

Sofitel Brisbane Central

$$$ UpscaleCBD249 Turbot StreetAbove Central station

Built directly above Central station, which makes it the easiest arrival in the city — the Airtrain from Brisbane Airport stops underneath the building, so you can land and reach your room without a taxi or a kerbside queue. Rooms are large by Australian standards, there is a heated outdoor pool, and connecting and family configurations are easy to get. The Queen Street Mall, the Botanic Gardens and the Anzac Square memorial are all a few flat blocks away.

Adults-only

Brisbane has no significant hotel that enforces a strict adults-only age policy, and we will not invent one to fill a heading. The grown-up stay here is a matter of choosing well: a high river-facing floor at W Brisbane, a quiet upper room at the Emporium, or a courtyard room at The Calile away from the pool. Ask for a floor away from lifts, pool decks and function rooms when you book, and say why — Brisbane front desks are obliging about it.

Spa & pool

Brisbane’s best water is outdoors and mostly free: Streets Beach in the South Bank parklands is a lifeguarded saltwater lagoon in the middle of the city, open year round at no charge. For a hotel pool, the rooftop at the Emporium South Bank and the long ground-floor pool at The Calile are the two best in the city, and both are described above. The day spa at The Calile takes outside bookings.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask us most when choosing where to stay in Brisbane.

What is the best area to stay in Brisbane for first-time visitors?

South Bank for most people, and the CBD if you would rather be among the shops and the stations. South Bank puts the parklands, the Queensland Art Gallery and GOMA, the museum, Streets Beach and the weekend markets outside your door, with the city centre one footbridge away. The CBD is better for arrivals and departures, because Central and Roma Street stations are in it. Fortitude Valley suits repeat visitors who come for the restaurants and the shopping.

How much do Brisbane hotels cost?

Expect roughly 120 to 200 Australian dollars a night for clean budget and value rooms, about 200 to 340 for comfortable four-star and apartment stays, and from around 450 upward for the best suites. Brisbane is usually a little cheaper than Melbourne and clearly cheaper than Sydney. Rates climb for the Ekka in August, for big matches at the Gabba and Suncorp Stadium, and through the school holidays; they are softest in the wet, humid weeks of January and February.

How do I get from Brisbane Airport to the city?

The Airtrain runs from both airport terminals into Central, Fortitude Valley and Roma Street stations and on to the Gold Coast, and it is the simplest way in — Sofitel Brisbane Central sits directly above the Central stop. Taxis and rideshare take about twenty to thirty minutes to the CBD outside the peaks. Check the current fare and timetable on the day, because both change.

Is Brisbane a walkable city?

The centre is, with one caveat: it is built on a bend in the river and on a slope, so some blocks are steeper than a map suggests, and the subtropical summer is humid. The CBD, South Bank and the Botanic Gardens are comfortably linked by footbridges and riverside paths. For anything further, the CityCat and CityHopper ferries are the pleasantest transport in the city, and the free CityHopper has long run along the inner river — confirm the current fares when you arrive.

Which Brisbane hotels take dogs?

We are not going to name one until we have read the policy on the hotel’s own site, because weight limits, fees and room-type conditions make a vague answer useless. Several central Brisbane hotels do accept dogs in specific rooms. Browse the city list on this page, shortlist two or three, and telephone to have the policy confirmed before you pay.

Do I need a car in Brisbane?

No, not for the city. Trains, buses and the river ferries cover everything a visitor needs on a tap-and-go card, and CBD parking is expensive. Hire a car only for the day trips — the Sunshine Coast, the Scenic Rim, Moreton Bay or the Gold Coast hinterland — and pick it up on the morning you leave town.
