Lake Fishing in Pennsylvania & Ohio — Lake Erie, Five Inland Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Lake Erie from both shores, Pymatuning on the state line, Raystown Lake, Lake Wallenpaupack, Indian Lake and Mosquito Creek Lake: what swims in each, the rules from both states’ fishing agencies, the licence a visitor needs, where to launch a boat, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission and the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, 19 November 2026

Pennsylvania and Ohio share one of the great walleye fisheries of the world. Lake Erie is the shallowest and warmest of the Great Lakes, and its western basin off Port Clinton fills each spring with walleye; the deeper eastern water off Erie and Conneaut adds steelhead and lake trout. Inland, the two states share Pymatuning, a big reservoir split down the middle by the state line. Pennsylvania adds Raystown Lake, a long reservoir in the ridges known for striped bass, and Lake Wallenpaupack in the Poconos. Ohio adds Indian Lake, a saugeye and crappie lake with a summer-resort town, and Mosquito Creek Lake near Warren, a walleye and crappie lake crossed by causeways.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, and anyone who wants a charter for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 19 November 2026 from the agency that sets it — the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission, Pennsylvania’s Department of Conservation and Natural Resources, and the Ohio Department of Natural Resources — and is dated. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

Lake Erie from Pennsylvania — Erie and Presque Isle Bay

Pennsylvania’s short stretch of Lake Erie runs past the city of Erie, where the sandy peninsula of Presque Isle shelters Presque Isle Bay.

Lake Erie, Presque Isle Bay and the tributaries

What swims there. Walleye and yellow perch on the open lake; steelhead, brown trout and lake trout in the deeper eastern water and, in autumn and winter, in the creeks; smallmouth bass on the rocks; muskellunge and northern pike in the bay.

How people fish it. Charter boats out of Erie for walleye and perch in summer. Shore anglers fish the bay and the Presque Isle shoreline. From autumn to spring, steelhead anglers wade the tributary creeks.

Keeping a fish (Pennsylvania waters). Walleye: at least 15 inches, 6 a day; open 1 January to 14 March and from 2 May (the first Saturday in May) to 31 December. Yellow perch: 30 a day; at least 7 inches from 1 December to 30 April, no minimum from May to November. Bass: 4 a day at 15 inches for most of the year, but from 11 April to 12 June only 1 bass a day, at least 20 inches. Steelhead and salmon at least 15 inches; trout and salmon together 5 a day until Labor Day, then 3. Muskellunge 1 at 40 inches; northern pike 2 at 24 inches.

The Lake Erie permit. Anyone 16 or older fishing the lake, the bay or the tributaries needs a Lake Erie permit (or the combined trout and Lake Erie permit) on top of an annual licence. The 1-day tourist licence includes it.

Confirmed with the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission, 19 November 2026 — 2026 Pennsylvania Fishing Summary (Lake Erie, Presque Isle Bay and tributaries) and the commission’s 21 April 2026 announcement of the 2026 Lake Erie walleye and perch limits. Tributaries have their own closures and night rules; read the Summary before you fish the creeks.

Nearest town: Erie. Our New York lake fishing guide covers Lake Erie’s New York shore, just to the east.

Lake Erie from Ohio — Port Clinton, the islands and the eastern harbours

Ohio holds the longest share of Lake Erie’s south shore, from Toledo to Conneaut. Port Clinton and Marblehead face the shallow western basin and the Lake Erie islands; Ashtabula and Conneaut sit on the deeper central basin near Pennsylvania.

Lake Erie, Ohio waters

What swims there. Walleye above all, with yellow perch, smallmouth and largemouth bass, white bass, channel catfish, freshwater drum and, in the east, steelhead.

How people fish it. Charter boats from Port Clinton, Marblehead and Sandusky to the western-basin reefs and the islands; charters and private boats from Ashtabula and Conneaut on the central basin. There is shore fishing from the piers and breakwalls at the state parks.

Keeping a fish (Ohio waters). Walleye (with sauger and saugeye): 6 a day, at least 15 inches, from 1 May 2026 to 30 April 2027. Yellow perch, no minimum size, by zone: 30 a day in the West Zone, 10 in the Central Zone and 20 in the East Zone. However many zones you fish, no more than 6 walleye and 30 perch a person a day. Black bass: 5 a day at 14 inches, except from 1 May to 26 June 2026, when the limit is 1 bass at 18 inches. Trout and salmon: at least 12 inches; 2 a day from 1 September to 15 May, 5 a day from 16 May to 31 August.

Confirmed with the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, Division of Wildlife, 19 November 2026 — Lake Erie Walleye & Yellow Perch 2026–2027 Daily Bag Limits (effective 1 May 2026 to 30 April 2027) and the 2026–27 Ohio Fishing Regulations. The perch zone lines are drawn at 82°30′ and 81°20′ west; see the department’s map before you fish near a line.

Boat ramps. East Harbor State Park near Marblehead has a marina launch (fee) and two free public ramps in the campground; Catawba Island State Park has four ramps onto Lake Erie and a fishing pier; South Bass Island State Park has a launch ramp and a fishing pier. Rental docks are at nearby Oak Point State Park.

Nearest towns: Port Clinton, Marblehead and Sandusky for the western basin; Ashtabula and Conneaut for the central basin. The Michigan lake fishing guide covers the waters to the north-west.

Pymatuning — one lake, two states, one licence from a boat

Pennsylvania’s Department of Conservation and Natural Resources gives Pymatuning Reservoir 17,088 acres, on the Ohio line west of Meadville. Ohio’s pages give smaller figures, and part of the upper lake is a wildlife sanctuary.

Pymatuning Reservoir, from Linesville and Jamestown to Andover

What swims there. Walleye and muskellunge, with crappie, black bass, bluegill, yellow perch, white bass and channel catfish.

How people fish it. In small boats — motors are limited to 20 horsepower — from the marinas and launches on both shores, from the bank, and through the ice in winter.

One licence or two? From a boat, either state’s licence is good anywhere on the lake, and both states recognise either licence for ice fishing. From the shore you need the licence of the state you are standing in: Pennsylvania on the Pennsylvania shore and the islands, Ohio on the Ohio shore.

Keeping a fish. Pymatuning has its own rules, open all year: walleye and saugeye 6 a day together, at least 15 inches; crappie 20 a day at 9 inches; bass 5 a day at 12 inches; muskellunge and tiger muskellunge 2 a day together at 30 inches. Up to 5 devices each for ice fishing in Pennsylvania.

Confirmed with the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission, the Pennsylvania Department of Conservation and Natural Resources and the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, 19 November 2026 — 2026 Pennsylvania Fishing Summary (Pymatuning), 2026–27 Ohio Fishing Regulations (Pymatuning), the Pymatuning State Park pages of both states and Ohio’s horsepower-limit list.

Boat ramps and boats. On the Pennsylvania side, Pymatuning State Park has marinas at Jamestown, Espyville and Linesville, with pontoon boats, motorboats, rowboats, canoes and kayaks to rent in season, and accessible launches at Jamestown Marina and Manning. On the Ohio side, Pymatuning State Park lists ramps at Padanaram, Birches Landing, Cabin Beach, the campground and New Bowers, and boat rental at Birches Landing. Both parks have cabins.

Nearest towns: Linesville and Meadville in Pennsylvania (Jamestown, Pennsylvania, and Andover, Ohio, are the lake towns at the south and west ends; our partner’s search could not pin either town, so they have no button — use Linesville or Meadville).

Raystown Lake — Pennsylvania’s deep mountain lake

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers gives Raystown Lake 8,300 surface acres, in the ridges of Huntingdon County, south of the town of Huntingdon.

Raystown Lake, south of Huntingdon

What swims there. The Corps of Engineers names striped, largemouth and smallmouth bass, muskie, walleye and crappie. Trout are managed here too.

How people fish it. By boat — the Corps says there is no limit on boat size or horsepower, so summer weekends are busy — with guides chasing striped bass in the cool months. Houseboats and pontoons can be rented at the marina.

Keeping a fish. Raystown has no striped-bass rule of its own, so Pennsylvania’s statewide rule applies: striped bass and hybrid striped bass together 2 a day, at least 20 inches, all year. On the lake and the Raystown Branch, trout are open all year at 7 inches, 5 a day in the regular season and 3 after Labor Day. Walleye and bass follow the statewide seasons in the licence section below.

Confirmed with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission, 19 November 2026 — Corps of Engineers Raystown Lake map brochure (lake size, species, seven public launch ramps, no horsepower limit) and the 2026 Pennsylvania Fishing Summary (Huntingdon County special rules and the statewide striped bass rule).

Boat ramps. The Corps lists seven public launch ramps, some in fee areas. Seven Points Marina has a launch too.

Nearest town: Huntingdon.

Lake Wallenpaupack — the big lake of the Poconos

The Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission gives Lake Wallenpaupack 5,700 acres, about 13 miles long, in Pike and Wayne counties. It is owned and run by Brookfield Renewable.

Lake Wallenpaupack, from Hawley to Lakeville and Greentown

What swims there. Smallmouth and largemouth bass, walleye, pickerel, yellow perch, trout, striped bass, channel catfish and panfish. The commission stocks striped bass (pure and hybrid), walleye and brown trout.

How people fish it. By boat, and through the ice in winter with a guide. In summer the lake is a holiday lake with heavy weekend traffic; early mornings and the cool months are calmer.

Rules on the water. No horsepower limit. From the Saturday before Memorial Day to Labor Day, the speed limit is 45 mph from sunrise to sunset on weekends and holidays, and 25 mph between sunset and sunrise. Fish limits follow Pennsylvania’s statewide rules in the licence section below.

Confirmed with the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission, 19 November 2026 — the commission’s Lake Wallenpaupack page and the 2026 Pennsylvania Fishing Summary. Before you eat what you keep: Pennsylvania’s 2026 advisory lists walleye from the whole lake at one meal a month (mercury).

Boat ramps. The commission’s free launch is at Mangan Cove, off Route 590 within half a mile of Route 6. Brookfield’s launches at Caffrey, Ironwood Point, Ledgedale and Wilsonville charge a fee, waived for campers.

Nearest towns: Hawley and Lakeville.

Indian Lake — Ohio’s saugeye lake

The Ohio Department of Natural Resources gives Indian Lake 5,100 acres, in Logan County north-west of Columbus, with the village of Russells Point on its south shore.

Indian Lake, from Russells Point to Lakeview

What swims there. The department calls it one of Ohio’s best saugeye lakes. Crappie, largemouth bass, white bass, bluegill, yellow perch, channel catfish and flathead catfish too.

How people fish it. From boats among the islands and channels, from the shore and from the park’s accessible kayak launch. There is no horsepower limit.

Keeping a fish. Crappie: 30 a day, at least 9 inches (a rule for this lake). Saugeye and walleye follow Ohio’s statewide inland rule, 6 a day; Indian Lake is not on the 2026–27 list of lakes with a 15-inch minimum. An older department page still mentions a 15-inch saugeye minimum here — the 2026–27 regulations are the ones that count.

Confirmed with the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, 19 November 2026 — Indian Lake State Park page, the Indian Lake fishing-area page and the 2026–27 Ohio Fishing Regulations. Ohio’s fish advisory lists crappie, bluegill, channel catfish, carp and saugeye from Indian Lake as unrestricted.

Boat ramps. Indian Lake State Park lists ramps at Lakeview Harbor (2 lanes), Blackhawk Marina (2), Moundwood Marina (6), Chippewa Marina (2) and Cranetown Island (1), and an accessible kayak launch at Moundwood.

Nearest towns: Russells Point and Lakeview on the lake; Bellefontaine, the county seat, for more hotels.

Mosquito Creek Lake — walleye and crappie near Warren

Built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in 1944, Mosquito Creek Lake lies in Trumbull County north of Warren. The Ohio Department of Natural Resources gives it 7,850 acres on one page and 7,241 on another.

Mosquito Creek Lake, from Cortland to the State Route 88 causeway

What swims there. Walleye, bass, crappie and northern pike.

How people fish it. By boat from the state park ramps, and from the shore. Unlimited horsepower is allowed on parts of the lake; there is a no-wake zone within 300 feet of shore and a 10 mph limit north of the State Route 88 causeway. A buoy line marks a wildlife refuge at the north end.

Keeping a fish. Walleye, saugeye and sauger together 6 a day, at least 15 inches; crappie 30 a day, at least 9 inches (rules for this lake).

Confirmed with the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, 19 November 2026 — Mosquito Lake State Park page, the Mosquito Creek Lake fishing-area page and the 2026–27 Ohio Fishing Regulations. Before you eat what you keep: Ohio’s advisory lists carp at one meal a month and bluegill at one meal a week here.

Boat ramps. Mosquito Lake State Park lists the Main ramp, the Marina Concession ramp, a campground ramp for campers, the Causeway ramps, the Wildlife launch area beside the refuge buoy line, the Trumbull Boat Club and State Route 305. Mosquito Lake Marina rents fishing boats, pontoons and kayaks in season.

Nearest town: Warren (Cortland is the village on the lake; our partner’s search sends “Cortland” to New York, so it has no button).

The licence and the limits, plainly

Two states, two licences — except from a boat on Pymatuning, where either one will do.

Pennsylvania fishing licences (2026)

Who needs one. Anyone 16 and older. Under-16s need no licence and no permits.

For a visitor. Non-resident annual $60.97; 7-day tourist $39.47; 3-day tourist $31.97; 1-day tourist $31.97 (the 1-day licences are not valid from 15 March to 30 April). The 1-day tourist licence includes the trout and Lake Erie permits.

For a Pennsylvanian. Resident annual (16–64) $27.97; senior annual (65+) $14.47; senior lifetime $86.97; resident 1-day $14.47.

Permits. Lake Erie permit $9.97; trout permit $14.97; combined trout and Lake Erie permit $20.97. All prices include a $1.00 agent fee and a $0.97 transaction fee.

Fish for free. Pennsylvania’s 2026 Fish-for-Free days were 24 May and 4 July. The 2027 dates were not yet published when we checked.

Confirmed with the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission, 19 November 2026 — the commission’s 2026 annual fishing licence pricing page and the 2026 Pennsylvania Fishing Summary. Prices change. Buy from the agency or a licensed agent.

Ohio fishing licences (2026–27)

Who needs one. Anyone 16 and older. A one-year licence is good for 365 days from the day you buy it.

For a visitor. The 2026–27 regulations booklet lists non-resident 1-day $27.04, non-resident 3-day $52.00 and non-resident 1-year $76.96. Ohio’s licence web page showed lower non-resident prices on the same day, so check the price when you buy.

For an Ohioan. Resident 1-year $25.00; resident 1-day $14.00; resident senior 1-year (65 and over) $10.00.

Free fishing days. Ohio residents fished free on 20 and 21 June 2026. The 2027 dates were not yet published when we checked.

Confirmed with the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, Division of Wildlife, 19 November 2026 — 2026–27 Ohio Fishing Regulations (effective 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027). Prices change. Buy from the agency or a licensed agent.

Boats, kayaks and life jackets

Pennsylvania. A kayak, canoe or other unpowered boat launched at a Fish and Boat Commission lake or access, or at a state park or state forest, needs a boat registration, a commission launch permit or a state parks launch permit; other states’ launch permits are not recognised. From 1 November to 30 April, everyone on a boat under 16 feet, or in any canoe, kayak or paddleboard, must wear a life jacket. Children 12 and under must wear one while under way on boats of 20 feet or less.

Ohio. Canoes, kayaks, pedal boats and inflatables must be registered (paddleboards and float tubes are exempt). Every boat must carry a wearable life jacket for each person aboard, and children under 10 must wear one on a boat under 18 feet.

Confirmed with the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission (unpowered boat and cold-weather life jacket pages) and the Ohio Department of Natural Resources (boat registration and life jacket pages), 19 November 2026.

Eating what you keep

Ohio’s advisory (Ohio Department of Health, updated May 2026) says no more than one meal a week of most sport fish and two of yellow perch and sunfish; from Lake Erie, steelhead, smallmouth bass, white bass, channel catfish, freshwater drum and lake trout are one meal a month. Pennsylvania’s 2026 advice is no more than one meal a week of sport fish, with Lake Erie walleye, steelhead, smallmouth bass and several other species at one meal a month, and walleye from Lake Wallenpaupack at one meal a month. Advice changes; read the current tables for the water you fish before you keep fish to eat.

Questions

Can I fish Pymatuning with an Ohio licence or a Pennsylvania licence?

Yes, from a boat. Pymatuning Reservoir sits on the state line, and both states honour each other’s fishing licence anywhere on the lake when you fish from a boat, and on the ice. From the shore it is different: on the Pennsylvania shore and on the islands you need a Pennsylvania licence, and on the Ohio shore an Ohio licence. Pymatuning has its own limits, the same in both states: walleye and saugeye 6 a day in total at 15 inches or longer, crappie 20 a day at 9 inches, bass 5 a day at 12 inches, and muskellunge 2 a day at 30 inches. Checked with the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission and the Ohio Department of Natural Resources on 19 November 2026.

What does a fishing licence cost a visitor in Pennsylvania and Ohio?

Pennsylvania, from the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission’s 2026 price list: non-resident annual $60.97, a 3-day tourist licence $31.97, a 7-day tourist licence $39.47 and a 1-day tourist licence $31.97 (fees included). On Lake Erie and its tributaries an annual licence also needs a Lake Erie permit ($9.97); the 1-day tourist licence includes it. Anglers under 16 need no Pennsylvania licence. Ohio, from the 2026–27 Ohio Fishing Regulations booklet: non-resident 1-day $27.04, 3-day $52.00 and 1-year $76.96; residents pay $25.00 for a year. Ohio’s own licence web page showed lower non-resident prices on the day we checked, so confirm with the Ohio Department of Natural Resources before you buy. Anglers under 16 need no Ohio licence.

How many walleye can I keep on Lake Erie?

Six a day, at least 15 inches long, in both states. In Ohio waters that limit runs from 1 May 2026 to 30 April 2027. In Pennsylvania waters the limit is 6 at 15 inches, and walleye season is closed from 15 March to the first Saturday in May (2 May in 2026). Ohio also caps the day at no more than 6 walleye and 30 yellow perch per person, whichever zones you fish.

Do I need to register my kayak?

In Pennsylvania, an unpowered boat launched at a Fish and Boat Commission lake or access, or at a state park or state forest, must carry a boat registration, a Fish and Boat Commission launch permit or a state parks launch permit — one is enough, and other states’ launch permits are not recognised. In Ohio, canoes, kayaks, pedal boats and inflatables must be registered; paddleboards and float tubes are exempt. Pennsylvania also requires everyone in a canoe, a kayak or a boat under 16 feet to wear a life jacket from 1 November to 30 April.

Is Lake Erie fish safe to eat?

Read the advice before you keep fish to eat. Ohio’s sport fish advisory (updated May 2026) gives Lake Erie walleye the statewide rate of one meal a week and yellow perch two meals a week, and limits steelhead, smallmouth bass, white bass, channel catfish, freshwater drum and lake trout from Lake Erie to one meal a month. Pennsylvania’s 2026 advisory lists Lake Erie walleye, steelhead, smallmouth bass and several other species at one meal a month. Children and anyone pregnant should take extra care.

Seven honest gaps

Ohio’s non-resident prices. The 2026–27 regulations booklet and Ohio’s licence web page gave different non-resident prices on 19 November 2026. We print the booklet’s; check with the Ohio Department of Natural Resources (1-800-WILDLIFE) when you buy.

Raystown launch names and fees. The Corps of Engineers says there are seven public launch ramps, some in fee areas, but we could not read the list of ramps or the fees. Ask the Raystown Lake office before you go.

Lake sizes differ by source. Pennsylvania and Ohio give Pymatuning different acreages, and two Ohio pages disagree about Mosquito Creek Lake. We name the source each time.

Mosquito Creek’s refuge. A buoy line marks a wildlife refuge at the north end. We could not confirm its exact boundary or what it restricts; obey the buoys and the signs.

Lake Wallenpaupack’s winter level. The lake is a power reservoir and its level changes; we could not confirm a winter drawdown schedule from an official page.

Towns with no button. Jamestown (Pennsylvania), Andover and Cortland (Ohio) are real lake towns, but our partner’s search sends their names elsewhere, so they have no booking button. Use the neighbouring towns.

Hotel lists in small towns. Our hotel buttons are pinned to town numbers checked two ways with our own resolver. We do not open Trip.com’s hotel-list pages to count the hotels, so small towns may show few.
