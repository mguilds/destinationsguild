Where to Stay in Chitwan

Where to Stay in Chitwan — Sauraha, Barauli & the Park

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Chitwan is the other Nepal: flat, hot, green and full of animals, a few hours south of the hills in the lowland belt called the Terai. The national park holds one of the strongest populations of greater one-horned rhino anywhere, along with tigers, gaur, sloth bears, crocodiles and something over five hundred kinds of bird. Almost everyone sleeps outside the park boundary: in Sauraha, the busy village on the Rapti, in the quieter west around Barauli and Meghauli, or in a lodge along the river in between.

Choosing Your Area in Chitwan

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

Sauraha, on the Rapti

The main visitor village, on the river directly opposite the park. Everything is here — guides, jeeps, canoes, gear, restaurants, the elephant breeding centre and the sunset crowd on the riverbank — and it is the easiest place to arrive at with nothing arranged. It is also the busiest, and in high season the main lane is thick with visitors. Rooms set back from the strip, in gardens, are noticeably quieter for very little more.

The quiet west: Barauli, Meghauli and the river villages

An hour or so west of Sauraha, the villages along the park’s northern boundary are what Sauraha was twenty years ago: a few community-run lodges and homestays, Tharu villages, almost no traffic and the same park over the river. Choose this side if the point of the trip is quiet and you are happy to have your days arranged for you rather than shopping for them in the street.

Riverside lodges along the boundary

Scattered between the two, a number of larger lodges sit in their own grounds on the river with elephant grass and forest opposite. They are the comfortable option — pools, verandas, resident naturalists, everything included — and they suit travellers who want the wildlife without organising any of it. You will be driven to the village rather than walking to it.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Chitwan trip goes.

How to see the park, and what the Guild recommends

A jeep safari covers ground, a dugout canoe down the Rapti at first light is the best hour of the day for birds and crocodiles, and a walk with two licensed guides is the way to see the forest properly and feel appropriately small. The Guild does not recommend elephant-back safaris: a jeep, a canoe and a pair of boots see as much, cost less and ask nothing of an animal. Park entry permits are arranged through your lodge or at the park office, and every activity inside the park goes with a licensed guide.

The seasons, and the tall grass

October to March is the comfortable stretch: dry, mild, and good for everything. Through April and May it is very hot, but the grass is lower and the waterholes fewer, which concentrates the animals and makes it, for many, the best wildlife month of all. The monsoon, June to September, is wet, leech-ridden and often partly closed. After the rains the elephant grass stands well over head height until the annual cut in the new year, which genuinely affects what you will see from a jeep.

Getting there, and the heat

Chitwan is five to six hours by road from Kathmandu and about four from Pokhara, on routes that tourist buses run daily; there is also a short domestic flight to Bharatpur, the nearest town. The Terai is hot and humid for most of the year and the mosquitoes are serious at dusk — bring repellent, long sleeves for the evening, and do not count on your lodge having air conditioning unless it says so.

Chitwan Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Chitwan.

Where is the best place to stay in Chitwan?

Sauraha for convenience, company and the ability to arrange everything on the spot. The western villages around Barauli and Meghauli for quiet and a more local stay. A riverside lodge if you would rather have the whole thing organised for you. All three look at the same park across the same river.

How likely are you to see a rhino in Chitwan?

Very likely, and often from a canoe or a jeep in the first morning — Chitwan’s one-horned rhino are numerous and not especially shy. Tigers are a different matter: they are there in good numbers and are seen by a small fraction of visitors, usually by people who have given the park several days. Treat a tiger as a bonus rather than a plan.

How many nights do you need in Chitwan?

Two nights gives you one full day and is the minimum that is worth the journey. Three is better and lets you do a canoe morning, a jeep day and a walk without rushing any of them. Build the long road transfer into your thinking rather than treating arrival day as a sightseeing day.

When is the best time to visit Chitwan?

October to March for comfort, April and May for the wildlife. The cool dry months are pleasant and green and suit most travellers. The hot pre-monsoon months are hard going in the middle of the day, but the grass is shorter, the animals gather at water, and sightings are at their best. Avoid the monsoon unless rain and leeches are no obstacle.
