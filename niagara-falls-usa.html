Niagara Falls, New York

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

We have the view. They have the closeness. If you have only ever seen the Falls from Ontario, you have only seen half of them — and the other half is one bridge away.

Find Hotels on the US Side Tickets & Tours

Every Canadian knows the Falls, and most of us think we've seen them. We haven't — not entirely. From Ontario you get the great panoramic view straight into the Horseshoe, and it is magnificent, and no American vantage point matches it. But the water is over there, across the gorge, behind a railing and a row of hotels. Cross the Rainbow Bridge and something different happens: on the American side you walk out onto Goat Island, which sits between the two cataracts, with the rapids racing past on both sides. You descend the Cave of the Winds walkways until you are standing at the foot of Bridal Veil Falls, at the edge of the Hurricane Deck, being comprehensively soaked by a waterfall from below. And you do it inside Niagara Falls State Park — the oldest state park in the United States, laid out by Frederick Law Olmsted, the man who designed Central Park — which is green, quiet and deliberately unspoiled in a way that Clifton Hill is emphatically not. It is a twenty-minute drive and a completely different experience.

The geography is worth getting straight. The Horseshoe Falls — the big one — is mostly on the Canadian side, which is why our view is the famous one. The American Falls and the smaller Bridal Veil Falls are on the New York side, separated from each other by Luna Island and from the Horseshoe by Goat Island. That island is the key to the whole American experience: you can stand at Terrapin Point on its far tip and look directly down into the Horseshoe from above — a vertigo you simply cannot get from Ontario.

The American side is also markedly cheaper. Hotel rates in Niagara Falls, NY, are generally lower than on the Ontario side, and Buffalo — thirty minutes south, with a proper airport, a serious art museum and the chicken wing in its birthplace — makes an easy and underrated base.

Our side or theirs? Honestly.

The Canadian side wins on

  • The view — the full Horseshoe, head-on. Nothing beats it.
  • The nightly illumination and fireworks, seen best from Ontario.
  • Hotel rooms with a genuine falls view from the window.
  • Restaurants, nightlife and Clifton Hill, if that's what you want.

The American side wins on

  • Getting close — the Cave of the Winds decks, right at the foot of the falls.
  • Goat Island: standing between the two cataracts, rapids on both sides.
  • Terrapin Point — looking down into the Horseshoe from above.
  • The park itself: Olmsted's design, green and calm, not a midway.
  • Price. Hotels, food and parking all cost less.

The correct answer, if you have the time: see it from both. They are twenty minutes apart, and together they are one of the great sights on earth.

Crossing: what a Canadian needs to know

Documents. At the Rainbow Bridge and other land crossings, a passport, NEXUS card or enhanced driver's licence is normally accepted. Everyone in the vehicle needs their own — including children. If you cross more than twice a year, NEXUS will pay for itself in saved queueing.

The duty-free trap. This is the one that catches day-trippers. For a trip of under 24 hours there is no personal exemption at all — everything you buy is technically subject to duty and taxes on the way home. After 24 hours you may generally claim up to CAD $200; after 48 hours or more, up to CAD $800, which is when the alcohol and tobacco allowances open up. If you're crossing for the outlet mall, that arithmetic should shape your plans. Keep receipts. Declare everything.

Insurance and the car. Buy travel medical insurance even for a day trip — your provincial plan covers almost nothing in the US. Confirm your auto policy covers travel in the United States, and carry proof.

Rules change. Verify with U.S. Customs and Border Protection and the CBSA before you go. Nothing here is legal or customs advice.

Why cross to the American side

Four things you cannot do from Ontario.

Cave of the Winds

Wooden walkways descend into the gorge to the Hurricane Deck, right at the base of Bridal Veil Falls. You will be soaked. That's the point.

Goat Island

Stand on the island between the American and Horseshoe falls, with the rapids tearing past on either side of you.

Terrapin Point

Look straight down into the Horseshoe from directly above its lip. Genuinely unnerving, and unavailable from home.

Olmsted's park

The oldest state park in America, designed by the architect of Central Park — trees and paths, not a midway.

Top things to do

A full day, easily. Two if you go up the gorge.

● Cave of the Winds

The signature American experience — decks at the foot of Bridal Veil Falls. Seasonal, so check dates before building a trip around it.

● Maid of the Mist

The boat into the basin of the Horseshoe, from the US landing. Also seasonal. Yes, it's touristy. Yes, do it.

● Goat Island & Terrapin Point

Walk the island, stand between the falls, then look down into the Horseshoe from its edge. Free, and the best hour here.

● Whirlpool & Devil's Hole

Downriver, the gorge trails above the whirlpool rapids — wild, forested and almost empty compared to the falls.

● Old Fort Niagara

At the river's mouth on Lake Ontario: an 18th-century fort that changed hands between France, Britain and the US.

● The outlets

Fashion Outlets of Niagara Falls is a Canadian institution. Mind the 24-hour rule before you fill the trunk.

Where to stay

Niagara Falls, New York itself puts you a short walk from the state park and the falls, and its rooms are generally cheaper than the Ontario side — though the town is quieter and considerably less polished than Clifton Hill, and you should look at the specific hotel rather than the postcode. Buffalo, thirty minutes south, is the underrated choice: far more hotels, better restaurants, an excellent art museum, and an airport — many Canadians already drive down for cheaper US flights. Lewiston, on the gorge downriver, is the pretty, quiet option with some genuinely good places to eat. And if the view from the window is the whole point of the trip, book on the Ontario side and cross for the day — nobody will judge you.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Late spring to autumn is when the falls are at their fullest and the signature attractions run — but note that the Cave of the Winds decks and the Maid of the Mist boats are seasonal, so confirm the dates before planning a trip around them. Winter is extraordinary in a different register: ice shelves, frozen spray on the trees, and the falls floodlit at night — but the boats and the lower decks are shut.

Getting there

Driving is how most of us do it: the Rainbow Bridge crosses right at the falls, on foot or by car. The Queenston–Lewiston Bridge is often quicker if the Rainbow is backed up, and the Whirlpool Bridge is NEXUS-holders only. Flying: Buffalo Niagara (BUF) is 30–40 minutes away. Check border wait times before you set out — they swing wildly on long weekends.

Niagara Falls (US side) FAQ

Which side is better, Canadian or American?

They do different jobs. The Canadian side has the better view — you look straight into the Horseshoe, and that panorama is unbeatable. The American side gets you closer to the water: the Cave of the Winds walkways put you at the foot of Bridal Veil Falls, and the state park is quieter, greener and more natural than Clifton Hill. If you've only ever seen it from Ontario, you've only seen half of it.

What do Canadians need to cross?

At a land crossing like the Rainbow Bridge, a passport, NEXUS card or enhanced driver's licence is normally accepted — and everyone in the car needs their own, children included. Bring proof of vehicle insurance and confirm your auto policy covers US travel. Rules change; check with U.S. Customs and Border Protection before you go.

What can I bring back duty-free from a day trip?

When is the best time to visit the American side?

Late spring through autumn, when the falls are at their best and the Cave of the Winds and Maid of the Mist are running — both are seasonal, so check dates. Winter is beautiful in a completely different way, with ice in the gorge and the falls lit at night, but the boats and lower decks are closed.
