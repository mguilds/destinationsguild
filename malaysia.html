Why visit Malaysia

Few countries pack so much variety — cities, culture, food, beaches and jungle — into one easy, English-friendly trip.

Cities & skylines

The Petronas Towers and KL’s glittering skyline, colonial George Town and Malacca, and the buzz of some of Asia’s best shopping and nightlife.

The food

Arguably Asia’s finest street food — Malay, Chinese and Indian traditions meeting in nasi lemak, char kway teow, laksa, satay and roti, from hawker stalls to heritage kopitiams.

Beaches & islands

The Andaman sands of Langkawi, the Perhentians and Redang, and the reefs of Borneo — warm seas and world-class diving on both coasts.

Rainforest & wildlife

Some of the oldest rainforest on earth, orangutans and proboscis monkeys in Borneo, and mighty Mount Kinabalu rising over Sabah.

How the country divides up

Kuala Lumpur & the west coast

Most trips begin in Kuala Lumpur, and they should — the Petronas Towers, the Batu Caves, superb malls and some of Asia’s best street food. From the capital the west coast runs north through the tea-clad Cameron Highlands and colonial Ipoh to the island of Langkawi, with its rainforest resorts and duty-free beaches.

Penang & the north

Penang is the heart of Malaysian food and heritage — UNESCO-listed George Town, clan jetties, street art and hawker legends, with the beaches of Batu Ferringhi on the north shore. It makes an easy, rewarding pairing with the highlands and Langkawi.

Malacca & the south

Malacca (Melaka) is Malaysia’s most historic town, a Straits trading port where Portuguese, Dutch, British and Peranakan histories layer around the red square and Jonker Street. To the south, Johor Bahru is the busy gateway to Singapore, with theme parks and outlet malls.

Borneo — Sabah & Sarawak

Across the sea on the island of Borneo, Sabah offers Mount Kinabalu, the islands off Kota Kinabalu and the diving of Sipadan, while Sarawak centres on charming Kuching, its national parks and longhouse country. This is Malaysia’s wild, adventurous side.

Top things to do in Malaysia

The experiences worth building a trip around.

The Petronas Towers

Kuala Lumpur’s iconic twin towers — the skybridge and observation deck by day, and the fountains and light of KLCC Park by night.

Feast in Penang

Eat your way through George Town’s hawker stalls and kopitiams — char kway teow, assam laksa, cendol — arguably the best street food in Asia.

Rainforest & wildlife in Borneo

Meet orangutans at Sepilok, cruise the Kinabatangan River, dive Sipadan, or climb Mount Kinabalu — Sabah packs in a lifetime of nature.

Island time on Langkawi

Ride the SkyCab cable car, cruise the mangroves, and unwind on Pantai Cenang or at a rainforest resort on the Andaman Sea.

Historic Malacca

Wander the red square, the Melaka River and Jonker Street’s antique shops and night market in Malaysia’s most storied town.

The Batu Caves & highlands

Climb the rainbow steps to the Batu Caves shrine outside KL, then cool off in the tea plantations of the Cameron Highlands.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

December to April is the sweet spot for the west coast — Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi — with the driest, sunniest weather and calm island seas. This is peak season, so book ahead.

The east-coast islands and much of Borneo run the opposite way, best from about March to October; the east coast largely closes during the November–February monsoon. Malaysia is warm and humid all year.

See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Malaysia for weather, crowds and prices through the year.

Getting there

Kuala Lumpur International (KUL) is the main long-haul gateway, a major regional hub with flights across Asia, the Gulf and beyond, and home to AirAsia’s low-cost network.

Domestic flights make the country easy to string together — Penang (PEN), Langkawi (LGK), Kota Kinabalu (BKI) and Kuching (KCH) are all quick hops from KL, far faster than the long road and sea journeys.

The currency is the Malaysian ringgit. Cards are widely accepted in cities and resorts; carry some cash for hawker stalls, markets and small towns. Most visitors enter visa-free for up to 30–90 days — confirm the current rules with the official Malaysian authority before you travel.

Malaysia travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Malaysia?

For the west coast — Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi — December to April is driest and sunniest. The east-coast islands are best from March to October and largely close in the November–February monsoon, while Borneo is drier from March to September. Malaysia is warm and humid all year, so there is always a sunny region to head for.

What currency is used in Malaysia?

The Malaysian ringgit (MYR, symbol RM). Cards are widely accepted in hotels, malls, restaurants and shops in the cities and resorts, but carry cash for hawker stalls, markets, taxis and small towns. ATMs are widespread. It is best to pay in ringgit rather than accept a terminal’s home-currency conversion.

Do I need a visa to visit Malaysia?

Most visitors, including US, UK, Canadian, Australian and EU citizens, can enter visa-free for tourism, typically for up to 30 to 90 days depending on nationality. Malaysia also uses a Digital Arrival Card that visitors complete online before arrival. Requirements change, so always confirm the current rules with the official Malaysian authority before you travel.

How many days do you need in Malaysia?

For a first trip, 10 to 14 days lets you pair Kuala Lumpur with Penang and Langkawi at a comfortable pace, with time for the Batu Caves and perhaps the Cameron Highlands. Add three or four days for a Borneo add-on to Kota Kinabalu or the wildlife of Sabah. With a week, focus on KL plus one of Penang or Langkawi.

Is Malaysia safe for travellers?

Malaysia is generally safe and very easy for visitors, with widespread English, good transport and a warm welcome. Take normal city precautions against petty theft and pickpockets in crowded areas, use licensed or app taxis, and take care with traffic. Check your government’s current travel advisory for any specific regional notes before you go.
