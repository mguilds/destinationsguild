Pattaya Travel Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated September 2026

Thailand's nearest beach resort to Bangkok — a great teak temple on the shore, islands a boat-ride off the beach, tropical gardens inland, and a nightlife that needs no introduction.

Pattaya is the beach that Bangkok reaches for. Two hours south of the capital on the Eastern Gulf coast, it grew from a fishing village into a full-blown resort city in a single generation, and it carries a reputation that arrives before you do. But Pattaya is more than its reputation. Behind the neon there is a curve of sand at Jomtien long enough to lose the crowds, a boat ride to Koh Larn where the water turns clear and green, one of the strangest and most beautiful buildings in Thailand — a vast temple carved entirely from teak — and inland, a tropical garden that ranks among the finest in the world. It is a city of two faces, and the trick to enjoying it is knowing which face you are looking at, and choosing where you stand.

Pattaya is really a string of districts along one long bay. Central Pattaya is the heart of it — Beach Road, the shopping malls, the restaurants and, at the southern end, the pier and Walking Street. Jomtien, over the headland to the south, is where the beach opens out into a long, quieter stretch that families and long-stayers gravitate to. Naklua, at the northern end, is calmer and a notch more upscale, with the old fishing harbour and the Sanctuary of Truth on its shore. And on the hill between the two beaches sits Pratamnak, a leafy pocket with the viewpoint and the Big Buddha.

The thing every honest guide has to say about Pattaya, because it shapes the whole trip: this is a city of two personalities. It has some of the best family attractions in Thailand and, a few streets away, one of Asia's most notorious red-light nightlife scenes, concentrated on Walking Street in central Pattaya. The two rarely overlap — but where you stay decides which one is on your doorstep. Families and couples who want the beaches, the gardens and the temple without the strip should base themselves in Jomtien or Naklua, and simply never go near Walking Street after dark.

Pattaya is inexpensive by Western standards and offers extraordinary value away from the tourist strips — a plate of pad thai at a street stall costs a fraction of a hotel breakfast, and a songthaew (the shared pickup taxis, called baht buses here) runs the length of the beach for small change. Thailand keeps a single time zone, Indochina Time (UTC+7), so there is no clock-changing to worry about within the country.

Why visit Pattaya

Four reasons Thailand's easiest beach city earns its place.

Closest to Bangkok

The nearest real beach resort to the capital — two hours by road, no domestic flight needed. Ideal for tacking a few sea days onto a Bangkok trip.

Big family attractions

Tropical gardens, animal parks, water parks and island beaches. Few resort towns pack this much for children into such a small radius.

Famous nightlife

Love it or avoid it, Pattaya's after-dark reputation is real — a wall of bars, cabaret and neon that draws visitors from around the world.

Culture & viewpoints

Beyond the strip: the teak Sanctuary of Truth, the hilltop Big Buddha, and the viewpoint over the whole sweep of the bay.

Top things to do in Pattaya

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Pattaya & Jomtien beaches

Central Pattaya beach is busy and lively; Jomtien, over the headland, is longer and calmer — the better bet for a full day of sand and sea.

● The Sanctuary of Truth

A vast temple on the shore at Naklua, carved entirely from teak, every surface covered in wooden figures. One of the most extraordinary buildings in Thailand.

● Koh Larn day trip

A short ferry from the pier reaches Coral Island, where the water clears and the beaches are quieter. The classic Pattaya day out.

● Nong Nooch Garden

A world-class tropical garden south of the city — French-formal parterres, cycad collections and orchid houses, with cultural shows and elephants.

● Walking Street

The famous — and infamous — nightlife strip at the south end of the bay. Loud, neon-lit and unmistakably Pattaya; approach on your own terms.

● Viewpoint & Big Buddha

Climb Pratamnak Hill for the Pattaya Viewpoint over the bay, then visit the Big Buddha at Wat Phra Yai just above it.

Where to stay in Pattaya

Choose by the trip you want. Central Pattaya Beach puts you in the thick of it — walk out of the hotel into the shopping, the restaurants and, at the south end, the nightlife; it is busy, loud and never quiet. Jomtien, over the headland, has a longer and calmer beach and suits families and long-stayers who want the sea without the strip. Naklua, at the north end, is calmer still and more upscale, with the old harbour and the Sanctuary of Truth nearby. And Pratamnak Hill sits quietly between the two beaches — green, residential and handy for both. Families and couples looking for a beach holiday do best in Jomtien or Naklua; anyone who wants the full central buzz should base in Central Pattaya.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

November–February is the cool-dry best: warm, sunny days and lower humidity, the peak of the season. March–May is hot, and can be very hot by April. June–October is the rainy season, though the showers are usually short and heavy rather than all-day, and prices are at their lowest.

Getting there

Most people fly into Bangkok — Suvarnabhumi (BKK) or Don Mueang (DMK) — and take a 90-minute to two-hour bus, minivan or private car south to Pattaya. U-Tapao (UTP), about 40 minutes south of the city, handles some regional and low-cost flights and can be the quicker way in.

Pattaya travel FAQ

How do you get from Bangkok to Pattaya?

It's a straightforward journey of about 90 minutes to two hours by road. Most people fly into Bangkok — Suvarnabhumi (BKK) or Don Mueang (DMK) — and take a bus, minivan or private car south to Pattaya. Buses run frequently from Suvarnabhumi and the Bangkok terminals, and private transfers are inexpensive by Western standards. Traffic on the motorway is the main variable.

Is Pattaya family-friendly?

Yes — with a caveat. Pattaya has some of the best family attractions in Thailand: Nong Nooch Tropical Garden, the Sanctuary of Truth, Koh Larn's beaches, water parks and animal parks. It also has a well-known red-light nightlife, concentrated around Walking Street in central Pattaya. Families do best by staying in Jomtien or Naklua, away from the strip, and simply not going near Walking Street after dark.

When is the best time to visit Pattaya?

November to February is the best window — the cool, dry season, with warm sunny days and lower humidity. March to May is hot. June to October is the rainy season, though the showers are usually short and heavy rather than all-day, and prices are lower.

Where should I stay — Pattaya Beach or Jomtien?

Central Pattaya Beach puts you in the middle of everything, within walking distance of the nightlife and restaurants, but it's busy and loud. Jomtien, just south, has a longer, quieter beach and suits families and long-stayers. Naklua, to the north, is calmer and more upscale, and Pratamnak Hill sits quietly between the two.
