Belize, month by month

Best Time to Visit Belize

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Belize keeps two seasons rather than four, and the whole of the planning question is which half of the year you are in and how far south you are going. The dry months from late November to April are the reliable ones and the expensive ones. The green months from June to November are hotter, wetter, cheaper and emptier, with September and October the wettest of all and the peak of the hurricane season. What follows is the year as it actually behaves, including the shoulders where most of the value sits.

The short answer

Come between late January and April if you want the most dependable weather of the year and you are willing to pay for it. The rain has usually stopped, the humidity has eased, the sea inside the reef is flat and clear, and the inland roads and cave trips are all open. If the budget matters more than the certainty, late November and early December, or May and early June, give you much of the same weather at lower prices either side of the crowds. July and August are hot, wet and busy with families, and September and October are the wettest and the quietest months of the year, which is either the bargain or the mistake depending on your temperament.

Belize month by month

January and February

The heart of the dry season and, for many people, the best weather Belize has. Days are warm rather than punishing, humidity is at its lowest, and the water inside the reef is calm and clear. This is also when northers — cold fronts pushing down from North America — can arrive, bringing a day or two of grey skies, wind and a short chop that cancels boat trips. They pass in a day or so. Hotels are full, prices are high, and the cayes are busy.

March and April

Still dry and getting hotter, with the heat building noticeably through April. Visibility on the reef is excellent, the inland trails are firm, and this is the classic window for the caves and the Maya sites. Easter is a domestic holiday as well as an international one: Belizeans take to the cayes and the rivers, and prices and crowds both rise around it. Sargassum, when it comes, usually starts to show in this stretch.

May

The hottest month of the year, and the one when the rains return — usually late in the month, usually as an afternoon storm rather than a wet day. Prices drop as high season ends. For anyone who does not mind heat it is one of the better-value months, and the sea is very warm.

June, July and August

Green season proper: hot, humid, and raining most afternoons in a heavy burst that clears. Mornings are frequently fine, which is why the diving and the excursions continue through it. July and August bring the North American and European school holidays, so the cayes and the better lodges fill up again even though the weather is worse. Hurricane season has opened on 1 June, though activity this early is unusual. The rainforest is at its most alive, and the bird and howler-monkey activity inland is at its best.

September and October

The wettest months, and the peak of the Atlantic hurricane season. Rainfall is heaviest in the south, where Toledo can be genuinely saturated. A good many hotels, dive operators and lodges close for part of this period to carry out maintenance, so check that your choices are actually open before you commit. Belizeans themselves are out in force in September: Saint George’s Caye Day on the tenth and Independence Day on the twenty-first make the whole month a celebration, with parades and carnival in Belize City and the district towns. If you travel now, take insurance that covers weather disruption, and keep the itinerary loose.

November

The turn. Rain eases through the month, the hurricane season officially closes on the thirtieth, and by the last week the country is usually drying out and filling up. Garifuna Settlement Day on the nineteenth is one of the great days in the Belizean calendar, and Dangriga and Hopkins are the places to be for it. The last ten days of November are, in a good year, the best value in Belize: dry-season weather at green-season prices.

December

Dry, pleasant and increasingly busy. The first half of the month is quiet and reasonably priced; from about the twentieth the country is at its most expensive and most booked, and the cayes in particular need reserving months ahead for Christmas and New Year. Northers begin to appear.

Rain, heat and the sea

Two seasons, not four

Belize has a dry season and a wet one. The dry runs roughly from late November or December to April or May; the wet from June to November. The transitions move by several weeks from year to year, so treat the edges as approximate and the middles as reliable. There is also a short dry spell inside the wet season, locally called the mauger, which often falls in late July or August and can give a week or two of fine weather in the middle of the rains.

The north is drier than the south

This is the single most useful thing to know about Belizean weather. Corozal and Orange Walk in the north are markedly drier than Punta Gorda and the Toledo District in the south, which is one of the wettest places in Central America. The cayes sit between the two. A September trip to the northern cayes and a September trip to Toledo are not the same holiday.

Rain is usually an afternoon event

Outside the worst of September and October, green-season rain in Belize is typically a heavy downpour in the afternoon or overnight rather than continuous drizzle. Mornings are often clear. Excursions are therefore generally scheduled early, and a wet-season trip that starts each day at seven loses far less than visitors expect.

The sea is warm all year

Water temperatures inside the reef stay comfortable in every month; nobody needs a wetsuit for snorkelling. What changes is clarity and surface conditions. The dry months give the best visibility; northers in winter and storms in autumn stir things up for a few days at a time.

Hurricanes, honestly

The Atlantic season runs from 1 June to 30 November, with most activity between August and October. Most seasons pass over Belize without a direct hit, and the country has a well-practised warning and evacuation system. The realistic risk to a traveller is not danger so much as disruption: cancelled flights, closed boats and a few lost days. If you go then, buy the insurance and do not book anything you cannot move.

Dates worth planning around

Garifuna Settlement Day, 19 November

The commemoration of the Garifuna arrival on the Belizean coast in 1832, and a public holiday. Dangriga and Hopkins hold drumming, dancing and a dawn re-enactment of the landing by dory. It is the most distinctive thing in the Belizean year and worth shaping a November trip around.

Saint George’s Caye Day, 10 September, and Independence Day, 21 September

Together they make September a month of parades, carnival and street parties nationwide, in the middle of the wettest weather of the year. Accommodation in Belize City and the district towns tightens considerably around both dates.

Easter

A major domestic holiday. Belizeans head for the cayes, the rivers and the coast, and Good Friday to Easter Monday is busy and dear everywhere. Some businesses close for part of it.

Christmas and New Year

The most expensive and most booked fortnight of the year on the cayes and at the inland lodges. If this is your window, reserve many months ahead and expect minimum-stay requirements.

Lobster season

Belize closes its spiny lobster fishery for part of the year to let stocks recover, and the season reopens in the summer with lobster festivals on Ambergris Caye, Caye Caulker and in Placencia. The opening and closing dates are set by the fisheries authority and have been adjusted more than once, so check the current dates rather than relying on an old guide — and if lobster appears on a menu outside the season, that is a reason to ask questions rather than to order it.

What it costs, and when

Belize runs on a clear two-tier year. High season from roughly mid-December to April carries the highest room rates, the fullest boats and the firmest cancellation terms, with Christmas, New Year and Easter higher again. Low season from June to November is substantially cheaper, and the gap is wide enough that the same room can feel like a different class of hotel. The shoulders — late November to mid-December, and May into early June — are where the value sits: close to dry-season weather without peak pricing.

Two costs catch people out whatever the month. The domestic flights that make Belize work are expensive relative to everything else in the country, and a reef or atoll dive day is priced like a serious excursion rather than a beach activity. Both are worth the money; neither should be a surprise on the final bill. We do not print figures here because they move, and a stale number is worse than none.

Fishing here

The flats fish to their own calendar. Bonefish are available year round in Belize; permit are a year-round target too and are the reason many anglers come; tarpon are strongest in the warmer months, broadly from about March or April into the autumn, when the migratory fish are in. The dry-season months give the lightest winds and the easiest sight-fishing, and the northers of mid-winter are the main interruption. Getting in: what changed, and what has not

Most visitors from the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, the European Union and much of the Commonwealth may enter Belize as tourists without arranging a visa in advance, and are admitted for a limited stay that can usually be extended at an immigration office for a fee. A passport with sufficient remaining validity, onward or return travel, and an address for the first night are the ordinary requirements.

Beyond that we do not print the numbers, and nor should anyone else. Entry rules, the length of stay granted, the departure and border fees and the online arrival forms are exactly the details that change without notice. Check the current position with the Belize Immigration and Nationality Department and with your own government’s travel advice shortly before you fly.

Belize’s seasons — questions

What is the single best month to visit Belize?

February, if you are choosing on weather alone. It sits in the middle of the dry season, the humidity is as low as it gets, the sea inside the reef is calm and clear, and everything inland is open and dry. The price of that certainty is that February is also peak season, so rooms cost the most and the cayes are at their busiest. If you would rather trade a little certainty for space and value, late November is the month we would pick.

Is the rainy season a reason not to come?

Not in itself. Green-season rain in Belize usually arrives as a heavy afternoon downpour that clears, and mornings are frequently fine, so an early start loses you very little. The genuine cautions are September and October, which are the wettest months, the peak of the hurricane season, and the period when a good many hotels and dive operators close for maintenance. June, July, August and November are all perfectly workable months with the right expectations.

When is hurricane season, and how worried should I be?

It runs from 1 June to 30 November, with most activity from August into October. Most seasons pass without a direct hit on Belize, and the country has a practised warning system, so the realistic risk to a visitor is disruption rather than danger: cancelled flights, closed boats, lost days. If you travel in that window, buy insurance that covers weather disruption and avoid non-refundable bookings. Do not let it rule out June or November, which are usually fine.

When is the seaweed worst?

Sargassum influxes have generally been heaviest from about March to August in recent years, but the quantity varies enormously from year to year and from one shore to another, and nobody can predict a given week months ahead. The windward sides of the cayes take the worst of it. Hotels on affected frontage rake daily. It is worth asking a hotel directly what their own shoreline has been like recently rather than relying on any seasonal rule.

Can I see whale sharks at Placencia?

Possibly, and we would not plan a trip around it. Whale sharks have historically been reported off Gladden Spit near Placencia around the full moons of the spring months, when snapper spawn there. Sightings have been considerably less dependable in recent years than the marketing suggests. If the dates happen to suit you, take the trip and treat a sighting as a gift; if they do not, you are not missing a certainty.

When should I go for the Maya sites and the caves?

The dry season, from about January to April. Cave trips in particular depend on water levels, and operators will cancel when rivers are high after heavy rain, which is most likely from September to November. The sites themselves are open year round and are pleasanter in the cooler, drier months; climbing a temple in the May heat is hard work.

Is Christmas a good time to go?

The weather is usually excellent and the country is at its most festive, but it is the most expensive and most heavily booked fortnight of the year, particularly on the cayes and at the inland lodges, with minimum stays common. If you want those dates, book many months ahead. The first half of December gives you nearly the same weather at a fraction of the fuss.
