Before you go

Most of Yosemite's famous sights sit in one seven-mile valley, and the valley is open every day of the year.

Yosemite Valley is the heart of the park: El Capitan, Half Dome, Yosemite Falls, the meadows along the Merced River and the historic hotels, all reached by a loop road with a free, year-round shuttle. Beyond the valley, the park climbs to the high country of Tuolumne Meadows along the Tioga Road, out to Glacier Point on a ridge 3,000 feet above the valley floor, and south to the giant sequoias of the Mariposa Grove near Wawona.

The good news for 2026: Yosemite is not using an entry reservation system this year, after several summers of timed entry. You simply pay at the gate. Instead, the park is managing traffic and parking as it happens, and it asks visitors from spring through fall to arrive before 9 a.m. or after 5 p.m. to miss the worst of the lines. Nothing has been announced yet for 2027, so check the park's reservations page before you plan next year's trip.

Seasons rule the rest. The Tioga Road and Glacier Point Road close with the first big snows in fall, usually reopening in late May or June. They are both closed for the 2026–27 winter now. The Mariposa Grove Road is closed for the season too, though you can still walk in. The valley stays open all year, but chains may be required in winter, so carry them.

Plan your bed first. Rooms inside the park open for booking 366 days ahead and fill quickly, and the most popular campgrounds sell out within minutes of opening. The entrance stations take cards and mobile payments only; cash is not accepted.

No timed entry in 2026: the park dropped its peak-season entrance reservations for 2026, including the February firefall period, and is managing congestion with real-time traffic monitoring and active parking management in Yosemite Valley. Arrive before 9 a.m. or after 5 p.m. from spring through fall. Nothing has been announced for 2027.

Seasonal roads (as of November 20, 2026): Tioga Road and Glacier Point Road are closed for the season because of snow and usually reopen in late May or June. The Mariposa Grove Road is closed for the season; when open (roughly mid-April through November) it is open only to vehicles displaying a disability placard, and everyone else rides the free shuttle. Hetch Hetchy Road is open sunrise to sunset and closes when snow falls or is forecast.

Current work and closures: the El Capitan Bridge stabilization project brings 15-minute weekday delays (7 a.m. to 7 p.m.) in Yosemite Valley, and the Valley Loop trail over the bridge is closed on weekdays through December. The Four Mile Trail is closed near Union Point, with no access to Glacier Point. Big Oak Flat Road (Highway 120 west) has fire-safety work with 15-minute delays. The Half Dome cables are down for the season.

Wawona Hotel: closed. The park closed all buildings of the Wawona Hotel complex to the public from September 2, 2025, for rehabilitation, until further notice.

Visitors from outside the U.S.: since January 1, 2026, Yosemite is one of 11 national parks that charge non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older an extra $100 per person on top of the entrance fee. The America the Beautiful pass costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for nonresidents, and a pass covers that extra fee.

Best things to do in Yosemite National Park

Six experiences to build your visit around.

The first view

Tunnel View

Where Wawona Road comes out of its tunnel above the valley, with El Capitan, Bridalveil Fall and Half Dome laid out in front of you. There is a parking area right at the viewpoint.

Easy walk

Lower Yosemite Fall

A short loop to the foot of Yosemite Falls, among the tallest waterfalls in the world, at its fullest in May and June. The eastern side of the loop is wheelchair accessible, with seating at the viewing area.

Valley by shuttle

Yosemite Valley Loop

Ride the free shuttle (7 a.m. to 10 p.m., all year) to Yosemite Village, The Ahwahnee and Curry Village. Stop for lunch, a ranger walk and a long look up at Half Dome.

Giant sequoias

Mariposa Grove

More than 500 mature giant sequoias near the South Entrance, including the 3,000-year-old Grizzly Giant. The 0.3-mile Big Trees Loop is flat and wheelchair accessible.

Summer & fall

Glacier Point

A viewpoint 3,000 feet above the valley, facing Half Dome and the High Sierra. The road is open only from late spring to the first heavy snow; it is closed now.

High country

Tioga Road & Tuolumne Meadows

Highway 120 crosses the park at up to 9,945 feet, past Olmsted Point, Tenaya Lake and the broad meadows of Tuolumne. A summer-only drive, usually open from late May or June into fall.

Come early or come late. Entry is reservation-free in 2026, but the park asks visitors to arrive before 9 a.m. or after 5 p.m. Inside the valley, park once and use the free shuttle; the parking lots are the hardest thing to find at midday.

When to visit Yosemite

Spring (April to June) is waterfall season, when snowmelt sends Yosemite, Bridalveil and Vernal falls roaring. The valley turns green and the dogwoods bloom. Tioga and Glacier Point roads usually reopen in late May or June, depending on the winter's snow.

Summer is the busiest time, with warm valley days, full campgrounds and the high country at its best. Expect lines at the entrances at midday. Fall brings cooler air, thinner crowds and gold in the black oaks; the waterfalls run low, and the high roads close with the first big storm.

Winter is quiet and beautiful. The valley stays open, the shuttles run, and the hotels are easier to book. Snow can come to the valley floor, chains may be required, and the high roads stay closed. In mid to late February, the setting sun can light Horsetail Fall on El Capitan like a ribbon of fire, an event that draws big crowds to a few viewing areas.

How to get to Yosemite National Park

By car

Three highways reach Yosemite Valley year-round: Highway 140 from Merced through Mariposa and El Portal to the Arch Rock Entrance, served all year by the YARTS bus; Highway 41 from Fresno through Oakhurst and Fish Camp to the South Entrance near Wawona; and Highway 120 from Manteca and Groveland to the Big Oak Flat Entrance, where the limit is 45 feet for a single vehicle. From the east, Highway 120 crosses Tioga Pass from Lee Vining in summer only.

Big-rig limits inside the park: Glacier Point Road beyond the Sentinel Dome trailhead allows 30 feet with no trailers, Hetch Hetchy Road 25 feet long and 8 feet wide, and the Mariposa Grove Road 21 feet with no trailers. Tioga Road has grades of up to 8 percent.

By air, train & bus

Fresno Yosemite International (FAT) is the closest large airport, on the Highway 41 side. The San Francisco Bay Area and Sacramento airports have more flights, about four hours away.

Amtrak sells combined train-and-bus tickets to Yosemite Valley, with the YARTS bus carrying passengers beyond Merced. YARTS runs all year on Highway 140 through Mariposa and El Portal, and in summer on Highway 41 from Fresno through Oakhurst and Fish Camp, and on Highway 120. Once in the valley, the free shuttle runs daily from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m.

Lodges inside the park

All lodging inside the park is run by the concessioner Yosemite Hospitality. Reservations open 366 days in advance and are strongly recommended. In Yosemite Valley, The Ahwahnee (the park's grand 1927 hotel, briefly renamed The Majestic Yosemite Hotel) and Yosemite Valley Lodge, near the base of Yosemite Falls, are open all year. Curry Village (tent cabins, cabins and motel rooms below Glacier Point) and Housekeeping Camp are seasonal.

Up on Tioga Road, White Wolf Lodge and Tuolumne Meadows Lodge are summer-only tent-cabin camps; White Wolf Lodge did not operate in 2025, so confirm either one is running before you plan around it. The High Sierra Camps are booked by lottery.

The historic Wawona Hotel near the South Entrance is closed. It shut in December 2024 for a condition assessment, and since September 2, 2025, the whole hotel complex has been closed for rehabilitation until further notice. For a base on the south side, look to Fish Camp and Oakhurst.

RV travel & camping at Yosemite

Yosemite welcomes RVs, but no campsite in the park has hookups of any kind, and several campgrounds have tight length limits.

Campground reservations are made on recreation.gov and are released at 7 a.m. Pacific time. They sell out within minutes, so log in early. Upper Pines, Lower Pines, North Pines, Wawona and Hodgdon Meadow open five months ahead, on the 15th of each month; Crane Flat and the other high-country campgrounds release on a rolling 14-day window, and half of Tuolumne Meadows opens two months ahead.

Open all year: Upper Pines in the valley (RVs to 35 feet, trailers to 24 feet) is also home to the park's only year-round dump station. Wawona (RVs and trailers to 35 feet) and Hodgdon Meadow near the Big Oak Flat Entrance (RVs to 35 feet, trailers to 30 feet) stay open too, with Hodgdon first-come, first-served in the off-season. Camp 4 is walk-in tent camping only.

Seasonal: Lower Pines and North Pines (RVs to 40 feet, trailers to 35 feet) run roughly late April to late October. Crane Flat (35 feet) and Tuolumne Meadows (RVs to 35 feet, trailers to 24 feet) are summer only, and White Wolf takes RVs to 27 feet only and had no water in 2026. Summer-only dump stations are near Wawona and Tuolumne Meadows, though the Tuolumne station is closed until further notice.

For full hookups, look to private RV parks in the gateway communities along Highways 140, 41 and 120.

Fishing in Yosemite

New in 2026: fishing in Yosemite is open all year, after the park removed the old seasonal closures. California fishing regulations apply, and everyone 16 and older needs a valid California fishing license. Bait of any kind, live, dead or scented, is prohibited, and lead weights must be a quarter-ounce or less.

In Yosemite Valley, on the South Fork of the Merced and on the Tuolumne River and its tributaries, use artificial lures or flies with barbless hooks only, and release rainbow trout. Elsewhere the general limit is five trout a day and ten in possession. You may not fish from bridges or piers, or from the top of O'Shaughnessy Dam at Hetch Hetchy. Accessible travel at Yosemite

What is available for visitors with limited mobility, and the passes that help.

Shuttles: the free Yosemite Valley shuttles are fully accessible, with wheelchair lifts and tie-downs, and run all year.

Wheelchairs and scooters: according to the park's Accessibility Guide, wheelchairs are available year-round at the Yosemite Valley Lodge front desk, and the bike stand there rents a limited number of wheelchairs and motorized scooters that can be reserved ahead. Select rooms at Yosemite Valley Lodge are wheelchair accessible, with accessible parking near the registration office.

Accessible sights: the eastern side of the Lower Yosemite Fall loop is wheelchair accessible and ends at a viewing area with seating at wheelchair height. In the Mariposa Grove, the 0.3-mile Big Trees Loop is flat and accessible, and drivers with a disability placard may drive the grove road to the Grizzly Giant parking area when the road is open.

Guides and services: the park publishes an Accessibility Guide (also in Braille), runs a Deaf Services Program, offers an audio-described park brochure through the UniDescription app, and has a visual guide for visitors with aphasia. Questions go to the park's accessibility coordinator through the main park line, 209-372-0200.

Park passes that save you money. U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability can get the free lifetime Access Pass. Visitors 62 and over can buy the Senior Pass ($80 for life, or $20 a year). Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass. Each covers entrance fees at every national park.

Traveling on dialysis? The nearest dialysis clinics are in Merced, about 80 miles west of Yosemite Valley by Highway 140. Book your treatment at a clinic there several weeks before you go; the large dialysis companies run travel desks that will arrange it with your home unit.

Yosemite National Park FAQ

Do I need a reservation to enter Yosemite in 2026?

No. Yosemite is not using a timed entry or vehicle reservation system in 2026, including the February firefall period. You pay the entrance fee at the gate. Lodging, camping and wilderness trips still need reservations.

How much is the Yosemite entrance fee?

$35 per private vehicle, $30 per motorcycle and $20 per person on foot, bicycle or horse, each good for 7 consecutive days. Ages 15 and under are free. The park does not accept cash.

When does Tioga Road open?

Tioga Road (Highway 120 through the park) closes for the season with the first major snow and usually reopens in late May or June, depending on conditions. It is closed for the 2026–27 winter.

Is Glacier Point Road open?

Not in winter. Glacier Point Road closed for the season in November 2026 because of snow and usually reopens in late May or June. The Four Mile Trail is also closed near Union Point, so there is no access to Glacier Point now.

Is the Wawona Hotel open?

No. The Wawona Hotel closed in December 2024, and since September 2025 the entire hotel complex has been closed for rehabilitation until further notice.

How far ahead can I book a room in Yosemite?

In-park lodging, run by Yosemite Hospitality, opens 366 days in advance. The Ahwahnee and Yosemite Valley Lodge are open all year and fill early.

Can I bring an RV to Yosemite?

Yes, but no campsites have hookups. Upper Pines (RVs to 35 feet) is open all year and has the park's only year-round dump station. Some roads have limits, such as 25 feet on Hetch Hetchy Road and 21 feet with no trailers on the Mariposa Grove Road.

Is there a fishing season in Yosemite?

Starting in 2026, all park waters are open to fishing all year. A California fishing license is required at 16 and older, bait is prohibited, and barbless artificial lures and catch-and-release of rainbow trout apply in Yosemite Valley and some other waters.
