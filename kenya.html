Kenya — The Rift Valley, the Mara and a Warm Indian Ocean

Kenya Travel Guide — Where to Stay, Region by Region

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Kenya is the country that invented the safari and it has spent a century getting good at it. But it is much more varied than the one picture suggests: a capital at 1,800 metres with a national park on its edge, a chain of Rift Valley lakes full of birds, a snow-capped mountain on the equator, high plains in the north where the rhino conservation work happens, and a long Indian Ocean coast with four hundred years of Swahili and Omani history in its ports.

This guide is about choosing between those regions and knowing what each one asks of you. It is also honest about two things that matter in 2026: the entry rules changed in October, and there are parts of the country that official advice tells visitors to stay out of. Both are set out plainly below rather than buried.

Where to stay — the Guild's Kenya city guides

Five places cover most first trips, and each has its own guide:

Nairobi — the arrival and departure city, and more interesting than its reputation. Westlands for convenience, Karen and Langata for the elephants and the safari airstrip.

Naivasha — the freshwater Rift Valley lake ninety minutes below Nairobi. Birds, hippos, and two parks you are allowed to walk in.

Nanyuki — the market town on Mount Kenya's shoulder, at the equator, and the base for the Laikipia conservancies.

Mombasa — the old port and the north coast beaches, with Fort Jesus and the Old Town on the island.

Diani Beach — the south coast's long white strip, reached best by air into Ukunda.

For the Maasai Mara itself, the camps and lodges inside the reserve and the conservancies are booked as packages rather than as hotel rooms. The nearest town with ordinary rooms, and the place most road trips break the journey, is Narok.

Why go

Because the wildlife is still the best introduction to Africa there is, and because Kenya has the infrastructure to make it comfortable without making it fake. Because you can be at 7,000 feet in cold clear air in the morning and in the Indian Ocean two days later. Because the Swahili coast is a genuine piece of world history that most visitors walk past on the way to a beach lounger. And because the country is set up for it: a dense network of small aircraft, decades of guiding tradition, and lodges that range from a tent with a bucket shower to places that would not look out of place in Europe.

At a glance

Getting in

Electronic travel authorisation applied for online in advance, plus Kenyan government travel health insurance for most stays under twelve months since 5 October 2026. Apply at least two weeks ahead.

Main airports

Jomo Kenyatta International at Nairobi for long-haul, Wilson Airport in Nairobi for safari flights, Moi International at Mombasa and Ukunda for Diani.

Money

The Kenyan shilling. Card payment is normal in hotels and city restaurants, and mobile money is used for almost everything else. Carry some cash for park gates and tips.

Language

Swahili and English are both official and English is widely spoken, which makes Kenya an easy country to travel in independently.

Altitude

Nairobi about 1,800 metres, Naivasha about 1,890, Nanyuki about 1,950. Plan an easy first day. Mount Kenya is a serious high-altitude climb.

Health

Malaria risk on the coast and in the lowlands, lower in the highlands. Travel health advice at least eight weeks before you fly. An imported Ebola case was confirmed on 6 October 2026 and public health measures remain in place.

The regions — and the parts to stay out of

Nairobi and the central highlands

The capital, the tea country around it and the farming highlands north towards Mount Kenya. Temperate, green, densely populated and the part of the country where most Kenyans live. Nairobi is where almost every trip begins; the Aberdares and Mount Kenya are within half a day of it.

The Rift Valley

The great trench running the length of the country, with a chain of lakes along the floor of it: Naivasha and Elementaita and Nakuru in the south, Baringo and Bogoria further north, Turkana far beyond. This is the easiest and cheapest part of Kenya to enjoy, because the lakes are close to Nairobi and the birding and walking cost very little.

Browse stays in the Rift Valley at Naivasha

Laikipia and the north

Above Mount Kenya the country opens into high, dry plains held largely as private and community conservancies. This is where a great deal of Kenya's rhino and wild dog conservation happens and where the game viewing is least crowded. It is also remote: the Foreign Office names Laikipia among counties where security operations continue against banditry and intercommunal violence, though it does not advise against travel there. Take local advice before driving into open country.

Browse stays at Nanyuki for Laikipia

The Maasai Mara and the southern plains

The Mara is the northern continuation of the Serengeti plains and the most famous wildlife area in Kenya, with Amboseli under Kilimanjaro and Tsavo to its east. The wildebeest herds are usually here somewhere between July and October. Most visitors fly in; those who drive stop at Narok.

The coast

From the Tanzanian border up past Mombasa to Malindi and Watamu: warm sea, reefs, and old Swahili ports. Hot and humid all year. The resorts are north and south of Mombasa, and Diani is the main one on the south coast.

The parts official advice says to avoid — stated in full

We would rather print this than leave a reader to find it out. The UK Foreign Office, on advice still current at 21 November 2026 and last updated on 6 November 2026, advises against all travel to Mandera County; to the Tarbaj and Wajir East constituencies of Wajir County, except Wajir Town; to areas of Wajir County within 60km of the Somali border; to Garissa County, except Garissa Town, the constituency of Lagdera and the parts of Balambala constituency north and west of the main road from Saka up to the C81; and to Lamu County, except Lamu Island and Manda Island.

It advises against all but essential travel to Garissa Town; to Tana River County north of the Tana River as far as Saka; to the strip within 15km of the east coast between the Tana River and the Galana — Athi-Galana-Sabaki — River; and to Moyale Town and up to 5km around it, including the Ethiopian border, where violence across the border since October 2026 has brought stray gunfire and unexploded ordnance onto the Kenyan side.

There is a separate note for Lamu Island and Manda Island, which are outside the advise-against area: travel there by air to Lamu airport and not by road. Mount Elgon has a significant security presence because of armed clashes. And rural areas of the north and north-east see cattle rustling and banditry, where foreign nationals are not usually the target but care is needed.

None of the five places in this guide is inside any of those areas. We checked each one against the list rather than assuming it, and we have not put a booking link anywhere official advice tells people not to go. There is also a high threat of terrorism across Kenya generally, which official advice says could affect hotels, beaches, shopping centres and safari parks, so be alert in crowded places wherever you are.

What to do

Game viewing is the reason most people come and it is worth doing properly: fewer parks, more nights in each, and a good guide rather than a long itinerary. Beyond that, Kenya rewards the things that are not a game drive. Hell's Gate and Crescent Island at Naivasha let you walk and cycle among animals instead of watching from a vehicle. Mount Kenya is a real climb with huts and routes and a genuine altitude problem. The Old Town at Mombasa and Fort Jesus are Indian Ocean history on the ground. Karura Forest and Nairobi National Park are both inside the capital. The Rift Valley lakes are among the best birding in Africa, particularly during the short rains when the migrants arrive.

Park and conservancy entry fees are set by the Kenya Wildlife Service and by the individual conservancies, and they are revised from time to time. We do not print the amounts, because we will not quote a figure we cannot source to the body that sets it. Confirm the current rate when you book.

When to go

Late June to October is the main dry season and the best wildlife viewing, with July and August the busiest and dearest weeks. January and February are the other dry spell: hot, very good for game, and much quieter. April and May are the long rains and the weakest weeks of the year. November and early December are the short rains, which are usually lighter, and the best birding of the year. The coast is hot and humid throughout and wettest in April and May. Fishing here

Kenya does not yet have its own book in the Guild's fishing shelf, so we will be straight about what is here rather than sell it. The Indian Ocean off Watamu, Malindi and Shimoni is genuine big-game billfish water with a long charter tradition behind it, fished mainly between about November and March. Lake Victoria in the west holds Nile perch and tilapia and is fished from Kisumu and the lake towns. Lake Naivasha has a modest freshwater fishery. The highland streams on Mount Kenya and in the Aberdares were stocked with trout in the colonial period and some of that fishing survives, which is the closest thing Kenya has to the fly water the Guild's fishing pages are mostly about. Licences, closed seasons and access rules vary by water and by county, so arrange it through an established local operator rather than turning up with a rod. The rest of the shelf is in The Guild Fishing Library.

Travelling in your later years

Kenya is a good country to visit later in life, with two things to plan around. The first is altitude rather than heat: Nairobi, Naivasha and Nanyuki are all high enough that the first day feels harder than it should, so give yourself an easy arrival and do not book a dawn start for the morning after a long flight. The second is the driving. Distances are long, the unpaved park roads are rough, and a full day in a vehicle is tiring at any age. The answer is the small aircraft: flying between Nairobi, the park airstrips and the coast turns six-hour drives into forty-minute hops, and it is the single best money a less mobile visitor can spend here.

Beyond that, choose fewer places and more nights in each. The lakeside hotels at Naivasha and the country hotels around Nanyuki are gentle bases with level gardens and short walks. On the coast the north Mombasa beaches are the easiest to get around. Game drives themselves are sitting down, which suits many people very well; the part to ask about is how far the vehicle has to travel before the animals start, and whether a camp's tents are reached by a flat path or a flight of steps.

Getting in — the rules as they now stand

These changed recently enough to catch people out, so here they are from the source. The UK Foreign Office entry requirements for Kenya, still current at 21 November 2026, state:

Electronic travel authorisation. You must apply online in advance of travel. The Kenyan authorities recommend applying at least two weeks before departure. Anyone who already holds a visa can continue to travel on it until it expires.

Mandatory government travel insurance. From 5 October 2026, most travellers visiting Kenya for less than twelve months must have Kenyan government-provided travel health insurance. If you need an authorisation, you buy the insurance as part of that application; if you already hold a valid one, you buy it through the Kenyan eCitizen insurance portal. Foreign travel insurance policies are not accepted in place of this. The cover lasts 90 days and allows multiple entries in that period. It does not apply to Kenyan citizens, Kenyan residents, citizens of East African Community partner states, or transit passengers who do not pass through Kenyan immigration control.

Passport. An expiry date at least six months after the date you arrive, and at least two blank pages.

Two further rules worth knowing before you land: you must carry identification at all times, and a copy of your passport is normally accepted though an officer may ask for the original; and it is illegal to photograph official buildings and airports. On the coast, which is mainly Muslim, official advice asks visitors to dress conservatively away from the resorts. Same-sex sexual activity is illegal in Kenya and public affection between same-sex partners could lead to arrest, which LGBT+ travellers should know before booking rather than after.

Questions readers actually ask

Do I need anything new to enter Kenya in 2026?


Yes, and it is recent enough that many sites have not caught up. You must apply online for an electronic travel authorisation before you travel, and since 5 October 2026 most visitors staying under twelve months must also hold travel health insurance provided by the Kenyan government. You buy that insurance as part of the authorisation application, or through the eCitizen insurance portal if you already hold a valid authorisation. A policy bought at home is not accepted in its place. Your passport needs an expiry date at least six months after you arrive and at least two blank pages. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements for Kenya, still current at 21 November 2026.

Which parts of Kenya does official advice say not to visit?


The UK Foreign Office advises against all travel to Mandera County; to the Tarbaj and Wajir East constituencies of Wajir County except Wajir Town; to areas of Wajir County within 60km of the Somali border; to Garissa County with named exceptions; and to Lamu County except Lamu Island and Manda Island. It advises against all but essential travel to Garissa Town, to Tana River County north of the Tana River as far as Saka, to the strip within 15km of the east coast between the Tana River and the Galana River, and to Moyale Town and 5km around it on the Ethiopian border. None of the places in this guide is inside those areas, and we checked each one against the list rather than assuming.

How long do I need for a first trip to Kenya?


Ten days to two weeks for the classic shape: a night in Nairobi, two in the Rift Valley or on Mount Kenya's shoulder, three or four in the Mara or another big park, and four or five on the coast. A week can be done but it will be mostly travelling. Kenya is large and the internal flights, rather than the roads, are what make a short trip work.

Is Kenya safe for tourists?


It is visited by hundreds of thousands of people a year without incident, and it also has a high terrorist threat and frequent violent crime in the cities and on the highways, both of which official advice states plainly. The honest answer is that it is safe enough for an ordinary holiday if you take ordinary precautions seriously: move by arranged car at night, do not walk alone in quiet places even in daylight, keep away from the districts and counties named in official advice, and take local guidance about remote country. This guide names the specific places rather than leaving you to guess.

Can I combine a safari with the beach?


That is what most two-week Kenya trips are, and it works. The windows that suit both the parks and the coast are roughly July to October and January to February. Avoid May, when the long rains make the tracks hard and the coast wet at the same time. Flights link Nairobi's Wilson Airport, the park airstrips, Mombasa and Ukunda, and they are the reason the combination is practical at all.
