Where to Stay in Flagstaff — Downtown, Route 66 & the Pines

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Flagstaff is not the Arizona most people picture. It sits at about 7,000 feet in a ponderosa pine forest, at the crossing of Interstate 17 and Interstate 40, about 150 miles north of Phoenix and about 80 miles south of the Grand Canyon. Summers are mild, winters bring real snow, and the night sky is dark enough that the city was named the world’s first International Dark Sky City in 2001. Most visitors use it as the comfortable base for the Grand Canyon’s South Rim, and many end up wishing they had stayed longer. Where you stay decides whether your evenings are spent on foot downtown or in the car, so this page sorts the town into its real choices and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in Flagstaff

Four ways to stay in Flagstaff, each suited to a different trip.

Historic downtown, for walking to dinner

Downtown is the old railroad town on Route 66: brick buildings, the Amtrak station, breweries, coffee houses and restaurants, all within a few blocks. If you want to park once and spend your evenings on foot, this is the place, and the two historic hotels here are part of the reason people come.

The trade-off is noise and parking. The railway runs right through downtown and the bars stay busy late, so light sleepers should ask for a quiet room.

Milton Road and the university, for families and easy parking

Milton Road runs south from downtown past the Northern Arizona University campus towards the interstates. It is where many of the familiar hotel names cluster, often with their own parking, pools and breakfast included.

Downtown is a short walk or ride away in the evening, and the logistics are simpler with children.

East Flagstaff, along Butler Avenue and Route 66

The east side, around Butler Avenue near Interstate 40, is the practical choice for a road trip along I-40 or an early start. It is quieter than downtown and has some of the town’s larger hotels, including one set in its own pine forest.

Downtown is a short drive away; this side of town suits travellers who mostly want a good night’s sleep between long days out.

Cabins in the pines, and out along I-40

South of town, Lake Mary Road leads into the forest towards the lakes, and there are cabins here that feel properly in the woods while still minutes from town. Further out stands a casino resort run by Navajo Gaming, for travellers who would rather stay in one large place with everything on site.

Both mean driving for most meals and outings. In winter, check the road conditions before you set out.

Weatherford Hotel

DowntownHistoric

A historic hotel at 23 North Leroux Street in the middle of downtown, with three bars and restaurants in the same building — Charly’s Pub & Grill, the Zane Grey Bar with its wrap-around balcony, and the Gopher Hole pub in the basement. Its own website describes four room types, from king rooms to historic European rooms, and calls it a lively stay with entertainment. It is also the home of Flagstaff’s New Year’s Eve Great Pine Cone Drop, which the hotel says began in 1999 to mark its 100th anniversary. Choose it for atmosphere, not for an early night.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Flagstaff details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Flagstaff

Most visitors drive. From Phoenix it is about 150 miles north on Interstate 17. Interstate 40 brings you in from the east or west, Highway 89 from Page and Lake Powell, and Highway 180 runs about 81 miles north-west to the Grand Canyon’s South Rim.

Flagstaff Airport has daily American Airlines flights to and from Phoenix and Dallas–Fort Worth, and Amtrak’s Southwest Chief stops at the downtown station once a day in each direction, both according to the Flagstaff Convention and Visitors Bureau.

Coming from Sedona? Check Oak Creek Canyon first

The scenic road between Sedona and Flagstaff is State Route 89A through Oak Creek Canyon. After the flooding of 1 November 2026, the Sedona Tourism Bureau’s update of 3 November 2026 reported that 89A was closed in both directions between Sedona and Kachina Village with no reopening time announced. Until it reopens, the usual way round is State Route 179 and Interstate 17. Check az511.gov before you set out.

When to go

Flagstaff has four real seasons. The visitors bureau says summer temperatures rarely go above 90°F, with monsoon thunderstorms from July to September; autumn brings changing leaves; and winter snowfall averages 108 inches, with skiing close by. Spring and autumn are mild and dry. Mornings are cold even in summer, so pack a layer.

Summer and the Grand Canyon season are the busiest for rooms. In winter, the roads can close in storms — build a spare day into a winter trip.

The night sky

Flagstaff became the world’s first International Dark Sky City on 24 October 2001, and Lowell Observatory, where Pluto was discovered, runs public evening programmes on Mars Hill. On a clear night, step away from the streetlights before you go to bed.

Flagstaff Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best place to stay in Flagstaff?

Historic downtown if you want to walk to restaurants, breweries and the train station and do not mind some noise. Milton Road near the university for families and easy parking. East Flagstaff near Interstate 40 for a quieter road-trip stop. A cabin on Lake Mary Road if you want the forest.

How far is Flagstaff from the Grand Canyon?

About 80 miles. The Flagstaff Convention and Visitors Bureau gives the drive to the South Rim as about 81 miles north-west on Highway 180. Allow extra time in winter weather.

Can I fly into Flagstaff?

Yes. Flagstaff Airport has daily American Airlines flights to and from Phoenix and Dallas–Fort Worth, according to the visitors bureau. Many visitors instead fly into Phoenix and drive about 150 miles north on Interstate 17.

Does it snow in Flagstaff?

Yes. At about 7,000 feet, Flagstaff averages 108 inches of snow a year by the visitors bureau’s figures, and winter brings skiing nearby. Summers are mild, rarely above 90°F, with afternoon storms from July to September.

Is the road from Sedona to Flagstaff open?

Check before you go. The Sedona Tourism Bureau reported on 3 November 2026 that State Route 89A through Oak Creek Canyon was closed between Sedona and Kachina Village after flooding, with no reopening time announced. Interstate 17 is the usual alternative. Current conditions are at az511.gov.
