Best Time to Visit Trinidad and Tobago

By the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Two seasons, not four: dry from January to May, wet from June to December. On top of that sits a calendar of its own — Carnival in February, the Tobago Heritage Festival in July, Emancipation Day on 1 August, parang at Christmas — and those decide the price of a room far more than the rain does. Here is the year, month by month, with the dates we could check against the organisers rather than remember.

Month by month

Temperatures barely move across the year — it is hot every month. What changes is the rain, the price and what is happening on the islands.

January

Dry season settling in, and the Carnival season already loud: panyards rehearsing most nights, calypso tents open, fetes every weekend. Warm days, cooler nights than you expect in the hills. Good diving visibility in Tobago. Prices firm but not yet at Carnival levels.

February

The month, in 2027. Carnival Monday and Tuesday fall on 8 and 9 February. Port of Spain fills completely; rooms and flights go many months ahead and cost multiples of normal. If you are not coming for Carnival, the rest of February is lovely and Tobago barely notices.

March

Arguably the best all-round month. Driest weather, the Carnival crowd gone, hot sun, flat sea on Tobago’s leeward side and the clearest water of the year for diving. Easter can fall at the end and brings a domestic rush to Tobago.

April

Still dry, still hot, and the quietest of the good months once Easter has passed. Leatherback turtles are coming ashore to nest on the north coast beaches. Buccoo’s goat and crab races are an Easter Tuesday fixture.

May

The dry season winds down and the first serious afternoon showers arrive, usually late in the month. Rates soften, the islands empty out, and the land greens up fast. The second half of May is one of our two favourite windows.

June

The wet season proper. Heavy afternoon rain that clears, humid mornings, bargain rooms. The hurricane season opens on 1 June across the Atlantic, but these islands sit well south of the usual track and rarely take a direct hit.

July

Wet, green, and the best cultural month of the second half of the year. The Tobago Heritage Festival runs through the second half of July into Emancipation Day on 1 August — in 2026 it ran from 16 July to 1 August. Dates for 2027 had not been published when this page was written.

August

Emancipation Day on 1 August closes the Heritage Festival with a parade in Tobago and is a serious national occasion in Trinidad too. Rain continues; so do low rates. The Great Race powerboat run from Trinidad to Tobago is a mid-August fixture.

September

The wettest stretch for the wider Caribbean and the peak of hurricane season elsewhere, though some years bring a drier spell here called the petit caréme. Cheapest month to travel. Some small Tobago properties close for a few weeks.

October

Rain easing a little towards the end. Tobago now runs its own October carnival, a separate and much newer event from the national one in February; in 2026 it fell on 30 October to 1 November. Divali lights up central Trinidad in October or November, depending on the lunar calendar.

November

The last wet month, drying out week by week. Good value, few visitors, warm sea. A sensible month if you want Tobago to yourself and do not mind an afternoon downpour.

December

The dry season returns, the parang season fills Trinidad with Spanish-influenced Christmas music, and cruise ships start calling again. Rates climb through the month and the fortnight around Christmas and New Year is booked early.

The dry season and the wet season

January to May — dry

The settled half of the year. Showers are short and infrequent, the trade wind takes the edge off the heat, and the sea on Tobago’s leeward side goes flat enough for easy snorkelling and good diving visibility. It is also when the islands are busiest and dearest, and Carnival week stands apart from everything else.

June to December — wet

Afternoon rain most days, often heavy and often over within the hour, with bright mornings before it and a greener country afterwards. Humidity is higher. Rooms cost noticeably less and the beaches on Tobago can be empty. Some years bring a drier spell in September, locally called the petit caréme.

Where the hurricanes are, and are not

This matters enough to say plainly. Trinidad and Tobago lie at roughly ten and a half degrees north, further south than the rest of the Caribbean island chain, and below the corridor most Atlantic hurricanes follow. That is why insurers price yachts differently here and why boats come down to sit out the season. It is a materially lower risk than Barbados or the Leewards, not an exemption: tropical storms and the rain on their fringes do reach the islands between June and November, and Tobago is more exposed than Trinidad. Buy insurance that covers weather and do not treat the season as irrelevant.

Events worth planning around

Dates checked against the organisers’ or the tourism agencies’ own published calendars. Where a 2027 date has not been published yet, we say so rather than guess.

Trinidad and Tobago Carnival

Monday 8 and Tuesday 9 February 2027, with J’Ouvert from four in the morning on the Monday. The national tourism board gives those dates. Not public holidays in law, but the country closes. Book a year out.

Buccoo Goat & Crab Race Festival

Easter Tuesday in Tobago, at Buccoo. The Tobago tourism agency listed 7 April for 2026. Genuinely a village event rather than a staged one.

Tobago Jazz, Music & Golf Weekend

Early May. The 2026 dates given by the Tobago tourism agency were 1 to 3 May.

Tobago Heritage Festival

Second half of July into 1 August; 16 July to 1 August in 2026, the thirty-ninth festival, ending with the Emancipation Day parade. Storytelling, folk music and re-enactments including the Ole Time Wedding.

The Great Race

Powerboats from Trinidad to Tobago in mid-August; 15 August in 2026. Crowds at both ends.

Tobago Blue Food Festival

October, at Bloody Bay on Tobago’s north coast — a festival built around dasheen and the island’s root crops. 18 October in 2026.

Tobago October Carnival

A separate, newer event from the February national Carnival; the 2026 edition, called the fifth, ran 30 October to 1 November. Do not confuse the two when booking.

Divali and parang

Divali falls in October or November on the lunar calendar and lights up central Trinidad; parang, Spanish-influenced Christmas music, runs through December. Neither needs a ticket.

Travelling in your later years

Heat and humidity are the thing to plan around, not rain. Even in the dry season the middle of the day is hard work, so take the mornings for anything active and the afternoons slowly. In the wet season the downpours are short but the pavements and boat steps get slippery.

Carnival is not a gentle few days. The crowds are enormous, the music is loud enough to feel, the roads close and taxis become difficult. If you want to see it without being in it, ask your hotel about a seated grandstand ticket rather than standing on the route, and come at the quieter end of the Monday. If you would rather avoid it altogether, March and the second half of May are the easy months.

The weeks we would pick

For the weather: the first three weeks of March. Driest, clearest, calmest sea, and the Carnival rush has gone.

For the country at its most itself: the ten days before Carnival. Panyards every night, fetes every weekend, prices below Carnival week and the whole island in a good mood.

For value: the second half of May, or November. The rain has either not properly started or is on its way out, rooms are cheap and Tobago’s beaches are quiet.

For culture without the February crowds: the second half of July in Tobago, for the Heritage Festival and Emancipation Day.

Questions about when to visit

What is the single best month to visit Trinidad and Tobago?

March, if you want weather above everything else: it is the driest month, the sea on Tobago’s leeward side is at its flattest, underwater visibility is at its best and the Carnival crowds have gone home. If you want the country at full volume instead, it is February and nothing else comes close.

When is Carnival in 2027, and do I need to book early?

Carnival Monday and Tuesday are 8 and 9 February 2027. Book a year ahead if you can. The whole of Port of Spain is full, rates multiply, and flights into Piarco sell out. Neither day is a public holiday in law — the holiday calendar we checked is clear about that — but the country stops regardless, so do not plan business or long drives around it.

When is the Tobago Heritage Festival?

It runs in the second half of July into the start of August and finishes with the Emancipation Day parade on 1 August. The Tobago tourism agency gives 16 July to 1 August for the 2026 festival, its thirty-ninth. Dates for 2027 had not been published when we wrote this page, so check before you book flights around it.

Is there a hurricane risk?

Less than almost anywhere else in the Caribbean, because the islands lie at about ten and a half degrees north, south of the track most Atlantic storms follow. That is why yachts come here to sit out the season. It is a lower risk rather than no risk: tropical storms and heavy rain do arrive between June and November, so buy insurance that covers weather.

Which months are cheapest?

September and October, with June close behind. You are buying rain to get them, and a handful of small Tobago hotels close for a few weeks in September. The best value for money rather than the lowest price is the second half of May, or November once the rain is easing.

Is Tobago’s October carnival the same as Trinidad Carnival?

No. The national Carnival is the February one, on both islands. Tobago also runs a separate October carnival that started only a few years ago — the Tobago tourism agency called the 2026 edition the fifth — and in 2026 it fell on 30 October to 1 November. It is a much smaller event aimed partly at visitors, and the two should not be confused when you are booking.

When can I see the leatherback turtles?

Nesting on the north coast beaches of Trinidad runs through the first half of the year, with the heart of it in the spring. Go with a licensed guide and a permitted group only: the beaches are protected, lights and flash disturb the animals, and the turtles matter more than the photograph.
