A short history

Long before it was a capital, the land along the Potomac was home to the Piscataway and Nacotchtank peoples. In 1790 Congress carved a diamond-shaped federal district from Maryland and Virginia as a neutral seat of government, named for George Washington, and laid out by Pierre L'Enfant with broad avenues radiating from the Capitol and White House.

The city was burned by British forces in the War of 1812, rebuilt, and grew through the 19th and 20th centuries into the symbolic heart of American democracy, hosting the Lincoln Memorial, Civil Rights marches, and the Smithsonian Institution's museums. Its population is majority Black for much of its modern history, giving it a distinct musical and cultural identity often called 'Chocolate City.'

Today Washington, D.C. combines federal government, international diplomacy, and a lively local scene of neighbourhoods like Georgetown, Adams Morgan, and the H Street corridor. Residents lack full voting representation in Congress despite paying federal taxes, a long-running political grievance, while the city itself thrives on tourism, think tanks, and a growing tech and biotech sector.

Bodhi

Pierre L'Enfant laid out D.C.'s grand avenues and traffic circles in 1791, and the city sits in a federal district carved out so no single state could claim the capital as its own. The Smithsonian itself started with a gift from an Englishman, James Smithson, who never even visited America.

Nolie

Hit the National Mall's memorials after dark, the Lincoln and Jefferson Memorials lit up at night with almost nobody around beats the daytime crowds every time. And for lunch, duck into Georgetown's side streets along M Street instead of the tourist strip, that's where you'll find the good little cafes.

When to go

Spring (cherry blossoms) and autumn are the best times to visit.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Cold and occasionally snowy; museums are blissfully uncrowded and hotel rates drop.

Spring (Mar–May)

Cherry blossoms around the Tidal Basin in late March/early April draw huge crowds; book early.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Hot, humid and thick with tourists; mornings are best for outdoor monument visits.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Mild, dry and colourful — arguably the most comfortable season to walk the Mall.
