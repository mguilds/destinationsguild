Lake Fishing in Louisiana & Mississippi — Six Southern Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Toledo Bend, Caddo Lake, Lake D’Arbonne, Ross Barnett Reservoir, Sardis Lake and Grenada Lake: what swims in each, the rules from the Louisiana and Mississippi wildlife agencies, the licence a visitor needs, where to launch a boat, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries and the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks, 17 November 2026

Louisiana and Mississippi are bass and crappie country, and these six lakes show both sides of it. On the Texas line, Toledo Bend is one of the biggest man-made lakes in the South, and Caddo Lake is its opposite: a maze of bayous and cypress trees hung with moss. In north Louisiana, Lake D’Arbonne is a quiet crappie lake beside the town of Farmerville. In Mississippi, Ross Barnett Reservoir sits on the edge of Jackson, and the two big flood-control lakes in the north, Sardis and Grenada, are among the best-known crappie waters in the country — with their own crappie rule to match.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, and anyone who wants a guide for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 17 November 2026 from the agency that sets it — the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries, the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks and, for the Texas side of the border lakes, Texas Parks and Wildlife — and is dated. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The best months, in plain words

Deep-South lakes: fishing all year, a short mild winter, and a long hot summer that sends fish deep and anglers out at first light.

LakeBest monthsWhy
Toledo BendFebruary to May; October to DecemberOur guidance: bass move shallow to spawn in late winter and spring, the big-bass months on this lake; autumn is cooler and quieter. Crappie gather on brush and bridges in winter and spring.
Caddo LakeMarch to May; October and NovemberOur guidance: spring for bass and crappie in the bayous; autumn for the colour in the cypress. In high summer, floating weed can choke the backwaters.

Toledo Bend — the giant on the Texas line

The Sabine River Authority of Texas gives Toledo Bend about 185,000 acres of water and about 1,130 miles of shoreline, on the Sabine River between Louisiana and Texas.

Toledo Bend, from Zwolle and Many to the Texas shore

What swims there. Largemouth bass are why most visiting anglers come; spotted bass, crappie, white bass, striped and hybrid bass, and blue, channel and flathead catfish are all managed here by both states.

How people fish it. Mostly from a boat, on a lake so big that local guides are worth a day’s fee: bass anglers work the grass, points and standing timber; crappie anglers fish brush piles and bridges. There is bank and pier fishing at the parks and resorts.

Keeping a fish. Toledo Bend has its own limits, and Louisiana and Texas list the same ones: largemouth bass at least 14 inches (no minimum on spotted bass), 8 black bass a day in total; crappie 25 a day; white bass 25; blue and channel catfish 50 a day with no more than 5 over 30 inches; flathead catfish at least 18 inches, 10 a day.

Confirmed with the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries and Texas Parks and Wildlife, 17 November 2026 — LDWF 2026 Louisiana Recreational Fishing Regulations and the TPWD Toledo Bend regulation page: the limits above. Lake size from the Sabine River Authority of Texas.

One licence or two? Louisiana and Texas residents may fish the whole lake on their own state’s resident licence. Visitors from other states: see the questions at the end of this page.

Boat ramps. North Toledo Bend State Park near Zwolle has a double-lane launch, a fish-cleaning station and cabins. Resorts and marinas on both shores have their own launches (listed below).

Nearest towns: Many and Zwolle in Louisiana; Hemphill on the Texas side. Our Texas lake fishing guide covers Toledo Bend from the Texas shore.

Caddo Lake — bayous, cypress and a protected slot for bass

Texas Parks and Wildlife’s survey gives Caddo Lake about 27,500 acres, a little under half of it in Texas and the rest in Louisiana, north-west of Shreveport.

Caddo Lake, from Mooringsport to Uncertain

What swims there. Largemouth bass, crappie, white and yellow bass, bream, catfish and the old swamp fish such as bowfin (choupique) and gar.

How people fish it. In a small boat, canoe or kayak among the cypress, or from the piers. The marked boat roads through the bayous matter: the backwaters are easy to get lost in, and a local guide on the first day is money well spent.

Keeping a fish. Caddo has a protected slot: largemouth bass from 14 to 18 inches must go back. The limit is 8 black bass a day, no more than 4 of them 18 inches or longer. Crappie 25 a day; white bass 25; blue and channel catfish 50 a day with no more than 5 over 30 inches; flathead at least 18 inches, 10 a day.

Confirmed with the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries and Texas Parks and Wildlife, 17 November 2026 — LDWF 2026 regulations and the TPWD Caddo Lake regulation page: the slot and limits above. Before you eat what you keep: Louisiana has a mercury advisory for bowfin on Caddo (no more than two meals a month for adults), and Texas Parks and Wildlife notes a consumption advisory on the lake. Read both before you keep fish to eat.

Weeds and your boat. Giant salvinia, an invasive floating fern, grows on Caddo. In Texas it is illegal to carry it on a boat or trailer, and you must drain all water from live wells, bilges and motors when you leave the lake. Clean your boat before you go to another lake.

Boat ramps. Caddo Lake State Park near Karnack, Texas, has a boat ramp, a fishing pier, canoe rental and historic cabins. The resorts and marinas at Uncertain have ramps too.

Nearest towns: Uncertain and Karnack on the Texas side; Mooringsport on the Louisiana shore; Shreveport and Jefferson, Texas, for the most hotels.

Lake D’Arbonne — north Louisiana’s crappie lake

Louisiana State Parks gives Lake D’Arbonne 15,250 acres, beside the town of Farmerville in Union Parish.

Lake D’Arbonne, around Farmerville

What swims there. Crappie above all, with largemouth bass, catfish and bream.

How people fish it. From a boat over the old creek channels and brush, and from the five fishing piers at Lake D’Arbonne State Park — a good place for a family to start.

Keeping a fish. D’Arbonne has its own crappie rule: 50 a day, no more than 7 of them over 12 inches; no more than 50 in possession on the water. Otherwise Louisiana’s statewide limits apply.

Confirmed with the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries, 17 November 2026 — LDWF 2026 regulations: the D’Arbonne crappie rule (in force since November 2022). Lake size from Louisiana State Parks.

Boat ramps. Lake D’Arbonne State Park has a boat launch, five fishing piers, cabins, two lodges and kayak rental.

Nearest towns: Farmerville, on the lake; Ruston and West Monroe, a short drive away, for more hotels.

Ross Barnett Reservoir — “the Rez” on the edge of Jackson

The Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks gives Ross Barnett about 33,000 acres on the Pearl River: some 24,000 acres of open lake and 9,000 acres of river water above Highway 43.

Ross Barnett Reservoir, from Ridgeland to the Highway 43 bridge

What swims there. Crappie, largemouth bass, bream and catfish. The department’s 2026 fishing forecast says the reservoir was producing “numerous two-pound crappie” this year.

How people fish it. From a boat on the main lake and up the river, and from the bank and the spillway below the dam. With Jackson next door, this is the easiest of the six for a short trip.

Keeping a fish. Black bass on Ross Barnett must be at least 14 inches, 7 a day. Crappie follow the statewide limit of 30 a day. In the spillway each angler may use only one pole, and in the Pearl River below it stripers and hybrids are 6 a day, at least 15 inches, with only one over 24 inches.

Confirmed with the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks, 17 November 2026 — statewide creel-limit rule (40 Miss. Admin. Code Part 3, Rule 1.4, updated May 2024) and the Ross Barnett lake page: the limits above. Lake facts from the department and the Pearl River Valley Water Supply District.

Boat ramps. The Pearl River Valley Water Supply District, which runs the reservoir, lists 33 boat launches and two kayak launches. It warns that launches are affected when the water is below 296 feet.

Nearest towns: Ridgeland on the west shore, Brandon on the east, and Madison just to the north.

Sardis Lake — north Mississippi’s crappie lake

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers built Sardis Dam on the Little Tallahatchie River in 1940. The Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks gives the lake about 32,100 acres at summer pool and 10,700 in winter, with the 400-acre Lower Lake below the dam.

Sardis Lake, from Batesville and Sardis to Oxford

What swims there. Crappie, crappie and crappie: the department’s 2026 Reel Facts sheet says 79 per cent of the lake’s anglers in 2025 were fishing for them. White bass, largemouth bass and catfish too.

How people fish it. From a boat, trolling or using live sonar over the old river channel; crappie guides run four- to eight-hour trips. The Lower Lake below the dam is a calmer place for a family.

Keeping a fish. Sardis has Mississippi’s special crappie rule: at least 12 inches, 10 a day per angler, no more than 25 a boat when three or more anglers are aboard, and no more than four poles each with two hooks or lures on a pole. In the spillway: 10 a day, no length limit.

Confirmed with the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks, 17 November 2026 — statewide creel-limit rule and the department’s crappie announcement of June 2024: the crappie rule above. Lake facts from the department’s Sardis “Reel Facts” for 2026 and from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, Vicksburg District, which gives a 9,800-acre conservation pool.

Boat ramps and marinas. John W. Kyle State Park at Sardis has a boat ramp, cabins and a campground on the Lower Lake. Sardis Lake Marina has the lake’s on-water fuel dock and rents pontoons.

Nearest towns: Batesville and Sardis, close to the dam; Oxford, to the east, for more hotels.

Grenada Lake — Mississippi’s biggest lake

The Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks gives Grenada about 35,820 acres at summer pool and 9,800 in winter, and calls it “the state’s largest lake”. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers built it in 1954.

Grenada Lake, from the town of Grenada to the Yalobusha

What swims there. The department calls Grenada “a popular destination for crappie and catfish anglers”. Bass and bream too.

How people fish it. As at Sardis: from a boat with a crappie guide, or on your own over the river channels in spring.

Keeping a fish. Grenada shares the special crappie rule with Sardis, Enid and Arkabutla: at least 12 inches, 10 a day, no more than 25 a boat with three or more anglers, four poles each. Otherwise Mississippi’s statewide limits apply.

Confirmed with the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks, 17 November 2026 — statewide creel-limit rule and the Grenada lake page (report updated 10 November 2026). Before you eat what you keep: the Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality lists a mercury advisory to limit how much largemouth bass and catfish you eat from Grenada Lake and the Yalobusha River below the dam. Read its table before you keep fish to eat.

Boat ramps. Hugh White State Park has a boat launch, duplex cabins and campsites, and sells fishing licences. Its main ramps are generally unusable from 1 November to 1 March, when the lake is low; low-water ramps elsewhere on the lake stay open, and the Carver Point ramp is closed.

Nearest town: Grenada, beside the dam.

The licence and the limits, plainly

Two states, two licences. Louisiana and Texas residents share the border lakes; Mississippi’s lakes on this page need a Mississippi licence.

Louisiana fishing licences

Who needs one. Anglers 18 and older. Anyone 17 or under, resident or not, fishes without one. Louisiana residents 60 and over use the Senior licence instead of the Basic.

For a visitor. Non-resident Basic season fishing licence $68; non-resident five-day licence $30. These freshwater lakes need only the Basic licence — the saltwater add-on is for south of the saltwater line.

For a Louisianan. Basic fishing licence $17.

Where to buy. Online at LouisianaOutdoors.com (you receive a temporary licence number straight away), from a licence retailer, or at the department’s headquarters in Baton Rouge.

Free fishing weekend. Louisiana holds one each June (6–7 June in 2026). The 2027 dates were not yet published when we checked.

Confirmed with the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries, 17 November 2026 — 2026 Louisiana Recreational Fishing Regulations and the recreational licence page: the prices and age rules above. Prices change. Buy from the agency or a licensed agent.

Mississippi fishing licences

Who needs one. Mississippi residents aged 16 to 64, and non-residents 16 and older. Residents 65 and over carry proof of age and residency instead.

For a visitor. Non-resident annual freshwater fishing licence $68; three-day $30; one-day $10. Agent and processing fees are added.

For a Mississippian. The department lists combined licences, such as the All Game Hunting and Freshwater Fishing licence at $25 and the Sportsman’s licence at $45, and a three-day freshwater licence at $3.

Where to buy. Online through the department’s licensing site, by phone on 1-800-5GO-HUNT, from licence vendors, or at state park offices such as Hugh White.

Confirmed with the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks, 17 November 2026 — licence price page and licence requirements page: the prices and rules above. The price page carries no year label; annual licences run one year from the day you buy. Prices change. Buy from the agency or a licensed agent.

Boats and life jackets

Louisiana. Anyone born after 1 January 1984 must pass an approved boating course to run a motorboat over 10 horsepower. Children 16 and under must wear a life jacket while under way on a boat under 26 feet. On boats under 26 feet steered by a tiller outboard over 10 horsepower, the engine cut-off lanyard must be attached to the driver.

Mississippi. Anyone born after 30 June 1980 must complete a boater education course and carry proof. Children under 12 may drive a boat only with someone 21 or older aboard. Children 12 and under must wear a life jacket while the boat is under way, and every person aboard needs one.

Confirmed with the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries (2026 regulations, boating section) and the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks (boater education and boating rules pages), 17 November 2026. On the Texas side of Toledo Bend and Caddo, Texas boating law applies.

Questions

Do I need two licences to fish Toledo Bend or Caddo Lake, on the Louisiana–Texas line?

Not if you live in Louisiana or Texas. Under the reciprocal agreement described in the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries 2026 regulations, a Louisiana or Texas resident holding a resident fishing licence of their own state (or exempt from needing one) may fish the border waters — Caddo Lake, Toledo Bend, the Sabine River, Sabine Lake and Sabine Pass — without buying the other state’s licence. Visitors from other states are not covered by that agreement as we read it: buy the licence of the state whose side you will fish, and check with the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries or Texas Parks and Wildlife if you plan to cross the line. On Toledo Bend the two states’ limits match.

What does a fishing licence cost a visitor in Louisiana and Mississippi?

Louisiana, from the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries: a non-resident Basic season fishing licence is $68 and a non-resident five-day licence is $30; Louisiana residents pay $17 for Basic. Anglers 17 and under need no Louisiana licence. Mississippi, from the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks: non-resident annual freshwater fishing $68, three-day $30 and one-day $10, plus agent and processing fees. Non-residents under 16 need no Mississippi licence. Prices were read on 17 November 2026 and do change.

What is the crappie rule on Sardis and Grenada lakes?

Mississippi has a special crappie rule on Sardis, Grenada, Enid and Arkabutla: crappie must be at least 12 inches long, the limit is 10 a day per angler, a boat with three or more anglers may keep no more than 25, and each angler may use no more than four poles with no more than two hooks or lures on each. In the spillways below the dams the limit is 10 a day with no length limit. Elsewhere in Mississippi the crappie limit is 30 a day.

Is there a slot limit on Caddo Lake bass?

Yes. On Caddo Lake largemouth bass from 14 to 18 inches are protected and must go back. The daily limit is 8 black bass, and no more than 4 of them may be 18 inches or longer. Louisiana and Texas both list this rule for Caddo. On Toledo Bend the rule is different: largemouth must be at least 14 inches, with 8 black bass a day.

Can I fish Ross Barnett Reservoir with a Louisiana licence?

No. Mississippi’s reciprocal agreement with Louisiana covers the Pearl and East Pearl Rivers where they form the state line, certain state-line lakes and the Mississippi River — not Ross Barnett, which lies wholly inside Mississippi. Visitors need a Mississippi licence there. On Ross Barnett, black bass must be at least 14 inches, with 7 a day.

Seven honest gaps

Visitors on the border lakes. The Louisiana–Texas agreement is written for residents of the two states. We could not confirm in writing what a visitor from a third state needs to cross the line on Toledo Bend or Caddo. Buy the licence of the state whose water you fish, and ask the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries or Texas Parks and Wildlife before you cross.

Texas licence prices. This page gives Louisiana and Mississippi prices only. For the Texas side, check with Texas Parks and Wildlife.

Mississippi’s year label. Mississippi’s price page carries no licence-year label. Check with the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks before you buy.

Lake sizes differ by source. The Corps of Engineers and the Mississippi department give different acreages for Sardis (the lake rises and falls by tens of thousands of acres), and Caddo’s figure depends on who is counting. We name the source each time.

Ramps and fees on Ross Barnett. We give the number of launches the reservoir district lists, but no launch fees or no-wake zones. Ask the district before you go.

Lake D’Arbonne businesses. We found only one private business on D’Arbonne with a current website of its own. Others exist; we list only what we could open.
