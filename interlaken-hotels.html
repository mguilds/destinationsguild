Where to Stay in Interlaken

Where to Stay in Interlaken — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Interlaken sits on a flat plain between two lakes, and that flatness is the reason we recommend it to older travellers over the prettier villages above it. There are no hills in town at all: two railway stations about a mile apart, joined by the Höheweg, a level tree-lined promenade you can walk end to end in fifteen to twenty unhurried minutes. The one decision that really matters is which station you sleep near. Interlaken Ost is where every mountain train leaves from — Grindelwald, Lauterbrunnen, Wengen and the Jungfraujoch — and staying within five minutes of it means you never repeat that walk on tired legs at six in the evening. Interlaken West has the shops, the pharmacies and the mainline trains. Below are the hotels we would send our own family to, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

The whole town is flat — this is the point

Interlaken sits on an alluvial plain between Lake Thun and Lake Brienz, and there is no hill anywhere in the centre. The Höheweg is paved, level, tree-lined and lined with benches, and the two stations are about 1.2 kilometres apart. Compare that with Grindelwald, which is built on a hillside where nearly every hotel involves a climb from the station. If short, easy walks matter to you, Interlaken is the right base and the villages above are the day trips.

Stay near Interlaken Ost if you are here for the mountains

Every mountain train leaves from Ost, and so do the Lake Brienz boats. If your holiday is built around excursions, a hotel within five minutes of that station spares you an extra half hour of walking every single day. West is the practical end — supermarkets, pharmacies, banks, more restaurants, Lake Thun boats, and the first stop for mainline trains from Bern and Zurich. Both are fine; the difference is real.

The Jungfraujoch is extraordinary, expensive and weather-dependent

It is Europe’s highest railway station at 3,454 metres, open every day of the year, and costs roughly CHF 235 to 240 return in the summer season — about half that with a Half Fare Card, and a quarter off with a Swiss Travel Pass. In cloud you pay all of it to stand inside a fogged-in building, so check the summit webcam the morning you go and be willing to swap days. Harder Kulm, ten minutes by funicular from beside Ost, gives you a glorious view for a fraction of the price.

Altitude deserves a plain word

You climb from about 570 metres in town to 3,454 at the Joch in under two hours, with no time to acclimatise. Breathlessness on the stairs and light-headedness are common and usually harmless. Move slowly, take the lift rather than the steps, drink water, skip the alcohol, and keep the visit to ninety minutes or two hours. If you have a heart or lung condition, uncontrolled blood pressure or recent surgery, ask your doctor first.

Luxury

Interlaken’s two grand hotels are also the only two in town with real swimming pools — and both have lifts, porters and level access.

The grande dame of Interlaken, facing the Höhematte meadow with the Jungfrau straight ahead. Adults-only & quiet

Carlton-Europe Vintage Adults Hotel

An 1898 house reworked as a vintage adults hotel with a real sixteen-and-over policy — no children, no stag parties, which is exactly the point. Individually styled rooms, a garden with a natural swimming pond and a small relaxation area. The central Höheweg position gives a short flat walk to Interlaken Ost and the Jungfrau trains. Interlaken Hotels — common questions

Should I stay in Interlaken Ost or Interlaken West?

Ost if your holiday is built around the mountains, West if it is built around shops and services. Every mountain train leaves from Interlaken Ost, so sleeping within five minutes of it saves you an extra twenty-minute walk in each direction every day. West has the supermarkets, pharmacies and banks, is where mainline trains from Bern and Zurich stop first, and is generally cheaper. The two stations are about 1.2 kilometres apart along a completely flat promenade, and one short train hop connects them.

Is Interlaken walkable for older travellers?

Exceptionally so, and it is the main reason we recommend it as a base over the villages above. The town sits on a flat plain, the main routes are paved rather than cobbled, there are benches along the Hoheweg, and you can cross the whole centre in twenty minutes. Local buses run between the two stations, and the guest card most hotels issue usually covers them, so ask at check-in. The mountain villages are a different matter: Grindelwald and Murren are on slopes.

Is the Jungfraujoch worth the money?

In clear weather, yes, and it is genuinely extraordinary. In cloud you pay roughly 235 to 240 francs return to stand inside a fogged-in building, so check the summit webcam on the morning and be willing to swap days. A Half Fare Card roughly halves it and a Swiss Travel Pass takes about a quarter off. If the price or the altitude gives you pause, Harder Kulm at 1,322 metres is ten minutes by funicular from beside Interlaken Ost, costs a fraction, and the view is glorious.

Will the altitude bother me?

It may, and it is worth knowing in advance. You go from about 570 metres in Interlaken to 3,454 at the Jungfraujoch in under two hours with no time to acclimatise, and shortness of breath on the stairs and mild light-headedness are common. Move slowly, use the lift rather than the steps, drink water, avoid alcohol that day, and keep the visit to about ninety minutes. Anyone with a heart or lung condition, uncontrolled blood pressure or recent surgery should ask their doctor first.

When is the best time to visit Interlaken?

Mid-June to late September is when everything runs, with the wildflowers at their best in late June. July and August are the most crowded, and the Jungfraujoch trains and Grindelwald buses can be standing-room only by mid-morning, so take one of the first departures. Late May to early June and late September to mid-October give you good conditions with far fewer people, and the autumn colour in October is lovely. Avoid November, when the region half shuts down for maintenance.

What is closed in the shoulder season?

A great deal, and it is the single most important thing to check. Swiss mountain lifts close twice a year for maintenance, roughly mid-April to mid-June and mid-October to early December. In 2026 the Schynige Platte railway runs only from mid-June to late October, Grindelwald First closes from late October to late November, and the Mannlichen cableways close from late October into December. The Jungfraujoch tunnel railway itself runs every day of the year. Always check the specific lift's own dates before booking flights.
