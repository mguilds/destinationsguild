Where to Stay in Punakha — the Valley and Khuruthang

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Punakha is the warm one. You come over the Dochula pass from Thimphu with its row of chortens and its view of the high peaks on a clear morning, and then you drop a long way — our ten-year figures put Punakha’s January afternoons around 61°F (16°C) against Thimphu’s 48°F (9°C). The valley floor is rice terraces and river, and the Punakha Dzong stands on the spit where the Pho Chhu and the Mo Chhu meet. Rooms divide between resorts spread along the valley and plainer hotels in Khuruthang, the small modern town. Trip.com files both under the same Punakha number, and every hotel below was opened on its own listing and its address checked before we wrote about it.

Punakha questions, answered

How long does it take to drive from Thimphu to Punakha?

Allow about three hours for roughly seventy kilometres, and do not be tempted to shave it. The road climbs to the Dochula pass at around 3,100 metres, where the 108 chortens stand and where on a clear winter morning you can see the high Himalaya, then descends a long way in turns to the valley floor. Most itineraries stop at Dochula for twenty minutes and again at Lobesa for the short walk to Chimi Lhakhang, which makes it a half-day rather than a transfer.

Is Punakha warmer than Thimphu?

Noticeably, and it is the main reason people enjoy it. Punakha sits far lower in the hills, and ten-year reanalysis figures put its January afternoons around 61F (16C) against about 48F (9C) in Thimphu, with June to August afternoons in the low 80sF (around 27C). That is why it was the winter capital, why rice grows on the valley floor, and why a night here is a pleasant break from the cold in December and January. In high summer it is the hot and humid end of the standard route.

How many nights should you spend in Punakha?

One is the common answer and two is the better one. A single night gets you the dzong and the drive, which is the minimum that makes the journey over Dochula worthwhile. A second lets you walk out to Chimi Lhakhang through the fields, cross the long suspension bridge over the Pho Chhu, and climb up to the Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chorten on the ridge, which is an hour or so up through rice terraces and the best short walk in the valley.

Should you stay in the valley or in Khuruthang town?

In the valley if you can. Khuruthang is a functional modern grid built when the old settlement by the dzong was moved, and the hotels there are cheaper and perfectly adequate, but the valley is what you came for - the river, the terraces and the look of the place in the early morning. Khuruthang earns its keep on a budget, on a one-night stop, or when the valley hotels are full in October and November.

Is Phobjikha worth the detour?

In winter, yes. The black-necked cranes arrive from Tibet in the late autumn and stay until early spring, and the valley - a wide, flat, treeless glacial bowl at around 3,000 metres with the Gangtey monastery at its head - is unlike anywhere else on the standard route. It is a long side road off the way east and there is very little accommodation, so it needs planning rather than improvising. Out of the crane season it is a beautiful detour but a less compelling one.
