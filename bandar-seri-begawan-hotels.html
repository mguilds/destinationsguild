Where to Stay in Bandar Seri Begawan — Centre, Gadong, Berakas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Bandar Seri Begawan is a capital of about a quarter of a million people spread thinly over a wide river delta, and that shape decides where you sleep. The ceremonial centre — the Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque on its lagoon, the Royal Regalia building, the jetties where water taxis leave for the stilt village of Kampong Ayer — is small enough to walk. Gadong, a few minutes west, is where Bruneians actually shop and eat. Berakas, to the north, holds the airport. There is no bad choice among the three, only a choice about what you want on your doorstep. The centre, on foot

The few blocks between the mosque, the Kianggeh market and the waterfront. Stay here if you would rather walk to things than order a car for every one of them — in this city that is a real decision, not a preference.

Bandar Seri Begawan questions, answered

Is Bandar Seri Begawan worth more than a night?

Two nights is right for most people, three if you want a day out to Temburong. The capital holds the Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque, the larger Jame’ Asr Hassanil Bolkiah Mosque, the Royal Regalia building with the sultan’s coronation chariot in it, the Brunei Museum side of town, and Kampong Ayer — a water village of several thousand people on stilts over the river, reached by water taxi from the embankment for a few dollars. None of it takes long, but together it makes a proper short stay rather than a stopover.

Where is the best area to stay in Bandar Seri Begawan?

The centre, on your first visit. It is the only part of the city built to a walking scale, and the mosque, the market, the Regalia building and the water-taxi jetties are all within a few minutes of each other. Gadong is the better choice if eating and shopping matter more to you than monuments, and Berakas if you have a dawn flight. With a car, all three are the same city.

Can you buy alcohol in Brunei?

No. Brunei does not sell alcohol — not in hotels, not in restaurants, not in shops. Non-Muslim visitors have long been allowed to bring in a personal quantity for their own consumption and must declare it on arrival; the allowance is set by the Royal Customs and Excise Department, and we are not printing a figure here because we could not read the current one off the department’s own site today. Check it before you fly. What is certain is that nothing is for sale once you are there, so a hotel bar is not a thing you will find in this country.

Do you need a car in Bandar Seri Begawan?

In the centre, no. Beyond it, more or less yes. Public transport is thin, taxis are few and ride-hailing is patchy, so most visitors who want to see Jerudong, Tutong or the Belait coast hire a car or arrange a driver through their hotel. Within the central blocks you will walk, and the water taxis across to Kampong Ayer are part of the pleasure of the place.

How far is the airport from the city?

Close. Brunei International Airport sits just north of the city in Berakas, a short drive from the centre rather than an hour away, which is why an ‘airport hotel’ in Bandar Seri Begawan is still an ordinary city hotel and why you do not need to move out there the night before a morning flight unless you want to.
