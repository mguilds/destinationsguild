Historic Hotels of Indiana

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

5 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 5 in Indiana, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1845

French Lick Springs Hotel

French Lick, Indiana · Historic Hotels member since 2000

Dr. William A. Bowles bought a three-story frame building near the mineral springs in 1845 and named it the French Lick Springs Hotel, calling its spring Pluto's Well. Fire took most of it in 1897. Thomas Taggart, an Indianapolis politician, rebuilt it with partners in 1901 as a resort, and Taggart, sole owner from 1905, added wings and bottled the water as Pluto Water. Bing Crosby, Louis Armstrong and Lana Turner came, golfers Walter Hagen and Jim Barnes played the Hill Course, and Presidents Truman and Reagan visited.

Est. 1884

Fairfield Inn & Suites Madison Historic Eagle Cotton Mill

Madison, Indiana · Historic Hotels member since 2023

As railroads bypassed Madison, Robert Rankin and James White chose to reinvest, and in 1884 began a four-story mill on St. Michaels Avenue beside the Ohio River. The Eagle Cotton Mill started production in 1885, shipping twine, candle wick and cloth by river. Its mostly female workforce struck for better conditions in 1889. The Johnson family owned it from 1890 into the 1930s, and Meese, Inc. later made canvas and wartime gear here. It appears in the 1958 film Some Came Running and reopened as a hotel in 2021.

Est. 1902

West Baden Springs Hotel

West Baden Springs, Indiana · Historic Hotels member since 2015

Dr. John A. Lane earned land from French Lick's founder, picked a marshy spot called Mile Lick and opened an inn; the village was soon named West Baden, after Wiesbaden in Germany. The Monon Railroad arrived in 1887, and in 1888 Lee Wiley Sinclair bought the hotel, adding a bicycle track where the Cubs, Reds and Pirates held spring training. Today's domed hotel was built in a single year, its 200-foot atrium once the largest dome of its kind. Al Capone and General John J. Pershing stayed.

Est. 1913

Omni Severin

Indianapolis, Indiana · Historic Hotels member since 2010

Grocery heir Henry Severin Jr. bought the old Grand Hotel of Indianapolis, standing since 1876 by Union Station, and tore it down to build anew. Carl G. Fisher and James A. Allison, founders of the Indianapolis Motor Speedway, helped pay for it, and Vonnegut and Bohn designed it, led by Kurt Vonnegut Sr., whose son became the novelist. The twelve-story Hotel Severin opened in 1913. Its Austrian crystal chandelier and 1913 mailbox remain, and the marble stair's railings were found in a barn and returned.

Est. 1926

InterContinental Indianapolis

Indianapolis, Indiana · Historic Hotels member since 2025

A.L. Block, who headed the L. Strauss Company, wanted offices grand enough to inspire his staff and customers, and hired Rubush and Hunter, the firm behind many buildings on Monument Circle. The ten-story Illinois Building, finished in June 1926 between the Circle and the State Capitol, used Italian marble, African mahogany and terrazzo floors, with big shop windows on the ground floor. It housed offices for decades, slid into disrepair after a 1970s sale, and has since been restored, its gilded cornice and lobby kept.
