Where to Stay on Paradise Island — Best Hotels & Resorts

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Written in August 2026. Every hotel named here was checked against the operator’s own information this month — including which Atlantis tower is closed.

Paradise Island is a strip of land about four miles long, joined to Nassau by two bridges, and for a great many American families it is the Bahamas. Atlantis stands across the middle of it with its towers and its aquariums and its water park; the Riu sits next door on Cabbage Beach; and at the far eastern end, behind a discreet gate, is the quietest and most expensive hotel in the country.

Three corrections will save you money and disappointment, so we will make them at the top. Atlantis is not all-inclusive — the resort says so in its own words, and every meal is a separate bill. The resort fee is compulsory and runs from $77 to $82.50 a night depending on the tower, plus ten per cent VAT. And the Beach tower has been closed since around 2021, so any cheap rate you find under that name is a listing nobody has taken down.

The island divides simply. The Atlantis complex in the centre — Royal, Coral, Cove and Reef — is where most visitors stay and where all the noise and all the entertainment is. Cabbage Beach, along the north shore, is the beautiful public beach the resorts front onto. The harbour side, facing Nassau, is quieter and cheaper and has water views rather than surf. And the far east end is a different holiday altogether: small, still and costly.

Every hotel below is a real, currently trading property that we checked against the operator’s own information this month. Where a category honestly has only one answer on this island — and pet-friendly does — we say so plainly rather than filling the space.

Hurricane season, honestly. The Atlantic season runs 1 June to 30 November and the genuine peak for the Bahamas is mid-August through October. Nassau and Paradise Island came through Hurricane Dorian in September 2019 far better than Abaco and Grand Bahama did, but the risk in those months is real and nobody should pretend otherwise. The 2026 season has been very quiet and NOAA has held to a below-normal forecast — though a quiet season still only needs one storm. If you travel between mid-August and the end of October, book a flexible rate and buy your travel insurance before a storm is named.

Budget & Value

There is no cheap hotel on Paradise Island — the lowest genuine rates we found start around two hundred dollars a night. What there is, is one very clever piece of value.

The best-known trick on the island, and it is a legitimate one. Comfort Suites sits immediately next to Atlantis, and its guests get complimentary daily access to Atlantis — the Aquaventure water park, the pools, the lazy river and the beaches — without paying the Atlantis room rate. What You Need to Know Before You Book

The Atlantis resort fee, and what it actually buys

It is compulsory, it is charged per night, and it is not small. The Coral and The Royal: $77 a night including VAT. The Cove and The Reef: $82.50. Harborside: about $20.52 per person per day. In return you get daily entry to the Aquaventure water park, unlimited access to The Dig, the Predator Tunnel and the marine habitats, Wi-Fi for four devices (six at the Cove and Reef), the fitness centre, bottled water, poolside and theatre films, local calls and the resort shuttle. Water-park entry is included for hotel guests; day visitors have to buy a pass. What the fee does not buy is a single meal.

One tower is closed — do not book it

The Beach tower at Atlantis has been shut since around 2021 and no longer appears on the resort’s own accommodation pages; it is earmarked for redevelopment. The Royal, Coral, Cove and Reef are all open and all renovated in the last few years, and Harborside continues as villa-style accommodation across the marina. If you find a cheap ‘Beach Tower’ rate on a third-party site, it is a stale listing.

Getting to Nassau, and the bridge

Two bridges connect Paradise Island to Nassau, one in each direction. Pedestrians cross free; cars pay about $2 and taxis about $3, and your driver will add it to the fare. From the airport, expect roughly $45 plus the toll and twenty-five to thirty minutes. Many taxis do not take cards, so carry US notes. The nicer way into town is the water taxi from the Paradise Island ferry dock to Prince George Wharf — about ten minutes and around $7 a head — though it involves a step down into the boat, which is worth knowing in advance. Jitney buses do not run on Paradise Island. Walking the bridge is possible and free but exposed, uphill and unpleasant in the heat.

Cabbage Beach is public — with a catch

The beach itself is public along its whole length. In practice the western end in front of Atlantis and the Riu is lined with resort loungers and reads as private. The free public access point is a sandy path off Casino Drive near the Riu, about fifteen minutes’ walk from the ferry dock; it is easy to miss and a secondary entrance has been closed at weekends. Loungers on the public stretch are rented by vendors rather than free, and vendor approaches on that section are persistent. A friendly, firm ‘no thank you’ is normal and expected here.

Money and taxes

The Bahamian dollar is pegged one-for-one to the US dollar and both circulate freely, so there is nothing to change. VAT is ten per cent and applies on top of nearly everything, including the resort fee. Between VAT, the resort fee and gratuities, budget roughly a quarter more than the room rate you were quoted.

Bringing a dog

Only the Ocean Club accepts pets on Paradise Island, and every animal entering the Bahamas needs an import permit from the Department of Agriculture in Nassau, obtained in advance and physically carried with the animal. The permit costs about $11 with VAT, the animal must be over six months old, and you will need a veterinary health certificate issued within ten days of travel showing current rabies immunity and freedom from internal and external parasites. Apply six to eight weeks out.

Two safety notes worth repeating

The US State Department advisory of 28 July 2026 places the Bahamas at Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution — ordinary care, not alarm. Two specifics apply directly here. US government employees are prohibited from renting jet skis on Paradise Island and New Providence following repeated assaults by operators; we would take that as a firm no for anyone. And firearms and ammunition are absolutely prohibited, including a single overlooked cartridge in a carry-on, which has led to American visitors being arrested. Check your bags before you fly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Atlantis all-inclusive?

No. Atlantis says so itself: 'Atlantis is not an all-inclusive Resort; we are a full-service Resort.' You sign for meals and drinks and settle the bill at the end, and optional meal plans are expensive. What you do get is the compulsory resort fee — $77 a night at The Coral and The Royal, $82.50 at The Cove and The Reef — which covers daily Aquaventure water-park entry, the marine habitats, The Dig, Wi-Fi, the gym and the shuttle. The only two genuine all-inclusives on Paradise Island are Hotel Riu Palace Paradise Island and the Warwick.

Which Atlantis tower should I book?

For the shortest walks, The Coral — it is the most central and closest to the casino, the aquariums and the main water-park entrances, which matters more than most people realise on a resort this size. For a kitchen and a quieter oceanfront position, The Reef. For the most expensive and most secluded rooms with their own beach and two private pools, The Cove. The Royal is the landmark building with the towers in every photograph. The Beach tower has been closed since around 2021 — if you see it for sale, that listing is out of date.

Can I stay somewhere cheaper and still use Atlantis?

Yes, and Comfort Suites Paradise Island is the legitimate way to do it: guests get complimentary daily access to Atlantis, including the Aquaventure water park, the pools, the lazy river and the beaches, plus a free hot breakfast. Read the limitation, though. Access runs only from the official three o'clock check-in on arrival day to eleven o'clock on departure day, so both travel days are lost, and you must collect wristbands each morning.

Is Paradise Island walkable?

In parts, and not in the way people expect. Within Atlantis the distances are long and there is a free internal shuttle you should use without embarrassment. Comfort Suites, Bay View and the Riu are all within a flat five to ten minutes of Cabbage Beach and of Atlantis. But the island as a whole is spread out, the Ocean Club at the eastern end is a drive from everything, and the bridge to Nassau is a hot uphill walk you would not want to make twice. Plan on taxis and the ferry.

What does it cost to get across to Nassau?

Two ways. A taxi over the bridge is the standard method: reckon on the metered zone fare plus a bridge toll of about $3 for a taxi, and note that many drivers do not take cards, so carry US notes. Or take the water taxi from the Paradise Island ferry dock across to Prince George Wharf in downtown Nassau — about ten minutes and roughly $7 a person, and much the nicer trip. It does involve stepping down into a boat. From the airport to Paradise Island, expect around $45 plus the toll.

Are there any adults-only hotels on Paradise Island?

Two, and both are all-inclusive. Hotel Riu Palace Paradise Island is strictly eighteen and over, directly on Cabbage Beach, and lively — loud pool music, persistent beach vendors, and a building that this year's guests describe as dated. The Warwick is quieter and on the harbour side, but its age rule is more complicated than 'adults only': eighteen to book, sixteen to be accommodated, and twenty-one and over between 15 February and 15 April. It also adds a mandatory $30 per person per night.

Can I bring my dog to Paradise Island?

Only to one hotel. The Ocean Club, A Four Seasons Resort accepts dogs of about twenty-five pounds or less with no pet fee; cats are not accepted, and dogs are barred from the spa, gym, pools and restaurants. Atlantis takes no pets at all, the Warwick permits service animals only, and no other property publishes a policy. Everything else you will see advertised as pet-friendly here is a private rental. On top of the hotel question, the Bahamas requires an import permit from the Department of Agriculture for every animal, applied for in advance, plus a veterinary health certificate issued within ten days of travel. If you want a choice of pet-friendly hotels, stay in Nassau instead.
