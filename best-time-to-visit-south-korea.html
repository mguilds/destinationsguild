When to Go to South Korea

The Best Time to Visit South Korea

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: the two shoulder seasons — spring (April to mid-May) and autumn (late September to early November) — are the clear winners. You get mild, dry, sunny days for palaces, temples and mountain trails, framed by cherry blossom in spring and blazing foliage in autumn. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the summer monsoon and the crowds, and the best timing for Seoul, Busan and Jeju.

South Korea has four sharply drawn seasons, and the country you meet changes completely with each one. A pink April morning under the cherry trees of a Seoul palace is a different world from the grey, streaming rain of the July monsoon, or a crisp, brilliant October afternoon on a maple-lit hillside, or a still, sub-zero January night with snow on the palace roofs. Pick your dates to match what you want to see, and the whole trip falls into place. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$$$ PeakMild & bloomingCherry blossomBook early

The signature season. As the cold breaks, the cherry blossom front sweeps north from Jeju and Busan in late March to Seoul in early April, followed by canola, azalea and a wave of festivals. Days warm into the comfortable teens and low 20s°C, ideal for palaces and hiking. The trade-offs are peak crowds and prices at blossom time, and occasional hazy days of yellow dust blown in from the mainland.

Summer — June to August

$$ MixedHot & humidMonsoon rainsBeach peak

Hot, sticky and green. The jangma monsoon soaks the country with heavy rain from late June through most of July, then August turns hot and humid, often in the low 30s°C, with a tail-end risk of typhoons. It's the domestic beach and festival peak, so the coast and Jeju are lively and pricey while the rainy weeks keep city rates softer. Pack light layers, an umbrella and a flexible plan.

Autumn — September to November

$$$$ PeakClear & crispBest foliageBook early

For many travelers the finest season of all: dry, stable, gloriously clear skies, and comfortable temperatures once the humidity lifts in September. The maples and ginkgos turn from the northern mountains down to the cities through late October and early November, drawing big autumn-leaf crowds. Early September can still be warm and carry a typhoon risk, but October is close to perfect.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold & drySki & snowCheapest cities

Cold, dry and often dazzlingly bright, thanks to Siberian winds. Seoul frequently sits below freezing and January nights are bitter, but the crowds thin and city prices ease. It's prime time for the ski resorts of Gangwon, snowy palace scenes, hot spring baths and steaming street food. The Lunar New Year holiday is the one busy, higher-priced spike in the season.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ Low seasonColdestSki & snow

The coldest month, dry and clear, with Seoul often below freezing and hard frosts inland. City sightseeing is quiet and good value, if you bundle up. It's peak ski season in Gangwon, and snow-dusted palaces and hot spring towns are at their most atmospheric. Watch for a surge around the Lunar New Year holiday.

February

$ Low seasonCold & dryQuiet cities

Still firmly winter, though the very worst of the cold begins to ease by month's end. Skiing continues, crowds stay light and prices low outside the Lunar New Year window. A smart month for budget-minded city travelers who don't mind chilly days and can chase the first hints of spring in the far south.

March

$$ ValueWarming upEarly blossom south

Winter loosens its grip and spring arrives from the south. Days grow milder, and by late March the earliest cherry blossom and canola are opening on Jeju and around Busan. It's a fine-value shoulder window before the April rush, though the north stays cool and the odd yellow-dust day can blur the skies.

April

$$$$ PeakCherry blossomFestival season

The blossom peak reaches Seoul and central Korea in the first half of the month, and the whole country turns pink and white. Mild, bright and beautiful, it's one of the two busiest, priciest times to visit, with festivals like Jinhae in full swing. Book flights and hotels well ahead and expect crowds at the famous viewing spots.

May

$$$ ShoulderWarm & greenGreat hiking

Arguably the most comfortable month of all: warm, dry and green, with lush hills and long, pleasant days before the humidity builds. Superb for hiking, temples and city walking. Domestic holidays early in the month can briefly fill popular spots, but overall it's a glorious, slightly calmer alternative to blossom season.

June

$$$ ShoulderWarmingMonsoon begins

Early June is warm and largely dry, a pleasant tail of late spring, but the jangma monsoon usually sets in toward month's end, first in the south. Humidity climbs steadily. A decent-value window if you go early, with green landscapes and manageable crowds before the summer rains and beach peak fully arrive.

July

$$ ValueWettestHot & humid

The heart of the monsoon: the wettest month, hot and sticky, with heavy downpours interspersed with muggy sun. City rates soften with the weather, so it can be surprisingly good value if you plan around the rain and lean on museums, cafés and indoor culture. The beaches get busy once the rains break late in the month.

August

$$$ Beach peakHottestTyphoon risk

The hottest, most humid month, often in the low 30s°C, and the domestic holiday peak, so beaches, Jeju and the coast are lively and pricier. The monsoon eases but the typhoon risk rises. Great for a beach-and-island summer if you love the heat; cities can feel heavy and steamy in the midday sun.

September

$$$ ShoulderCooling & clearingChuseok holiday

The humidity fades and the skies begin to clear into lovely early-autumn weather, though a late typhoon is still possible and early September can stay warm. The Chuseok harvest holiday brings a domestic travel surge and some closures, so check dates. Late September is a fine, uncrowded run-up to the foliage season.

October

$$$$ PeakClear & crispSweet spot

Arguably the best all-round month: dry, sunny and crisp, with the autumn foliage turning from the northern mountains southward through the second half of the month. Everything is comfortable and open, and the light is superb. It's a top season for travel, so crowds and prices are high — book ahead for the popular leaf-peeping spots.

November

$$$ ShoulderLate foliageCooling fast

The foliage reaches the cities and southern regions early in the month, a last flush of colour before the cold sets in. Clear, cool and increasingly brisk, with prices easing after the October peak. A lovely, good-value time for the palaces and city sights, though pack warm layers as late November edges toward winter.

December

$ Low seasonCold & clearFestive lights

Winter proper returns: cold, dry and often brilliantly clear, with the first snows in the mountains and ski resorts opening. Cities are quiet and well priced, and Seoul dresses up with festive lights and markets. Bundle up for sub-zero nights, and enjoy the hot spring baths, saunas and steaming winter street food the season is made for.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Winter — December to February, plus the July monsoon lull

$ Rock bottomFlights & city hotelsSkip Lunar New Year

Airfares and city-hotel rates are at their lowest in the cold months, when demand thins — January and February are the best-value weeks of the year, with December close behind. The clear exceptions are the Lunar New Year holiday and the ski resorts of Gangwon, which stay busy and priced accordingly. The other soft spot is the wettest stretch of the July monsoon, when city prices dip outside the domestic beach peak.

Want reasonable weather and a deal? Target the shoulder edges: late May, just before the humidity builds, and November, after the October foliage rush. You get comfortable days at well below peak-season prices. As a rule, midweek flights beat weekends, and booking a month or two out lands the best fares and rooms in Seoul.

Beating the Weather & the Crowds

South Korea's busy and difficult periods are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the weather to sidestep

Cherry blossomAutumn foliageJangma monsoonLunar New Year & Chuseok

The two great crowd-and-price peaks are cherry-blossom April and foliage October, when the famous viewing spots fill up and hotels book out. The Lunar New Year and Chuseok holidays bring domestic travel surges and some closures. On the weather side, the jangma monsoon from late June through July is genuinely wet, the tail of summer carries a typhoon risk, and midwinter is bitterly cold. None of these need ruin a trip — they just reward planning.

To enjoy the sights with more room, come in the calmer shoulder weeks — May and late September to early November away from the leaf-peak crush — and start early at the big-name spots. For where to base yourself, see our guide to where to stay in South Korea.

Jeju Island & the Mountains

Mild, green Jeju is loveliest in spring, when canola fields blaze yellow, and in the clear early autumn, though it's wetter and windier than the mainland and feels the summer typhoons. The mountains tell the opposite story: the Gangwon ski resorts and snowy high peaks shine from December to February, while summer and autumn bring superb hiking on Seoraksan and Hallasan.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to South Korea.

What is the best time to visit South Korea?

Spring, roughly April to mid-May, and autumn, from late September to early November, are the two standout windows. Both bring mild, dry, sunny days that are perfect for palaces, hiking and city walking. Spring is famous for cherry blossom, autumn for fiery foliage, and both sit between the cold of deep winter and the heat and rain of high summer. If you want the classic postcard, aim for the blossoms in April or the leaves in late October.

What is the cheapest time to visit South Korea?

Winter, from December to February, is generally the cheapest for flights and city hotels, apart from the Lunar New Year holiday and the ski resorts, which stay busy. The hot, humid depths of the summer monsoon in July are also softer on price outside the domestic beach peak. If you can handle cold days or a little rain, these are the best-value stretches of the year.

When should I avoid South Korea because of weather?

The summer monsoon, known locally as jangma, brings heavy rain from late June through most of July, and the tail of summer into September carries a risk of typhoons. Midwinter, especially January, is bitterly cold, with Seoul often below freezing. Spring can also bring hazy days of yellow dust blown in from the mainland. None of these ruin a trip, but they reward planning and a flexible itinerary.

When do cherry blossoms bloom in South Korea?

The blossom front sweeps up the country from the south. Jeju Island and the southern coast around Busan usually bloom in late March, while Seoul and the central region follow in early to mid April. Each city holds its peak for only about a week to ten days, so timing is a little bit of a gamble; the famous Jinhae festival near Busan and the palaces of Seoul are among the most spectacular places to catch it.

Is South Korea worth visiting in winter?

Yes, if you come prepared for the cold. Winter is dry and often brilliantly clear, the crowds thin, prices ease, and the mountains of Gangwon offer excellent skiing and snowy festivals. Seoul's palaces look magical under snow, and Korea's heated floors, hot spring baths and steaming street food are made for the season. Just pack proper layers, as January nights regularly fall below freezing.
