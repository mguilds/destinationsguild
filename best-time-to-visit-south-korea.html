When to Go to South Korea

The Best Time to Visit South Korea

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: spring (April–May) and autumn (late September–November) are the sweet spots. You get mild, dry days, cherry blossom in spring and blazing foliage in autumn — perfect for Seoul, Gyeongju and the mountains. Below is the full picture: Korea’s four distinct seasons, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the monsoon and the crowds, and the best timing for Seoul, Busan, Jeju and Gyeongju.

South Korea has four genuinely distinct seasons, and timing changes the trip completely. The country you meet under April’s cherry blossom — warm, pink-petalled, buzzing — is a different place from the humid grey of the July monsoon, the fiery red-and-gold hillsides of late October, or the crisp, snow-dusted palaces of January. Choose your dates well and everything is more beautiful and more comfortable. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$$$ PeakCherry blossomMild & dryBook early

Korea at its prettiest. Days warm steadily from a chilly March into a glorious April and May, and the cherry blossom sweeps north from Jeju and Busan to Seoul across late March and early April. Skies are mostly dry and clear, ideal for the palaces, Gyeongju and the mountains. The trade-off is popularity: blossom weekends draw crowds and top prices, so book flights and hotels well ahead.

Summer — June to August

$$ MixedHot & humidMonsoon & beachesPeak on the coast

Hot, humid and green. The jangma monsoon brings heavy rain from late June into late July, and August is steamy with a chance of typhoons — but it’s also prime beach season, when Busan and Jeju fill up and prices there rise. Inland cities are muggy and quieter, with lower rates. Pack light layers, an umbrella and a sense of humour, and lean on the coast and air-conditioned museums.

Autumn — September to November

$$$$ PeakFoliageCrisp & clearBest all-round

For many travelers the finest season of all. The humidity breaks in September, October brings crisp, sunny, low-rainfall days, and the autumn foliage blazes across the mountains and temples from mid-October into November. It’s superb for sightseeing everywhere, from Seoul’s palaces to Gyeongju’s tombs. Like spring, it’s popular and pricey at the peak, and the Chuseok holiday adds a domestic travel surge.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold & drySki seasonCheapest

Cold, dry and often bright, with sub-zero days in Seoul and snow in the mountains — and the lowest prices of the year outside the Lunar New Year holiday. It’s ski season at the resorts, the palaces look magical under snow, and the sights are blissfully quiet. Bundle up for real cold, especially the wind, and note that Jeju is milder than the mainland. Great value for hardy sightseers and skiers.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ Low seasonColdestSki & quiet sights

The coldest month, dry and often clear, with sub-zero days in Seoul and snow in the mountains. Prices are low and the sights quiet, and the ski resorts are in full swing. Watch for the Seollal (Lunar New Year) holiday, which sometimes falls in late January and packs the transport network. Bundle up and enjoy the calm.

February

$ Low seasonCold, easingLate ski

Still cold but slowly softening toward spring, with good late-season skiing and low prices. Seollal often lands here, so check the dates and book trains early. A fine month for quiet palaces, winter scenery and value, if you don’t mind the chill.

March

$$ ValueWarming upBlossom in the south

Spring stirs: chilly early on, milder by month’s end, with the first cherry blossom appearing in Jeju and around Busan in the last week or so. Crowds and prices are still moderate before the April rush. A great-value window to catch early blossom in the south.

April

$$$$ PeakCherry blossomGlorious

The headline month: warm, dry and wrapped in cherry blossom, which reaches Seoul in the first half of April. It’s peak season for good reason, and one of the loveliest times to see the whole country. Book everything well ahead, and remember each area blooms for only a week or two.

May

$$$ ShoulderWarm & greenSuperb weather

Arguably the best all-round month — warm, dry, green and comfortable, with lush landscapes and rolling festivals. The blossom is gone but the weather is at its most reliable. Still busy and priced accordingly, especially over holiday weekends, but a fabulous time to travel anywhere in Korea.

June

$$ ValueWarmMonsoon nears

Warm and increasingly humid, with early June often lovely before the jangma monsoon arrives late in the month. Prices ease from the spring peak and the sights are quieter. A good-value window if you catch the dry first half; pack an umbrella for the second.

July

$ CheapestMonsoon rainHot & humid

The wettest month, with the monsoon’s heavy downpours and high humidity, though rain often comes in bursts rather than all day. Prices for flights and inland hotels are among the lowest. Best for travelers who don’t mind wet weather, or who’ll base themselves on the beaches and in air-conditioned attractions.

August

$$$ BeachesPeak heatTyphoon watch

Hot, humid and the top domestic holiday month, so Busan and Jeju beaches are packed and their prices peak while inland cities stay muggy and cheaper. The monsoon eases but typhoons are possible. Great for a coast-and-water trip, less so for heavy city sightseeing; start early and hydrate.

September

$$$ ShoulderHumidity breaksWarm seas

A turning point: the humidity fades and clear, warm days return, though early September can still be hot and typhoon season lingers. The Chuseok holiday often falls here, packing transport for a few days. As the month goes on it becomes a lovely, good-value time to travel, with the sea still warm.

October

$$$$ PrimeAutumn foliageSweet spot

The autumn showpiece: crisp, sunny, low-rainfall days and blazing foliage spreading from the northern mountains into the temples and cities. It’s glorious everywhere and one of the two best months to visit — and busy and pricey to match. Book ahead and time the mountains for peak colour in the second half.

November

$$$ Rising then easingLate foliageCrisp & clear

Early November carries the tail of the foliage into Seoul and the south, with cool, clear, beautiful days and easing crowds. By late month it turns properly cold and prices soften toward the winter low. A quietly excellent, often underrated time to travel with fine weather and thinner crowds.

December

$$ → lowColdSki & lights

Cold and dry, with the ski resorts opening and the cities lit for the season. Prices are low outside any holiday spikes, and the sights are quiet. Great for winter scenery, skiing and a peaceful city break — just pack seriously for the cold and the wind.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Winter and mid-summer — with January and July the lowest

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSki or beach trade-offs

Long-haul airfares and city hotels bottom out in the cold months of December to February (outside the Lunar New Year holiday) and again during the wet heat of July, when the monsoon thins the crowds. Winter has the bonus of ski season and snow-dusted palaces; summer suits a beach-first trip. Either way you get the lowest prices and the quietest sights.

Want good weather and value? Target the shoulder edges: March (before the April blossom peak) and late November (after the foliage rush). You get comfortable sightseeing at well below peak prices. As a rule, midweek flights beat weekends, and avoiding the Seollal and Chuseok holidays saves both money and stress on the transport network.

Beating the Weather & the Crowds

Korea’s busy and testing periods are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the weather to sidestep

Blossom & foliage weekendsSeollal & ChuseokJangma monsoonAug typhoons

The sharpest crowds and prices come on the cherry-blossom weekends of April and the autumn-foliage weekends of October, plus the domestic holidays of Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok, when the country is on the move and transport sells out. On the weather side, the jangma monsoon soaks late June into July, and August can bring typhoons. None of these need ruin a trip — they just reward planning.

To have the great sights closer to yourself, travel on weekdays and in the shoulder windows — March, May and late November — and start early: Seoul’s palaces, Gyeongju’s tombs and the mountain temples are quietest at opening. Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to South Korea.

What is the best month to visit South Korea?

April, May, October and early November are the strongest all-round months. Spring brings warm, dry days and the famous cherry blossom, which rolls north from late March in Jeju and Busan to mid-April in Seoul; autumn delivers crisp, clear weather and spectacular foliage. Both seasons are ideal for Seoul, Gyeongju and the mountains, though they are also the busiest, so book ahead.

What is the cheapest time to visit South Korea?

Winter, roughly December to February (outside the Lunar New Year holiday), and the hot, wet heart of summer in July are the cheapest for flights and hotels, because the cold and the monsoon rains thin the crowds. If you can handle sub-zero days or humid heat, you will find the lowest prices and quiet sights, and winter is also ski season in the mountains.

When should I avoid South Korea because of weather or crowds?

The monsoon (jangma) brings heavy rain from late June into late July, and August can be hot, humid and prone to typhoons, so pack for wet weather then. For crowds, avoid the Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok holidays, when much of the country travels and cities can feel empty while transport is packed, and expect peak prices during cherry-blossom and autumn-foliage weekends.

When is cherry blossom and autumn foliage season in South Korea?

Cherry blossom usually begins in late March on Jeju and around Busan and the south, reaching Seoul in early to mid-April, with each area in bloom for only about a week to ten days. Autumn foliage runs the other way, starting in the northern and mountain areas in mid-October and reaching Seoul and the south from late October into early November. Both are weather-dependent, so watch the seasonal forecasts.

What about visiting during Korean holidays like Seollal and Chuseok?

Seollal (Lunar New Year, in January or February) and Chuseok (the autumn harvest festival, in September or October) are the two big family holidays, when millions travel to their hometowns. Trains, buses and flights sell out and many small shops and family restaurants close for a few days, though palaces, big attractions and hotels stay open. The dates shift each year on the Western calendar, so check them and book transport well ahead.
