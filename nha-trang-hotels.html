Where to Stay in Nha Trang

Where to Stay in Nha Trang — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Nha Trang is really two holidays wearing one name. The city runs along Tran Phu, a six-kilometre curve of public beach backed by a wall of hotels, with pavement cafes, seafood restaurants, a night market and a pharmacy always within ten minutes. The Bai Dai strip toward Cam Ranh, twenty to forty minutes south, is the opposite: wide empty sand, big self-contained resorts with private beachfront, almost nothing to walk to, and the airport fifteen minutes away. North of the city, Ninh Van Bay is reachable only by boat. Below are the coast’s most reliable hotels by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Choosing Your Neighbourhood

Where you sleep shapes the whole trip. Here is the honest lay of the land.

City beachfront, the Cam Ranh strip, or a boat-in bay?

Stay on Tran Phu in the city if you want to walk to dinner, browse the night market and have shops and a doctor to hand — accepting motorbike traffic, some construction, and a beach shared with the whole city. Stay on the Bai Dai and Cam Ranh strip if you intend to settle into one resort, swim on private sand and value being fifteen minutes from the airport rather than an hour; there is very little to walk to, so plan on eating in. Ninh Van Bay, north and boat-access only, is quieter and more beautiful still, but the crossing and the steep, rocky terrain make it a poor fit for anyone with limited mobility or a fixed medication schedule — and rough weather can cancel the boat. Two honest notes for 2026. First, genuinely private beach is now scarce in the city itself: the province has been returning seafront land to public use, and the old Ana Mandara resort on Tran Phu was demolished and is becoming a public promenade — a great deal of stale material online still lists it, so do not book it. Second, the InterContinental Nha Trang is open but currently carries its own advisory about construction noise nearby, which light sleepers should weigh.

Luxury

Nha Trang’s true top tier has migrated out of the city to the peninsulas and coves, where resorts are built around the landscape rather than squeezed onto Tran Phu.

Six Senses Ninh Van Bay

$$$$ LuxuryNinh Van BayBoat access onlyPrivate-pool villas

The property that made this coastline famous, and it still earns the reputation — sixty-odd timber-and-thatch villas scattered along a boulder-strewn cove that no road reaches. Villas come with private pools and outdoor bathrooms, and the spa sits over the water. One practical caution: the ground is genuinely steep in places, so ask for a Beachfront or Water Pool villa rather than a Hilltop or Rock villa if stairs and buggy transfers are a concern. Budget a half-day from Cam Ranh airport including the mandatory boat, and confirm the transfer well ahead, as the resort requires.

Villa Le Corail, A Gran Meliá Hotel

$$$$ LuxuryBai Tien, north shoreAll villasEight onsen pools

An all-villa resort tucked below Fairy Mountain with Hon Tre island in the middle distance, and the most polished new-build luxury address on this coast. There is a large infinity pool, three swimming lagoons, and a row of eight hot onsen pools facing the sea — a genuinely restorative combination for older joints. The Spanish restaurant is the one that earned the property its culinary reputation, and the spa runs a hydrotherapy circuit. Note that it is on the northern shore beyond Tran Phu, about fifteen to twenty minutes from the city beach, not down at Cam Ranh as is often assumed.

Boutique

Genuinely small properties here mean thirty to eighty villas on a private cove rather than a design hotel in town — the city centre’s ‘boutique’ label is usually applied to ordinary mid-range hotels.

An Lam Retreats Ninh Van Bay

$$$$ BoutiqueNinh Van Bay37 villasPlunge pools

The small, softer neighbour to Six Senses in the same bay — thirty-seven timber villas with plunge pools, and a mood closer to a private house party than a resort. Because it is compact, staff learn your name by the second morning, which many older travellers value more than square footage. The trade-off is identical to its neighbour’s: nothing arrives except by boat, so pack for the whole stay and expect the crossing to be cancelled in rough weather. Roughly fifty-five minutes by road from Cam Ranh airport to the mainland lounge, then the launch.

Mia Resort Nha Trang

$$$ BoutiqueBai Dong, Cam LamPrivate coveAward-winning spa

Built into a low cliff above its own crescent of sand, Mia remains the most tasteful mid-size property on the Cam Ranh road — about eighty rooms and villas rather than five hundred. The beach restaurant sits right on the sand and the spa is a long-standing award winner. The Cliff Villas involve real steps down to the water, so request a Condo or Garden category if that matters; the buggies run constantly regardless. It is roughly halfway between the airport and the city, which suits travellers who want quiet but still intend to go into town for dinner.

Budget & Value

Nha Trang offers the best price-to-comfort ratio of any Vietnamese beach city — a renovated four-star with a rooftop pool routinely costs under fifty dollars, so there is little reason to accept a genuinely cheap room.

Rosaka Nha Trang Hotel

$ ValueCity centreRooftop infinity poolRenovated 2024

The value pick with a view — a hundred and forty rooms, two restaurants, and a rooftop infinity pool that guests routinely call the best in the city, open from early morning until late evening. It was renovated in 2024, so the finishes are newer than the price suggests. The location puts the night market and a wide choice of restaurants within an easy flat stroll, which matters a good deal if you would rather not rely on taxis after dark.

Family-friendly

The island and Cam Ranh mega-resorts are purpose-built for children, with water parks, kids’ clubs and suite layouts, and Hon Tre island adds a theme park next door.

Vinpearl Resort & Spa Nha Trang Bay

$$ FamilyHon Tre islandKids’ clubCable car or speedboat

The grandparents-with-grandchildren choice: a large, well-run island resort with a kids’ club, babysitting, family rooms, a shallow water-park area and a discount at the theme park next door. Getting there is part of the fun — the cable car over the bay is one of the longest sea-crossing gondolas in the world, though a speedboat is available if heights are unwelcome. The grounds are big, so use the shuttle buggies rather than walking the length of them in the afternoon heat.

Alma Resort Cam Ranh

$$$ FamilyBai Dai, Cam RanhTwelve poolsWater park

Built for multi-generational groups: every unit is a suite or villa with a kitchenette, twelve pools cascade toward eight hundred metres of beach, and the water park has a wave pool and a lazy river. Adults-only

Nha Trang has no strictly adults-only hotel, and we checked the filters rather than trust them — the market here is built on Vietnamese, Korean and Russian family holidays. Several resorts do have adults-designated pools or quiet wings, which in practice achieves much of the same thing; the villa resorts of Ninh Van Bay and Mia are the quietest addresses on this coast. Pet-friendly

A short and mostly small-hotel list. Only one internationally branded property publishes a proper written policy, and several hotels widely tagged pet-friendly online in fact refuse animals — the Novotel Nha Trang and The Arena Cam Ranh among them — so confirm in writing before you fly.

Citadines Bayfront Nha Trang

$$ Pet-friendlyTran Phu beachfrontServiced apartmentsWritten pet policy

The only proper international-brand address in Nha Trang with a published, unambiguous pet policy — dogs, cats and fish welcome, a maximum of two per apartment, a weight limit of about fifteen kilograms, certain breeds excluded, and a stated nightly fee per animal. The three hundred-odd units are studios through three-bedroom duplexes with kitchenettes, which suits a longer stay or anyone managing a special diet. There is a pool, a gym and a twenty-four-hour reception, and the beach is directly across Tran Phu.

Beachfront

Genuinely private beach is now scarce in the city itself, since the province has been returning seafront land to public use. Private-sand seekers should look north to Bai Tien or south to Bai Dai.

Amiana Resort Nha Trang

$$$ BeachfrontBai Tien, north shorePrivate beachSaltwater lagoon pool

Amiana occupies its own sheltered bay north of the city, with a genuinely private beach — a rarity now that Nha Trang has been reclaiming its shoreline. Three pools include a heated freshwater pool and a natural saltwater lagoon you can swim in, and the resort has its own mud-bath spa, the local wellness ritual, without the coach parties of the commercial mud-bath complexes. It calls itself barefoot luxury, and the rooms and villas are spread low and wide rather than stacked. Ten to fifteen minutes north of Tran Phu.

All-inclusive & Resort

Real all-inclusive is a recent import to Vietnam, concentrated on the Cam Ranh strip and largely run by Turkish operators. Many other Vietnamese resorts advertise ‘all-inclusive’ as an optional add-on, so always check whether drinks are actually in the rate.

Run by an operator that knows the all-inclusive form properly, this delivers what American travellers actually expect from the phrase: meals, drinks including beer, wine and cocktails, entertainment programming, pools, a water park and a kids’ club all folded into one rate. At around five hundred rooms it is large and lively rather than intimate, and the evenings involve organised entertainment, which some guests love and others do not. Spa & Pool

This coast’s speciality is the mineral mud bath, and its standout value proposition is the all-spa-inclusive model, where daily treatments come with the room rather than the bill.

Fusion Resort Cam Ranh

$$$ SpaBai Dong, Cam RanhDaily massage includedTwo warm pools

Fusion’s signature idea remains the best-value wellness proposition in Vietnam: every rate includes daily spa treatments, so two massages a day between a couple is simply part of the room price rather than a considerable surprise at checkout. There are two warm pools — worth knowing, since many Vietnamese resort pools are distinctly cold from December to February — and the celebrated ‘breakfast anytime, anywhere’ policy, which is a kindness when jet lag has you awake at four in the morning. Villa-style accommodation throughout.

Nha Trang Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Nha Trang.

Should I stay in Nha Trang city or out at Cam Ranh?

Stay in the city on Tran Phu if you want to walk to dinner, browse the night market and have shops and a pharmacy to hand — accepting traffic and a public beach. Stay on the Bai Dai and Cam Ranh strip if you intend to settle into one resort, swim on private sand and appreciate being fifteen minutes from the airport instead of an hour. There is very little to walk to down there, so plan on eating within the resort.

Is Evason Ana Mandara still open?

No. The former Ana Mandara resort on Tran Phu closed in 2022 and was demolished; the province took the seafront land back for public use and the site is becoming a public promenade. A good deal of stale material online still lists it, so do not book it. A separate and unrelated property, Ana Mandara Cam Ranh, is open and trading down on Bai Dai — they are two different hotels.

Does Nha Trang have real all-inclusive resorts?

Yes, and it is the one part of Vietnam where the phrase means what Americans expect. Selectum Noa Resort Cam Ranh and Aquamarine Cam Ranh By Swandor both include meals, drinks and entertainment in the rate. Elsewhere in Vietnam ‘all-inclusive’ is usually an optional add-on package — at Alma Resort, for instance, it is an extra — so always confirm what is actually in the price.

When is the best time to visit Nha Trang?

Nha Trang keeps its own calendar and is the exception on this coast. It is dry and sunny from January to August, which makes it the sensible beach choice in June and July when central Vietnam is baking and the south is wet, and it turns wet from October to December, exactly when Hanoi and the north are at their loveliest. See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Vietnam.

Is Ninh Van Bay suitable for older travellers?

It is beautiful and it is demanding. The resorts there are reachable only by boat, the crossing can be cancelled in rough weather, and the terrain is steep and rocky, with steps between villas and the water. If you go, ask specifically for a beachfront or water villa rather than a hilltop or rock villa, confirm the buggy service, and do not plan it around a fixed medical appointment. For a gentler luxury stay, the flat, buggy-served resorts at Bai Dai are the better choice.
