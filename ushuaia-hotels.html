Where to Stay in Ushuaia — Best Hotels at the End of the World

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Ushuaia is built on a mountainside that drops straight into the Beagle Channel, and that one fact should decide where you stay. The town centre, along Avenida San Martín and the waterfront Avenida Maipú, is flat where it counts and within a few minutes’ walk of the restaurants and the cruise pier. Everything up the hill — and some of the finest hotels in Argentine Patagonia are up the hill — has the views, but the streets are genuinely steep and you will use the shuttle. Happily, most of the out-of-town hotels run a free one, and we say so below. If you are here to board an Antarctic voyage, come a day early: flights south are weather-dependent and ships do not wait. Do not underestimate the hills. Ushuaia’s side streets climb hard from the waterfront, and a hotel three blocks up can be a serious walk with a suitcase. If you are joining a ship, a hotel on Avenida Maipú or San Martín will put you within five minutes of the pier on the level. Pack layers whatever the month: this is the far south, and a bright morning turns to sleet by lunchtime with no warning at all.

Luxury

$$$$ LuxuryCerro Alarkén, ~2 km above townHeated outdoor poolHourly shuttle

There is a lift, children of all ages are welcome, and pets are not accepted. Crucially, the free shuttle into town runs hourly — you do not need a car, but you will not be walking down either.

Ushuaia Hotels — Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of Ushuaia should I stay in?

In the town centre, along Avenida San Martín or the waterfront Avenida Maipú, especially if you are joining a ship — the Albatros, Lennox, MIL810 and Alto Andino are all within a few minutes of the pier. Up the hill, at the Arakur or the Wyndham Garden, you get the views and the pools, and you use the free shuttle. Out on the water at Los Cauquenes you get quiet and a private beach, ten minutes from town.

Which Ushuaia hotels have a lift?

Trip.com confirms lifts at the Arakur, Los Cauquenes, the Wyndham Garden, MIL810, the Albatros, the Lennox and the Alto Andino. It does not mention a lift at the Tolkeyen, so confirm before booking if stairs are difficult. Bear in mind that in Ushuaia the streets outside matter as much as the lift inside: the side streets climb hard from the waterfront.

Are there adults-only hotels in Ushuaia?

Not that we can verify. Every property we checked on Trip.com either welcomes children of all ages or accepts them in some rooms, so we have not listed an adults-only section for Ushuaia — we would rather tell you the category is empty than fill it with something that does not fit.

Can I bring a dog to Ushuaia?

Not to a hotel, as far as we can establish. Every property we checked states plainly on Trip.com that pets are not allowed. If you are travelling with an animal, look at self-catering rentals instead and confirm the policy directly with the owner before you book.

Which Ushuaia hotel is best if I am joining an Antarctic cruise?

The Hotel Albatros on Avenida Maipú, which Trip.com places roughly five minutes’ walk from the pier on level ground — no hill and no taxi on sailing morning. MIL810 and the Lennox are close behind and cost less. Whichever you choose, arrive at least a day early: flights to Ushuaia are weather-dependent and a ship will not wait for you.

When is the best time to visit Ushuaia?

For most visitors, November to March — the southern summer, when the Antarctic season runs, the Beagle Channel boats sail daily and there is daylight until late. June to August is the ski and winter season, beautiful in its way but with only a few hours of light. Bring layers and a windproof coat in any month.
