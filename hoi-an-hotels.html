Where to Stay in Hoi An

Where to Stay in Hoi An — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Hoi An divides cleanly into three, and the choice matters more here than in most places. The Ancient Town and the riverside put the UNESCO lanes, the tailors and the lantern-lit evenings within a flat walk. The beaches are ten to fifteen minutes out by car — An Bang has kept its sand and gained a relaxed cafe village; Cua Dai’s northern end has been restored while its southern end is still a construction site. The countryside at Cam Thanh and the Tra Que herb gardens offers rice-paddy quiet and the best value per square metre. Below are Hoi An’s most reliable hotels by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Choosing Your Neighbourhood

Where you sleep shapes the whole trip. Here is the honest lay of the land.

Ancient Town, beach or countryside — and a word about the sand

Choose the Ancient Town or the riverside if you would rather not depend on a shuttle timetable or a taxi after dinner — though the central lanes are lively until late and are closed to traffic at peak hours. Choose the beach for sea air and swimming, and here one honest caution matters: Cua Dai has suffered years of severe erosion. Its northern end, toward An Bang and Cam An, has genuinely recovered, with submerged breakwaters and sand replenishment restoring a walkable beach; the southern end toward the river mouth is still under construction, with a 3.4-kilometre erosion-prevention project running through 2026. Several excellent resorts on that southern stretch have rock revetment rather than open sand in front of them, so if you are booking for the beach, ask the hotel to confirm its current frontage. An Bang never eroded and remains the dependable choice. The countryside around Cam Thanh suits a longer, slower stay, at the cost of needing the shuttle for everything. One administrative note: since the July 2025 provincial merger, Hoi An addresses now read ‘Da Nang City’ rather than ‘Quang Nam Province’. Same town, new paperwork.

Luxury

Hoi An’s top tier is villa-and-garden rather than tower-and-lobby, and the two leaders sit at opposite poles: coastal grandeur fifteen minutes out, or a walk to the Japanese Bridge.

Four Seasons Resort The Nam Hai, Hoi An

$$$$ LuxuryHa My BeachAll villasThree infinity pools

The grandest address on the central Vietnamese coast, and it has aged into its reputation rather than out of it. Every unit is a free-standing villa raised on a plinth with a platform bed at its heart, and the three descending infinity pools that step toward the sea are among the most photographed in Asia. For older travellers the important detail is the buggy service: the grounds are large and staff will fetch you from anywhere within a couple of minutes, which removes the walking problem that sinks many resorts this size. Its beach is on Ha My, north of the erosion zone entirely, so the question of sand simply does not arise here. Fifteen minutes from the Ancient Town.

Boutique

The town’s small-hotel scene clusters along the Thu Bon riverside, where four-star polish costs what a mid-range room does elsewhere in Asia.

Budget & Value

Hoi An is exceptionally generous at the lower end: a genuine pool, a garden and a free shuttle are standard around fifty dollars, so nobody need trade comfort for price here.

Family-friendly

Look for flat, low-rise, garden-set resorts with a shuttle running to both town and beach, which lets three generations pursue different days and meet at the pool.

Palm Garden’s reputation among repeat visitors rests on a simple fact: through the worst of the erosion years it was consistently named as one of the properties that retained a good-sized beach, while neighbours lost theirs to rock. Adults-only

Hoi An has no genuinely adults-only hotel. Booking sites do offer an ‘adults only’ filter here that returns sixty-odd properties, but not one of them actually enforces a minimum guest age — several simultaneously advertise family amenities. We would rather say so than let a filter tempt anyone. Couples wanting quiet should book a low-room-count riverside boutique such as Little Riverside, or a villa resort like the Nam Hai, which achieves the same end without the label. These are the two full-service hotels we could verify, and in both cases the policy is carried by third-party directories rather than the hotel’s own published terms, so confirm directly at booking.

Beachfront

All-inclusive & Resort

True all-inclusive barely exists in Vietnam, so the two real options near Hoi An both involve a trade-off — distance from town in one case, a compromised beach in the other. We have named both trade-offs plainly.

The nearest thing to a genuine all-inclusive in the Hoi An region, and the only one at scale — marketed explicitly as all-inclusive, with an eighteen-hole course attached and a long, uncrowded private beach that has nothing to do with Cua Dai’s troubles. Hoi An Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Hoi An.

What is the best area to stay in Hoi An?

For most visitors, the riverside just outside the Ancient Town — flat, quiet, and a walk of well under a kilometre into the lanterns and the tailors, with no shuttle timetable to keep. Choose An Bang beach if sea and sand are the point, since An Bang never suffered the erosion that troubled Cua Dai. Choose the countryside at Cam Thanh for rice-paddy quiet and the best value, accepting that you will use the shuttle for everything.

Is the beach at Hoi An still eroded?

Partly. Cua Dai is really a gradient. The northern end, toward Cam An and An Bang, has genuinely recovered — submerged breakwaters and sand replenishment have brought back a walkable beach. The southern end toward the river mouth is still compromised and remains a construction site, with a 3.4-kilometre erosion-prevention project running through 2026. If you are booking a resort for its beach, ask the hotel to confirm its current frontage in writing.

How far is Hoi An from Da Nang airport?

About forty minutes by car in normal traffic, and hotels will arrange the transfer. Hoi An has no airport or railway station of its own, so Da Nang (DAD) is the gateway for everyone. Many travellers split their stay between the two, which works well: Da Nang for the beach and the choice of resorts, Hoi An for the atmosphere.

When should I visit Hoi An?

February to May is ideal — warm, dry and without the ferocity of the June and July heat. Avoid October and November if you can: that is the wettest, most storm-prone part of the year, and in most years the Thu Bon River rises far enough to put water in the Ancient Town’s lanes for a few days. If you are travelling in autumn, choose a hotel set back from the riverbank.

Does Hoi An have adults-only resorts?

No. Booking sites offer an ‘adults only’ filter for Hoi An, but the properties it returns do not actually enforce a minimum guest age — several advertise family amenities at the same time. For a genuinely quiet, grown-up stay, book a small riverside boutique with a low room count, or an all-villa resort such as the Nam Hai, where the layout achieves the same effect.
