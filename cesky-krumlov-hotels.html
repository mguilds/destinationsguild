Where to Stay in Český Krumlov

Where to Stay in Český Krumlov — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Český Krumlov is a town of about thirteen thousand people wrapped inside a double loop of the Vltava, with a vast painted castle on the ridge above it. Almost everyone sees it as a day trip and almost everyone is wrong to: the coaches arrive around eleven and are gone by five, and the hours either side are when the place is genuinely magical. It is also, we should say plainly, the most physically demanding town in this book — steep, entirely cobbled, and full of protected buildings with narrow stairs and no lift. Below are the places to stay by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com. Choosing Where to Sleep

The town is tiny, so this is not really about neighbourhoods. It is about how far uphill you are willing to walk with a suitcase.

Inside the bend, below the castle, or out on the level?

OrientationSteepCobblesLuggage

The inner town — the loop of streets around Svornosti Square inside the river bend — is where you want to be for the evening and the early morning, and it is where nearly every hotel in this guide sits. It is compact, beautiful, entirely cobbled and never flat for long. Latrán, the street running below the castle on the other side of the river, is equally central and a shade less steep, but it carries the main flow of day visitors up to the castle gate. The Plesivec and outer areas across the water are quieter, cheaper and a five to ten minute walk in — the sensible choice if stairs are a concern, because the ground there is comparatively level. One practical point that matters more here than anywhere: most of the historic centre is closed to cars. Ask your hotel exactly where you may stop to unload and how far the door is from that point, because the answer is sometimes several hundred metres of cobbles.

Luxury

A small town with two genuinely grand hotels, both in historic buildings and both with the views you came for.

Hotel Růže

$$$ UpperInner townFormer Jesuit collegeRiver views

The grandest address in the town — a sixteenth-century former Jesuit college on the edge of the inner bend, with vaulted ceilings, a terrace over the river and a good deal of Renaissance stonework left where it was. It is the hotel people remember from a Krumlov trip. The building is genuinely old, which is the appeal and the caution: rooms vary considerably in size and the historic fabric means stairs in places. Ask about lift access and how many steps from reception to your room.

Boutique & Character

This is what the town does best — small houses, painted ceilings, six or eight rooms and a proprietor who knows the place.

Hotel Zlatý Anděl

$$ MidSvornosti SquareOn the main squareHistoric house

The Golden Angel sits directly on the town’s main square in a Renaissance house with a painted facade — about as central as it is possible to be. A long-established, characterful place with a restaurant below. Being on the square means the day-visitor bustle is right outside until late afternoon, and then it is silent; ask for a room at the back if you are a light sleeper.

A word about pushchairs

Honest noteCobblesSteepPlan ahead

We will be blunt, because nobody else will be: Český Krumlov is a hard town for a pushchair or a wheelchair. The streets are steep, the cobbles are large and rounded, and the historic buildings rarely have lifts. It is entirely possible — families do it every day — but choose a hotel on the flatter outer side, use a carrier rather than a buggy for the castle, and do not plan a day that involves crossing the town more than twice.

Rather than dress up an ordinary hotel as something it is not, here is the practical advice: the quietest grown-up stays in Krumlov are the small historic houses with six or eight rooms — Konvice and the Monastery Garden rooms above — and the back rooms of the larger hotels away from Svornosti Square. Whatever you book, the town itself goes quiet after the last coach leaves, which is the real luxury here.

Český Krumlov Hotel FAQ

Is it worth staying overnight rather than visiting from Prague?

Yes, and it is the single best piece of advice in this guide. The day-trip coaches arrive from around eleven and are gone by five. Before and after those hours the town is quiet, beautifully lit and almost empty. Visitors who only see it between noon and four often come away puzzled by its reputation.

How do I get there from Prague?

By express coach, not by train. The direct bus takes roughly three hours and is comfortable, frequent and inexpensive; the rail route is indirect and requires a change. Coaches also run to Vienna, Salzburg and České Budějovice. Book a seat in advance in summer.

How steep and difficult is the town really?

Genuinely steep and entirely cobbled. The castle sits well above the town and the streets climb in both directions from the river. The setts are large, rounded and slippery when wet. If you have knee, hip or balance trouble, stay on the flatter outer side of the river and treat the castle as one deliberate outing rather than a casual stroll.

Can I drive to my hotel?

Usually not to the door. Most of the historic centre is closed to traffic, with car parks on the edge of town. Ask your hotel in advance exactly where you may stop to unload and how far the walk is from there — the answer is sometimes a few hundred metres over cobbles, which changes what luggage you should bring.

How many nights do I need?

One night is enough to get the evening and the early morning, which is the whole point. Two nights lets you do the castle properly, take the tower, see the baroque theatre if it is open, and perhaps raft a stretch of the river without hurrying.

When is it least crowded?

November to March, and any day before ten in the morning or after five in the afternoon. Winter is cold and some smaller places close, but the town under snow with nobody in it is a memory worth the temperature.

Do hotels here have lifts?

Many do not. This is a town of protected medieval and Renaissance houses where lifts often could not be fitted. Larger hotels such as OLDINN are the safer bet if stairs are a problem. Always ask the property directly: is there a lift, and how many steps from the street to it?
