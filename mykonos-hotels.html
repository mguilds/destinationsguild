Where to Stay in Mykonos

Where to Stay in Mykonos — Best Hotels, Quiet Beaches & Loud Ones

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Mykonos has a reputation, and it is only half deserved. Yes, it holds Greece’s most famous club beaches; it also holds long, quiet coves where nothing happens after nine in the evening. The difference between a wonderful week and a sleepless one is knowing which is which before you book. Agios Ioannis, Elia, Kalafatis and Ornos are the calm addresses. Platis Gialos is busy but wholesome and practical. Psarou is not a club beach but it is loud, moneyed and music-driven all afternoon, and Paradise and Super Paradise should be avoided altogether if you value sleep. Mykonos Town is enchanting by day and genuinely noisy after midnight. Below are our picks by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Quiet corners and loud ones

Agios Ioannis, Elia, Kalafatis and Ornos are the calm addresses — the first two are residential and end-of-the-road quiet. Platis Gialos is busy but sensible, with a paved seafront, gentle shelving water and the island’s main bus and beach-boat connections. Avoid Paradise and Super Paradise if you value sleep. If you stay in Chora, ask for a room away from the bar lanes.

Walking in Chora

The old town is a deliberate maze of narrow lanes paved in irregular cobbles and stone flags, with whitewashed grout lines that are repainted constantly and turn slick when wet or freshly done. There are steps, sudden changes of level and almost no handrails. Wear closed shoes with real grip rather than sandals, carry a small torch for the walk back after dinner, and remember that no vehicle can reach most of the town — bring a bag you can manage over cobbles, or arrange a porter through your hotel.

What it really costs

Mykonos is the most expensive Greek island alongside Santorini, and the gap over Naxos, Paros or Milos is large rather than marginal — roughly double for comparable lodging, and far more at the fashionable beach clubs, where a pair of loungers can run well into three figures for the day. Taxis are few and dear; hotel transfers and the public buses are better value. Ordinary tavernas away from the waterfront remain reasonable, so the island is manageable if you choose your beaches and your dinners deliberately.

Late May, June or September

These are the months when the sea is warm, the days are long, the restaurants are all open and the prices sit a third to a half below August. August is the crush: Greek and Italian holidays coincide, hotels sell out and the meltemi wind can blow hard for days. Early May and October are lovely and cheap, but the sea is bracing and some seasonal hotels and beach clubs are shut. For older travellers, mid to late September is the single best recommendation.

Luxury

Two full-service resorts on very different beaches — one calm, one glamorous.

Santa Marina, a Luxury Collection Resort

A large Marriott Luxury Collection resort spread over a headland above Ornos Bay, with its own small private beach and a well-regarded restaurant scene. Budget & Value

Mykonos Hotels — common questions

Which part of Mykonos is quiet, and which is loud?

Agios Ioannis, Elia, Kalafatis and Ornos are the calm addresses; the first two in particular are residential and end-of-the-road quiet. Platis Gialos is busy but wholesome, with a paved seafront and gently shelving water. Psarou is not a nightclub beach but it is loud, moneyed and music-driven all afternoon. Paradise and Super Paradise are the island’s daytime-into-night club beaches and should be avoided if you value sleep. Mykonos Town is charming by day and genuinely noisy after midnight, so if you stay there ask for a room away from the bar lanes.

Is Mykonos Town easy to walk around?

It is a pleasure to wander but it demands attention. The old town is a deliberate maze of narrow lanes paved in irregular cobbles and stone flags, with whitewashed grout lines that are repainted constantly and become slippery when wet or freshly done. There are steps, sudden level changes and almost no handrails. Wear closed shoes with real grip rather than sandals, carry a small torch for the walk back after dinner, and note that vehicles cannot reach most of the town, so bring a bag you can manage over cobbles or arrange a porter through your hotel.

Is Mykonos as expensive as people say?

Yes, and the gap over other Greek islands is large rather than marginal. Expect roughly double what you would pay on Naxos, Paros or Milos for comparable lodging, and considerably more at the fashionable beach clubs, where a pair of loungers can run well into three figures for a day. Taxis are few and dear, so hotel transfers and the public buses are better value. Ordinary tavernas away from the waterfront remain reasonable, which means the island is quite manageable if you choose your beaches and your dinners deliberately.

When is the best time to visit Mykonos?

Late May, June and September are the sweet spot: warm sea, long days, everything open, and prices roughly a third to a half below August. August is the crush, when Greek and Italian holidays coincide, hotels sell out and the meltemi wind can blow hard for days at a time. Early May and October are lovely and inexpensive, but the sea is bracing and some seasonal hotels and beach clubs have not opened or have already closed. For older travellers, mid to late September is the best single recommendation of the year.

Should I hire a car on Mykonos?

For most visitors, no. The island is small, the public buses connect Chora with the main beaches frequently in season, and hotels run shuttles. Parking in and around Chora is genuinely difficult in July and August, and the town itself is closed to vehicles. A car is worth having only if you want to explore the quieter north coast or move between several beaches each day; otherwise, buses, hotel shuttles and the occasional taxi are simpler and cheaper.

Is it worth taking the boat to Delos?

If you have any interest in the ancient world, yes — Delos is one of the most important archaeological sites in Greece, and it is a thirty-minute boat ride from Mykonos Town. Go on the first boat of the morning: there is almost no shade on the island, and by midday in summer the heat is punishing. The ground is uneven and rocky throughout, so wear proper shoes, take a hat and water, and allow about three hours including the crossing.
