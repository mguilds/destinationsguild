Where to Stay in Albufeira

Where to Stay in Albufeira — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Albufeira is the busiest resort on the Algarve, and it is really several different places wearing one name. There is the pretty whitewashed Old Town on top of a cliff; there is The Strip at Areias de São João, a mile east, which is loud, young and best simply avoided; and there is quiet pine-shaded hotel country spreading west to Salgados and east to Olhos de Água, Praia da Falésia and Vilamoura. The single fact that decides a holiday here is one the brochures never put plainly: the town sits on top of a twenty-to-thirty-metre cliff and the beaches sit at the bottom of it. Some hotels solve that with a lift. Others mean a hundred steps down and a hundred back up. We say which is which. Below are the hotels we would send our own family to, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

The cliff-and-steps problem, and where the level beaches are

This is the thing to get right. Albufeira’s Old Town stands on a cliff and its sand lies below. Praia do Peneco is the kindest: a free public lift, the Elevador do Peneco, drops twenty-eight metres from Rua Latino Coelho, and a pedestrian tunnel off Rua 5 de Outubro reaches Praia dos Pescadores on the level. It does go out of service, so know the tunnel as your fallback. Elsewhere expect staircases — around a hundred steps at some cliff-top hotels. For genuinely flat sand, go west to Salgados, where a boardwalk crosses the dunes, or east to Santa Eulália and Vilamoura.

Getting there from Faro airport

Faro is the region’s only airport, about forty-six kilometres east, and forty to fifty minutes by car on the A22 motorway. A pre-booked private transfer at forty to fifty-five euros is the sensible choice after a transatlantic flight: a fixed price, a driver holding your name and no wrestling with luggage. Metered taxis run fifty to seventy euros and up. Shared shuttles cost fifteen to twenty-five a head but stop repeatedly and can take ninety minutes. The public bus is about six euros and runs only a handful of times a day.

June and August are two different towns

In June the days reach the high seventies, the loungers are half empty, the evenings are long and the room rates sit well below peak; the sea is bracing but swimmable. In August it is the high eighties, every bed is taken, restaurants need reservations and the beaches are genuinely crowded. September is the connoisseur’s month: because the Atlantic lags the air by two months, the water is at its warmest of the entire year just as the crowds go home, and rates fall sharply. May and October are cheaper still, with warm days and the odd shower.

Where the noise is — and it matters here

The Strip, properly Areias de São João, is a straight run of bars, clubs and neon that stays loud until four in the morning and is aimed squarely at people forty years younger than our readers. Do not book near it. The Old Town is livelier than its cobbles suggest — charming by day, but music carries around the square until late, so ask for a room at the back. For quiet, look to Olhos de Água, an old fishing village popular with older visitors, or the pine-wood hotels at Falésia, Açoteias and Salgados.

Albufeira Hotels — common questions

Do we need a rental car in Albufeira?

It depends entirely on where you stay, and it is worth deciding before you book the hotel. In the Old Town, no — you can walk to beaches, restaurants and the bus station, and taxis are plentiful and cheap. But most of the hotels on this coast sit outside town at Falésia, Açoteias, Salgados or Olhos de Água, where without a car you depend on taxis for every dinner. If you plan day trips to Lagos, Silves or Cape St Vincent, take the car. If you intend to sit by a pool for a week, save the money and the parking headache.

Which area should older travellers choose?

Olhos de Água and Santa Eulália are the usual answers: quiet, level, with a promenade and a good restaurant scene. Salgados suits anyone who wants a flat beach with no steps at all. Praia da Falésia and Açoteias are peaceful and green but need a car. The Old Town works well if you want to walk to everything and can cope with cobbles and slopes — but pick a hotel with a lift. Avoid Areias de São João altogether, and be cautious about Praia da Oura in high summer.

How difficult is getting down to the beach, really?

Harder than the brochures suggest. Albufeira’s cliffs are twenty to thirty metres high and most hotels sit on top of them. The free Elevador do Peneco solves it in the Old Town when it is running, and the tunnel off Rua 5 de Outubro gives step-free access to Praia dos Pescadores. Pine Cliffs and Hotel Sol e Mar have their own lifts. Everywhere else, ask the hotel directly how many steps there are before you book — “a five-minute walk to the beach” here frequently means a hundred stairs each way.

Is Albufeira too young and rowdy for us?

Only in one district, and it is easily avoided. The reputation comes from The Strip at Areias de São João — perhaps eight hundred metres of bars catering to stag and hen parties — and from parts of Praia da Oura in July and August. The rest of the municipality is quite different: the Old Town is pretty and family-run, the marina is calm, and Olhos de Água, Santa Eulália, Falésia and Salgados are positively sedate. Choose your neighbourhood carefully and Albufeira is an easy, comfortable, English-speaking base.

What is the sea actually like?

Cool. This is the Atlantic, not the Mediterranean, and even in August the water reaches only about seventy-two degrees — refreshing rather than warm, and noticeably chilly in June. In winter it falls to the high fifties. The beaches shelve fairly steeply in places and there can be a shore break; blue flags and lifeguards are the norm in season. Most older visitors end up doing their swimming in a heated hotel pool and using the sea for paddling and long, flat walks at low tide, which is no bad holiday at all.

When should we book, and roughly what will it cost?

For July and August, book by January — the best rooms at the big cliff-top resorts go early and peak rates roughly double. For June, September or October, eight to twelve weeks ahead is usually enough and you will pay noticeably less; September in particular runs something like forty percent below August for a comparable room. As a rough guide, a simple three-star in the Old Town starts under a hundred dollars off-peak, four-star apartments from about a hundred and seventy, and the five-star clifftop resorts climb well past three hundred and fifty in high summer.
