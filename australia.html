Australia Travel Guide

A whole continent to itself — sunlit harbour cities, an ancient interior the colour of rust, and reefs and rainforests where the map runs out. From the white sails of the Sydney Opera House to a silent dawn at the foot of Uluru — this is the Guild's chapter on Australia.

Why visit Australia

Few places offer this much on one landmass — great cities, world-class coast, ancient desert and wildlife found nowhere else.

Cities on the water

Sydney's harbour and surf beaches, Melbourne's laneways and coffee, Brisbane's river and warmth — cosmopolitan, walkable, and edged by the sea.

Reefs and coast

The Great Barrier Reef off Queensland, the drama of the Great Ocean Road, and thousands of miles of empty white-sand beach in between.

The red heart

The outback is the oldest of stories — Uluru glowing at sunset, star-filled desert skies, and the living cultures of the world's oldest continuous civilisation.

Wildlife found nowhere else

Kangaroos and koalas, wombats and platypus, penguins and whales — a whole ark of creatures that evolved on their own island for millions of years.

How the country divides up

Sydney & New South Wales

Most trips begin in Sydney, and they should. The great harbour with its Opera House and Bridge, the ferry to Manly, the coastal walk from Bondi to Coogee, and a easy escape west into the Blue Mountains — eucalypt gorges, waterfalls and the Three Sisters, an hour and a half from the centre. North up the coast, Byron Bay is the country's easternmost point and its most relaxed surf town.

Melbourne & the southeast

Melbourne is the cultural counterweight — laneway coffee, live music, sport and some of the best eating in the country — and the start of the Great Ocean Road to the Twelve Apostles. West lies gracious Adelaide and its wine country; across the Bass Strait, Hobart anchors wild, green Tasmania with its MONA gallery and empty highlands.

Queensland & the Great Barrier Reef

The tropical northeast is Australia's reef-and-rainforest country. Cairns is the launch point for the Great Barrier Reef and the ancient Daintree, the world's oldest rainforest. Down the coast, Airlie Beach opens the sailing paradise of the Whitsundays, river-city Brisbane is the sunny state capital, and the Gold Coast stacks high-rises behind long surf beaches and theme parks.

The Red Centre & the outback

The interior is the oldest story on the continent. Uluru rises alone from the desert, glowing red at dawn and dusk, sacred to the Anangu people; nearby stand the domes of Kata Tjuta and the canyons of Watarrka. Alice Springs is the frontier hub, and far to the north Darwin opens the tropical Top End and the vast wetlands of Kakadu.

Top things to do in Australia

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Sydney Harbour

Tour or climb the Harbour Bridge, catch a performance at the Opera House, ride the Manly ferry and walk the coast from Bondi to Coogee.

Dive the Great Barrier Reef

Snorkel or dive the world's largest reef system off Cairns or the Whitsundays — coral gardens, turtles and thousands of fish.

Sunrise at Uluru

Watch the great monolith change colour at dawn, walk its base with an Anangu guide, and take in the desert sky after dark.

The Great Ocean Road

Drive Victoria's cliff-edge coast to the Twelve Apostles, through surf towns, koala forests and crashing Southern Ocean surf.

Meet the wildlife

Kangaroos and koalas, penguins on Phillip Island, whale-watching in season, and quokkas on Rottnest Island off Perth.

Eat and drink Melbourne

Laneway cafes and rooftop bars, then day-trip to the Yarra Valley or Barossa vineyards for some of the world's great wine.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Remember the seasons are flipped: summer runs December–February, winter June–August. Summer is beach season in the south but fierce heat inland.

Spring (September–November) and autumn (March–May) are the all-round sweet spots for the cities and the southeast — mild, and quieter.

For tropical Queensland and the Top End, travel in the dry season (May–October); the wet (November–April) is hot and stormy. The Red Centre is most comfortable April–September.

Getting there

Sydney (SYD) and Melbourne (MEL) are the main international gateways, with Brisbane (BNE) and Perth (PER) also taking long-haul flights.

Distances are vast, so most travellers fly between the big stops — Cairns (CNS) for the reef and Ayers Rock/Uluru (AYQ) for the Red Centre are quick domestic hops.

The currency is the Australian dollar, and the country is largely cashless — cards and tap-to-pay work almost everywhere. Most visitors need an ETA or eVisitor authorisation, so check your current visa and passport requirements before booking.

Australia travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Australia?

Remember the seasons are flipped: Australian summer runs December–February and winter June–August. Spring (September–November) and autumn (March–May) are the all-round sweet spots for the big cities and the southeast, with mild days and thinner crowds. For tropical Queensland and the Top End, aim for the dry season, roughly May–October; the wet, November–April, is hot, humid and stormy. The Red Centre around Uluru is most comfortable in the cooler months, April–September.

What currency is used in Australia?

The Australian dollar (AUD, symbol A$). Australia is largely a cashless country — cards and mobile wallets are accepted almost everywhere, from city restaurants to remote roadhouses, and tap-to-pay is the norm. Carry a little cash for markets, small rural towns and the occasional surcharge, but you'll rarely need much of it.

Sydney, Melbourne or Cairns — which should I choose?

Sydney is the headline city — the Opera House, the Harbour Bridge and the great ocean beaches, and the easiest place to arrive. Melbourne is the cultural counterpoint: laneway cafes, live music, sport and food, and the gateway to the Great Ocean Road. Cairns is the base for the Great Barrier Reef and the Daintree rainforest. Most first-timers pair a city with either the reef or the Red Centre rather than trying to see all of it.

How many days do you need in Australia?

Australia is enormous — the size of the continental United States — so distances eat time. For a first trip, ten to fourteen days lets you pair a city or two with one big-nature experience: Sydney and the reef, say, or Melbourne and Uluru. Two to three weeks lets you add a third region. Fly between the major stops rather than driving the whole way.

Is Australia safe for travellers?

Australia is a very safe, well-organised destination, and the main tourist areas are travelled easily by millions of people every year. The real hazards are environmental: the sun is fierce, so cover up and use high-SPF sunscreen; swim only between the flags on patrolled beaches and heed rip-current and marine-stinger warnings; carry plenty of water in the outback; and keep a sensible distance from wildlife. Check your government's current travel advice before you go.
