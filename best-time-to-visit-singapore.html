When to Go to Singapore

The Best Time to Visit Singapore

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: February to April is the sweet spot — the driest, sunniest window of the year, right after the wet Northeast Monsoon. Singapore sits almost on the equator, so it is warm and humid every month; the real question is rain, not temperature. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to plan around the rains and the haze, and the best timing for gardens, beaches and the big events.

Singapore is a true year-round city, and that is the first thing to understand about timing a trip. Being one degree north of the equator, it barely has seasons in the temperate sense: days run to a warm 31–33°C and nights to a sticky 23–25°C in January and in July alike, with humidity that hovers around 82% the year through. What changes is the rain. The wet Northeast Monsoon soaks the city from December into early March, the drier stretch runs February to April, and short, dramatic afternoon thunderstorms can crop up in any month. Choose your dates around the rain, the haze and the calendar of events, and the rest looks after itself. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

Singapore's year is shaped by monsoon winds rather than hot-and-cold seasons — here is what each phase feels like for weather, crowds and price.

Northeast Monsoon — December to early March

$$$$ Festive peakWettestCooler nightsFebruary dries out

The wet season, with the heaviest and most persistent rain of the year — December is the single rainiest month, and downpours can last hours rather than minutes, especially December into early January. Nights are marginally cooler. It coincides with the festive Christmas–New Year peak on Orchard Road and, later, Chinese New Year, so prices and crowds are high. The bright exception is February, which turns notably dry and sunny toward the end of the monsoon.

First Inter-Monsoon — late March to May

$$$ ShoulderDriest & sunniestHot afternoonsBest all-round

The pick of the year for weather. Winds go light and variable, the persistent monsoon rain fades, and you get the most reliable sunshine — though this is also the warmest stretch, with May the hottest month on average and short, sharp afternoon thunderstorms a near-daily possibility. Crowds ease after Chinese New Year, making late February through April a genuine sweet spot of good weather and softer rates.

Southwest Monsoon — June to September

$$$ MixedWarmSqualls & haze riskJune crowds, Sept F1

Warm and fairly bright between showers, but with two quirks. Pre-dawn “Sumatra squalls” can bring sudden strong gusts and rain, and this is when the regional haze from land fires may drift in — most likely August and September, though it varies hugely year to year. The June school holidays fill the city, and the Formula 1 weekend, usually in September, is a sharp crowd-and-price spike of its own.

Second Inter-Monsoon — October to November

$$ Quieter valueThunderyBuilding to wetFestivals

A transitional, thundery stretch as the winds swing back around and rainfall builds toward the December peak — expect frequent, lively afternoon and early-evening storms. It is one of the quieter, better-value windows before the festive rush, and a colourful one, with Deepavali lighting up Little India and the year-end decorations starting to go up. Pack for rain and keep plans flexible.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best. Remember: temperatures barely move, so this is really a guide to rain and events.

January

$$$ Wet peakRainyCooler nights

Firmly in the Northeast Monsoon, so one of the wetter months, with spells of prolonged rain and slightly cooler evenings. The New Year holiday keeps early January busy. Good for indoor Singapore — museums, malls, hawker centres — and a fine time if you don't mind carrying an umbrella between the showers.

February

$$$$ CNY peakDriest monthSunny

The driest, sunniest month of the year and, weather-wise, the best time to come — the monsoon rains ease right off. The catch is Chinese New Year, which falls in February in 2026, filling hotels and closing some family-run shops for a few days while the city turns festive. Book well ahead if your dates overlap the holiday.

March

$$$ ShoulderDry & brightFewer crowds

The tail of the dry window and a lovely time to visit: warm, largely sunny days with the crowds thinning after Chinese New Year. Afternoon showers begin to reappear late in the month as the inter-monsoon sets in, but they pass quickly. One of the best-value combinations of good weather and manageable prices all year.

April

$$$ ShoulderWarmAfternoon storms

Warm, bright and pleasant, though the heat is building and short afternoon thunderstorms grow more frequent — typically a downpour that clears in an hour. Crowds are moderate. Great for gardens, the bay and outdoor sightseeing if you plan mornings outside and keep the sticky mid-afternoon for something indoors.

May

$$ ValueHottestHumid

The hottest month on average, hot and very humid, with reliable afternoon thunderstorms. Still relatively dry overall and often good value before the June rush. Best suited to travelers happy to pace themselves — early starts, air-conditioned breaks and the occasional dip — in exchange for softer rates and long, bright mornings.

June

$$$ BusyWarmSchool holidays

The Southwest Monsoon settles in: warm, fairly bright between showers, with the odd pre-dawn squall. It's a busy month, thanks to the June school holidays and the summer sales, so book ahead. A good all-round time to be out and about, with attractions in full swing and generally shorter, less relentless rain than mid-winter.

July

$$ ValueWarm & drierWatch for haze

One of the drier, brighter months, warm and comfortable for sightseeing between passing showers. Crowds ease after the June holidays, helping value. The only wildcard is the regional haze, which can begin to appear this time of year — usually mild if it does — so keep an eye on air-quality reports if you are sensitive.

August

$$$ RisingWarmNational Day

Warm and fairly dry, with National Day on 9 August bringing parades, fireworks and a patriotic buzz — a lively, colourful time to be in the city, and a busy one around the date. Haze risk is at its seasonal highest through August and September, though many years pass with little or none. A festive, energetic month overall.

September

$$$$ F1 spikeWarmGrand Prix crowds

Warm and fairly bright, but dominated by the Formula 1 Singapore Grand Prix, usually held this month: a spectacular night race that sends hotel prices soaring and fills the city for its weekend. Superb if that's why you're here — expensive and crowded if it isn't. Haze can still linger. Book far ahead for race dates, or dodge them deliberately.

October

$$ ValueThunderyDeepavali

The second inter-monsoon brings livelier, more frequent afternoon and evening thunderstorms as the year tips back toward the wet season. It's a quieter, better-value month once the F1 crowds clear, and a colourful one, with Deepavali lighting up Little India. Plan outdoor time for the mornings and let the storms have the late afternoons.

November

$ Lowest demandWetterBest rates

Rainfall climbs as the Northeast Monsoon approaches, making this one of the wetter, cloudier months — and, not coincidentally, one of the cheapest, with some of the softest hotel rates of the year before the festive surge. A smart pick for budget-minded travelers who'll happily trade a few showery afternoons for lower prices and thinner crowds.

December

$$$$ Festive peakWettestChristmas dazzle

The rainiest month on average, deep in the Northeast Monsoon, with frequent and sometimes prolonged downpours. It's also the festive high point — Orchard Road ablaze with lights, Gardens by the Bay in full sparkle — so the Christmas–New Year fortnight is packed and pricey. Magical if you love the season; just book early and pack a good umbrella.

The Cheapest Time to Go

Singapore's prices track the calendar of holidays and events more than the weather, which makes the bargains easy to find.

The wetter, quieter windows — November, and the weeks after New Year outside Chinese New Year

$ Best ratesFlights & hotelsBring an umbrella

Hotel rates and airfares soften most when demand dips: November, as the rains build before the festive rush, and the quieter weeks after the New Year holiday that fall outside Chinese New Year, are the reliable value windows. You trade a few showery afternoons for noticeably lower prices — a good deal in a city where so much of the best sightseeing, from the museums to the food halls to the malls, is happily indoors anyway.

Want good weather and a fair price? Aim for March and, in a normal year, the drier stretches of July: comfortable, largely bright, and clear of the sharpest peaks. As a rule, avoid Christmas–New Year, Chinese New Year, the June school holidays and the September Grand Prix if the budget matters, and book midweek where you can.

Rain, Haze & the Crowds

Singapore's busy and tricky periods are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the weather to plan around

Christmas & NYChinese New YearJune holidaysSeptember F1Haze: Aug–Sep

The crowd-and-price crunches are the Christmas–New Year fortnight, Chinese New Year (February in 2026), the June school holidays and the Formula 1 Grand Prix weekend, usually in September. On the weather side, the Northeast Monsoon (December into early March) brings the heaviest rain, and the regional haze can drift in during the Southwest Monsoon, most often August and September — though some years see almost none. None of this need spoil a trip; it just rewards a little planning.

For the best balance of good weather and thinner crowds, target late February through April, and plan outdoor time — Gardens by the Bay, the zoo, Sentosa — for the mornings, when storms are least likely and the heat is kindest. Best Time by Interest

With temperatures steady all year, the ideal month depends less on where you go than on what you came to do.

Gardens, the Bay & outdoor sightseeing

Best: Feb–AprMornings winAvoid: Nov–Dec rain

Gardens by the Bay, the Botanic Gardens, the zoo and long walks along Marina Bay are at their best in the drier, sunnier February-to-April window, when you'll lose the fewest hours to rain. Whatever the month, start early: mornings are the driest and least fierce part of the day, with afternoon thunderstorms most likely from midday on.

Sentosa & the beaches

Best: Feb–Apr, JulWarm sea year-roundWatch afternoon storms

The sea is warm every month, so beach days on Sentosa come down to sunshine. The dry window from February to April is ideal, and July often delivers bright, drier spells too. Keep an eye on the sky in the afternoons and, in a haze year, on the air-quality reading — both are most likely to nudge a beach day indoors between August and October.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to Singapore.

What is the best time to visit Singapore?

February to April is the strongest all-round window. Singapore sits almost on the equator, so it is hot and humid every month of the year, but this stretch is the driest and sunniest, following the wet Northeast Monsoon. February is the single driest month. You still get short afternoon downpours, but you will lose fewer hours to rain than in the November to January peak of the wet season.

What is the cheapest time to visit Singapore?

The softest hotel rates tend to come in the quieter, wetter windows — roughly November, and the weeks just after the New Year rush that fall outside Chinese New Year. Airfares and rooms spike around Christmas and New Year, Chinese New Year, the June school holidays and the Formula 1 weekend in September, so travelling in the gaps between those peaks is the reliable way to save.

When is the rainy season in Singapore?

The wettest stretch is the Northeast Monsoon, from December into early March, with December the rainiest month of the year on average. Rain in this period can be prolonged and widespread rather than a quick afternoon burst. That said, Singapore has no true dry season — showers and thunderstorms are possible any month — so an umbrella is a year-round item. February is the notable dry exception within the monsoon.

Should I worry about the haze in Singapore?

The haze, caused by regional land and forest fires, can drift over Singapore during the Southwest Monsoon, most often between June and October and typically at its worst in August and September. It varies a great deal from year to year — some years bring almost none — and mainly affects outdoor comfort and air quality rather than closing attractions. If you are sensitive to air pollution, the February to April window sidesteps it entirely.

What are the busiest and most expensive times in Singapore?

The sharpest peaks are the Christmas and New Year fortnight, when Orchard Road is dressed for the festive season, and Chinese New Year, which falls in February in 2026. The June school holidays and the Formula 1 Singapore Grand Prix, usually held in September, also push hotel prices up and fill the city. Book well ahead for any of these, or travel in the calmer weeks between them.
