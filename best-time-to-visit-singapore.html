When to Go to Singapore

The Best Time to Visit Singapore

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: Singapore is hot and humid all year, so you can visit any time — but February to April is the driest and most comfortable window. Sitting just north of the Equator, the city has no cool season and no true off-season; instead it has wetter and drier spells and a calendar of big events. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to handle the heat and rain, and the best timing by interest.

Seasons at a Glance Month by Month Cheapest Time Heat, Rain & Crowds Best Time by Interest FAQ

Singapore's equatorial climate is remarkably steady: daytime highs hover around 31–32°C every month, nights stay warm and muggy near 25°C, and the humidity rarely lets up. What changes through the year is the rain and the crowds, not the temperature. The two monsoons — a wetter one at the turn of the year and a drier one at mid-year — shape the rhythm, and the events calendar does the rest. Choose your dates well and you dodge the heaviest downpours and the priciest peaks. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

Singapore doesn't have spring, summer, autumn and winter — it has monsoon and inter-monsoon spells. Here's the quick read on each.

Northeast Monsoon — December to early March

$$$$ Year-end peakWettest Nov–JanFestive & Chinese New YearBook early

The wetter half of the year, with the heaviest and most frequent rain from November through January — usually dramatic afternoon downpours rather than all-day grey. It brings the festive season and Chinese New Year, so the city is at its most colourful and its busiest and priciest around the holidays. By February the rain eases markedly into one of the nicest stretches of the year.

First Inter-Monsoon — late March to May

The sweet spot. The rains recede, the sun comes out and February to April rank among the driest, brightest months — ideal for the gardens, Sentosa and long walks between hawker meals. It's also the hottest and most humid stretch, with occasional afternoon thunderstorms, so pace outdoor plans for mornings. Crowds and prices are moderate outside the school-holiday and event peaks.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best. Remember the temperature barely moves; it's the rain and the events that vary.

January

$$$ Wet startRainyChinese New Year nears

One of the wettest months, with regular heavy afternoon downpours, though plenty of sun between them. The festive glow lingers and Chinese New Year often falls late in the month or early February, bringing colour and higher prices. Pack an umbrella and lean on the malls and museums when it pours.

February

$$$ ImprovingDrying outChinese New Year

The rains ease noticeably and the sun returns — one of the driest, most pleasant months. Chinese New Year (date varies) is a vibrant, busy peak with pricier rooms and some closures. Outside the holiday itself, February is a superb time to visit with bright skies and comfortable-for-Singapore conditions.

March

$$$ PrimeDry & sunnyGreat all-round

Among the best months: dry, sunny and warm, ideal for the gardens, the zoo and Sentosa. Crowds are moderate once Chinese New Year passes, and events like arts festivals fill the calendar. A reliable choice for first-timers who want the most comfortable weather Singapore offers.

April

$$$ PrimeHot & brightOccasional storms

Warm, bright and mostly dry, though the heat and humidity are at their yearly peak and afternoon thunderstorms can build. Excellent for sightseeing if you start early and break for air-conditioning midday. Prices are moderate, making it a strong value-and-weather combination.

May

$$ ValueHotGreat Singapore Sale

Hot and humid with scattered storms as the southwest monsoon nears. The Great Singapore Sale season and Vesak Day add appeal, and crowds are moderate. A good-value month for shoppers and anyone happy to plan outdoor time around the midday heat and the odd downpour.

June

$$ ValueDrier monsoonSchool holidays

The southwest monsoon brings drier air but sharp morning squalls. It's a local school-holiday month, so family attractions are busier, yet international crowds are moderate and rates reasonable. A dependable time to travel, with the sales still on and plenty of indoor backups for the showers.

July

$$ ValueRelatively dryFood festival

One of the relatively drier months, though still hot and humid with occasional squalls. The Singapore Food Festival celebrates the city's greatest asset, and crowds and prices sit at comfortable mid-levels. A fine month for food-focused trips and for combining the city with wider Southeast Asia travel.

August

$$$ RisingPossible hazeNational Day

Hot and generally drier, but the month when regional haze is most possible in some years, occasionally dimming the skyline. National Day on 9 August brings patriotic celebrations and busier weekends. Check air-quality readings if haze is in the news, but most years August is a solid, event-filled time to visit.

September

$$$$ F1 peakWarmFormula 1 night race

Home to the marquee event of the year: the Formula 1 Singapore Grand Prix night race, which fills the city and sends hotel prices soaring for its week or two. Weather is warm with building afternoon storms. Book far ahead for the race, or come just after it for a quieter, more affordable city.

October

$$$ ShoulderRain buildingDeepavali

The inter-monsoon rains build with more frequent afternoon thunderstorms, but mornings are often bright. Deepavali lights up Little India and cultural events abound. Moderate shoulder prices before the year-end surge make it a good-value month for travellers who don't mind planning around the showers.

November

$$$ WetWettest nearsFestive lights begin

One of the wettest months as the northeast monsoon sets in, with regular heavy downpours — though rarely all day. The Orchard Road festive lights switch on and the year-end buzz begins. Prices are moderate until the holidays approach; a rewarding month if you embrace the rain and the indoor delights.

December

$$$$ Festive peakRainyHolidays

Wet and festive. Heavy monsoon rains continue, but the city dazzles with Christmas lights, year-end events and a countdown at Marina Bay. School holidays and the festive weeks make it the busiest, priciest stretch of the year. Book well ahead, pack an umbrella, and enjoy Singapore at its most sparkling.

The Cheapest Time to Go

Singapore's prices track events and school holidays more than weather, since the climate barely changes. The pattern is easy to plan around.

Between the peaks — wetter months and non-holiday weeks

$ Best valueAvoid CNY, F1 & year-endMidweek flights

Airfares and hotel rates climb hardest around Chinese New Year (Jan/Feb), the Formula 1 night race in September, and the year-end festive season, plus local school holidays. Travel just outside those windows — think late January into early February before CNY, or parts of the June–September monsoon and the wet November stretch — and you'll find the softest prices and thinner crowds.

Want decent weather and a deal? Aim for the drier edges outside events: late February to March and quieter weeks of July. As a rule, midweek flights beat weekends, and booking a few months out lands the best fares into one of Asia's busiest hubs. Because rain here is short and sharp, even the wetter, cheaper months rarely cost you much sightseeing.

Beating the Heat, Rain & the Crowds

Singapore's busy peaks and weather quirks are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the weather to plan around

Chinese New YearF1 in SeptemberYear-end holidaysNov–Jan rain

The year-end festive season and Chinese New Year are the crowd-and-price crunches, with the Formula 1 week in September a sharp, localised spike. On weather, the November–January monsoon is the wettest, the April heat and humidity peak, and August–September can bring occasional haze. None of these need spoil a trip — they simply reward a little planning.

Beat the heat by front-loading outdoor sights — the Botanic Gardens, Sentosa, walking tours — into the morning, and retreat to air-conditioned museums, malls and hawker centres at midday. Carry a compact umbrella year-round, keep a flexible plan, and stay hydrated. For where to base yourself, see our guide to where to stay in Singapore.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travellers ask most when timing a trip to Singapore.

What is the best month to visit Singapore?

February to April is the strongest window overall. These months are among the driest and sunniest of the year, so sightseeing at Gardens by the Bay, Sentosa and the hawker centres is as comfortable as Singapore's steady heat allows. It sits after the wet year-end monsoon and before the mid-year rains, and it lines up with lively events, so it blends the best weather with plenty going on.

What is the cheapest time to visit Singapore?

The quieter, cheaper stretches are usually the wetter months and the periods between major holidays — roughly late January to early February (outside Chinese New Year), and parts of the mid-year monsoon from June to September and the wet November window. Airfares and hotel rates rise around Chinese New Year, the Formula 1 night race in September, the year-end holidays, and school breaks, so travelling just outside those peaks saves the most.

When is the monsoon and rainy season in Singapore?

Singapore has two monsoons. The wetter Northeast Monsoon runs from roughly December to early March, with the heaviest, most frequent rain from November to January. The Southwest Monsoon from June to September is drier but can bring sudden squalls and, in some years, haze from regional fires. Rain is usually short, intense afternoon downpours rather than all-day grey, so it rarely ruins a trip — just carry an umbrella and plan indoor options.

Is Singapore worth visiting in the rainy season?

Yes. Because the rain typically falls as brief, heavy afternoon showers, you can see plenty around them, and the city is superbly set up for wet weather with vast air-conditioned malls, museums, the Jewel at Changi and covered hawker centres. The wetter months can also mean lower prices and thinner crowds outside the holidays. Bring an umbrella, keep a flexible plan, and the monsoon is no barrier to a great trip.

Does it matter what time of day I sightsee in Singapore?

It helps. The heat and humidity peak in the early afternoon, so it is smart to tackle outdoor sights like the Botanic Gardens, Sentosa beaches and walking tours in the morning or late afternoon, and save the middle of the day for air-conditioned museums, malls and long hawker lunches. Afternoon showers also cluster around midday to early evening, which is another reason to front-load outdoor plans.
