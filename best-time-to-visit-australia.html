When to Go to Australia

The Best Time to Visit Australia

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: spring (September–November) and autumn (March–May) are the sweet spots. Remember that the seasons are upside down here: December to February is high summer, and July is the month you pack a coat. The shoulder months give you mild, dry days in Sydney and Melbourne, a comfortable Red Centre, a Great Barrier Reef out of the wet season, and prices well below the Christmas peak. Below is the full picture — the seasons, month by month, the cheapest time to go, and the right timing for each corner of a country the size of the continental United States.

Australia is not one climate but several, and the calendar you grew up with does not apply. Christmas is the hottest week of the year, the school holidays fall over New Year, and while Melbourne is shivering through a grey July, Cairns and Darwin are enjoying their finest, driest weather of the whole year. That is the single most useful thing to understand before you book: the south and the north run on opposite timetables. Choose your dates around which Australia you have come to see, and the trip gets easier, cheaper and a great deal more comfortable. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits. Southern-hemisphere dates throughout.

Summer — December to February

$$$$ PeakBeach seasonSchool holidaysWet in the north

The southern cities are at their liveliest: Sydney averages highs around 28C (82F) in January, the harbour and the beaches are the whole point, and the New Year fireworks are a genuine spectacle. It is also the most expensive and most crowded stretch of the year, because the entire country is on holiday from mid-December to late January. Meanwhile the tropical north is in its wet season — hot, humid, stormy and cyclone-prone — and the Red Centre is punishingly hot. Wonderful for a southern beach trip; a poor choice for the Reef, Darwin or Uluru.

Autumn — March to May

$$$ ShoulderMild & settledWet season endsExcellent value

One of the two best windows for a broad trip. The summer heat and the holiday crowds drain away, Sydney and Melbourne settle into warm, comfortable days, and by late April the tropical north has dried out and opened up for its own best season. The vineyards of the Hunter, the Yarra and the Barossa are in harvest, and Tasmania puts on real autumn colour. Watch only for Easter, which is a short, sharp domestic peak, and book around it rather than through it.

Winter — June to August

$$ Value in the southPeak in the northDry seasonWhales

The great inversion. Melbourne can sit around 14C (57F) with grey drizzle and Tasmania is properly cold, so southern city hotels are cheap and museums, galleries and long lunches come into their own — and the Snowy Mountains and Victorian Alps have a genuine ski season. At the same time the north is at its glorious best: Cairns, Darwin and the Kimberley enjoy warm, dry, low-humidity days, and this is the peak season up there, with prices to match. Humpback whales pass along both coasts. Choose your half of the country and winter is superb.

Spring — September to November

$$$ ShoulderBest all-roundWildflowersWarm seas

For most visitors, the finest season of the year. The southern cities warm up without the summer glare, the north is still comfortably dry into October, the Red Centre is at its best, Western Australia’s wildflowers are extraordinary and Sydney’s jacarandas turn whole suburbs purple in November. Whales head south with their calves close to shore. Prices sit below the summer peak until the December rush begins, which makes September and October the most efficient weeks of the year to be in Australia.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best. Temperatures are given for Sydney and Melbourne unless stated otherwise.

January

$$$$ PeakHottestSchool holidays

The hottest, busiest, priciest month. Sydney averages about 28C (82F), Melbourne about 26C (79F) with occasional 40C (104F) spikes, and every beach is full because the schools are out until late in the month. The Australian Open fills Melbourne and the Sydney Festival runs all month. Avoid the north and the Red Centre entirely; enjoy the southern coast if you can bear the crowds.

February

$ CheapestStill hotCrowds gone

Our value pick. It is nearly as warm as January but the holidays are over, so flights and hotels drop sharply and the beaches empty out. Sydney is humid and gets its wettest average month, though usually in short bursts. The north is still deep in the wet season. For a southern-cities trip on a budget, this is the month.

March

$$$ ShoulderLovely warmthFestival season

Warm, settled and thoroughly pleasant — Sydney around 26C (78F), Melbourne 24C (75F) — with the sea still warm enough for swimming and the crowds long gone. Melbourne hosts its comedy and food-and-wine festivals, Adelaide its Fringe, and the wine regions are in harvest. One of the most enjoyable months of the year in the south.

April

$$$ ShoulderMild & dryNorth opens up

A first-rate all-rounder. The southern cities are mild and comfortable for walking, autumn colour arrives in the Blue Mountains and Tasmania, and the tropical north is drying out and beginning its own high season. Easter is the one thing to plan around, as domestic prices spike for that fortnight. Otherwise, excellent value and excellent weather.

May

$$ ValueCool in the southDry season begins

The dry season opens in the north, which makes May the first genuinely good month for Cairns, Darwin and the Kimberley — warm, dry and not yet at peak prices. The southern cities cool down and quieten, hotel rates fall, and the first humpback whales appear off the east coast. Very good value if you are heading north.

June

$$ MixedCold in the southPeak in the north

Winter proper. Melbourne sits around 14C (57F) and Sydney around 18C (64F) with its second-wettest month, so pack a coat for the south. The north is glorious and busy, the ski fields open in the Alps, whale watching is in full swing, and Vivid Sydney lights the harbour into mid-June. Late June brings a school-holiday bump in prices.

July

$$$ North peakColdestSki season

The coldest month in the south and the best month in the north. Cairns and Darwin are dry, warm and clear, and booked out accordingly; Uluru has perfect walking weather by day and near-freezing nights. Melbourne and Hobart are cold and atmospheric, with the best of the galleries, the food and the football. School holidays run through the first half of the month.

August

$$ Value in the southDry & clearWildflowers begin

Still cool in the south but brightening, and southern city hotels are among the year’s cheapest. The north stays dry and busy, the Red Centre is close to ideal, and Western Australia’s wildflower season is spreading through the midwest. Melbourne runs its international film festival. A quietly rewarding month for value travelers.

September

$$$ PrimeSpring beginsBest all-round

One of the two best months of the year. Spring arrives in the south with warm, clear days and blossom, the north is still dry, Uluru is comfortable, and the wildflowers reach Perth. Whales begin their southbound migration with calves in tow. The AFL Grand Final fills Melbourne at month’s end. Book ahead but expect below-summer prices.

October

$$$ PrimeSweet spotWarm & dry

If you can only pick one month, pick this one. Sydney averages around 24C (76F) and Melbourne 20C (68F), the north remains dry until late in the month, the desert is warm rather than fierce, and the crowds have not yet arrived. Whale watching is at its best off Sydney, and the first jacarandas open. The single most reliable month for a multi-region trip.

November

$$$ RisingJacarandasWet season starts

Warm, long-evening spring in the south, with Sydney’s jacarandas at their purple peak and the Melbourne Cup carnival filling that city in the first week. The tropical north tips into the build-up: humid, stormy and the start of stinger season on the beaches. Prices climb through the month toward the December surge, so travel early in November if you can.

December

$$ → $$$$Summer arrivesBook far ahead

A month of two halves. The first fortnight is warm, lively and still reasonably priced — genuinely one of the better-value windows of the summer. From about the middle of the month the whole country goes on holiday, rates double or worse, and Sydney fills for New Year’s Eve. The north is wet and stormy. Book anything over Christmas many months in advance.

The Cheapest Time to Go

Long-haul airfare is the biggest line in an Australian budget, so timing it well is worth real money.

February first, then May to August — and never mid-December to January

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsAvoid Christmas

February is the cheapest month overall: the Australian school holidays have finished, Easter has not yet arrived, and the weather in the southern cities is still summery. The cooler months of May to August follow close behind for flights and southern-city hotels — with the important exception of the far north, where those same months are peak season and prices go the other way. The most expensive period by a wide margin is mid-December to late January, when the entire country holidays at once, followed by the Easter fortnight.

Want good weather and value? Target the shoulder edges: late March into April before Easter, and September into early October before the spring rush. You get comfortable sightseeing at well below peak prices. As a rule, book long-haul airfares four to eight months out rather than late, fly midweek where you can, and remember that a mid-December arrival before the school holidays begin can still be surprisingly reasonable.

Beating the Weather & the Crowds

Australia’s busy and difficult periods are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the weather to sidestep

Christmas & JanuaryEasterTropical wet seasonDesert heat

The sharpest crowds and prices come with the summer school holidays from mid-December to late January and again at Easter, when domestic flights, coastal hotels and national-park campgrounds all fill. There are shorter school breaks in April, late June to mid-July and late September, which lift prices for a fortnight at a time. On the weather side, the tropical wet season soaks the north from roughly November to April, with cyclones possible and box jellyfish making unprotected ocean swimming unwise from about November to May; and the Red Centre is dangerously hot in December and January, when Uluru regularly passes 40C (104F).

To have the great sights closer to yourself, travel in the shoulder windows — February, May, September and October — and start early: Uluru at sunrise, the Sydney coastal walks before nine, the Reef boats on the first departure. Sun protection is not optional here; the ultraviolet index is among the highest in the world, so a hat, long sleeves and sunscreen matter even on a mild day. Best Time by Region

Australia spans nearly 3,000 miles from tropical north to sub-Antarctic south. The right month depends entirely on where you are going.

Sydney, Melbourne & the southeast

Best: Mar–May, Sep–NovPeak: Dec–JanValue: Feb, Jun–Aug

The big southern cities are walking cities, so the mild shoulders win outright: March to May and September to November give you comfortable days for the harbour, the coastal paths, the laneways and the galleries. Summer is glorious on the beaches but hot, crowded and expensive; winter is cool and grey but cheap, and Melbourne in particular is built for it. See where to stay in Sydney and Melbourne.

The Great Barrier Reef & tropical north

Best: May–OctAvoid: Dec–MarStingers: Nov–May

Cairns, the Whitsundays, Darwin and the Kimberley run on the dry season, and it is emphatic: May to October brings warm, dry, low-humidity days, calm seas and the clearest water for snorkelling and diving. The wet season from November to April is hot, humid and stormy, with cyclone risk and reduced visibility, and box jellyfish make unprotected swimming unwise from roughly November to May — wear the stinger suit the operators provide. Coral spawning, an extraordinary sight for divers, falls around November and December.

Uluru & the Red Centre

Best: May–SepAvoid: Dec–FebCold nights

The desert is a shoulder-and-winter destination. From May to September the days are warm and clear, ideal for the base walk at Uluru and the Valley of the Winds at Kata Tjuta, while nights fall close to freezing — pack proper layers, whatever the brochure photographs suggest. December to February regularly passes 40C (104F), which makes walking genuinely unsafe in the middle of the day and puts trail closures in play. Flies are worst in the warmer months, so bring a head net.

Tasmania & the far south

Best: Dec–MarAutumn colour: Apr–MayCold, wild winters

Tasmania is the one part of Australia where summer is the right answer: December to March gives long days, open walking tracks on Cradle Mountain and the Overland Track, and warm enough weather for the coast at Freycinet. April and May bring lovely autumn colour and far fewer people. Winter is cold, wet and wildly atmospheric, with the Dark Mofo festival in June for those who like their midwinter with a little theatre. Pack for four seasons in one day whenever you come.

Perth & Western Australia

Best: Sep–Nov, Mar–MayWildflowers: Jun–NovHot, dry summers

Perth has a Mediterranean climate with hot, bone-dry summers and mild, wet winters, so the spring and autumn shoulders are the pick for the city, Rottnest Island and the Margaret River wine country. The headline act is the wildflower season, which rolls south across roughly June to November, reaching the midwest and Coral Coast in late winter and the southwest forests by October and November. The far north of the state, the Kimberley and Broome, follows tropical rules: go in the dry, from May to October.

Festivals & Wildlife by Season

Some months are worth choosing for what is happening rather than for the weather alone.

The events worth planning a trip around

Jan: Australian OpenMay–Jun: Vivid SydneyNov: Melbourne Cup

Summer opens with the Sydney Festival through January and the Australian Open tennis in Melbourne in the second half of the month. Late summer brings the Sydney Gay and Lesbian Mardi Gras in February and March and Adelaide’s Fringe, while March and April belong to Melbourne’s Comedy Festival and its food-and-wine season. In late May and June, Vivid Sydney turns the Opera House, the Harbour Bridge and half the city into a light show for three weeks — in 2026 it ran from 22 May to 13 June. Spring closes with the AFL Grand Final in Melbourne in late September and the Melbourne Cup carnival in the first week of November, both of which fill that city’s hotels.

For wildlife, humpback whales migrate along both coasts from about May to November, travelling north between June and August and returning south with their calves from September to November — the southbound leg passes closer to shore and is the better watching from Sydney’s headlands. Coral spawning on the Reef falls around November and December, sea turtles nest on the southern Reef from November to March, and Western Australia’s wildflowers bloom from June through November. Little penguins come ashore at Phillip Island every evening of the year, though the largest numbers are in summer.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to Australia.

What is the best month to visit Australia?

For a trip that takes in more than one part of the country, the best months are September, October, March and April. These are the shoulder seasons at either end of the Australian summer, when Sydney and Melbourne are mild and dry, the tropical north has finished or not yet begun its wet season, the Red Centre is warm by day rather than punishing, and the summer-holiday crowds and prices have eased. If you must choose one month, October is the safest all-round bet.

Are Australia’s seasons really reversed?

Yes. Australia is in the southern hemisphere, so summer runs from December to February, autumn from March to May, winter from June to August and spring from September to November. Christmas is a beach-and-barbecue affair, and July is the month to pack a coat for Melbourne or Tasmania. The far north works differently again, splitting the year into a hot, humid wet season roughly from November to April and a warm, dry season from May to October.

What is the cheapest time to visit Australia?

February is the single cheapest month, sitting in the gap after the Australian school holidays end and before Easter, and the cooler months from May to August are the next best value for flights and city hotels, particularly outside the late-June and July school break. The most expensive stretch by a wide margin is mid-December to late January, when the whole country takes its summer holiday at once, followed by Easter. Booking long-haul airfares four to eight months ahead usually beats booking late.

When should I avoid parts of Australia because of weather?

Avoid the tropical north, meaning Cairns, Darwin and the Kimberley, from about December to March, when the wet season brings heavy rain, extreme humidity and the risk of cyclones, and when box jellyfish make unprotected ocean swimming unwise from roughly November to May. Avoid the Red Centre around Uluru in December and January, when daytime temperatures regularly exceed 40C, or 104F. Southern Australia has no month that must be avoided, though bushfire risk rises across the hottest, driest summer weeks.

When is the best time for the Great Barrier Reef, Uluru and Tasmania?

The Great Barrier Reef is at its best in the dry season from May to October, when the water is clear, the humidity drops and the stinger risk is lowest. Uluru and the Red Centre are most comfortable from May to September, with warm days and cold desert nights that call for layers. Tasmania is a summer destination, best from December to March, when the days are long and the walking tracks are open, though autumn in March and April brings gorgeous colour and far fewer people.

When can I see whales, wildflowers and the jacarandas?

Humpback whales migrate along the Australian coast from about May to November, heading north between June and August and returning south with calves from September to November, which makes the southbound leg the better viewing off Sydney. Western Australia’s wildflower season sweeps south from about June in the Pilbara to November in the southwest. Sydney’s jacaranda trees bloom purple across late October and November, which is one of the loveliest weeks in the city’s year.
