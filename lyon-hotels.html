The areas, and a word about the hills

Lyon divides neatly. The flat Presqu'île between the two rivers is the walkable heart and where most of the good hotels are; the Renaissance old town of Vieux Lyon is the most atmospheric but the most uneven underfoot; and the two hills, Fourvière and Croix-Rousse, hold the best views at the cost of a climb — though funiculars and a metro spare your legs. We say plainly under each hotel below how central and how level it is, and whether a lift is confirmed.

Presqu'île (Bellecour & the peninsula)

The flat, central spine between the rivers — the great square of Place Bellecour, the shops of Rue de la République, and most of the city's best hotels. Level, walkable and central: our first suggestion for older travellers.

Vieux Lyon (Old Lyon)

The Renaissance old town beneath Fourvière — cobbled lanes, hidden traboules and the classic bouchons. Wonderfully atmospheric, but hilly, and the cobbles are hard going for anyone unsteady on their feet.

Fourvière & Croix-Rousse (the hills)

Lyon's two hills hold the big views — the basilica and Roman theatres on Fourvière, the old silk-weavers' quarter on Croix-Rousse. Funiculars and the metro do most of the climbing, but expect slopes around your hotel.

Confluence & Part-Dieu

The modern south, where the rivers meet — the Confluence museum, shopping and riverside — and the business and rail district around Part-Dieu station. Handy for trains and roomy family hotels, a short tram ride from the old centre.

Luxury

Lyon's grandest addresses — one on the flat Presqu'île by the river, one high on the Fourvière hill for those who want a view above all.

Lyon — common questions

Which area of Lyon should an older traveller stay in?

The Presqu'île around Place Bellecour is our first suggestion: flat, central, walkable, and where most of the good hotels are, with the shops and restaurants on the doorstep. Vieux Lyon is the most atmospheric but the hilliest and most uneven underfoot, so choose it only if cobbles and slopes are no trouble. For trains and modern, roomy family hotels, Confluence and Part-Dieu are convenient, a short tram ride from the old centre.

Is Lyon a walkable city, or is it very hilly?

The Presqu'île, the long peninsula between the Rhône and the Saône where you will spend most of your time, is flat and very walkable, and the riverbanks are level and pleasant. The two hills — Fourvière and Croix-Rousse — are genuinely steep, but each is served by a funicular or the metro, so you rarely have to climb unless you want to. In Vieux Lyon the cobbles are the thing to mind, especially after rain.

How do I get from Lyon–Saint-Exupéry Airport into the city?

The Rhônexpress tram runs from the airport to Part-Dieu station in about thirty minutes, every fifteen minutes through the day; from Part-Dieu a metro, tram or short taxi reaches your hotel. A taxi the whole way takes roughly thirty to forty-five minutes depending on traffic and costs a good deal more. You can tap a contactless card for the Rhônexpress and the city transport alike.

What will I pay in Lyon, and are cards accepted?

France uses the euro, and cards — including contactless and phone payments — are taken almost everywhere, so you need little cash beyond a few euros for a small bouchon, a tip or a market stall. A small nightly taxe de séjour (tourist tax) is usually added at check-out rather than shown in the booked rate. When a terminal offers to charge you in dollars, decline and pay in euros to avoid a poor built-in exchange rate.

Is Lyon safe?

Yes — Lyon is generally a safe city to walk in, including after dark in the main districts, and violent crime against visitors is rare. The usual sensible caution applies to petty theft around Part-Dieu station, on crowded public transport and in busy tourist spots, so keep valuables in a zipped inside pocket. The more everyday hazard for an older visitor is footing: the cobbles and steep lanes of Vieux Lyon are slippery when wet, so wear sturdy shoes and take the funicular rather than the steps when in doubt.
