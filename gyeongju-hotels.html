Gyeongju’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Gyeongju — Best Hotels by Type

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Gyeongju was the thousand-year capital of the Silla kingdom, and it wears its history in the open — grassy royal tombs rise between the streets, and Bulguksa Temple and Seokguram Grotto sit on the wooded slopes above town. Most visitors choose between the resort hotels ringing Bomun Lake, the hanok guesthouses and cafes of the old town, and the quiet temple-side hotels near Bulguksa. We’ve grouped Gyeongju’s most dependable, well-run places to stay by type, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can simply choose your dates.

Luxury

Gyeongju’s grandest hotels gather at the Bomun Lake Resort, a landscaped complex a few minutes’ drive east of the tombs. Here you get full-service spas, indoor and outdoor pools and calm lake or mountain views — a comfortable, self-contained base, with the temples and old town a short taxi ride away.

Hilton Gyeongju

$$$$ LuxuryBomun LakeSpa & poolsLake views

A polished, full-service Hilton right on the shore of Bomun Lake, with spacious rooms, a spa, indoor and outdoor pools and several restaurants. Many rooms look over the water or the surrounding hills, and the setting is especially pretty when the lakeside cherry trees bloom in spring. It’s an easy, reliable luxury base for travelers who like resort comforts after a day among the tombs and temples.

Boutique

Gyeongju’s character stays run from a Silla-palace-styled resort on the lake to a small European-themed hotel by the temple, and, in the old town, a growing crop of hanok guesthouses. If you want somewhere with a distinct personality rather than a chain lobby, start here.

The theming gives it real character and a sense of place that the glass-and-steel resorts nearby lack. A fun, atmospheric pick for travelers who want their hotel to feel like part of Gyeongju’s story.

Budget & Value

Gyeongju is kind to careful budgets. A tidy chain room or a large mid-range hotel here costs a fraction of Seoul, and the old-town hanok guesthouses trade marble lobbies for genuine character within walking distance of the tombs. Spend the savings on temple entries, bike rentals and cafe-hopping on Hwangnidan-gil.

A sensible, fuss-free value base for travelers who want to walk to the old-town sights and keep costs down.

Family-friendly

Families do well in Gyeongju: the resort hotels have pools and space to spread out, and two of the region’s biggest draws for kids — the Gyeongju World amusement park and a large water park — sit right in the Bomun area. These picks pair room to roam with easy access to the fun.

A big condo-style resort by Bomun Lake, close to Gyeongju World and the California Beach water park, with roomy family units and pools that keep children happy. Gyeongju hotels — quick questions

What is the best area to stay in Gyeongju?

It depends on your trip. The Bomun Lake Resort district, a short drive east of the center, is where the big resort hotels cluster, with lake views, spas and pools. The old town around Hwangnam-dong and Hwangnidan-gil puts you on foot among the royal tombs, Cheomseongdae observatory and the best cafes, and it is where the hanok guesthouses are. Staying near Bulguksa Temple on the slopes of Tohamsan is quieter and handy for the temple and Seokguram Grotto.

Is Gyeongju walkable?

The historic core is very walkable. You can stroll between the Daereungwon tombs, Cheomseongdae, Wolji Pond and the Hwangnidan-gil cafe street in a single afternoon. But Gyeongju's headline sights are spread out: Bulguksa, Seokguram and the Bomun Lake resorts are all several kilometers from the center, so you will want city buses, taxis or a rental car to link them.

When is the best time to visit Gyeongju?

Spring and autumn are loveliest. Early April brings cherry blossoms around Bomun Lake and the royal tombs, and October to November brings golden foliage and mild, clear days. Summers are hot and humid with a rainy spell, while winters are cold but quiet and uncrowded.
