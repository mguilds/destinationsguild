Where to Stay in Athens

Where to Stay in Athens — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Athens rewards a central bed more than almost any capital in Europe. The whole classical city — the Acropolis, the Agora, the Plaka lanes, the Acropolis Museum — sits inside a walkable half-mile, and if you sleep inside that circle you will spend your days strolling rather than sitting in traffic. The choice is really between four neighbourhoods: Syntagma for level ground and the airport metro; Plaka and Makriyianni for the shortest walk to the ruins; Koukaki for the same at a gentler price; and Kolonaki if you would rather have the good shops and do not mind a slope. It is a walking city — with two cautions

Syntagma, Plaka, Monastiraki, Makriyianni and Koukaki sit within fifteen to twenty-five minutes’ walk of one another. But Plaka’s marble paving is genuinely slippery once it is worn or wet, and the approach to the Acropolis rock itself is a steep, uneven climb. There is a lift on the north side of the rock for visitors with limited mobility; arrange to use it before you go.

Take the metro from the airport

Line 3 runs straight from the airport to Syntagma and Monastiraki in about forty minutes with no change of train. The X95 express bus runs around the clock and is the better bet late at night. Airport taxis charge a fixed tariff into the centre with a higher night rate — use the official rank and confirm the posted price rather than accepting an offer inside the terminal.

Spring and autumn, not August

April to early June and mid-September to late October are the comfortable months — warm days, cool evenings, manageable crowds. July and August often pass 95°F, and during a heatwave the Acropolis closes in the middle of the day for safety, which matters if you have a heart or breathing condition. November to March is mild, cheap and quiet, with some rain and shorter hours at the ancient sites.

Where the noise is

Monastiraki and Psyri are central and full of life, and loud well past midnight — lovely for some, hard on a light sleeper. Plaka, Makriyianni and Koukaki settle down early. Kolonaki is quiet and handsome but built on a hill. If you want level ground, a taxi rank and the metro at the door, Syntagma is the answer.

Luxury

Athens’ grand hotels sit on Syntagma Square, a level walk from Plaka and directly above the metro.

Hotel Grande Bretagne, a Luxury Collection Hotel

Opened in 1874 on Syntagma Square, this is the address every Athenian recognises — marble halls, an unhurried old-school staff and a rooftop restaurant looking straight across to the Parthenon. Rooms are traditionally furnished rather than fashionable, and the Parliament building and the metro are directly outside the door. It suits travellers who want a sense of occasion and would rather pay for reliability than for novelty. One current note: the indoor spa pool is under renovation, though the rooftop pool is open, and dogs are welcome for a fee.

Athens Hotels — common questions

Which neighbourhood should I stay in in Athens?

Syntagma is the safest first choice: level ground, taxis and the airport metro at the door, and a ten-minute walk to Plaka. Plaka and Makriyianni put you closest to the Acropolis and the Acropolis Museum and are quiet in the evening. Koukaki is much the same but more residential and better value. Kolonaki has the best shops and restaurants but is built on a hill, and Monastiraki and Psyri are central and lively but noisy well past midnight.

Is Athens a walkable city for older travellers?

Largely yes. The historic core is compact, and most visitors walk between the major sites in fifteen to twenty-five minutes on flat ground. Two things to watch: the marble paving in Plaka becomes slippery when worn smooth or wet, so wear shoes with real grip, and the climb up to the Acropolis itself is steep and uneven. There is a lift on the north side of the rock for visitors with limited mobility, and it is worth arranging its use in advance.

How do I get from Athens airport to the city?

Metro Line 3 runs directly from the airport to Syntagma and Monastiraki in roughly forty minutes with no change of train. There is an airport supplement on the fare, so check the current price at the machine. The X95 express bus to Syntagma runs around the clock and is the best option late at night. Taxis charge a fixed tariff into the centre with a higher rate at night; use the official rank and confirm the posted price rather than accepting an offer inside the terminal.

When is the best time to visit Athens?

April to early June and mid-September to late October are the comfortable months, with warm days, cool evenings and manageable crowds. July and August are hot, frequently above 95 degrees Fahrenheit, and during a heatwave the Acropolis site closes in the middle of the day for safety. November through March is mild, inexpensive and uncrowded, with some rain and shorter opening hours at the archaeological sites.

How many days do you need in Athens?

Three full days is the comfortable answer: one for the Acropolis and the Acropolis Museum, one for the Ancient Agora, the National Archaeological Museum and Plaka, and one for a day trip — Cape Sounion, Delphi or a one-day island cruise. Two days will cover the headline sights if you are pressed. Most travellers then move on to an island, and Athens makes a natural bookend at either end of the trip.

Is Athens safe for travellers?

Athens is generally safe and very easy to visit, with widespread English and good public transport. The main thing to guard against is petty theft — pickpockets work the crowded metro, Monastiraki and the busiest tourist sights, so keep valuables secure and your bag in front of you. Use licensed taxis or an official app, and check your government’s current travel advisory before you go.
