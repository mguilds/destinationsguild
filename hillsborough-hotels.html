Carriacou & Hillsborough

Where to Stay in Carriacou

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Carriacou is the second of Grenada’s three islands: thirteen square miles of dry hills, small beaches and boatbuilding, about twenty minutes by air from Grenada or a longer and occasionally rough ferry crossing. Hillsborough is its one town. There are no large resorts here and there is not going to be one — you stay in guest houses and cottages, eat where the island eats, and the day’s entertainment is a beach, a boat or a walk. One thing has to be said before anything else: Hurricane Beryl struck Carriacou and Petite Martinique on 1 July 2024, and the Grenada Tourism Authority still carries a notice saying the islands were affected and have started welcoming visitors once again. Ask any property directly what is open before you book.

The areas, and which one suits you

Where the beds actually are, and the honest word on steps, hills, road noise and how far it really is to the sand.

Hillsborough

The town, the ferry jetty and the main street, with the shops, the bank and the island’s small guest houses along or just off it. Walkable end to end in twenty minutes, and the obvious base for a first visit because everything practical is here.

Paradise Beach and L’Esterre

A long pale strip of sand on the west coast a few minutes south of town, looking across to Sandy Island. The island’s best-known beach and the easiest swimming, with a handful of places to eat along it.

Tyrrel Bay

The sheltered anchorage on the south-west side, full of yachts and the services that follow them, with the boatyard, the chandlers and a run of bars and restaurants on the waterfront. More harbour than beach, and the liveliest corner of the island in the evening.

Bogles and the north

The quiet end: a scattering of houses, the road up to the high ground, and a couple of small places to stay. Beautiful, and far enough from the town that you will want a vehicle or a willingness to walk in the heat.

Windward

The Atlantic side, and the home of Carriacou’s boatbuilding tradition — sloops still built on the beach by eye. Worth the trip across the island even if you are sleeping in town.

Getting about

Minibuses run from Hillsborough to Tyrrel Bay and Windward and cost very little, but they thin out in the afternoon and stop early. Taxis are few and should be arranged rather than hailed. Roads are rough in places. Plan on the island being small enough to walk and hilly enough that you will not want to.

What your money is worth in Carriacou
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Grenada uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged at EC$2.70 to the US dollar, so a price of EC$27 is about ten US dollars. US dollars are accepted in most hotels, restaurants and taxis, and change often comes back in local money. Cards work in hotels, supermarkets and the larger restaurants; carry cash for minibuses, market stalls and small bars. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills — ask what is included when you book. Carriacou is cheaper than the main island for almost everything except getting there. Groceries come across by boat and the choice is narrower, so bring anything you cannot do without. Cash matters more here than at Grand Anse: carry small Eastern Caribbean notes, and do not assume a card will work in a small guest house or a beach bar.

Green Roof Inn

HillsboroughFive rooms and two cottagesBy the seaNo pool

Green Roof Inn gives its address as Hillsborough, Carriacou, Grenada, West Indies, and says on its own site that it is located by the sea on the island of Carriacou, a twenty-minute flight from Grenada. It states five guest rooms with sea views in the main building, plus two private cottages. The restaurant, Ocean Terrace, is upstairs with sea views and sunsets and the site says the menu is predominantly seafood. It describes a private jetty, a dinghy dock for water taxis and steps down to a small cove for swimming and snorkelling, and a lounge and yoga pavilion next to the water. There is no pool, which the site does not claim and nor do we.

Bogles Round House

BoglesSelf-contained cottages60ft from the waterRestaurant Thu–Mon

Bogles Round House describes itself as restaurant and cottages at Bogles, Carriacou — note that this is the quiet north of the island, not Hillsborough. Its own site names two cottage pages, Mango and Plum Cottages and Lime Cottage, but does not publish a total count, and it calls them fully self-contained cottages just sixty feet from the water’s edge. It offers a complimentary breakfast when you book direct. Its restaurant hours are stated plainly and are worth reading before you plan your evenings: open Thursday to Monday from 4pm, with takeaway available five days a week. On an island this size, a restaurant’s closed days decide your week.

Travelling in your later years

Carriacou is rewarding and it is not the easy option, and we would rather say so. In its favour: it is small, it is quiet, nothing is far, the guest houses are run by people who will look after you personally, and Green Roof Inn states on its own site that it is by the sea with steps down to a small cove. Against it: the roads are rough, the hills are steep, minibuses stop early and taxis have to be arranged, several properties are up or down a slope from the water, and medical facilities on the island are limited — which is worth real thought if you travel with a condition that needs watching. Getting here is a twenty-minute flight in a small aircraft or a ferry crossing that can be lively; if the sea does not agree with you, fly. And because Hurricane Beryl struck the island in July 2024, ring the property and ask what is open, what is rebuilt and what the walk from your room to the water actually involves.

Questions about staying in Carriacou

Is Carriacou open after Hurricane Beryl?

Yes, with the honest caveat that it is a small island still putting itself back together. Hurricane Beryl struck Carriacou and Petite Martinique on 1 July 2024, and the Grenada Tourism Authority continues to carry a notice on its own site saying the islands were affected and have started welcoming visitors once again. What that means in practice is that some properties, restaurants and services are open and some are not, and the position changes. Ring or email the place you want to stay and ask directly. On an island of this size the owner will tell you straight.

How do I get to Carriacou?

Two ways. There is a small airport on the island and Green Roof Inn's own site describes the hop from Grenada as a twenty-minute flight. Or there is the ferry from St George's, which is a proper sea passage rather than a harbour hop and can be rough enough to matter if you are prone to seasickness. If the sea does not agree with you, fly, and if you are travelling with a lot of luggage, check the aircraft's allowance before you pack.

What is there to do on Carriacou?

Less than on the main island, which is the point. Beaches - Paradise Beach and the sand bar at Sandy Island are the famous ones - swimming, snorkelling, diving, a boat trip, and walking in the hills. Tyrrel Bay is where the yachts and the evening life are. Windward, on the Atlantic side, is where sloops are still built on the beach by eye, and is worth crossing the island for. If you need a nightclub or a water park, this is emphatically the wrong island.

Should I stay in Hillsborough or elsewhere?

Hillsborough for a first visit: the ferry, the shops, the bank and most of the guest houses are there, and the town is walkable end to end. Paradise Beach and L'Esterre, a few minutes south, for the best swimming. Tyrrel Bay if you want the boats and the restaurants. Bogles and the north for real quiet, accepting that you will need a vehicle or strong legs. Bogles Round House, for example, states its own address as Bogles rather than Hillsborough, which is a different kind of week.

Do I need cash on Carriacou?

More than you would on the main island. Carry Eastern Caribbean dollars in small notes and do not assume a card will be taken in a small guest house, a minibus or a beach bar. There is a bank in Hillsborough, but do not plan a week around one cash machine on a small island. Prices are generally lower than at Grand Anse for everything except the crossing itself.

Is a week here too long?

It depends entirely on what you want from a week. If your idea of a holiday is a beach, a book, a boat and dinner with the same friendly faces, a week on Carriacou is close to perfect and plenty of people make it two. If you need variety, a choice of restaurants every night and something new to see each day, split the trip: three or four nights here and the rest at Grand Anse or on the south coast, which is what we would suggest for a first visit.
