The night of April 14, 1865

Five days after the surrender at Appomattox, the President went to the theatre.

On the evening of Good Friday, April 14, 1865, President Abraham Lincoln and Mary Todd Lincoln watched the comedy Our American Cousin from the presidential box at Ford's Theatre. The Civil War was all but over, and the city was in a mood to celebrate.

At about 10:15 that night, the actor John Wilkes Booth stepped into the box and shot the President in the back of the head. Booth leapt to the stage, breaking his leg, and escaped into the night. The wounded President was carried across 10th Street to the Petersen House, a boarding house, where doctors kept watch through the night. Abraham Lincoln died there at 7:22 the next morning, April 15, 1865.

Today the theatre, a museum beneath it, the Petersen House and the exhibits beside it make up the Ford's Theatre National Historic Site, run by the National Park Service with the Ford's Theatre Society. The theatre is also still a working stage, with plays and musicals through the year.

What you will see

Start here

Ford's Theatre Museum

Beneath the theatre, the story of Lincoln's presidency and the Civil War, and of Booth's conspiracy, with artifacts from that night.

The scene

The theatre and the presidential box

The restored theatre looks much as it did in 1865. The flag-draped box where Lincoln sat is just above the stage on the right.

Across the street

The Petersen House

The small back bedroom where Lincoln died, and the rooms where his family and cabinet waited through the night.

After the shot

The Aftermath Exhibits

The twelve-day hunt for Booth, the trial of the conspirators, the funeral train home to Illinois, and the nation's mourning.

A sight to remember

The tower of books

A towering column of books written about Abraham Lincoln, rising through the stairwell of the education center beside the Petersen House.

Live performance

Plays and musicals

Ford's is a working theatre. Seeing a performance in the same hall is an experience in itself. See fords.org for the season.

Getting there

By Metro

Metro Center (Red, Blue, Orange and Silver lines) and Gallery Place–Chinatown (Red, Green and Yellow lines) are both a short walk away.

On foot

Ford's Theatre is on 10th Street NW between E and F Streets, a few blocks from Chinatown and the National Portrait Gallery, and about a 15-minute walk from the White House.

Accessibility

Parts of the site have elevator access, but the Petersen House is a historic building with steps up to the entrance. If you use a wheelchair or have limited mobility, contact Ford's Theatre at (202) 347-4833 before your visit to ask about access to each part of the site.

Ford's Theatre FAQ

Where was Lincoln shot?

President Abraham Lincoln was shot in the presidential box at Ford's Theatre, 511 10th Street NW in Washington, D.C., on the night of April 14, 1865.

Where did Lincoln die?

Lincoln was carried across the street to the Petersen House, a boarding house, where he died at 7:22 a.m. on April 15, 1865. The house is open to visitors as part of the Ford's Theatre National Historic Site.

Do you need tickets for Ford's Theatre?

Yes. Every visitor needs a ticket. Advance tickets are sold online at fords.org with a $5 reservation fee per ticket. A limited number of free walk-up tickets are available at the box office, first come, first served.

What are Ford's Theatre's hours?

The historic site is open daily, with timed entries every half hour from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. It is closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas Day, and the theatre itself is sometimes closed for performances or rehearsals.

Can you see the box where Lincoln was shot?

Yes. The restored presidential box can be seen from the theatre during a visit, when the theatre is open to visitors.

What is the closest Metro station to Ford's Theatre?

Metro Center and Gallery Place–Chinatown are both a short walk from Ford's Theatre.
