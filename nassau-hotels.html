Where to Stay in Nassau — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Written in August 2026. Every hotel named here was checked against the operator’s own information this month, and the closed ones are named so you do not book a ghost.

Nassau is the easiest island in the Caribbean to reach from the United States and, for a first-time visitor, one of the easiest to get wrong. It has a proper capital city with a working harbour, a mile of duty-free shopfronts, and a resort strip at Cable Beach that includes the largest hotel complex in the country — and the difference between those two addresses will shape your whole week.

There are three places to consider. Cable Beach, ten to fifteen minutes west of town, is the resort coast: Baha Mar’s three hotels, Sandals and Breezes, a long crescent of sand, and a taxi for everything beyond the gate. Downtown, at the head of Bay Street, is where you stay if you want to walk — flat ground, no hills, the Straw Market and the restaurants a few minutes away, and the cruise port next door for better and for worse. West of Cable Beach, out along West Bay Street, is quieter, more residential and entirely dependent on a car.

Two facts matter more here than anywhere else in the region. First, Baha Mar is not all-inclusive, whatever the photographs suggest — Nassau has only two genuine all-inclusives, and both are listed below. Second, almost every Nassau hotel charges a compulsory resort fee on top of the rate and on top of ten per cent VAT, and those fees range from about twenty dollars a head to over ninety dollars a night. Compare hotels on the total, not the headline.

Hurricane season, honestly. The Atlantic season runs from 1 June to 30 November, and the real peak for the Bahamas is mid-August through October. Nobody should soften that for you: Dorian came ashore on Abaco and Grand Bahama on 1 September 2019 as a Category 5. If you travel between mid-August and the end of October, book a flexible rate and buy travel insurance before a storm is named, not after.

Luxury

Nassau’s luxury is concentrated in one place — the Baha Mar complex on Cable Beach. Both hotels below are European plan: the room rate buys the room, and every meal is extra.

Rosewood Baha Mar

The smallest and by some distance the quietest of the three hotels at Baha Mar, and the one we would send a couple over fifty-five to. It has its own private stretch of Cable Beach, villas with plunge pools, butler service in the upper categories, and Sense spa. And this is not all-inclusive: dinner at Café Boulud is a separate bill each evening.

Boutique

Nassau is a big-resort town, so genuinely small hotels are scarce. These two are the real thing — and neither of them is on a beach, which is the trade you are making.

A Georgian mansion from the 1740s beside Government House, with sixteen rooms and cottage suites, one of the largest wine cellars in the Caribbean, a chocolatier and a cigar rollery on the premises. What You Need to Know Before You Book

Do not book these — they no longer exist

Booking sites are slow to retire dead listings, and Nassau has three ghosts still on the shelves. Meliá Nassau Beach on Cable Beach closed in 2020 and has since been demolished; the site is being redeveloped. Compass Point, with its famous painted huts, has been pulled down. Crystal Palace was imploded in 2018 and the Baha Bay waterpark stands on it. If a price looks too good on one of those names, that is why. And British Colonial is open and excellent but is no longer a Hilton.

The resort fee is the story in Nassau

Almost every hotel here adds a compulsory nightly fee on top of the rate, on top of ten per cent VAT. As a rough guide: SLS Baha Mar around $93 a night, Grand Hyatt around $75 plus a service charge of about $22, Margaritaville around B$75, The Island House B$66, British Colonial B$49.50, and Holiday Inn Express $19.90 per person. Rosewood charges 15.4 per cent of the room rate, which grows with the season. The only two hotels in Nassau where the quoted price is the price are Sandals Royal Bahamian and Breezes, because they are all-inclusive. Work the fee into your comparison before you decide anything.

Baha Mar is not all-inclusive

This catches more American visitors than anything else on the island. The Baha Mar resort fee buys you the waterpark, Wi-Fi, a couple of bottles of water and the gym. Every meal and every drink is charged separately across forty-odd outlets, at resort prices. A week for two there can cost half as much again as the room rate suggests.

Cruise days, and how to dodge them

The rebuilt Nassau Cruise Port is designed for six full-size ships at once, and on 8 June 2026 it set a record of 33,254 passengers in a single day. On a day like that, Bay Street, the Straw Market, Junkanoo Beach and the Queen’s Staircase are shoulder to shoulder from about ten in the morning until four in the afternoon, and taxis queue. The fix is easy once you know: check the day’s ship schedule at the port’s own website, do the town early or late, and spend the middle of a heavy day out at Cable Beach, fifteen minutes west of the crush.

Walking Nassau, plainly

Bay Street itself is flat, paved and pleasant, and the Straw Market is about three minutes from the pier. Beyond the main tourist run the pavements narrow and break up, and curb cuts are inconsistent. The hills are real. Government House, Graycliff and the Queen’s Staircase all sit on the rise behind town — the staircase itself is sixty-six steps cut into coral rock, reached by a ten-minute uphill walk. Our advice for anyone with knees, hips or a heart to think about: take a taxi up and walk down, carry water, and do your walking before eleven.

Take taxis, not a rental car

The Bahamas drives on the left, but almost every rental car is a left-hand-drive American model, so you sit on the wrong side of the car for the side of the road you are on. Add roundabouts and cruise-day parking and the case makes itself. Taxi fares are set by zone and the cars are not metered: expect roughly $30–$36 from the airport to Cable Beach, $32–$40 to downtown, $18–$24 from the cruise port out to Cable Beach, about $3 for each extra passenger and around a quarter more between midnight and six. Agree the fare before you get in. If a driver will not quote, use the next car in the rank. Tip ten to fifteen per cent. Jitney buses cost about $1.25 and are cheerful, but they are not accessible.

Money

The Bahamian dollar is pegged one-for-one to the US dollar and the two circulate side by side — you will pay with a twenty and get change in a mix of both, and that is normal. There is no need to change money at all. VAT is ten per cent and is generally shown on the bill. Cards are widely taken; carry some small US notes for taxis and tips.

Safety, in the State Department’s own words

The Bahamas is a Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution destination as of the advisory issued 28 July 2026, which is the same level as France or Italy, so do not let the phrase alarm you. The specifics are worth knowing all the same. Violent crime concentrates in a few parts of Nassau, and the ‘Over the Hill’ area south of Shirley Street is named directly — that is immediately inland from the tourist strip, so know where Shirley Street is. US government staff are prohibited from renting jet skis on New Providence and Paradise Island after repeated assaults by operators; treat that as a firm no. Do not swim alone or after dark on the downtown beaches. And check your bags meticulously: a single stray cartridge has put American visitors in a Bahamian jail.

Entry paperwork and bringing a dog

Americans, Canadians and Britons need no visa, and a valid passport is the practical requirement for arriving by air. A Digital Arrival Card went into a soft launch on 5 May 2026 on selected flights and runs alongside the old paper form; if you are offered it, use it, and if you are not, fill in the card on the aeroplane as before. Nassau has US preclearance, so you clear American immigration before you fly home and walk off as a domestic arrival — allow the extra time at the airport. If you are bringing a dog, the hotel is the easy part: you need an import permit from the Department of Agriculture (about $11 including VAT), the animal must be over six months old, and you need a veterinary health certificate issued within ten days of travel showing current rabies cover and freedom from parasites. Start that six to eight weeks ahead, not the week before.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Baha Mar all-inclusive?

No, and this is the single most expensive misunderstanding in Nassau. Baha Mar's own FAQ says plainly that it is not an all-inclusive resort. The nightly resort fee — roughly $75 at the Grand Hyatt, more at SLS, and a percentage of the rate at Rosewood — covers the Baha Bay waterpark, Wi-Fi, the gym and a couple of bottles of water. Every meal and every drink across the complex is charged separately at resort prices. Nassau has only two genuine all-inclusives: Sandals Royal Bahamian and Breezes Bahamas.

Which part of Nassau should I stay in?

Three choices, and they are not interchangeable. Cable Beach, ten to fifteen minutes west of town, is the resort strip: Baha Mar, Sandals and Breezes, long sand, and a taxi for anything else. Downtown, at the head of Bay Street, is the walkable choice — British Colonial, Margaritaville, the Courtyard and the Holiday Inn Express are all on flat ground within a few minutes of the shops and the port, at the cost of cruise-day crowds. West of Cable Beach, out towards Love Beach, is quieter and more residential, and you will need a car or taxi for everything. If walking to dinner matters, choose downtown.

What will the resort fee actually cost me?

More than you expect, and it is charged per night on top of the room and on top of ten per cent VAT. SLS Baha Mar is around $93 a night, the Grand Hyatt around $75 plus a service charge of roughly $22, Margaritaville about B$75, The Island House B$66, British Colonial B$49.50, and the Holiday Inn Express $19.90 per person — so about $40 a night for a couple. Rosewood charges 15.4 per cent of the room rate, so it rises with the season. Sandals and Breezes charge no resort fee at all because they are all-inclusive.

Should I hire a car in Nassau?

We would not. The Bahamas drives on the left and most rental cars are left-hand-drive American models, which puts you on the wrong side of the car for the road. Nassau has numerous roundabouts and very little parking on cruise days. Taxis are set by zone and cover everything you need: roughly $30–$36 from the airport to Cable Beach, $32–$40 downtown, and $18–$24 from the cruise port to Cable Beach, plus about $3 per extra passenger. They are not metered, so agree the fare before you get in — every dispute we have read about traces back to skipping that step.

Can I bring my dog to Nassau?

Yes, and Nassau is unusually good at it — the Courtyard by Marriott takes dogs up to about 130 pounds, and the Grand Hyatt, Rosewood, SLS, Margaritaville, The Island House and several small hotels all accept pets on their own terms. Sandals Royal Bahamian does not, and its published policy does not admit service animals either. The real work is the paperwork: an import permit from the Bahamian Department of Agriculture (about $11 with VAT) must accompany the animal, the dog must be over six months old, and a veterinary health certificate issued within ten days of travel must show current rabies immunity and freedom from parasites. Begin six to eight weeks ahead.

How busy does Nassau get on a cruise day?

Very. The rebuilt cruise port takes six mega-ships at once and set a record of 33,254 passengers on 8 June 2026. Between roughly ten in the morning and four in the afternoon on a heavy day, Bay Street, the Straw Market, Junkanoo Beach and the Queen's Staircase are packed and the taxi ranks lengthen. It is entirely manageable if you plan around it: look up the day's ship schedule, see the town early or late, and spend the middle of the day out at Cable Beach.

Is Nassau safe?

The US State Department advisory of 28 July 2026 puts the Bahamas at Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution — the same level as much of western Europe. In practice, ordinary care covers it: stay in the tourist districts, do not walk the downtown beaches alone or after dark, and know that the 'Over the Hill' area south of Shirley Street is specifically named for gang activity. Two specific warnings deserve repeating. US government employees are barred from renting jet skis on New Providence and Paradise Island after repeated assaults by operators. And firearms and ammunition are absolutely prohibited, including a single stray round in a carry-on — check your bags before you fly.
