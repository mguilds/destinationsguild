Lake Fishing in Oklahoma — Five Big Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Lake Eufaula, Grand Lake o’ the Cherokees, Broken Bow, Lake Texoma and Keystone: what swims in each, the 2026–27 rules from the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, the licence a visitor needs, where to put a boat in, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, 13 November 2026

Oklahoma is a state of big man-made lakes, most of them built by the Army Corps of Engineers on the rivers that cross the state from west to east. They are working fishing lakes rather than postcard ones: striped bass running in packs on Lake Texoma and Keystone, crappie by the cooler-full on Grand Lake and Eufaula, bass and catfish nearly everywhere — and, below the clear deep water of Broken Bow Lake in the pine hills of the southeast corner, a cold river stocked with trout all year.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, bass and crappie anglers towing a boat, striper fishermen who want a guide, and fly anglers headed for the Lower Mountain Fork. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 13 November 2026 from the agency that sets it — mainly the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation (ODWC) — and is dated. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The best months, in plain words

Southern Plains lakes: long hot summers, short winters, and spring and autumn are the times to come — except below Broken Bow, where the trout water is cold all year.

Lake Eufaula — Oklahoma’s biggest lake

The Canadian River, dammed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in the east of the state. Oklahoma State Parks calls it a “102,000 acre reservoir” and the largest man-made lake in Oklahoma.

Lake Eufaula, from Eufaula to Checotah

What swims there. The Corps of Engineers names striped, largemouth and white bass, crappie, catfish, walleye and sunfish. Crappie and catfish are what most people come for.

How people fish it. From a boat on the brush piles, standing timber and bridge pilings for crappie; along the rocky banks and creek arms for bass; drifting or on jug lines for catfish. Lake Eufaula State Park has an enclosed, heated fishing dock — a good place for a family in cold weather.

Keeping a fish. Crappie: 20 a day on Lake Eufaula, no length limit listed. Everything else follows the statewide limits below. Snagging is closed for one mile below Eufaula Dam.

Confirmed with the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, 13 November 2026 — ODWC news release, June 2026: the Lake Eufaula crappie limit of 20 a day is adopted and “now in effect”. 2026–27 Regulations, Public Fishing Waters: snagging closed one mile below Eufaula Dam. Pickens Lake and the Kid’s Fish Out Lake inside the state park are for youth, seniors and disabled anglers only.

Boat ramps and hire. The Corps says every Corps park on the lake has a boat ramp. Lake Eufaula State Park, near Checotah, has several ramps and a marina with a tackle shop; the Arrowhead Area, at Canadian on the south side, has a ramp and a marina selling fuel, bait and tackle (Oklahoma State Parks). Evergreen, Fountainhead and Xtreme Cove marinas rent boats (details below).

Nearest towns: Eufaula, beside the dam end of the lake; Checotah, at the north end near the state park.

Grand Lake o’ the Cherokees — crappie country in the northeast

The Grand (Neosho) River, held back since 1940 by Pensacola Dam. The Grand River Dam Authority, which runs it, gives 46,500 surface acres; Oklahoma State Parks gives 1,300 miles of shoreline.

Grand Lake, from Grove and Bernice to Disney

What swims there. Crappie above all — Oklahoma State Parks calls the Bernice area the “Crappie Capital of the World” — plus largemouth and white bass, channel catfish, sunfish and paddlefish (spoonbill) in the river end.

How people fish it. Crappie on boat docks and brush piles, spring and autumn; bass on the rocky points and in the coves; paddlefish snagging in the upper lake and river in early spring, under the paddlefish rules below.

Keeping a fish. Crappie: 15 a day, each at least 10 inches, on the lake and all its tributaries up to the state line. Below Pensacola Dam, bowfishing, gigging, snagging and noodling are banned on the Grand River.

Confirmed with the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, 13 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations, Public Fishing Waters, Grand Lake o’ the Cherokees: crappie 15 combined daily, 10-inch minimum, “all tributaries” upstream to the state line. Grand River Dam Authority: 46,500 surface acres, Pensacola Dam completed 1940.

Boat ramps and hire. Honey Creek Area of Grand Lake State Park, at Grove, has a lit boat ramp, a courtesy dock, a fishing dock and a fish-cleaning station; the Twin Bridges Area near Miami has lighted ramps and sells bait and tackle (Oklahoma State Parks). Honey Creek Landing, Cedar Port, Shangri-La and H2O Sports Rental are below. Grand Lake is patrolled by the GRDA Police, not the Highway Patrol.

Nearest towns: Grove, the main town on the south end; Bernice, on the crappie water; Afton, for Monkey Island; Disney, beside Pensacola Dam.

Broken Bow Lake — clear water, pine hills and a trout river below

The Mountain Fork River in McCurtain County, the far southeast corner of the state, dammed by the Corps of Engineers. Beavers Bend State Park gives 14,220 surface acres and 180 miles of shoreline.

Broken Bow Lake

What swims there. Largemouth, smallmouth and white bass, crappie, and channel and flathead catfish (Oklahoma State Parks).

How people fish it. From a boat along the steep, rocky, wooded shoreline; the water is clear, so light line and an early start help. The lake has no special regulations of its own: the statewide limits below apply.

Boat ramps and hire. Beavers Bend Marina rents boats and sells fuel (details below). The state park lists a marina; we could not confirm the park’s ramps today, so check with the park.

Nearest town: Broken Bow, about ten miles south of the lake. The cabin country around Hochatown and Beavers Bend lies between the town and the dam — use the vacation homes button for it.

The Lower Mountain Fork River — trout all year

What it is. Twelve miles of river from the Broken Bow Lake spillway down to the US-70 bridge, about five of them inside Beavers Bend State Park, stocked with trout year-round. Cold water released from the bottom of the lake keeps it alive in summer.

The rules are its own. Barbless hooks only; bait is allowed; one rod and reel per angler. From the spillway to the Old State Park Dam: three trout a day in total, of which only one may be a rainbow over 25 inches, and only one a brown, which must be over 30 inches. From the Old State Park Dam to US-70: one rainbow, which must be over 25 inches, and one brown, which must be over 30 inches. No separate trout licence is needed. Didymo, an invasive alga, is in the river: clean and dry your waders and gear.

Confirmed with the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, 13 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations, Lower Mountain Fork River trout area: year-round stocking, barbless hooks, one rod and reel; Area 1 “3 trout in aggregate” with the rainbow-over-25 and brown-over-30 rules above; Area 2 rules as above. Trout section: a regular fishing licence covers trout.

Who to fish with. Beavers Bend Fly Shop, on Beavers Bend Road, guides on this river (details below).

Lake Texoma — the striper lake on the Texas line

The Red and Washita rivers, dammed at Denison by the Corps of Engineers. The Corps gives 89,000 surface acres; Texas Parks and Wildlife says about two-thirds of the lake is in Oklahoma.

Lake Texoma, from Kingston and Madill to Durant

What swims there. Striped bass that breed naturally in the lake — the Corps calls Texoma the “Striper Capital of the World” — plus largemouth, smallmouth, white and hybrid bass, crappie, and blue, channel and flathead catfish. Texas Parks and Wildlife rates its smallmouth bass, blue catfish and striped bass “Excellent”.

How people fish it. Stripers from a boat, following birds and bait schools across open water, usually with a guide on a first trip; catfish on the flats and river channels; bass on the rocky bluffs and points. The lake is big and the wind can make it rough — small boats should watch the forecast.

Keeping a fish (Oklahoma rules for Texoma). Striped bass and hybrids: 10 a day together, only 2 of them 20 inches or longer. Black bass: 5 a day together, largemouth and smallmouth at least 14 inches. White bass: 25 a day. Crappie: 37 a day, at least 10 inches. Flathead catfish: 5 a day, at least 20 inches. Alligator gar: closed in May.

Confirmed with the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, 13 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations, Lake Texoma: the limits above, and the Lake Texoma licence, $12, which covers both states’ waters and is “not valid below the Texoma Dam”. And with Texas Parks and Wildlife, 13 November 2026: “All fish landed in Texas must comply with Texas bag and length limits”; on the Texas side walleye and saugeye are 5 a day, only 2 under 16 inches. Zebra mussels are in the lake: clean, drain and dry your boat.

Boat ramps and hire. Lake Texoma State Park, at Kingston, has boat ramps, and Catfish Bay Marina is inside the park (Oklahoma State Parks). Alberta Creek rents boats; Jimmy Bishop’s and Advantage Guide Service take you striper fishing (details below).

Nearest towns: Kingston, by the state park; Durant, the largest town on the Oklahoma side; Madill, toward the Washita arm.

Keystone Lake — stripers half an hour from Tulsa

Where the Arkansas and Cimarron rivers meet west of Tulsa, dammed by the Corps of Engineers. Oklahoma tourism gives 23,600 acres at normal level, 330 miles of shoreline, 23 boat ramps and three marinas.

Keystone Lake, from Sand Springs and Mannford to Cleveland

What swims there. The Corps calls Keystone “the new home of the striped bass”, and names white bass, black bass, crappie and catfish as well.

How people fish it. Stripers and white bass up the two river arms in spring and over open water in summer; crappie on brush; catfish everywhere. It is the easiest big lake to reach from Tulsa for a day.

Keeping a fish. Keystone has no special lake rules of its own, so statewide limits apply: striped bass 5 a day, no length limit; hybrids 20 a day, no more than 5 over 20 inches; white bass no limit. Below Keystone Dam, the Arkansas River limit is 6 channel and blue catfish together.

Confirmed with the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, 13 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations: statewide striped bass “5 per day (except Lake Texoma)”; hybrid striped bass 20, no more than 5 over 20 inches; Arkansas River below Keystone Dam, channel and blue catfish 6 combined. Bowfishing is allowed in the Keystone tailwater.

Boat ramps and hire. Keystone State Park, at Sand Springs, has boat ramps and Pier 51 Marina; Keystone Harbor is at Mannford (details below).

Nearest towns: Sand Springs and Mannford on the south shore; Cleveland on the Cimarron arm to the north; Tulsa, the city base, a short drive east.

The licence and the limits, plainly

One agency for the whole state, a cheap one-day licence for visitors, and children fish free.

Oklahoma fishing licences, 2026–27 prices

Who needs one. Everyone 18 and older. Anglers 17 and under, resident or not, need no fishing licence. There is no general free pass for Oklahoma seniors, but residents 65 and over can buy a lifetime senior fishing licence for $30. Texas residents aged 65 and over need no Oklahoma fishing licence.

For a visitor. Nonresident annual fishing licence $81; nonresident one-day licence $26. There is no six-day fishing licence for nonresidents. Lake Texoma licence $12.

For an Oklahoman. Annual fishing licence $31; one-day $11. Lifetime fishing $375; senior lifetime fishing $30.

Where to buy. Online at GoOutdoorsOklahoma.com ($3 handling fee), in the Go Outdoors Oklahoma app, or from a licence agent. Carry your licence — a phone copy is allowed — and photo ID. Check the dates on your licence when you buy it: ODWC’s web page and its printed guide describe the annual licence period differently.

Free Fishing Days. ODWC lists 5–6 June 2027: anyone may fish without a state licence, but a (free) paddlefish permit is still required, and some city lakes still need their own permit.

Confirmed with the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, 13 November 2026 — 2026–27 Regulations, licence pages: resident annual $31, resident 1-day $11, nonresident annual $81, nonresident 1-day $26, Lake Texoma $12, lifetime $375, senior lifetime fishing $30; residents and nonresidents 17 and under exempt; GoOutdoorsOklahoma.com handling fee $3. Licence-fees page: annual licence “valid 365 days”. Regulation changes page: Free Fishing Days June 5–6, 2027. Prices change. Buy from ODWC or a licensed agent.

Paddlefish, briefly

Grand Lake and Keystone are paddlefish water. Everyone who fishes for paddlefish needs the free ODWC paddlefish permit, even children and lifetime-licence holders. Barbless hooks only; when snagging, one rod with one single or treble hook. A fish you keep must be tagged at once with your customer number, you stop snagging for the day, and you report it on E-Check within 24 hours. Catch-and-release with rod and reel is allowed all year. East of I-35 and north of I-40, snagging is closed from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. all year (ODWC, 2026–27 Regulations).

Boats and life jackets

Every person aboard needs a Coast Guard-approved life jacket. Young people aged 12 to 15 who drive a boat over 10 horsepower, a sailboat of 16 feet or more, or a personal watercraft must have passed an approved boating course and carry the card, with an adult aboard (or, on a personal watercraft, watching from within 500 yards) (Oklahoma Department of Public Safety; Grand River Dam Authority).

Eating what you keep

The Oklahoma Department of Environmental Quality publishes mercury advice for each lake. In its 2026 guidance Grand Lake has no advisory at all. Eufaula, Keystone and Texoma have advice for pregnant women, nursing mothers and children up to 15 on some large fish, such as big catfish, big largemouth and, on Texoma, stripers of 30 inches and more. Broken Bow has advice for everyone on some species, including larger smallmouth bass. Look up your lake in DEQ’s “2026 Fishing Guidance” before you plan a fish fry.

Questions

Do I need an Oklahoma fishing licence, and what does it cost a visitor?

Everyone 18 and older needs one; anglers 17 and under, resident or not, are exempt. In the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation's 2026-27 regulations a nonresident annual fishing licence is $81 and a nonresident one-day licence is $26. Oklahoma residents pay $31 a year or $11 for one day. There is no nonresident six-day fishing licence. Buy online at GoOutdoorsOklahoma.com, in the Go Outdoors Oklahoma app or from a licence agent.

Which licence do I need for Lake Texoma?

Lake Texoma sits on the Oklahoma-Texas line. A Lake Texoma licence, $12 for residents and nonresidents alike, lets you fish both the Oklahoma and Texas waters of the lake, but it is not valid below the Texoma Dam. Otherwise you may fish each state's water only with that state's licence. The Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation says anglers 15 and under are exempt from the Texoma licence; Texas Parks and Wildlife adds that fish landed in Texas must meet Texas limits.

How many striped bass can I keep on Lake Texoma?

Ten striped bass and hybrids together a day, and only two of them may be 20 inches or longer. That is the Lake Texoma rule in the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation's 2026-27 regulations. On other Oklahoma lakes, such as Keystone, the statewide rule is five striped bass a day with no length limit, and hybrids are 20 a day with no more than five over 20 inches.

What are the crappie limits on Lake Eufaula and Grand Lake?

On Lake Eufaula the daily crappie limit is 20; the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation adopted that change in June 2026, and it lists no length limit there. On Grand Lake o' the Cherokees, and its tributaries up to the state line, the limit is 15 crappie a day, each at least 10 inches long. Elsewhere in Oklahoma the statewide crappie limit is 37 a day with no length limit.

Do I need a trout licence to fish below Broken Bow Lake?

No. A regular Oklahoma fishing licence covers trout on the Lower Mountain Fork River below Broken Bow Lake. The 12-mile trout area is stocked all year, and the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation requires barbless hooks and one rod and reel per angler there. The daily limit is three trout in total, with only one rainbow over 25 inches and only one brown, which must be over 30 inches; below the Old State Park Dam the rules are stricter still.

Six honest gaps

Lake levels and ramps. Eufaula, Broken Bow, Texoma and Keystone are run by the Army Corps of Engineers and Grand Lake by the Grand River Dam Authority; levels change with the rain, and low water closes ramps. We checked only the state park ramps. Check the Corps or GRDA before you tow a boat.

Water releases below Broken Bow Dam. The Lower Mountain Fork rises when water is released. We did not check a release schedule; ask the fly shop or look one up before you wade.

Fishing guides. Every lake has more guides than we list, but we name only those whose own website we could open today.

2026 Free Fishing Days. We found ODWC’s 2027 dates but no official page for 2026, so we give only 2027.

Paddlefish harvest days. The 2026–27 regulations we read list no special weekday schedule; if you plan to keep a paddlefish, read ODWC’s paddlefish page first.
