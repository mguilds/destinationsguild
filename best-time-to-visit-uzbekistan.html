Best Time to Visit Uzbekistan

Researched and compiled by the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

The walls of the Itchan Kala, Khiva. Photo: Wikimedia Commons.

The short answer is April, May, September and October, and of those October is the one most people come home recommending. Uzbekistan has a hard continental desert climate: the summers are genuinely punishing and the winters are genuinely cold, and since seeing these cities means walking around them for hours, the weather matters more here than it does in a country you would see from a car.

The figures below are ten-year averages for 2015 to 2024, worked out from daily NASA POWER records pulled for this page. Two honest notes on them. First, they are averages and not forecasts. Second, the rainfall column is the median of the ten years rather than the mean: the dataset carries one freak December at Khiva and one freak April at Bukhara, each many times the normal month, and a plain average of ten years would let those two readings misrepresent a desert as a wet place.

Season by season

Spring — March to May

The country turns green, the fruit trees blossom, and Navruz on 21 March fills the streets. April and May are two of the four best months: warm rather than hot, with afternoons in the seventies and eighties Fahrenheit and evenings you can sit out in. This is also when most of what little rain Uzbekistan gets still falls, and when the trains and the good small hotels fill up first. Book early.

Summer — June to August

Hot, dry and bright. Bukhara and Khiva both average around 104°F (40°C) in July; Tashkent is a degree or two behind. It is a dry heat and the hotels are air-conditioned, but the old towns are open brick and stone with little shade, and the middle of the day is simply not sightseeing time. The compensation is that this is the quietest season at the monuments and the cheapest for rooms.

Autumn — September to November

The best stretch of the year. September still carries summer’s warmth without its weight; October is close to perfect, with warm days, cool nights and the clearest air; November cools quickly but stays comfortable for walking. The bazaars are at their best in September and October, when the melons, grapes and pomegranates come in.

Winter — December to February

Cold, quiet and underrated. Days are short and nights drop well below freezing across the country, but the monuments are nearly empty, the low winter light on blue tile is the best photography of the year, and prices are at their lowest. Expect some of the small courtyard hotels in Bukhara and Khiva to close for the season, and pack properly: this is not a mild winter.

Uzbekistan weather questions, answered

What is the best month to visit Uzbekistan?

October, narrowly, then April and May. October gives you warm days, cool nights, the clearest air of the year and the bazaars full of melons and grapes; April and May give you green countryside and blossom but a slightly higher chance of rain. All three are comfortable for the long days of walking that sightseeing in Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva actually involves.

How hot does it really get in summer?

Hot enough to change how you travel. Averaged over the last ten years, July afternoons reach about 100°F (38°C) in Tashkent and about 104°F (40°C) in both Bukhara and Khiva. That is an average, not a peak. The cities are dry rather than humid, which helps, but the brick and stone of the old towns hold the heat into the evening. If you must travel in July or August, see things before ten in the morning and after six at night, use a car between sites rather than walking, and do not plan a long day at the Registan in the middle of the afternoon.

Is winter a bad time to go?

Not bad — different, and much quieter. January highs run around 42°F (5°C) in Tashkent and 37°F (3°C) at Khiva, with nights well below freezing, so it is properly cold. In exchange you get the monuments nearly to yourself, the best light of the year on the tilework, and the lowest prices. Pack for real winter, expect some small family-run hotels in Bukhara and Khiva to be closed, and accept that the days are short.

Does it rain much?

Not much, and almost none of it falls in summer. What rain there is comes between November and April, and it is heaviest in Tashkent and Samarkand, which sit higher and closer to the mountains. Bukhara and Khiva are out in the desert and drier still. From June to September you can more or less discount rain anywhere in the country.

When are the festivals?

Navruz, the spring new year, falls on 21 March and is the big one — a public holiday celebrated across the country. The dates of Ramadan and the two Eids move each year against the Western calendar, so we do not print them here; check for the year you are travelling, because they affect opening hours and how busy the trains are.

Is one season better for a particular city?

The pattern is the same everywhere, but the extremes differ. Khiva is the most severe at both ends: the hottest summer afternoons and the coldest winter nights of the four, because it sits furthest out in the desert. Tashkent and Samarkand are a little higher and a shade milder in summer. If you are going in high summer, that is an argument for starting at Khiva early in the trip and getting it done in the cooler part of the day.
