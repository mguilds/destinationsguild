Amman — Seven Hills, a Roman Theatre and the Country’s Front Door

Where to Stay in Amman — the Best Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Almost every visit to Jordan begins and ends in Amman, and most people give it a night at each end. That is about right — but which hill you sleep on changes the city completely. Amman is built over a series of steep ridges with the old centre in the valley between them, so the difference between a good night and an awkward one is usually a question of gradient and taxis rather than the hotel itself. Here is how the city divides up.

Jabal Amman and Rainbow Street — the first choice for most visitors

The ridge immediately above downtown, and the part of Amman that feels like a city worth walking in. Rainbow Street runs along it, lined with restored villas from the 1920s and 1930s, cafes, small galleries and a Friday market. Most of Amman’s small, characterful hotels are here or just off it. You can walk down to the Roman theatre and the souks in fifteen minutes — and you should plan to take a taxi back up, because it is a real climb. This is the area we would point a first-time visitor to.

Downtown — Al-Balad, in the valley

The oldest and busiest part of the city, where the Roman theatre, the Nymphaeum, the gold and spice souks and the cheapest beds all sit within a few streets of each other. Staying here puts you in the middle of ordinary Amman life, which is the appeal, and it is the best-value part of the city. The trade-offs are honest ones: it is noisy well into the evening, traffic is constant, rooms are plainer, and everywhere else in the city is uphill from you.

Jabal al-Weibdeh — the quiet one

The next ridge over from Jabal Amman, older and more residential, with galleries, bookshops, small cafes and a slower pace. There are fewer hotels here and more guesthouses and apartments. If you have been to Amman before, or you want somewhere to sit still for a few days rather than tick off sites, this is the address to look at. Still hilly, still a taxi ride from most things, but calmer than anywhere else central.

Abdali and Shmeisani — the modern city

North of the centre, this is where the international chains, the business hotels, the malls and the newer towers are. It is flat, it is easy, the rooms are reliable and the facilities are what you would expect anywhere in the world — and it has very little to do with Jordan. Worth choosing if you want a pool, a gym and a predictable bed, or if your trip is business rather than sightseeing. Not worth choosing for atmosphere.

Out by the airport — for one reason only

Queen Alia International sits about 35 kilometres south of the city, and there are hotels beside it. The only good reason to use one is a flight that leaves before dawn or lands close to midnight, where the drive would cost you the sleep. Otherwise it is a long way from everything and you will see nothing of Amman at all.

What to know before you book

Hills, and a lot of them

Amman is not a flat city and it is not a forgiving one on foot. The climb out of downtown is steep enough that locals take taxis for it. Ask any hotel how far it is from the nearest level street, not just how far it is from the sights.

Taxis and getting about

Metered yellow taxis are cheap and everywhere; ride-hailing apps work well and remove the fare conversation entirely. There is no metro. Traffic is heavy from mid-morning to late evening, and worst on Sunday to Thursday, which is the Jordanian working week.

The airport run

Queen Alia is about 35 km and usually 35 to 50 minutes, longer in traffic. There is an airport bus to the city as well as taxis. Agree the fare or use an app rather than settling it at the end.

Friday mornings

Friday is the first day of the Jordanian weekend and the city is at its quietest until around midday. Some shops and offices stay shut. It is a pleasant morning to see the Citadel and a poor morning to go shopping.

Noise

Amman is a loud city — traffic, call to prayer, and in the summer evenings, everyone outdoors. If you sleep lightly, ask for a room away from the street, particularly downtown.

A base for day trips

Jerash is about an hour north and is the single best day trip in the country. The Dead Sea is about an hour west. Madaba and Mount Nebo are about forty minutes south and sit conveniently on the way to the airport.

Amman — questions

Which area should I stay in for a first visit to Amman?

Jabal Amman, around Rainbow Street. It has most of the city's characterful small hotels, it is the one part of Amman that rewards walking, and you can get down to the Roman theatre and the souks on foot. Budget for taxis back up the hill rather than walking them.

Is downtown Amman a good place to stay?

It is the best value and the most atmospheric, and it is genuinely noisy. If you want to be in the middle of ordinary city life and do not mind a plainer room and traffic until late, it is a good choice. If you are a light sleeper or you want a quiet base at the end of a long travelling day, stay up on Jabal Amman instead.

How long should I give Amman?

One or two nights at the start and one at the end is the usual shape, and it works. Add a full day if you want to do Jerash properly, which is worth it. Amman is not a city most people come to Jordan for, but it is a pleasant place to begin and end.

Do I need a car in Amman?

No, and we would not recommend one. Traffic is heavy, parking is difficult and the hills make navigation confusing. Taxis and ride apps are cheap. Hire the car when you leave the city for Petra and the south, not before.

How far is Amman from Petra?

About 230 kilometres and roughly three hours on the Desert Highway, or most of a day if you take the King's Highway and stop at Madaba, Mount Nebo and Kerak on the way. The scenic road is the better use of the day if you have it to spare.
