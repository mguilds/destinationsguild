Where to Stay in Amsterdam — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Amsterdam gives you the most walkable historic centre in Europe and, at the same time, some of its steepest staircases — so where you stay is partly a question of taste and partly a practical one about lifts and legs. Below are the city’s most enduring hotels arranged by style, each with an honest note on what it is like and whether it has a lift, and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

The areas, and a word about stairs

Amsterdam’s historic core is small and almost perfectly flat — you can cross the whole canal ring on foot in about forty minutes, and walking is genuinely the best way to see it. The trams fill in the longer hops and are low and step-free at most stops. Two honest cautions matter more here than in most cities: the bicycles, which are fast, silent and have the right of way, so look before you step off any kerb; and the staircases. Many of the loveliest canal-house hotels have famously steep, narrow stairs and no lift at all. We say plainly under each hotel below whether a lift is confirmed — and where we could not confirm one, we tell you to ask before booking.

Canal Ring (Grachtengordel)

The postcard Amsterdam and the most walkable base — step out of the door straight into the view. It is also where the steep-stairs, no-lift problem is most acute, so choose your hotel’s lift with care.

Museum Quarter (Oud-Zuid)

Leafy, spacious and grand, with the Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh on the doorstep. The calmest area and generally the easiest underfoot — our first suggestion for older travellers who want space and quiet, at the cost of a short tram ride into the centre.

Jordaan

Just west of the canal ring: quieter, low-rise, full of small restaurants and brown cafes. Lovely for a second visit or anyone who wants neighbourhood life over headline sights.

Centrum, near Centraal

Most convenient for the station and the airport train and best for short stays, but the busiest and noisiest, and the streets immediately east of the station are the ones to avoid at night.

Luxury

Amsterdam’s grandes dames, and — importantly for anyone wary of canal-house stairs — the two easiest luxury addresses in the city both have proper lifts and step-free public rooms.

De L’Europe Amsterdam

$$$$ LuxuryCentrum, on the AmstelMichelin-starred FloreIndoor pool & spa

A grand nineteenth-century hotel on the bend of the Amstel, family-owned in feel and formal in service, with the Michelin-starred Flore among five separate eating and drinking rooms. It is the least fashion-driven of the city’s top hotels and the better for it — thick carpets, river light, staff who remember your name by the second morning. For older American travellers it is the single easiest luxury address in Amsterdam: the hotel confirms lifts, step-free entrances, adapted guest rooms and level access throughout, and the spa has a proper swimming pool.

Boutique

Amsterdam’s restored canal houses make it one of Europe’s great small-hotel cities — and one where you must check for a lift, because most of these buildings were never designed for one.

The Dylan Amsterdam

$$$$ BoutiqueKeizersgracht, Nine StreetsTwo Michelin stars at VinkelesLift confirmed

A discreet forty-one-room hotel behind a canal facade, where the two-Michelin-star Vinkeles occupies the vaulted ovens of an eighteenth-century almshouse bakery and a cobbled courtyard swallows the street noise the moment you step through the gate. It is intimate without being precious. Reassuringly for anyone wary of Amsterdam stairs, a lift serves its levels and both buildings — genuinely unusual for a canal-house property this size; note that pets are not accepted.

Budget & Value

In a city where a modest room is dear, these two are modern buildings — which means beds you reach by lift rather than by a canal-house staircase.

The German design-budget formula done properly: compact, spotless rooms, good beds, a handsome lounge-bar, and rates that start where very little else in Amsterdam does. Family-friendly

In a city of tiny historic rooms, the useful family hotels are the purpose-built ones — full lift access, connecting rooms, and no stairs to the bedrooms.

A big, modern waterfront tower next to the Muziekgebouw with proper fifty-two-square-metre family connecting rooms that sleep four through an interconnecting door — the single most useful thing a family can book in a city of small historic rooms. Views over the IJ are the draw, and Centraal Station is a short walk or one tram stop away. Adults-only

An honest word: Amsterdam has almost no true adults-only hotels, and most online “adults-only” lists for the city are aggregator noise. We found exactly one property that genuinely restricts itself to adults, and we would rather tell you that than pad the list. For a couples’ feel, a small canal-house boutique is the more reliable route — browse current options below.

Breitner House

$$$ Adults-onlyOosterpark, Amsterdam OostTwo suites onlyOwner-run townhouse

A two-suite luxury guesthouse in a handsome townhouse on Oosterpark, where the owners let out part of their own home as the Breitner and Israëls suites. Its own booking terms state plainly that it welcomes adults only, with no cribs and no rollaway beds, so the calm is structural rather than aspirational — it suits couples wanting a private, residential Amsterdam away from the centre. One caution for this readership: lift access is not stated, and a townhouse of this type characteristically has steep stairs, so ask directly before booking if that matters.

Pet-friendly

Two hotels with clear, published dog policies — and, as it happens, two of the best accessibility records in the city.

Pulitzer Amsterdam

$$$$ Pet-friendlyPrinsengracht, Nine StreetsDogs welcome in the gardenAccessible rooms

Twenty-five restored canal houses stitched into a single hotel, so the corridors wander and no two rooms match — the most characterful large hotel in the city, with one of its nicest hidden gardens. Its own FAQ confirms one small dog or cat per room, welcome in the Pulitzer Garden though not in the restaurants. For mobility, the hotel also confirms accessible guest rooms, shower aids and a house wheelchair on request — but in a warren of twenty-five historic houses you should specify your needs when you book, not on arrival.

Amsterdam — common questions

Which area of Amsterdam should an older traveller stay in?

The Museum Quarter (Oud-Zuid) is our first suggestion: leafy, spacious, generally the easiest underfoot, and with the Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh Museum on the doorstep, at the cost of a short tram ride into the canal ring. The Canal Ring itself is the most beautiful and walkable base but the place where steep, liftless staircases are most common, so choose the hotel’s lift carefully. For a short stay near the trains, Centrum near Centraal is convenient but the busiest and noisiest.

Do Amsterdam hotels have lifts?

Many canal-house hotels do not — the buildings are centuries old, protected, and have famously steep, narrow staircases. This is the single most important thing for an older visitor to check, and you should confirm a lift in writing before booking rather than assuming one. The modern hotels and the big international names almost all have lifts; the small historic boutiques often do not. Every hotel on this page notes whether a lift is confirmed.

Is the tourist tax really that high?

Yes. Amsterdam charges 12.5 per cent of the room rate, among the highest tourist taxes in Europe, and it is usually added at check-in rather than shown in the rate you booked. Combined with the standard 21 per cent VAT that replaced the old 9 per cent hotel rate in January 2026, the total tax on an Amsterdam room is now roughly a third. Budget for it, and consider sleeping in Haarlem, Utrecht or Delft, where rooms are cheaper and the tax is lower.

How do I get from Schiphol Airport to my hotel?

By train. Schiphol has a railway station directly beneath the terminal — you take a lift or escalator down to the platforms — and trains run into Amsterdam Centraal in roughly fifteen minutes, where you can pick up a tram or a short taxi to your hotel. You can tap a contactless card at the gates; there is no need to arrange a transfer in advance. Do allow generous time on arrival, though, because the new border checks have made passport control at Schiphol slow in 2026.

Is Amsterdam safe?

Yes, it is a safe city to walk in, including after dark in the main districts, and violent crime against visitors is rare. The two real risks are mundane: bicycles, which travel fast and silently in red-paved lanes that look like pavement and have the right of way, so look before you step off a kerb; and pickpockets on crowded trams toward the museums and in the crush around Dam Square and Centraal. Keep valuables in a zipped inside pocket, and mind the wet cobbles and steep stairs.
