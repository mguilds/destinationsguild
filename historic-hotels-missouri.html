Historic Hotels of Missouri

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

6 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 6 in Missouri, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1894

St. Louis Union Station Hotel, Curio Collection by Hilton

St. Louis, Missouri · Historic Hotels member since 2014

Theodore Link won the design contest for this station, which opened on September 1, 1894. He modeled its Grand Hall on Carcassonne, a walled city in France, under a barrel-vaulted ceiling 65 feet high. For decades it was among the busiest depots in America, and Harry Truman was photographed here after beating Thomas Dewey in 1948. Link's plan included a Terminal Hotel. Rail travel faded, and a major renovation in 1985 brought the old station back to life.

  • Stifel Theatre, the former Peabody Opera House

Est. 1909

Hotel Indigo St. Louis Downtown

St. Louis, Missouri · Historic Hotels member since 2022

Architect Isaac S. Taylor, fresh from his work on the St. Louis World's Fair, designed this 13-story office tower at Broadway and Olive in 1908. It was named the LaSalle Building after the French explorer René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle, and wore white terra cotta on its lower two floors. Later owners stripped some of that away, but The Korte Company restored it in the 1980s, and ViaNova Development made it a hotel.

Est. 1922

The Royal Sonesta Chase Park Plaza St. Louis

St. Louis, Missouri · Historic Hotels member since 2025

Attorney Chase Ullman built The Chase Hotel beside Forest Park, and architect Preston J. Bradshaw gave it Italian Renaissance arches and plasterwork. It opened in 1922. Across the street the Koplar brothers raised the Art Deco Park Plaza, and Sam Koplar went on to manage The Chase, where the Starlight Roof drew big names. Guests over the years included Amelia Earhart, Billie Holiday, Elvis Presley and Frank Sinatra.

Est. 1926

21c Museum Hotel St. Louis  

St. Louis, Missouri · Historic Hotels member since 2023

After an accident left its old Central Branch unsound in 1920, the St. Louis YMCA built a new ten-story home at 16th and Locust. Architects Louis LaBeaume and Eugene S. Klein gave it their own take on Renaissance Revival, and it opened in 1926 as the Downtown YMCA Building. Jefferson College taught job skills on its second to fourth floors during the Depression. Developers began turning it into a hotel and art museum in 2018.

Est. 1928

The Raphael Hotel

Kansas City, Missouri · Historic Hotels member since 2001

The McCanles brothers bought land from developer J.C. Nichols on Country Club Plaza and hired architect Alonzo Gentry, who designed a nine-story Italian Renaissance Revival building. It opened in 1928 as the Villa Serena Apartments. The J.C. Nichols Company later turned it into a small European-style hotel, reborn as The Raphael in 1975. Philip Pistilli bought it in 1989, and in 2005 it passed to the Walkers, a family from Salina, Kansas.

  • Nelson-Atkins Museum of Art
  • Brush Creek and Theis Park

Est. 1929

Rockwood Motor Court

Springfield, Missouri · Historic Hotels member since 2025

Route 66 was created in 1926, and Springfield became one of its hubs. Three years later Deverne Ruckman opened a roadside lodge on St. Louis Street, on the highway's first alignment, with cottages set around a U-shaped courtyard. Around 1948 the cabins were faced in sandstone and Ozark fieldstone, and the place took the name Rockwood Motor Court. Historic Hotels of America lists it as the longest-running motor court on the old highway.
