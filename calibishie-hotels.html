The north-east coast, and what it is for

Calibishie is a village on Dominica’s north-east coast, on the Atlantic side of the island. It is the quietest corner in this guide and the one with the fewest beds — which is the point, and also the problem.

The village

A small coastal village in the parish of Saint Andrew, on the north-eastern side of the island facing the Atlantic. There is no resort strip, no cruise berth and no shopping. People come for the coast, the quiet and the drive, and they stay in cottages and small estates rather than hotels.

Which coast this is

This is the windward side, open to the Atlantic rather than sheltered on the Caribbean. That changes the sea, the breeze and the feel of a day here compared with Roseau or Portsmouth on the west coast. We have deliberately not described the swimming or the surf at any particular beach, because we could not read a source that does — ask locally and ask your host.

Pointe Baptiste

The headland beside the village, and the address of the one property on this page we could verify. Its own site says two beaches are within a five to ten minute walk, which is as good a description of what this coast offers as we can honestly give you.

Marigot and the airport side

Marigot is the next place of any size along this coast and resolves to its own hotel listing. Douglas–Charles Airport — the island’s jet airport, with its 6,352-foot runway — is on this side of Dominica too. We could not source a drive time from the airport to Calibishie on any page we could read, so we are not printing one; ask your host, who will know it to the minute.

What it is not

It is not a base for seeing the whole island. The great sights — the World Heritage park, the Boiling Lake, the sulphur springs — are a long way across slow mountain roads from here. Treat Calibishie as two or three nights of genuine quiet at one end of a trip, not as the place you unpack for a fortnight.

Getting about

You will want a vehicle or a host who drives. The UK Foreign Office says Dominica’s roads are sometimes narrow and in poor condition with few road signs, that it is easy to get lost, and that car hire should be arranged before you arrive. It also says the main highway from Douglas–Charles Airport is prone to landslides after bad weather, with sheer drops where the road has fallen away, and not to drive it at night. That is the road that brings most people to this coast.

Where to stay — one place we could verify

Pointe Baptiste Estate

CalibishieOne cottageChocolate factory on site

Pointe Baptiste Estate gives its address as Pointe Baptiste Estate, Calibishie, Dominica, Eastern Caribbean, and it is an estate with a working chocolate factory rather than a hotel — which is the first thing to understand before you book. The accommodation it publishes is a single unit, which it calls The Cottage and also Our Chocolate Cottage, on the upper floor above the factory, with a king-sized bed. The site says the estate is open every day from 9.30 to 5, and it prices the factory tour at 10 EC dollars per person, ending with a tasting. It also says two beaches are within a five to ten minute walk. One cottage means exactly what it says: there is no second room to move to and no front desk, so write to them directly, confirm the dates in writing and ask what is included, as you would with any small owner-run place. If you want a quiet few nights on the Atlantic coast with chocolate being made underneath you, there is nothing else quite like it in this guide.

Travelling in your later years

Be honest with yourself about the road before you choose this coast. Calibishie is reached from the island’s jet airport along the highway the UK Foreign Office singles out for warning — prone to landslides after bad weather, with sheer drops where the road has fallen away, and not to be driven at night. For many travellers in their later years that is an argument for arranging a local driver for the arrival and departure legs and leaving the hire car out of it altogether. Beyond the road, the attractions of this coast suit an unhurried pace rather well: short level walks, a beach within ten minutes on foot of the one property we could check, and a working chocolate estate that you visit rather than hike to. Two cautions. There is one cottage at that property, so there is no alternative room if something does not suit, and no reception desk to solve it — confirm everything in writing before you travel. And the government hospital is in Roseau, the length of the island away: the Foreign Office says it can cope with many types of treatment but that serious cases may require emergency evacuation, so take insurance that covers it and keep your medicines in your hand luggage.

Questions about staying in Calibishie

Where is Calibishie?

On Dominica's north-east coast, in the parish of Saint Andrew, facing the Atlantic. It is a village rather than a town and a quiet one - no resort strip, no cruise berth, no shopping. It sits on the same side of the island as Douglas-Charles Airport, the island's jet airport, which makes it a practical first or last stop on a trip even though it is a poor base for seeing the whole country.

What is Pointe Baptiste Estate actually like?

It is an estate with a working chocolate factory rather than a hotel, and the accommodation is a single unit. Its own site calls that unit The Cottage, or Our Chocolate Cottage, describes it as being on the upper floor above the factory with a king-sized bed, and says the estate is open every day from 9.30 to 5. The factory tour is priced on the site at 10 EC dollars a person and ends with a tasting, and the site says two beaches are within a five to ten minute walk. One cottage means no second room and no front desk, so confirm everything in writing before you travel.

Should I base a whole holiday in Calibishie?

We would not. The sights that bring people to Dominica - Morne Trois Pitons National Park, the Boiling Lake, the sulphur springs, the west-coast diving - are a long way from here across slow mountain roads, and you would spend the holiday driving. Calibishie earns two or three nights of real quiet at one end of a trip, with Roseau or Portsmouth taking the rest. If you only have a week on the island and you want to see it, stay on the west coast and visit this one.

How do I get to Calibishie from the airport?

By road, along the highway that the UK Foreign Office singles out for warning: it says the main highway from Douglas-Charles Airport is prone to landslides after bad weather and that there are sheer drops where the road has fallen away, and it advises against driving that road at night. We could not find a published drive time from the airport to Calibishie on any page we were able to read, so we are not going to print one - your host will know it exactly, and arranging a local driver for the arrival is the sensible way to do this leg.

Is there anything to do on this coast?

Not in the sense of attractions and tours, and that is why people come. The honest list is a working chocolate estate you can tour, beaches within a short walk of it, the coast road, and the village itself. We have not described the swimming, the surf or the shade at any particular beach on this page, because we could not read a source that does and the Atlantic side of a mountainous island is not somewhere to guess about water. Ask locally before you swim, and ask your host what the sea is doing that week.

What money and what language?

English is Dominica's official language, with Dominican Creole French also spoken. The currency is the East Caribbean dollar, which Dominica shares with seven neighbouring territories as a member of the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank, and it is pegged to the US dollar at EC$2.70 - a fixed rate whose fiftieth anniversary the central bank's own website marks. In a village like this, carry cash: card acceptance at small owner-run places is not something to assume, and the one property on this page prices its chocolate tour in EC dollars.

Do cruise passengers come here?

This is not a cruise village and nothing on this coast is built around a ship's day ashore. We also have to be straight that we could not confirm Dominica's berth arrangements at all: the Dominica Air and Sea Ports Authority's own website refuses automated readers, so the Guild has no sourced description of where cruise ships tie up on this island. If you are arriving by ship, your cruise line's own port information is the version that counts, and Calibishie is almost certainly too far from any berth for a port day - the north-east coast is a place to stay, not a place to visit for six hours.
