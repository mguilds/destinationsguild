Caye Caulker

Where to Stay on Caye Caulker

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Caye Caulker is the small one. Five miles of sand and mangrove inside the same reef that serves Ambergris Caye, with no cars, three sand lanes, a channel at the north end where a hurricane cut the island in two, and a sign at the dock that says go slow. It has been the budget and backpacker island for decades and has grown more comfortable without losing the plot. If Ambergris Caye is a town, Caulker is a village, and the whole decision between them is whether you want more choices or fewer.

Where to stay on Caye Caulker, area by area

Caye Caulker is about five miles long and a few hundred yards wide, and the inhabited part of it is smaller still. There are three sand lanes — Front Street, Middle Street and Back Street — and almost everything is on one of them. The choice here is between the village and the ends of it, and between the lagoon side and the sea side.

The village centre

The few blocks either side of the main dock hold most of the restaurants, the bars, the dive shops, the tour desks and the small hotels. Staying here you are a two-minute walk from everything and you will never think about transport again. The cost is noise: a handful of bars on Front Street run music into the night, and the island is small enough that sound carries. If you are a light sleeper, ask for a room off Front Street or towards the back.

Towards the Split

The north end of the village runs up to the Split, the channel where a hurricane cut the island in two and now the island’s swimming spot, with a bar and a sun deck beside it. Staying near it puts you close to the water and to the liveliest corner of the island in the afternoon. It is the most sociable address on the caye and the least restful.

South of the village

Walk ten or fifteen minutes south along the shore and the village thins into quieter, more spread-out places with more room around them and the airstrip beyond. This is where to be if you want the island but not the music, and it is still an easy walk to dinner. The sand track is dark at night, so bring a torch.

Sea side or lagoon side

The eastern, seaward side faces the reef and gets the breeze, which matters a great deal for both temperature and mosquitoes; it is also the side that takes the sargassum when it comes. The western, lagoon side is calmer, often cheaper, and gets the sunsets. Neither is wrong, but the breeze on the east side is the reason most people prefer it.

What to expect of the rooms

Caye Caulker is a simpler island than Ambergris, and that is the point of it. There are comfortable, well-run small hotels with air conditioning, hot water and good showers, and there are very basic places that have none of those. Both are honestly priced for what they are. Ask specifically about air conditioning, hot water, insect screens and whether the power is on all night, and you will not be disappointed.

Getting around Caye Caulker

Getting here

The water taxi from Belize City takes roughly three quarters of an hour and runs several times a day; from San Pedro on Ambergris Caye it is about half an hour. There is also an airstrip at the south end with short flights from Belize City, which is worth the money if you are arriving with heavy luggage or on a tight connection.

On foot

This is the main point of Caye Caulker. There are no cars. The three lanes are soft sand, flat and short, and you can walk the length of the inhabited island in twenty minutes. Soft sand is harder work than it looks for anyone unsteady, and the lanes are unlit in places at night.

Bicycles and golf carts

Bicycles can be hired and are the usual way to cover the longer stretch south towards the airstrip. A small number of golf carts serve as taxis, mainly for luggage runs between the dock, the airstrip and the hotels; your hotel will arrange one if you ask when you book.

Boats

Everything else happens by boat: the reef, Hol Chan, the Blue Hole day trips, the manatee and snorkel tours, and the hop to San Pedro. Operators are concentrated around the main dock and on Front Street.

When to come, and what it costs

The island follows the Belizean year: dry and busy from December to April, green and cheap from June to November, with September and October the wettest months and the time when a number of the small hotels, restaurants and dive shops close for a few weeks. Caye Caulker is the least expensive of the main Belizean destinations in any season, and the gap between high and low season is wide here.

Winter northers can bring a windy day or two that stops the boats even in the best months, and sargassum generally falls between about March and August on the seaward side. The fuller picture is in Best Time to Visit Belize.

Getting in: what changed, and what has not

Most visitors from the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, the European Union and much of the Commonwealth may enter Belize as tourists without arranging a visa in advance, and are admitted for a limited stay that can usually be extended at an immigration office for a fee. A passport with sufficient remaining validity, onward or return travel, and an address for the first night are the ordinary requirements.

Beyond that we do not print the numbers, and nor should anyone else. Entry rules, the length of stay granted, and the departure and border fees are exactly the details that change without notice. Check the current position with the Belize Immigration and Nationality Department and with your own government’s travel advice shortly before you fly.

Caye Caulker — questions

Caye Caulker or Ambergris Caye?

Caye Caulker is smaller, sandier, cheaper and slower, with no cars, fewer rooms and a short walk to everything. Ambergris Caye is a proper town with hundreds of restaurants, a hospital, banks and a wide range of hotels, and golf-cart traffic to match. The reef is reachable from both and the snorkelling is comparable. If you want simplicity and to spend less, Caulker. If you want choice and services, Ambergris. The water taxi between them takes half an hour, so splitting a week between the two is easy and is what we would suggest for a first trip.

Is there a beach?

Not a broad sandy swimming beach, no. The shoreline is mostly sea grass and mangrove with sand along the front, and swimming happens from piers or at the Split, where the channel gives you deeper, clearer water and a ladder. There is sand to sit on and there are hammocks over the water. Anyone arriving expecting a wide beach to wade into will be disappointed; anyone arriving to snorkel, read and do nothing will not.

Is it noisy?

In the centre, at the weekend, yes — a few bars on Front Street play music late, and on an island this small it carries. A room a street back, or a hotel ten minutes south of the village, solves it completely. If quiet matters to you, say so when you book rather than hoping.

Can I do the Blue Hole from Caye Caulker?

Yes. Operators here run the same full-day trip to Lighthouse Reef as those on Ambergris Caye, leaving very early and returning late. It is a long day in a fast boat and a rough one if the sea is up. It is a dive trip; snorkellers on the boat see the two shallower sites rather than the hole itself, which is not meaningfully visible from the surface.

Are there ATMs and will cards work?

There are ATMs on the island, but they have been known to run out of cash or go offline, and a good many smaller restaurants, shops and boat operators want cash. Bring enough to cover a few days and treat the ATM as a convenience rather than a plan. US dollars are accepted.

Is there a doctor?

There is a small health centre and a pharmacy on the island for ordinary complaints. Anything beyond that means a boat or a flight to Belize City, which is one more reason to hold travel insurance that covers medical evacuation before you travel.
