Where to Stay in Bhaktapur

Where to Stay in Bhaktapur — The Old City & Nagarkot

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Bhaktapur is the best-preserved of the Kathmandu Valley’s three royal cities, and the one worth sleeping in rather than visiting. Brick lanes, carved windows, three great squares, potters turning bowls in the open air, and — after the last tour bus leaves around five — a medieval town that belongs to the people who live in it again. Stay inside the old city for that evening, outside the heritage zone to save money, or up at Nagarkot on the ridge above for the mountains at dawn.

Choosing Your Area in Bhaktapur

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

Inside the old city

A scatter of small guest houses and restored family houses around Durbar Square, Taumadhi and Dattatreya, most of them with rooftop terraces looking over the tiled roofs at the temples. This is the whole reason to stay the night: you are inside the ticketed zone, you can walk out at six in the morning before anyone else is about, and you are there at dusk when the squares empty. Rooms are simple, stairs are steep and narrow, and almost nothing takes a car to the door.

Outside the heritage zone

The modern town spreads out beyond the old walls, and hotels there are cheaper, larger and easier to reach by car, with the old city ten or fifteen minutes’ walk away. A sensible choice if you are travelling with heavy luggage, want a lift to the airport, or simply would rather not pay old-city prices for a small room. You lose the early morning, which is the thing most people remember.

Nagarkot, on the ridge above

Forty minutes up the hill to the east, at about 2,100 metres, Nagarkot is a ridge of hotels facing north for one reason: on a clear morning the Himalaya stand along the whole horizon, and in the best conditions Everest is among them. It is a common and good pairing — a night in Bhaktapur for the town, a night at Nagarkot for the dawn. Bring warm clothes; it is a different climate from the valley floor.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Bhaktapur trip goes.

The heritage ticket, and what it is for

Foreign visitors pay an entry fee to the old city, collected at the gates, and the money goes toward maintaining the monument zone. The fee has changed several times and differs for visitors from SAARC countries, so we do not print a figure here — check the current rate before you arrive and carry cash. Keep the ticket: if you are staying the night you can usually have it endorsed for the length of your stay rather than buying it again each day, which is worth asking about at the gate.

Earthquake damage and what has been rebuilt

The 2015 earthquake hit the valley’s monument zones hard, and Bhaktapur lost and damaged a number of its temples. A great deal has since been rebuilt, much of it by local craftsmen using the old methods, and the squares read as whole again today — but restoration continues and you may find a monument under scaffolding or a courtyard closed. That is a living town looking after itself, not neglect.

Steep stairs, brick lanes and juju dhau

The old city is unkind to wheels and to knees: brick paving, narrow steps, and guest-house staircases built more like ladders. If stairs are a problem, say so when you book and ask for a ground or first floor. Two things to do while you are there — stand in Pottery Square in the early morning while the day’s bowls are set out to dry, and eat the local juju dhau, the thick sweet buffalo curd served in a clay pot that the town is famous for.

Bhaktapur Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Bhaktapur.

Is it worth staying overnight in Bhaktapur?

Yes, and it is the single best piece of advice about the Kathmandu Valley. Almost everyone comes as a day trip from Kathmandu and leaves by late afternoon. Stay the night and you get the squares at dusk and again at six in the morning, with the town going about its own business and almost no other visitors in it. The hotels are simple; the hours are the point.

Where is the best place to stay in Bhaktapur?

Inside the old city, if you can manage the stairs — preferably with a roof terrace over Taumadhi or Durbar Square. Outside the heritage zone if you want more comfort or a car to the door. Add a night at Nagarkot on the ridge above if you want a chance at the mountains at sunrise.

How far is Bhaktapur from Kathmandu?

About 13 kilometres east of central Kathmandu, which in valley traffic means anywhere from forty minutes to well over an hour by taxi. Local buses run all day and cost very little. It is close enough to be an easy day trip and far enough that the evening feels like somewhere else entirely.

How long do you need in Bhaktapur?

One night and two half-days is the sweet spot: arrive in the afternoon, see the squares at dusk, walk them again at dawn, and leave after breakfast. A full second day is rewarding if you want the back lanes, the workshops and Changu Narayan on the hill nearby, which is one of the oldest temples in the valley.
