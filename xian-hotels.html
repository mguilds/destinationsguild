Xi'an’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Xi'an — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Xi'an wears its history lightly — a great grey Ming city wall still rings the old town, the Bell and Drum Towers glow at its centre, and the Terracotta Army waits an hour beyond the ring road. Most visitors are happiest staying inside or beside the walls, within a stroll of the Muslim Quarter and the metro, though the greener Big Wild Goose Pagoda district to the south has its own newer luxury names. We’ve grouped Xi'an’s most dependable, well-run hotels by character rather than address, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can just choose your dates.

Luxury

Xi'an’s top hotels cluster in two places: the sleek high-tech and Gaoxin districts west of the walls, and the leafy stretch around the Big Wild Goose Pagoda to the south. Book here when you want a spa, a proper pool and polished service after long days on your feet among the warriors and the wall.

Shangri-La Xian

$$$$ LuxuryGaoxin districtSpa & indoor poolBusiness-friendly

A large, gracious five-star in the modern Gaoxin business quarter west of the old city, with generous rooms, a full spa and a well-regarded indoor pool. Service is smooth and unfussy, and the Cantonese and buffet restaurants are a cut above. It sits a short taxi or metro ride from the walls, so it suits travellers who value comfort and calm over being in the thick of the old town.

Boutique

For character over chain polish, Xi'an rewards you inside the walls, where a handful of smaller hotels blend Tang and Ming design with quiet courtyards. These are the places for travellers who want a sense of the old city the moment they step into the lobby.

Sofitel Legend People's Grand Hotel Xian

$$$$ LuxuryHistoric 1953 estateGarden groundsInside the walls

Built in 1953 as a state guesthouse and beautifully restored, this Sofitel Legend occupies a walled garden estate within the old city, all mature trees, koi ponds and grand Soviet-era bones softened by French flair. Rooms are large and hushed, the spa is excellent, and the sense of stepping back in time is the whole appeal. It is a splurge, but a memorable, characterful one for those who love a hotel with a story.

Budget & Value

Xi'an is kind to careful budgets: comfortable, well-located rooms cost a fraction of what you’d pay in Beijing or Shanghai, and the best value sits close to the metro so day trips stay easy. You trade a little polish for location and price — and spend the savings on the warriors, the wall and the Muslim Quarter’s food.

Xi'an hotels — quick questions

Which area is best to stay in Xi'an?

Inside or right beside the Ming city walls, around the Bell and Drum Towers, is the most convenient base — you can walk to the Muslim Quarter, the markets and the metro, and reach the Terracotta Army by bus or car. The Big Wild Goose Pagoda area to the south is quieter and greener, with newer luxury hotels and the Shaanxi History Museum close by.

Is Xi'an walkable?

The old walled city is very walkable and flat, and you can even walk or cycle the full 14-kilometre loop along the top of the city wall itself. Beyond the walls the city is large, but the metro is clean, cheap and easy to use, and the Terracotta Warriors sit about an hour outside town, so set aside a half day for that trip.

When is the best time to visit Xi'an?

Spring (April to May) and autumn (September to October) are the most pleasant, with mild days and clear light on the wall and pagodas. Summers are hot and sometimes hazy, while winters are cold but quiet with the lowest room prices; the city wall and the Terracotta Army are worth seeing in any season.
