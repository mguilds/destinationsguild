Mexico Travel Guide

Two coastlines, a thousand years of ruins, and the best food on the continent. From the turquoise shallows of the Riviera Maya to the colonial streets of the interior — this is the Guild's chapter on Mexico.

Why visit Mexico

Few countries give you this much variety within a short flight.

Two coastlines

Caribbean turquoise on the east, Pacific surf and Baja's desert-meets-sea on the west — pick your water.

Ruins and cenotes

Chichén Itzá, Tulum and Cobá sit within easy reach of the beach, alongside freshwater cenotes you can swim in.

All-inclusive value

The Riviera Maya has one of the world's deepest all-inclusive markets, from family resorts to adults-only.

The food

Oaxacan moles, Yucatán cochinita pibil, Baja fish tacos, Mexico City street stalls — reason enough to go.

Top things to do in Mexico

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Chichén Itzá

The great pyramid of Kukulcán, a day trip from Cancún or Mérida. Go early to beat the heat and the buses.

Swim a cenote

The Yucatán is riddled with freshwater sinkholes — Dos Ojos, Ik Kil and Gran Cenote are the classics.

Dive Cozumel

Palancar and the Mesoamerican Reef make Cozumel one of the best drift-diving destinations in the hemisphere.

Tulum ruins

The only Maya city built on the coast — the temples sit on a cliff straight above the Caribbean.

Mexico City museums

The Anthropology Museum alone justifies the trip; then Frida Kahlo's Casa Azul and Teotihuacán's pyramids.

Eat Oaxaca

Seven moles, mezcal palenques and one of the great market cultures on earth.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

December–April is the dry season and the most reliable weather — also the busiest and most expensive, especially around the holidays and spring break.

May and November are the sweet spots: good weather, thinner crowds, better rates.

June–November is the Caribbean hurricane season. Trips can be excellent value, but consider flexible bookings and travel insurance.

Getting there

Cancún (CUN) is the gateway to the Riviera Maya, with the widest choice of direct flights from the US and Canada.

Mexico City (MEX) serves the interior; Los Cabos (SJD) and Puerto Vallarta (PVR) cover the Pacific.

The currency is the Mexican peso. Cards are widely accepted in resort areas. Always check your current entry and passport requirements before booking.

Mexico travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Mexico?

December through April is the dry season and the most reliable weather, which also makes it the busiest and priciest. May and November are good shoulder months. The Caribbean hurricane season runs roughly June to November — travel then can be great value, but book flexibly.

What currency is used in Mexico?

The Mexican peso (MXN). Cards are widely accepted in resort areas and cities, and US dollars are often taken in tourist zones — though you'll usually get a better rate paying in pesos.

Cancún, Playa del Carmen or Tulum — which should I choose?

Cancún has the biggest hotel zone, the easiest airport access and the widest range of all-inclusives. Playa del Carmen is more walkable and lively. Tulum is smaller, more boutique and design-driven, with ruins above the beach. Many travellers base in one and day-trip to the others.

How many days do you need in Mexico?

For a Riviera Maya beach trip, five to seven nights is comfortable — enough for beach time plus a ruins or cenote day. To pair a beach with Mexico City or Oaxaca, plan on ten days or more.

Is Mexico safe for travellers?

Conditions vary a great deal by state and region, and the main tourist areas are visited safely by millions of people every year. Because guidance changes, check your government's current travel advisory for the specific states you plan to visit, and take the usual big-city precautions.
