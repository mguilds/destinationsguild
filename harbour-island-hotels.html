Where to Stay on Harbour Island — Pink Sands Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Written in August 2026. Every hotel named here was checked against the operator’s own information this month — including which ones reach the beach by stairs.

Harbour Island is three and a half miles long, has no airport, no traffic lights and no large hotel, and is reached by a five-minute boat ride from Eleuthera. Down its ocean side runs three miles of genuinely pink sand — the colour comes from crushed foraminifera in the reef — and along its harbour side runs Dunmore Town, a row of clapboard houses in sherbet colours that has changed very little in two hundred years. It is the loveliest small place in the Bahamas and it is also the most expensive.

Three things will shape your week here more than the hotel photographs will. Nothing on this island is all-inclusive — we checked every property, and you should budget every meal separately. The ocean shore sits above a low bluff, so some hotels reach the sand on the level and others by a stairway, and the booking sites will not tell you which. And golf carts are the transport, they carry no insurance whatsoever, and you drive them on the left.

Where to base yourself divides neatly. Dunmore Town, on the harbour side, is flat, walkable, two minutes from the ferry and full of restaurants — the choice if you would rather not drive anything. The ocean side, on Pink Sands Beach, is where the great hotels are, and where you will want a cart. And Eleuthera itself — a hundred and ten miles long, reached by road from the same airport, no boat involved — is a genuinely different and often easier holiday, which is why we have included its best hotels here too.

Every property below is real and currently trading, checked against the operator’s own information this month. Where a category honestly has no answer here — and adults-only and all-inclusive both do — we say so and point you to the island where it does exist.

Harbour Island is small, low and dependent on a boat for everything, which is worth weighing: a storm here does not merely spoil a beach day, it can cut the island off. Luxury

Two hotels on the pink sand, both small, both expensive, and neither of them all-inclusive. Budget for every meal separately — see the honest cost note further down.

Pink Sands Resort

Twenty-odd cottages, villas and houses spread over twenty acres, directly on the three-mile Pink Sands Beach — not a walk to the beach, the beach itself. Boutique

Harbour Island is all boutique — there is not a large hotel on it. These two are the most characterful, and both are in town rather than on the beach.

Rock House

$$$$ LuxuryBay Street10 roomsTwo minutes from the ferryOn a hillNot beachfront

Ten individually decorated rooms above the harbour on the sunset side of Dunmore Town, with a heated pool, cabanas, a well-regarded restaurant and martini bar, and continental breakfast for guests. It is a two-minute walk from the Government Dock, which makes it the easiest arrival on the island — you step off the water taxi and you are there. Two things to know honestly: it is not on the beach, and the walk over to Pink Sands is ten minutes with a real hill in it (there are complimentary golf-cart shuttles, and we would use them). And it was for years an adults-oriented hotel; the new owners now allow children, so do not book it expecting a child-free house.

Budget & Value

Pet-friendly

The only hotel in this corner of the Bahamas with a clear published pet policy: dogs of about thirty pounds or less, house-trained and even-tempered, with a per-stay cleaning charge, and beds and bowls provided on request. All-inclusive

There is no all-inclusive hotel on Harbour Island or Eleuthera, and we verified that rather than assuming it. This is the single biggest budgeting difference between here and Nassau, so please read the money note below.

We checked property by property. Valentines publishes its plan plainly as European Plan, room only. Coral Sands, Rock House, Ocean View Club, Tingum Village and several others include breakfast and nothing further. What You Need to Know Before You Book

Getting here is four separate legs — plan it properly

There is no airport on Harbour Island. The journey is short but it has four handoffs, and your luggage is moved at each one.

One: fly to North Eleuthera (ELH) — American from Miami and Charlotte, Delta from Atlanta, Silver and Fly Aztec from Fort Lauderdale, or Bahamasair and Pineapple Air from Nassau. It is a very small airport with no jetways and bags handled outdoors. Two: a taxi from the airport to Three Island Dock, about $5–$10 a head and two to five minutes. Three: the water taxi across, about $5–$8 a head and five to ten minutes, leaving every ten or fifteen minutes with no booking needed. Four: a golf cart, a local taxi or your hotel’s pickup for the last few minutes.

Two things matter. The water taxis stop at around nine in the evening, so a delayed inbound flight can strand you — aim to land by mid-afternoon. And every leg is cash unless your hotel bundles it. Our strong advice: ask your hotel to arrange the whole transfer when you book. Rock House, Coral Sands and The Dunmore all do it. It removes the cash, the luggage handling and the uncertainty of stepping into an open boat, and it protects you from being over-quoted: one traveller last autumn was quoted $650 for a private charter against a public water taxi costing five dollars.

Golf carts are the transport, and there is no insurance

There are said to be more than eight hundred rental carts on an island three and a half miles long. Rates run about $55 a day for a two- or four-seater, $80–$150 for a six-seater, with a seventh day often free on a weekly hire. A valid driving licence is required and international licences are accepted. Three things Americans regularly get caught by: you drive on the left; there is no insurance available on rental carts at all, so every scratch is yours; and the roads have potholes and speed bumps. Inspect the cart and photograph any existing damage before you accept it, take the keys whenever you leave it, and let your hotel book it and bill it to your room — no cash up front, and someone local to call if it fails.

Can you manage without a cart?

Yes, if you stay on the harbour side and are content to walk. Dunmore Town is small and flat, and Valentines, The Landing, Rock House and Romora Bay are all within a few minutes of the dock and the restaurants. The ridge between the harbour side and the beach side is modest but it is a ridge. If you are staying on the ocean shore, you will want a cart.

What it actually costs — the honest numbers

Harbour Island is the most expensive place in the Out Islands, because everything arrives by barge. Rooms start around $160 a night and the mainstream hotels run $500 to $1,100; the two luxury houses go well beyond that. There is no budget tier below about $160. On like-for-like quality, reckon on two to three times Nassau prices.

Meals: a casual burger or Bahamian plate is $20–$30 a head, a mid-range dinner with a drink $40–$60, and fine dining with wine over $100. Local beer runs $6–$8 and a cocktail or a glass of wine $15–$25. Since nothing is all-inclusive, two people eating three meals a day realistically spend $150–$300 a day before the room. And add the fees: Coral Sands adds ten per cent VAT plus a ten per cent resort fee plus a ten per cent service charge; Valentines adds nearly twenty-four per cent in taxes, levies and gratuities; The Landing adds ten per cent VAT and twelve per cent gratuity. Assume twenty-five to thirty per cent on top of the quoted room rate. Ways to soften it: Angela’s Starfish and Queen Conch serve very good food at far kinder prices, Captain Bob’s is the grocery, and a unit with a kitchen — Tingum Village, Royal Palm, the Coral Sands cottages, Pineapple Fields on Eleuthera — makes a real difference.

Harbour Island and Eleuthera are two different holidays

Do not conflate them. Harbour Island is three and a half miles long, reached by boat, and run on golf carts. Eleuthera is a hundred and ten miles long with its own airports, and you will need a hire car. The Cove, Sky Beach Club, Pineapple Fields and French Leave are all on Eleuthera and reached by road — no water taxi. If steps, boats and luggage handling are a concern, that is a point in Eleuthera’s favour.

One name to avoid, and two to telephone

Do not book Cocodimama on Eleuthera. Its website is still live and still advertising, but it has not operated as a nightly-rate hotel for years and its restaurant closed long ago. Separately, The Dunmore and Romora Bay are both open but deserve a telephone call before you commit — the Dunmore’s public reviews thin out after April 2025, and Romora Bay had construction work and refund complaints in late 2024.

Money, entry and one small comfort

The Bahamian dollar is pegged one-for-one to the US dollar and both circulate, so there is nothing to change; VAT is ten per cent. Americans, Canadians and Britons need no visa and a valid passport is the practical requirement by air. A Digital Arrival Card began a soft launch on 5 May 2026 on selected flights, alongside the paper form. Bring insect repellent for evening dining, particularly on Eleuthera — it comes up in guest reviews more often than anything else. And carry cash: several of the small places here still prefer it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you actually get to Harbour Island?

In four legs, and the whole thing takes fifteen to twenty-five minutes once you land. You fly to North Eleuthera (ELH) — American from Miami and Charlotte, Delta from Atlanta, Silver and Fly Aztec from Fort Lauderdale, or Bahamasair and Pineapple Air from Nassau. Then a taxi to Three Island Dock, about $5–$10 a head. Then a water taxi across, about $5–$8 a head and five to ten minutes, running every ten or fifteen minutes. Then a cart or taxi to your hotel. Two cautions: the water taxis stop around nine at night, so aim to land by mid-afternoon; and ask your hotel to arrange the whole transfer at booking. One traveller was quoted $650 for a private charter against a $5 public boat.

Is anything on Harbour Island all-inclusive?

No. We checked property by property rather than assuming. Valentines publishes its plan as European Plan, room only. Coral Sands, Rock House, Ocean View Club and Tingum Village include breakfast and nothing more. Pink Sands sells room-only with breakfast on certain packages. The Cove Eleuthera is à la carte. The closest thing is Eleven Bahama House, which includes breakfast, an afternoon spread and in-room drinks, operates seasonally from about November to early August, and is often booked as a whole-house buyout. Budget $150–$300 a day for two people's food and drink on top of the room.

Which hotels have level access to the beach?

Coral Sands, Pink Sands Resort and The Dunmore all describe direct or level access onto Pink Sands Beach. Runaway Hill Inn sits on the bluff with a stairway down to the sand. Ocean View Club also reaches the beach by steps. Rock House is on a hill in town and is not on a beach at all — it runs complimentary cart shuttles over. If stairs are a concern, this is the single most important question to ask before you book, and the booking sites will not answer it for you.

Do I need a golf cart?

If you are staying on the ocean side, yes. If you are staying harbour-side at Valentines, The Landing, Rock House or Romora Bay, you can manage on foot — Dunmore Town is small and flat, though there is a modest ridge between the harbour and the beach. Carts run about $55 a day for a two- or four-seater and $80–$150 for a six-seater, and weekly hires often include a seventh day. Three warnings: you drive on the left, there is no insurance available on rental carts so all damage is yours, and the roads have potholes. Book through your hotel and bill it to the room.

How much more expensive is Harbour Island than Nassau?

Roughly two to three times, on like-for-like quality, because everything arrives by barge. Rooms start around $160 and the mainstream hotels run $500 to $1,100 a night; Nassau's listings start nearer $100 with resorts around $250. There is no budget tier here. Meals run $20–$30 a head casual, $40–$60 mid-range and $100-plus for fine dining, with beer at $6–$8 and a cocktail at $15–$25. And add twenty-five to thirty per cent on the room for VAT, resort fees and service charges.

Is there an adults-only hotel on Harbour Island?

No, and there is none on Eleuthera either. Rock House was the island's adults-oriented property for years, but new ownership now permits children, so its old reputation no longer holds. The nearest alternatives are The Landing, which allows only one child under ten per room, or taking a small property as a private buyout — Eleven Bahama House sleeps around twenty-two. For a genuinely child-free week in the Bahamas, look at Sandals Royal Bahamian or Breezes in Nassau, or the Riu Palace and the Warwick on Paradise Island.

Can I bring my dog?

Sometimes, and this corner of the Bahamas is better for it than most — but only one property has a policy we could actually read. The Cove Eleuthera publishes clear terms: dogs of about thirty pounds or less, house-trained, with a per-stay cleaning charge, beds and bowls provided, and no access to the pool area or restaurants. It is also on Eleuthera itself, reached by road, so no dog has to go in a boat. Ocean View Club on Harbour Island is listed as pet-friendly but publishes nothing itself; get it in writing before you travel. Sources conflict on Coral Sands. French Leave states plainly that pets are not allowed. And in every case the Bahamas requires an import permit from the Ministry of Agriculture obtained in advance, with a current rabies vaccination and a veterinary health certificate.
