Los Cabos Travel & Adventure Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Where the desert runs out of land and falls into the sea — the granite arch at Land's End, marlin off the point, and whales in the bay all winter.

Find Los Cabos Hotels Search Flights to SJD

Los Cabos is what happens at the very end of a thousand-mile desert peninsula. The Baja runs south from California through cactus and rock, and then simply stops — and at the stopping point, where the Sea of Cortés meets the Pacific, a wedge of granite has been carved by the ocean into El Arco, the arch that appears on every postcard of the region. This is not the Caribbean. The water is cooler, the land is desert, the light is harder, and the great pleasures are marine: marlin and sailfish offshore, grey and humpback whales calving in the bay from December, snorkelling in the sheltered coves along the Corridor. Los Cabos is really two towns — brash Cabo San Lucas and gentle San José del Cabo — with twenty miles of golf courses and luxury resorts strung between them.

The region is really a triangle. Cabo San Lucas sits at the very tip: a working marina, a wall of bars and restaurants, the sportfishing fleet, and Medano Beach — the one big beach in town where the water is calm enough to swim. San José del Cabo, twenty miles northeast, is the older settlement: a colonial plaza, a mission church, an art district that holds a weekly gallery walk, and a far slower pulse. Between them runs the Corridor, the coastal highway where most of the famous resorts and golf courses are, each on its own cove.

One thing to know before you book, because it catches people out: many Los Cabos beaches are not swimmable. The Pacific side takes heavy surf and a serious undertow, and a good number of beautiful beaches here are for looking at, not entering. Medano Beach in town is the reliable swimming beach; Chileno and Santa María bays on the Corridor are calm and excellent for snorkelling. Watch the flags, and if a beach has no flags and no people, there's usually a reason.

Why visit Los Cabos

Four reasons the end of the Baja is worth the flight.

El Arco

The granite arch at Land's End, where the Sea of Cortés meets the Pacific. Take a panga out at first light, before the tour boats.

The whales

From December to April greys and humpbacks come to calve. You'll see them from the boats — and often from the beach.

The marlin capital

Cabo built its name on sportfishing, and the offshore grounds for marlin, sailfish and dorado are still among the world's best.

Desert meets sea

Cactus, granite and cobalt water. It looks nothing like the Caribbean, and the light at dawn is worth the early alarm.

Top things to do in Los Cabos

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Boat to El Arco

A short panga ride from the marina takes you under the arch and past Lover's Beach, the sand spit between two oceans.

● Whale watching

December to April. Boats leave the marina daily; humpbacks breaching a hundred metres off the bow is not a rare event here.

● Go sportfishing

The fleet leaves before dawn for marlin, sailfish, dorado and tuna. Most charters will arrange for a restaurant to cook your catch.

● Snorkel Chileno Bay

One of the few genuinely calm, swimmable bays on the Corridor, with reef fish close in to the shore.

● San José's art walk

The gallery district of the old town opens its doors on Thursday evenings in season — the antidote to the marina.

● Day trip to Todos Santos

An hour up the Pacific coast: a surf town and artists' colony with a famous hotel and a very different mood.

Where to stay in Los Cabos

Choose by the trip you want. Cabo San Lucas is for people who want to walk out of the hotel into the marina, the restaurants and the noise — and it has Medano Beach, the one big swimmable town beach. The Corridor, the twenty miles of highway between the two towns, is where the great resorts sit: private coves, golf, spas, spectacular sea views — but you will drive or taxi for everything, and some of those beautiful beaches are not swimmable. San José del Cabo is for travellers who want the colonial plaza, the galleries and a quiet evening; it's near the airport and much calmer, with a growing crop of boutique hotels. Families and honeymooners generally do best on the Corridor; first-timers who want life outside the hotel should look hard at Cabo San Lucas.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

October–May is the sweet spot: warm, dry, sunny, with whale season running from December to April. Summer is hot and humid, and the peak hurricane risk falls roughly August to October — though rates drop steeply then. Note the desert nights: December and January evenings can be genuinely cool.

Getting there

Los Cabos International (SJD) sits near San José del Cabo, roughly 30–45 minutes from Cabo San Lucas, and is well served with direct flights from the western US and Canada. Pre-book a transfer if you can: the arrivals hall is notorious for aggressive timeshare touts posing as transport staff.

Los Cabos travel FAQ

What's the difference between Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo?

Cabo San Lucas is the busy one — the marina, the nightlife, El Arco and Medano Beach. San José del Cabo, about 20 miles east, is the older, quieter town, with a colonial plaza and a gallery district. The Corridor is the stretch of highway between them, lined with the big resorts and golf courses.

Can you swim at the beaches in Los Cabos?

At some, but not all — and this surprises people. Many Los Cabos beaches face the open Pacific with heavy surf and a strong undertow, and are not safe for swimming. Medano Beach in Cabo San Lucas is the main swimmable town beach, and Chileno and Santa María bays along the Corridor are calm and good for snorkelling. Always check the flags.

When can you see whales in Los Cabos?

Roughly December to April, when grey and humpback whales come to Baja's warm waters to calve. Boat trips run daily in season from the Cabo San Lucas marina, and it's common to see whales from the shore and even from hotel balconies.

When is the best time to visit Los Cabos?

October to May: warm, dry and sunny, with whale season layered on from December. Summer is hot and humid, and the peak hurricane risk runs roughly August through October — though prices in those months drop sharply.
