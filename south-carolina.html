A short history

South Carolina's coastal Lowcountry was home to the Cusabo, Catawba and other Indigenous nations before English settlers founded Charles Towne in 1670. The colony's economy grew wealthy on rice and indigo plantations worked by enslaved Africans, whose descendants shaped the distinct Gullah Geechee culture, language and cuisine still celebrated along the Sea Islands today.

South Carolina became the eighth state to ratify the Constitution in 1788, then made history again in 1860 as the first state to secede from the Union, and the first shots of the Civil War were fired on Fort Sumter in Charleston Harbor in 1861. Reconstruction and the civil rights era brought profound, often painful change across the state through the 20th century.

Charleston has since become one of America's most celebrated historic cities, its cobblestone streets and antebellum architecture carefully preserved alongside a renowned culinary scene built on shrimp, grits and Lowcountry boil. Meanwhile Myrtle Beach and Hilton Head anchor a booming golf-and-beach tourism economy, and inland Greenville has reinvented its old textile-mill downtown into a lively arts and dining destination.

Bodhi

Charleston's famous single houses are built one room wide with the narrow end facing the street, a design that let the sea breeze cool the place before anyone had heard of air conditioning. The city's Rainbow Row got its candy colors in the 1930s, when a preservationist named Dorothy Legge started painting her Georgian rowhouses in pastels to save them from demolition.

Nolie

Skip the crowded Battery at sunset and walk out to Waterfront Park's pineapple fountain instead, the light hits the harbor just as pretty with a fraction of the people. And for shrimp and grits, go where the shrimp boats actually dock, in Shem Creek, not the touristy stretch of King Street.

When to go

Spring and autumn are ideal; the coast is warm well into November.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Mild and mostly quiet, a good time for Charleston sightseeing without the crowds or humidity.

Spring (Mar–May)

Ideal weather with blooming gardens; peak season for Charleston tours and golf on the coast. Myrtle Beach is a classic spring-break stretch.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Hot, humid and busy at the beach resorts; expect afternoon thunderstorms and high hotel rates.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Warm well into November with fewer crowds, making it a favourite season for both coast and city.
