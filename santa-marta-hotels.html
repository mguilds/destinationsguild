Where to Stay in Santa Marta

Where to Stay in Santa Marta — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Santa Marta is the oldest surviving city in Colombia, founded in 1525, and it is nobody’s idea of a polished resort town. It is hot, busy, cheerful and a little rough at the edges — a working port with a small colonial heart. What makes it worth the journey is what lies on either side of it: Tayrona National Park half an hour east, where the jungle of the Sierra Nevada comes straight down to a run of perfect coves, and the cool green village of Minca in the hills behind, where the coffee grows and the temperature drops fifteen degrees.

Four areas matter, and they suit quite different holidays. The Centro Histórico is the atmospheric choice: a small grid of colonial streets, the best restaurants in the city, and the shortest drive to Tayrona and Minca — but the water in front of it is a working harbour. El Rodadero, ten minutes south, is the Colombian family beach: a curved bay, a wall of apartment towers, vendors, music, and a genuinely swimmable sea. Pozos Colorados and Bello Horizonte, further south towards the airport, hold the big resorts on wide quiet sand. And the Tayrona park road east of the city puts you at the gate first thing.

Two things about Tayrona to settle before you book flights. First, the park closes for several weeks each year at the request of the four indigenous peoples of the Sierra Nevada who own the land, so that it can rest — in recent years in February, June and October, but the dates move and are published in advance by Parques Nacionales. Check them before you commit to non-refundable travel, because a Santa Marta week with Tayrona shut is a different holiday. Second, since 28 April 2025 the park authority asks visitors to show proof of yellow fever vaccination at the gate. A waiver to sign is generally offered instead, but it is not something to rely on after an hour’s drive. The vaccine takes about ten days to work, so this belongs on the list three or four weeks before you fly — and it is a live vaccine that does not suit everyone, so ask your own doctor rather than us.

What You Need to Know Before You Book

Four things that decide how a Santa Marta week goes.

Which district, honestly

Centro for characterRodadero for the sea

Centro Histórico: our choice. A small colonial grid with the best restaurants in the city round the Parque de los Novios, a seafront promenade for the evening, and the shortest run to Tayrona and to Minca. The water in front is a working port — do not book here expecting to swim.

El Rodadero: ten minutes south, and a different country. A curved bathing bay backed by apartment towers, packed with Colombian families, vendors, jet-skis and restaurants. Cheerful and not remotely tranquil. The sea is warm, calm and genuinely swimmable.

Pozos Colorados & Bello Horizonte: the resort strip between Rodadero and the airport. Wide quiet beaches, big hotels, nothing outside the gates. Right if you want to stay put; wrong if you want to walk to dinner.

Minca: not the coast at all — a village in the hills forty minutes up a rough road, fifteen degrees cooler, full of birds, waterfalls and small eco-lodges. Two nights there is the best add-on this region offers, and the drive is not for a large car.

Doing Tayrona properly

Go earlyConsider the boat

The main El Zaino gate is about an hour east of the city. From there it is a shuttle to Cañaveral and then roughly an hour and a half on foot to Cabo San Juan — a hot, exposed, root-crossed path that catches a great many visitors out. Horses can be hired. The Calabazo entrance is shorter and steeper.

For most of our readers the sensible route is the boat from Taganga to Cabo San Juan, about forty-five minutes, which removes the walk entirely — though it can be a rough, wet crossing and is not for bad backs. The other easy option is Playa Cristal, reached by road to Neguanje and a short boat hop, with much less walking at either end.

Whichever way: go early, because the park caps daily numbers and the queue starts before eight; carry far more water than feels necessary; and remember there is almost no shade on the walk in. Check the closure dates and the yellow-fever rule before you book.

The heat, and it is worse than Cartagena

90°FDry & bright

Santa Marta runs a degree or two hotter than Cartagena and a good deal drier — the coast east of the city towards La Guajira is genuinely arid, and the sun is fierce. There is no cool season, only a drier one: December to March is the best stretch, breezy and reliable, and September and October are the wet months.

Plan around it rather than through it: out early, indoors or in the water from eleven to four, out again at five. Book a hotel whose air conditioning you can rely on, take a hat you will actually wear, and treat a Tayrona walk at midday as a bad idea rather than a challenge.

Getting about, and a word on Taganga

Use an appDon’t drive

Taxis are cheap — roughly 20,000 pesos from the Centro to Rodadero — and have no meters, so agree the fare first or use a ride app. Hotels will arrange a car and driver for Tayrona or Minca, which is worth doing rather than negotiating at the roadside; the Minca road in particular is rough and best driven by someone who knows it. Do not rent a car for a Santa Marta holiday.

Taganga, the fishing cove just north, is a popular day trip and the departure point for the Tayrona boat. It is also considerably rougher than the city after dark, with a reputation for drugs and petty crime that is not undeserved. Go by day, enjoy the fish lunch, and be back in town by evening.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions Guild readers ask most about staying in Santa Marta.

Which part of Santa Marta should I stay in?

It depends entirely on what you came for. The Centro Histórico is the atmospheric choice — a small colonial grid by the working harbour, with the best restaurants and the shortest drive to Tayrona and Minca — but the city beach there is a port beach and not for swimming. El Rodadero, ten minutes south, is the Colombian family resort strip: a curved bay, high-rise hotels, restaurants and vendors, busy and cheerful. Pozos Colorados and Bello Horizonte, further south towards the airport, hold the large resorts on quieter sand. For Tayrona itself, the villages along the park road east of the city put you at the gate in the morning.

Is the beach at Santa Marta any good?

Not in the city itself. The bay in front of the Centro is a working port with cargo ships at anchor, and it is not a swimming beach. El Rodadero is a proper bathing bay, if a busy and built-up one, and the resort beaches at Pozos Colorados and Bello Horizonte are wide and calm. But the beaches people come to Santa Marta for are in Tayrona National Park — Cabo San Juan, Playa Cristal, Neguanje — where the jungle of the Sierra Nevada runs down to the sea. Those are day trips from anywhere in the city, and worth planning the week around.

When is Tayrona National Park closed?

Tayrona closes for several weeks each year at the request of the four indigenous peoples of the Sierra Nevada who own the land, so that the park can rest. In recent years the closures have fallen in February, June and October, but the exact dates change and are published in advance by Parques Nacionales. Check before you book non-refundable travel. Since April 2025 the park authority also asks visitors to show proof of yellow fever vaccination at the gate; a liability waiver is generally offered instead, but do not rely on it. The vaccine needs about ten days to take effect.

How do I get to Tayrona from Santa Marta?

Three ways. The main El Zaino gate is about an hour east by road, and from there it is either a shuttle plus a long hot walk of an hour and a half to Cabo San Juan, or a horse. The Calabazo entrance is a shorter, steeper walk. Easiest by far for anyone who would rather not walk in that heat is the boat from Taganga to Cabo San Juan, about forty-five minutes, which can be rough — or the road entrance to Playa Cristal at Neguanje with a short boat hop. Go early: the park caps daily visitors, and the gate queue in high season starts before eight.

Is Santa Marta safe?

The Centro, El Rodadero and the resort strip are fine with ordinary city precautions, and the tourist police are visible in the historic centre. Santa Marta is a poorer and rougher city than Cartagena and it shows at the edges: keep to the lit streets of the Centro after dark, use a ride app or a hotel taxi rather than hailing, do not carry a phone in your hand, and be wary in Taganga at night, which has a livelier and less regulated reputation than the city. The Level 4 areas named in the U.S. advisory for Colombia are nowhere near Magdalena department — our Colombia guide sets that out in full.

How hot is Santa Marta?

Hotter and drier than Cartagena: highs around 90°F with strong sun, and the coast east of the city is genuinely arid. There is no cool season. December to March is the driest and breeziest stretch and the best time to come; September and October are wet. Plan the day around the heat — out early, indoors or in the water in the middle of the day, out again at five — and choose a hotel with air conditioning you can rely on. Carry more water than you think you need at Tayrona, where there is very little shade on the walk in.
