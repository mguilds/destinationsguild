Where to Stay in Yogyakarta

Where to Stay in Yogyakarta — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Yogyakarta — Jogja to almost everyone who lives there — is Java’s graceful cultural capital and the base for the two greatest monuments in Indonesia: Buddhist Borobudur and Hindu Prambanan. It divides neatly for visitors. Malioboro is the busy central spine, with the sultan’s palace, batik shops and street food within walking distance, for those who want to step out of the lobby into the thick of things. Prawirotaman, twenty minutes south, is quieter and leafier — an old guesthouse quarter grown into the city’s cafe-and-boutique district. Sleman, north towards Mount Merapi, holds the garden and resort hotels and is closest to Prambanan and the airport. And Magelang, about ninety minutes north-west, is rice-field country for travellers who would rather wake near Borobudur itself. Below are the city’s most rewarding places to stay by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Luxury

Yogyakarta’s finest stays are out of town — one facing Borobudur across the rice, the other in twenty-two hectares of garden north of the city.

Amanjiwo

$$$$ LuxuryBorobudurTemple viewsAman service

A domed limestone resort set in the Menoreh Hills above the Kedu Plain, about two miles from Borobudur. Every suite faces the temple across rice terraces, and the service is the quiet, anticipatory kind Aman built its name on. It suits travellers marking an anniversary or a once-in-a-lifetime trip, who value stillness and space more than nightlife or a choice of restaurants.

$$$ UpscaleSlemanBig gardensTerraced pool

A low-rise resort spread over some twenty-two hectares of tropical garden north of the city, with Mount Merapi on the horizon. Boutique

Yogyakarta’s Dutch colonial architecture makes for unusually characterful small hotels.

The Phoenix Hotel Yogyakarta - Handwritten Collection

$$$ UpscaleNear Malioboro1918 buildingCourtyard pool

A 1918 Dutch colonial building a short way from Malioboro, arranged around a green inner courtyard and pool, with high ceilings and period detail intact. It has real history — it served as a government building during the revolution — and the restoration is handsome without being precious. Best for readers who like a hotel with a story and prefer to be within walking distance of the old city. (It trades today under Accor’s Handwritten Collection, having formerly been an MGallery.)

Adults-only

Plainly: we could not verify a single genuinely adults-only hotel in Yogyakarta, and we will not invent one. The “adults-only” labels that appear on booking sites here are automatic filters describing atmosphere, not enforceable policies — several of the hotels carrying them openly accept children. Adults-only resorts in Indonesia are a Bali and Lombok phenomenon; they have not taken hold in Java. For a quiet, grown-up stay in this part of Java, Amanjiwo near Borobudur and the small Indies Heritage in Prawirotaman are the honest recommendations, both listed above. Spa & Pool

Near Borobudur

Borobudur is about ninety minutes from the city. Staying out here turns the pre-dawn drive into a five-minute walk.

A Javanese-styled hotel in Borobudur village itself, minutes from the temple gate, set in gardens with a conference centre attached. Rooms and pavilions draw on traditional architecture, and the proximity means a sunrise visit does not require a pre-dawn drive from the city. Suits travellers who want to give Borobudur a full unhurried day rather than a morning excursion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where should I stay to see Borobudur at sunrise?

You have two good options. Stay near the temple in Borobudur village or Magelang — at Manohara inside the park, Plataran Heritage in the village, or Amanjiwo across the rice fields — and the pre-dawn start is gentle. Or stay in Yogyakarta and take an organised sunrise trip, which means leaving around 3:30 in the morning for a 90-minute drive. If a very early start is hard going, stay near the temple.

How far apart are Borobudur and Prambanan?

They sit on opposite sides of Yogyakarta. Borobudur is about 90 minutes north-west of the city; Prambanan is about 30 minutes north-east, near the airport. Most visitors do Borobudur at dawn one day and Prambanan in the late afternoon of another, when the light is kind and the crowds have gone.

Which area of Yogyakarta is best to stay in?

Choose Malioboro for the palace, the markets and everything on foot. Choose Prawirotaman for a quieter, leafier base with good cafes. Choose Sleman, north of the city, for garden hotels with pools and easy access to Prambanan and the airport.

How many days do I need in Yogyakarta?

Three nights is the comfortable minimum — one for Borobudur, one for Prambanan and the Kraton palace, and one for batik workshops, silver work at Kotagede or a day trip towards Mount Merapi. Two nights will cover the two temples, but at a brisk pace.

Can I climb to the top of Borobudur?

Access to the upper terraces is now limited, ticketed and taken in guided groups with a set number of visitors each day, in order to protect the stone. Book ahead through the official operator, and be aware that reaching the top means a long flight of steep, uneven steps. The lower terraces and the surrounding grounds are far easier going and still magnificent.
