Bighorn River · Montana

Below Yellowtail Dam — Afterbay to Bighorn to Two Leggins

The water
Tailwater — near-perfect trout habitat, cold and nutrient rich
Signature hatch
Trico, August mornings
Season
Open all year. Ice-free; midges hatch in every month.
Base town
Fort Smith, Montana — the river town at the Afterbay — lodges and cabins. Note: Fort Smith MONTANA, not Arkansas

Colorado River at Lees Ferry · Arizona

Below Glen Canyon Dam — about fifteen miles of tailwater down to the Paria confluence

The water
Tailwater inside Glen Canyon National Recreation Area. Cold, clear, and at roughly 3,100 feet it never ices over — a naturally reproducing rainbow population, not a stocked one
What they eat
Midges and scuds rather than a hatch calendar. Lees Ferry Anglers fish “size #18 or #20 midge or pupae patterns” over “the gravel bars, backwaters, eddies and main river channel”, and put the fish at “14 to 22 inches. And larger”
Season
Open all year, and no closed trout season. It fishes in January, when Montana is locked up — which is the real argument for it.
Getting on it
Lees Ferry is the only place you can drive to the river. Every mile upstream of it is reached by running a boat up from the launch ramp
Base town
Marble Canyon, Arizona — a handful of lodges on US-89A by the Navajo Bridge, and the road down to the water

Yellowstone River · Montana

Paradise Valley & Livingston, plus the water inside the Park

The water
Freestone — the longest undammed river in the lower 48
Signature hatch
Salmonfly, late June into early July
Season
Montana water open all year (Central District). Inside Yellowstone National Park: Memorial Day Saturday to 31 October, and the river below Lower Falls does not open until 1 July.
Base town
Livingston, Montana — the fly-shop town, in the middle of Paradise Valley
Montana travel guide › Where to stay Hotels in LivingstonVacation homes
One thing most fly-fishing copy on the internet has wrong right now: the Arkansas White River is under emergency trout regulations after two hatchery failures. From Bull Shoals Dam to Norfork Access the daily limit is two rainbows under fourteen inches — every other trout, and every trout over fourteen inches, goes back. Plenty of published lodge and shop copy still describes the old five-fish limit. Fish it as catch-and-release and check Arkansas Game and Fish before you go.

Arizona — the newest water

Added 1 October 2026. Arizona is our home water, and it fishes when the north is frozen. The Colorado River at Lees Ferry now sits with the other rivers above, in alphabetical order, and in the Where Are They Biting picker — its month-by-month calendar was written from Lees Ferry Anglers, the AZ Fly Shop, Arizona Game and Fish and the Bureau of Reclamation’s own trout monitoring. The outfitters were read off their own websites before being listed.

The Lees Ferry rules catch people out, so here they are in full: under the Arizona Game and Fish Commission Order the limit is two rainbow trout a day, artificial fly and lure only, and barbless hooks only. A rainbow you keep counts against that bag and must be killed and kept; anything beyond it goes back at once. There is no limit at all on any other sport fish — brown trout, every species of bass including stripers, catfish and walleye — and that is deliberate: those are non-native predators the agencies want taken out of the river, not released. Barbless is the rule anglers most often turn up without. Check with Arizona Game and Fish before you fish.

Two more Arizona regions have verified outfitters and bookable rooms but no water write-up yet: the Verde River and Oak Creek down in the Verde Valley, which fish in winter, and the White Mountains streams and lakes between 6,500 and 9,000 feet, which fish in summer when the desert is unbearable. Both are in the guide list and the lodging table below. Their water pages are the next thing we write.

Anglers stay four to seven nights, not two. Come for the hatch, stay for the light.

Questions

What does “Where are they biting” actually do?

Pick a month and a water and it tells you what is hatching then, which flies match it, and whether that month is worth your holiday. Pick a month alone and it ranks every water in the library for that month, best first. The answers come from agency hatch data and the shops who fish the water daily, not from a booking calendar.

Do you sell the guide listings?

No. Nobody pays to be in the Guild's guide list and nobody can pay to move up it. We list outfitters we have verified from their own websites, we say when a licence number is published and when it is not, and we make our money from lodging bookings, not from guides.

How do you decide which month is best?

By what the fish do, not by what is easiest to sell. That is why we will tell you September beats July on the Madison, that the Mother's Day caddis on the Yellowstone is usually blown out, and that the Railroad Ranch is shut until 15 June.

Are the regulations on these pages current?

They were checked against the agencies on 21 September 2026 — Montana FWP, the National Park Service, Utah DWR, Idaho Fish and Game, Arkansas Game and Fish, the BLM and the Bureau of Reclamation. The Arizona water was added on 1 October 2026 and checked against the Arizona Game and Fish Department Commission Order. Rules change, sometimes mid-season, so always check the agency before you fish. Where something has changed recently and most published copy is still wrong — the White River's emergency trout rules, for one — we say so on the page.

I guide or outfit one of these rivers. How do I get listed?

Put yourself forward on the Guild Fly Fishing Guides page. We check the business against its own website and its state or federal licence, and listing is free.
