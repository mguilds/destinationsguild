Before you go

Most visitors see the canyon from the South Rim, and for good reason: it is open every day of the year and has the lodges, shuttles and paved paths.

The Grand Canyon is really two parks joined by a gorge. The South Rim, about an hour north of Interstate 40, is where most visitors go. It has the historic lodges of Grand Canyon Village, free shuttle buses, a paved Rim Trail and a road that runs 23 miles east to the Desert View Watchtower. The North Rim sits about 1,000 feet higher on the far side. It is only 10 miles away as the raven flies, but about 212 miles and four and a half hours by car, and it closes to cars every winter.

2026 has been a hard year for the park, and you should know why before you book. In July 2025 the Dragon Bravo Fire burned through the North Rim's developed area and destroyed the historic Grand Canyon Lodge and the North Rim Visitor Center. Then on August 29, 2026, a flash flood tore through Bright Angel Canyon, destroyed most of the footbridges across Bright Angel Creek and damaged the Transcanyon Waterline that carries drinking water to the South Rim. The park closed every in-park hotel to overnight guests from August 31. They reopened on November 20, 2026, and the park is still asking everyone to save water.

None of that changes the view. The rim viewpoints, the shuttles, the visitor centers and the drive to Desert View are all open. What has changed is the inner canyon: Phantom Ranch and the North Kaibab Trail are closed with no reopening date, and some restaurants are coming back slowly. If your plan is to stand at the rim, take the shuttle out Hermit Road and sleep in a lodge, the South Rim is ready for you.

Two practical points. The South Rim sits at about 7,000 feet, so take the first day easy, drink plenty of water and carry a layer even in summer. And the entrance stations take cards only; cash is not accepted.

South Rim hotels and water (2026): a flash flood on August 29 damaged the Transcanyon Waterline, and all in-park hotels closed to overnight guests from August 31. They reopened on November 20, 2026, as the park eased from Stage 4 to Stage 3 water conservation. Restaurants are reopening gradually; Yavapai Lodge's dining, tavern and coffee shop were still closed in late November. Mather Campground is dry camping only, and the campground showers and laundry (Camper Services) are closed until further notice.

Inner canyon closures: Phantom Ranch (cabins, canteen and Bright Angel Campground), the entire North Kaibab Trail, Cottonwood Campground, and the Black and Silver bridges over the Colorado River are closed, and the park gives no reopening estimate. The Bright Angel Trail is open to the River Resthouse, with rockfall work causing 30–45 minute delays above 3-Mile Resthouse from November 18. Water is turned off at Havasupai Gardens, 3-Mile and 1½-Mile resthouses; fill up at the trailheads.

North Rim after the Dragon Bravo Fire: the July 2025 fire destroyed the historic Grand Canyon Lodge and the North Rim Visitor Center. The North Rim reopened for 2026 with its viewpoints and paved roads, but there was no lodging, no drinking water anywhere on the rim, and the North Rim Campground stayed closed all season. The rim closes to cars for the winter after November 30, 2026 (sooner if a big storm comes), and the park has not yet announced 2027 dates or services.

Hermit Road: closed to private vehicles March 1 through November 30; ride the free Red shuttle instead. From December through February, cars under 22 feet may drive it, and the shuttle does not run.

Visitors from outside the U.S.: since January 1, 2026, Grand Canyon is one of 11 national parks that charge non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older an extra $100 per person on top of the entrance fee. The America the Beautiful pass costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for nonresidents, and a pass covers that extra fee.

Sources: the National Park Service website (nps.gov/grca: fees, lodging, camping, operations update, key hiking messages, North Rim status and shuttle pages), checked November 2026.

Best things to do in Grand Canyon National Park

Six ways to spend your days on the rim.

First look

Mather Point & the Rim Trail

Park at the Grand Canyon Visitor Center and walk a few minutes to Mather Point. From there the Rim Trail runs west along the edge, paved for long stretches, with a shuttle stop never far away.

The geology

Yavapai Geology Museum

A stone building perched on the rim, with big windows and exhibits that explain how the layers were laid down and cut. Open daily, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. in the fall.

History

Grand Canyon Village

El Tovar (1905), Bright Angel Lodge, Kolb Studio and Verkamps Visitor Center line the rim within a short walk of each other. Have lunch in the El Tovar dining room; reservations are required.

Shuttle drive

Hermit Road & Hermits Rest

Seven miles west along the rim to Mary Colter's 1914 stone rest house, with stops at Hopi, Mohave and Pima points. Ride the Red shuttle March through November; drive it yourself in winter.

Scenic drive

Desert View Drive & Watchtower

Twenty-three miles east to Colter's Desert View Watchtower, open to private cars all year, with Grandview, Moran and Lipan points along the way. The tower's upper floors open when staffing allows.

By rail

Grand Canyon Railway

Trains from Williams arrive daily at the historic depot in Grand Canyon Village, a short walk from the rim. It is an easy way to leave the car, and the driving, behind.

From the Guild

Watch sunset from Hopi Point or Mohave Point on the Red shuttle route, then ride the bus back. The shuttle runs until an hour after sunset, so you can stay for the last light without hunting for a parking place in the dark.

When to visit Grand Canyon

Spring (April–May) and fall (September–October) are the sweet spots on the South Rim: days in the 60s and 70s, cool nights and thinner crowds than summer. Summer is the busiest season. Parking at the visitor center fills by about 10 a.m., afternoon thunderstorms are common from July into September, and the inner canyon can top 110°F. Arrive before 10 a.m. or after 2 p.m. to shorten the line at the gate.

Winter is the quiet season and a lovely one. Snow often dusts the rim, rooms are easier to find, and from December through February you can drive Hermit Road yourself in a car under 22 feet. Roads and paths can be icy; bring traction for your shoes.

The North Rim keeps a short season. Highway 67 south of Jacob Lake closes to all cars from December 1 until mid-May. In 2026 the rim reopened in May on a limited basis after the fire, and the park has not yet said what will be open in 2027. Check the North Rim status page on nps.gov in early spring before planning a North Rim trip.

How to get to Grand Canyon National Park

By car

South Rim: from Interstate 40, take Highway 64 north from Williams (about an hour to the South Entrance) or Highway 180 from Flagstaff (about 90 minutes). From Phoenix, allow about four hours. The South Entrance is just north of Tusayan; the East Entrance at Desert View is open 24 hours.

North Rim: about 212 miles and four and a half hours from the South Rim, by Highway 64 east, US 89 north, US 89A west and Highway 67 south from Jacob Lake. The entrance station is 30 miles south of Jacob Lake. Fill up in Jacob Lake; gas on the North Rim is seasonal.

By air, train & shuttle

Flagstaff Pulliam Airport (FLG) is the closest airport with airline service. Phoenix Sky Harbor (PHX) and Las Vegas (LAS) have far more flights and rental cars, at about four and four and a half hours' drive.

The Grand Canyon Railway runs daily from Williams to the historic depot in Grand Canyon Village. Once inside the park, the free shuttle buses (Village, Kaibab Rim and Hermits Rest routes) connect the lodges, campgrounds, viewpoints and trailheads. The Tusayan park-and-ride shuttle runs in summer only.

Where to stay near Grand Canyon National Park

Sleep inside the park in Grand Canyon Village if you can get a room. Tusayan is the next best thing, a mile from the South Entrance; Williams and Flagstaff are good, less expensive bases with more choice.

Lodges inside the park

On the South Rim, Xanterra Parks & Resorts runs El Tovar Hotel, Bright Angel Lodge, Kachina Lodge, Thunderbird Lodge and Maswik Lodge, and Delaware North runs Yavapai Lodge (pets allowed for a fee) and the Trailer Village RV park. Book through the concessioners directly; the National Park Service lists Xanterra at (888) 297-2757 and Delaware North at (800) 780-7234. Rooms book up well ahead, especially for spring break, summer and fall weekends.

El Tovar Hotel and Bright Angel Lodge & Cabins are both members of Historic Hotels of America; see our Historic Hotels of Arizona page for their stories. All the South Rim hotels closed to overnight guests from August 31, 2026, during the water emergency and reopened on November 20. Dining is coming back in stages, so check current restaurant hours when you arrive.

Phantom Ranch, at the bottom of the canyon, is closed after the August 2026 flood, with no reopening estimate. When it reopens, its cabins and meals are offered by Xanterra through an online lottery about 15 months ahead. On the North Rim, the historic Grand Canyon Lodge was destroyed in the July 2025 fire; the park is planning the recovery, but there is no lodging inside the park on the North Rim for now.

RV travel & camping at Grand Canyon

The South Rim is RV-friendly, with one full-hookup park and a big campground, but water is still rationed in late 2026.

Trailer Village RV Park in Grand Canyon Village is the only campground in the park with full hookups. It is run by Delaware North, reservations are recommended, and it reopened on November 20, 2026, after the water emergency.

Mather Campground, also in the Village, is open all year with no hookups and is served by the free shuttle. Book on recreation.gov up to six months ahead; it fills early. For now it is dry camping only, the dump station next to Camper Services is temporarily closed, and the showers and laundry are closed until further notice, so arrive with full fresh water and empty tanks. Desert View Campground, 23 miles east, is closed for the season and opens again April 10 through October 15, 2027, by reservation on recreation.gov; it has no hookups. Plan on a 30-foot length limit at both Mather and Desert View, and confirm your site length when you book.

Just outside the park, the Kaibab National Forest runs Tusayan-Montane Campground (no hookups; next season May 15 to October 3, 2027) on recreation.gov, and Tusayan and Williams have private RV parks with full hookups. On the North Rim, the North Rim Campground was closed for all of 2026 and no 2027 dates have been set; the Forest Service's DeMotte Campground, 7 miles north of the park boundary, has no hookups and next opens May 16 to September 16, 2027.

Big rigs: Hermit Road is closed to vehicles over 22 feet even in winter. Leave the RV at your campsite and ride the shuttles; they reach every major viewpoint on the South Rim.

Fishing in Grand Canyon

The great fishing near the Grand Canyon is at Lees Ferry, on the Colorado River just below Glen Canyon Dam and upstream of the park, inside Glen Canyon National Recreation Area. The water comes out of the dam cold and clear, and it holds wild rainbow trout. From the South Rim, drive east through Cameron and north to Marble Canyon. Our fly fishing book covers Lees Ferry in detail, and Arizona's lakes are in our lake fishing guide.

An Arizona fishing license is required for anglers 10 and older. The stretch from the dam down to Lees Ferry has its own trout rules, including artificial flies and lures only and barbless hooks, and limits change from section to section downriver, so read the current Arizona Game and Fish regulations before you go. Wader-cleaning stations are at Lees Ferry; New Zealand mudsnails and quagga mussels are present, so clean, drain and dry your gear afterward. Fishing inside the canyon itself is mostly for river-trip and backcountry visitors, and Bright Angel Creek is closed with Phantom Ranch for now.

Accessible travel at Grand Canyon

What is available for visitors with limited mobility, and the passes that help.

Scenic Drive Accessibility Permit: visitors with mobility problems can get this free permit at the entrance stations and NPS visitor centers. It lets you drive your own vehicle into some areas closed to public traffic, such as Hermit Road from March through November. It does not cover parking in accessible spaces; you still need your state-issued placard for that.

Shuttle buses: every park shuttle is wheelchair accessible, with a ramp and a wheelchair space. Wheelchairs larger than 30 by 48 inches cannot be carried, and most motorized scooters will not fit, so ask for the accessibility permit if that is you.

Paths and programs: long stretches of the Rim Trail around Grand Canyon Village are paved, and the Greenway Trail from Monument Creek Vista to Hermits Rest is built to accessibility standards for wheelchairs. Many ranger programs are wheelchair accessible. Paper copies of the park's Accessibility Guide are available at the Grand Canyon Visitor Center and the Desert View Visitor Center.

Good to know: many of the historic buildings and rim viewpoints are uneven, and the altitude is about 7,000 feet, so a companion is a help. Call the park at 928-638-7888 with specific questions before you go.

Park passes that save you money. U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability can get the free lifetime Access Pass. Visitors 62 and over can buy the Senior Pass ($80 for life, or $20 a year). Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass. Each covers entrance fees at every national park.

Traveling on dialysis? The nearest dialysis clinics are in Flagstaff, about 80 miles south of Grand Canyon Village. Book your treatment at a clinic there several weeks before you go; the large dialysis companies run travel desks that will arrange it with your home unit.

Grand Canyon National Park FAQ

Do I need a reservation to enter Grand Canyon National Park?

No. Grand Canyon has no entry reservation or timed-entry system. You do need advance reservations for the lodges, Mather Campground and Desert View Campground.

How much is the Grand Canyon entrance fee in 2026?

$35 per private vehicle, $30 per motorcycle and $20 per person on foot or bicycle, each good for 7 days. Children under 16 are free. The entrance stations accept cards only, not cash.

Is the North Rim of the Grand Canyon open in 2026?

Partly, and only until November 30, 2026, or the first major snowstorm. After the 2025 Dragon Bravo Fire, the North Rim reopened for 2026 with viewpoints and roads open, but with no lodging, no drinking water and the campground closed. The park has not yet announced 2027 plans.

Was the Grand Canyon Lodge on the North Rim destroyed?

Yes. The historic Grand Canyon Lodge and the North Rim Visitor Center were destroyed by the Dragon Bravo Fire in July 2025. The park is planning the North Rim's recovery, but there is no reopening date for in-park lodging there.

Are the South Rim hotels open?

Yes. The South Rim hotels closed to overnight guests on August 31, 2026, after a flood damaged the park's water line, and reopened on November 20, 2026. Some restaurants are reopening gradually, and visitors are asked to conserve water.

Is Phantom Ranch open?

No. Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel Campground and the entire North Kaibab Trail have been closed since the August 29, 2026 flash flood, and the park has given no reopening estimate.

Can I drive Hermit Road?

Only from December through February, in a vehicle under 22 feet. From March 1 to November 30 the road is closed to private cars and you ride the free Red shuttle, unless you have the park's Scenic Drive Accessibility Permit.

How far is the Grand Canyon from Flagstaff and Phoenix?

The South Entrance is about 90 minutes from Flagstaff and about four hours from Phoenix. Williams is about an hour away.
