Guangzhou’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Guangzhou — Best Hotels by Category

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Guangzhou is a sprawling, energetic port city on the Pearl River, and the right hotel depends less on a single neighborhood than on the kind of trip you want. Do you want a sky-high luxury tower over the modern Tianhe skyline, an old-world boutique on leafy Shamian Island, a panda-and-pool family resort out at Chimelong, or an honest value room near the river? We’ve grouped our picks by character, each with a candid note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can just choose your dates.

Luxury

Guangzhou’s finest hotels live high inside the glass towers of Zhujiang New Town and Tianhe, where floor-to-ceiling windows frame Canton Tower and the river below. Book here when you want polished international service, superb restaurants and a room that feels like part of the skyline.

Four Seasons Hotel Guangzhou

$$$$ LuxuryZhujiang New TownSky-high viewsMichelin dining

Set on the upper floors of the landmark Guangzhou IFC tower, this is one of the highest hotels in the world, with a soaring sky-lobby and rooms that look straight across to Canton Tower and the Pearl River. The service is impeccable, the restaurants are among the city’s best, and the sense of arrival is unforgettable. It is the benchmark for luxury in Guangzhou, ideal when the view and the pampering are the whole point.

Boutique

Guangzhou’s boutique character clusters on and around Shamian Island, the tree-shaded former foreign concession where colonial mansions now house smaller, storied hotels. These are the stays for travelers who prize atmosphere, history and a sense of place over a big glass lobby.

Guangdong Victory Hotel

$$ Mid-rangeShamian IslandColonial heritageRiverside lanes

Housed in a restored early-twentieth-century building on car-free Shamian Island, the Victory carries genuine colonial-era charm on some of the prettiest streets in the city. Rooms are comfortable and unfussy, and stepping out among the banyan trees, old churches and cafes feels a world away from Guangzhou’s traffic. A characterful, well-priced base for travelers who want history and a stroll to the Pearl River waterfront.

Budget & Value

Guangzhou rewards a careful budget: reliable older hotels near the river and the metro cost a fraction of the skyline towers and put you close to the sights and the food. You trade a little polish for location and honest prices, and spend the savings on dumplings and day trips.

$ ValueHaizhu SquareRiver viewsMetro on the doorstep

A long-running, well-located hotel beside Haizhu Square and the Pearl River, with many rooms looking out over the water and Haizhu Bridge. It is not new, but it is tidy, sensibly priced and superbly placed for the old town, the waterfront night views and the metro. A sound, central value pick for travelers who want to be in the thick of things without paying tower prices.

Family-friendly

Families gravitate to the Chimelong resort out in Panyu, where the hotels sit right beside the safari park, water park and circus, and the whole day is built around children. These are destination stays in their own right — you barely need to leave the gates.

$$$ ResortPanyu / ChimelongBeside the safari parkPools & wildlife

The flagship resort hotel of the Chimelong complex, set right next to the Safari Park so that white tigers, flamingos and other animals are part of the grounds. Rooms are spacious and family-sized, there are big pools and easy access to the safari, water park and circus. Good to know before you book

Which area is best to stay in Guangzhou?

For first-time visitors, Zhujiang New Town in Tianhe is the easiest base — it holds the modern skyline hotels, the opera house and Canton Tower views, and is well connected by metro. For old-world charm choose leafy Shamian Island, and if you are here for the Canton Fair, stay near the Canton Fair Complex in Pazhou to cut the commute.

Is Guangzhou walkable?

Individual neighborhoods like Shamian Island, Zhujiang New Town and the old Liwan lanes are very walkable, but Guangzhou is a huge city and you will not cross it on foot. The metro is clean, cheap, extensive and signposted in English, so most travelers walk their local area and take the metro between districts.

When is the best time to visit Guangzhou?

Autumn, roughly October to December, is the most comfortable time — warm, drier days after the humid, rainy summer. Note that hotel prices spike during the twice-yearly Canton Fair (usually April and October), so if you are not attending the fair, book well ahead or travel just outside those weeks.
