A short history

Minnesota's lakes and forests have sustained Dakota and Ojibwe communities for millennia, with the Mississippi River's headwaters at Lake Itasca and rich fur-trade routes drawing French voyageurs in the 17th and 18th centuries. That legacy still echoes in place names, canoe routes and a modern paddling culture along the Boundary Waters and the northern border lakes.

Admitted to the Union in 1858 as the 32nd state, Minnesota grew on wheat milling, iron ore from the Mesabi Range, and waves of Scandinavian and German immigration that shaped its civic culture of cooperation and strong public institutions. Minneapolis and St. Paul grew together as the Twin Cities, a river-powered milling and rail hub that became a Midwestern centre of commerce and the arts.

Today Minnesota balances big-city sophistication with wilderness at its doorstep: the Twin Cities offer major museums, theatre and the Mall of America, while the north woods and ten thousand-plus lakes support a fierce outdoor culture that embraces every season, from summer canoeing to genuinely cold, celebrated winters.

Bodhi

Minneapolis and St. Paul grew up as rival twin cities on opposite banks of the Mississippi, and that friendly rivalry over milling, brewing and hockey still shapes how each city sees itself. Fort Snelling sits right at the river confluence and marks where the territory's earliest settlement took hold.

Nolie

Skip the Mall of America crowds and drive up the North Shore of Lake Superior in October, when the maples along the ridgeline turn and the roadside diners still have fresh walleye. Locals eat at the co-op cafes in Minneapolis, not the skyway chains downtown.

When to go

Summer and autumn for lakes; winter for the truly hardy.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Deeply cold with heavy snow; ice fishing, skiing and winter festivals define the season.

Spring (Mar–May)

A gradual thaw with lakes opening up by May, good for city sightseeing before the bugs arrive.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Warm and green, prime time for the Boundary Waters, North Shore and lake cabins.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Crisp air and brilliant colour along the North Shore before winter sets in.
