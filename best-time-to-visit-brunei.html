Best Time to Visit Brunei

Researched and compiled by the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Kampong Ayer, the water village on the Brunei River. Photo: Wikimedia Commons.

The short answer is February, March and April, and of those March is the driest month in our records. That is the whole of the seasonal advice for Brunei, and it is worth saying why it is so short: this is an equatorial country four degrees off the line, and the temperature simply does not have a season. Every month of the year averages an afternoon high between 83°F and 86°F. The only variable is how much rain falls on you while you are walking around in it.

The figures below are ten-year averages for 2015 to 2024, worked out from 3,653 daily NASA POWER records for each site, pulled for this page. Three honest notes on them. First, they are averages and not forecasts. Second, the rainfall column is the median of the ten years rather than the mean, and is labelled “typical” for that reason: single months swing violently here — July at the capital ran from 159 mm to 660 mm across our ten years — and a plain average would be pulled around by one freak year. Third, and we would rather say it than let you assume otherwise: Jerudong falls inside the same data cell as Bandar Seri Begawan, about ten miles away, so the two come back with identical numbers. We print one table and tell you it covers both, rather than two tables pretending to be separate measurements.

Season by season

The drier window — February to April

The best three months to come, and the only part of the year where the rain eases off enough to notice. March is the driest month in our ten years at both sites. April brings the warmest afternoons of the year at the capital along with the rain returning. Nothing here is dry in an absolute sense — you will still see heavy showers — but this is the window where a day of walking around Bandar Seri Begawan or a trip up the Temburong river is least likely to be rearranged by the weather.

Southwest monsoon — June to September

Steady, moderate rain, usually in bursts rather than all day, and the quietest stretch for visitors. Typical monthly totals sit between 250 mm and 290 mm at the capital. This is a good time for the rainforest: the rivers that carry you into Ulu Temburong run higher, and the forest is at its greenest. Take the showers as part of it.

The October peak — and the inter-monsoon

October is the wettest month in our figures at both Bandar Seri Begawan and Kuala Belait, a typical 384 mm at the capital. It is still a perfectly possible month to travel in, but plan indoor options into every day rather than hoping: the museums, the Royal Regalia building, the malls and food courts of Gadong, and a long lunch while it passes.

Northeast monsoon — November to January

The heaviest stretch of the year, with December and January the wettest of the monsoon proper and November the most reliably wet month of all — our ten years never saw it drop below 267 mm at the capital. Expect warm, humid, grey afternoons and real downpours. It is also the school-holiday and festival season for the region, so hotels and the coast road can be busier than the weather would suggest.

What the temperature does — which is almost nothing

Worth stating plainly, because it is the most useful single fact about Brunei’s climate: the difference between the warmest month and the coolest month at Bandar Seri Begawan is three degrees Fahrenheit. There is no cool season to wait for and no hot season to avoid. Pack for warm and humid every month of the year, add something for air-conditioning indoors, and choose your dates on rain and on holidays instead.

Brunei weather questions, answered

When is the best time to visit Brunei?

February, March and April — and of those March has the lowest rainfall in our ten years of records. Brunei sits four degrees north of the equator, so the temperature hardly changes: every month of the year averages a high between 83°F and 86°F (28°C to 30°C) in Bandar Seri Begawan. What changes is the rain, and the drier window from February to April is the only real seasonal choice you have to make.

Does Brunei have a dry season?

No, and any guide that gives you one is rounding. The driest month at the capital still has a typical 174 mm of rain — about seven inches — which in most of the world would be a wet month. What Brunei has is a wetter half and a less wet half: October to January are the heaviest, February to April the lightest. Rain here usually arrives as a heavy burst rather than a grey day, so a wet month does not mean a lost holiday.

Which is the wettest month?

October, in our figures, at both Bandar Seri Begawan and Kuala Belait — a typical 384 mm at the capital. December and January are close behind and are the proper northeast monsoon months. Note that single months swing hard from year to year here: July at the capital ranged from 159 mm to 660 mm across our ten years, which is why the tables below print the median of the ten rather than the average.

Is there a cyclone or typhoon season in Brunei?

Brunei lies close to the equator, where tropical cyclones are rare — it is well south of the track that crosses the Philippines and the South China Sea each year. Heavy monsoon rain, localised flooding and thunderstorms are the weather to plan around, not storms with names.

When are Ramadan and Hari Raya, and does it matter?

It matters more in Brunei than in most places, and we are deliberately not printing dates. Ramadan follows the lunar calendar and moves about eleven days earlier each year, so any date we printed would be wrong within a year or two. During the fasting month, restaurants keep different hours and visitors are expected not to eat or drink in public during daylight; hotels continue to serve their guests. Hari Raya that follows it is the biggest holiday of the year and fills hotels and roads. Check the dates for your year before you book, and if they fall in your window, book early rather than avoid it — it is an interesting time to be here.
