When to Go to Morocco

The Best Time to Visit Morocco

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the sweet spots. You get warm, comfortable days for Marrakech, Fes and the desert, without the fierce summer heat inland or the chilly nights of midwinter. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the heat and crowds, and the best timing for the imperial cities, the Sahara and the Atlantic coast.

Morocco is a country of many climates at once. On a single spring day you can breakfast in a warm Marrakech courtyard, drive up to snow on the High Atlas passes, and reach a mild Atlantic beach by evening. That variety is the key to timing a trip: the imperial cities and the desert are all about avoiding the summer furnace, while the coast is temperate almost year-round. Choose your dates well and everything is easier. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$$ Peak-ishWarm & greenBest all-roundBook early

Arguably the finest season: warm, comfortable days across Marrakech, Fes and the desert, wildflowers in the valleys, and lingering snow on the Atlas peaks. Everything is open and pleasant, from the souks to the dunes. The trade-offs are popularity and price — this is prime time, sharpest around Easter — and the odd hot, dusty spell inland by late May, so book ahead.

Summer — June to August

$ Split seasonHot inlandCoast shinesBeat the heat early

A season of two Moroccos. Marrakech, Fes and the Sahara turn fierce, with inland highs often 38–40°C and the desert best avoided at midday, which pushes prices and crowds down in the cities. Meanwhile the Atlantic coast — Agadir, Essaouira, Casablanca — stays gloriously mild and busy with holidaymakers. Sightsee at dawn inland, or simply head for the sea.

Autumn — September to November

$$$ ShoulderCooling downGreat valueWarm & clear

The mirror of spring, and a superb time to travel. The inland heat eases through September and October into ideal sightseeing weather, the desert becomes comfortable again, and the harvest light is lovely. Crowds and prices are gentler than spring until they climb toward the winter holidays, making October one of the best-value, best-weather months of the whole year.

Winter — December to February

$$ MixedCool daysCold nightsAtlas snow

Mild, sunny days in Marrakech and on the coast, but genuinely cold nights inland and in the mountains, with snow on the High Atlas and skiing at Oukaïmeden. It is a fine time for the cities and Atlantic winter sun, and the desert days are pleasant though the nights are freezing. The catch is the sharp Christmas–New Year peak in prices and crowds.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$$ ValueCoolestAtlas snow

The coolest month: mild, sunny days in Marrakech and on the coast, cold nights inland, and snow in the High Atlas for skiing. Fine for the cities and Atlantic winter sun by day, with the Christmas rush over. Pack warm layers for desert and mountain evenings, which can drop near freezing.

February

$$ ValueAlmond blossomCool & clear

Still cool but brightening, with almond blossom in the valleys and clear light for photography. Days are pleasant for city sightseeing and the coast, nights remain chilly inland and in the mountains. A quiet, good-value month before the spring crowds arrive.

March

$$$ ShoulderSpring beginsGreen valleys

Spring arrives: warm, comfortable days, green landscapes and wildflowers, and the desert pleasant by day. One of the best all-round months, with the Atlas still snow-capped as a backdrop. An excellent, well-rounded time to combine Marrakech, Fes and a desert trip.

April

$$$$ PeakIdeal weatherEaster spike

Prime time, with warm, sunny days perfect for everything from the souks to the dunes. Easter brings a short, sharp surge in prices and crowds, so book well ahead. The south is still comfortable and the coast is warming — one of the loveliest months to travel the whole country.

May

$$$ ShoulderWarmHeat building south

Warm and generally excellent, though Marrakech and the Sahara begin to heat up toward month’s end. Great for the imperial cities, the Atlas and the coast, with fewer crowds than April once Easter passes. Start desert and southern sightseeing early as the days lengthen and warm.

June

$$ Value inlandHot inlandCoast perfect

Summer proper inland: Marrakech and Fes are hot and the Sahara fierce, so temples of trade and tanneries are morning affairs. Prices ease in the cities as crowds thin. The Atlantic coast, by contrast, is at its mild, sunny best — the moment to lean toward Agadir and Essaouira.

July

$ Cheapest inlandVery hot inlandBeach peak

One of the hottest and cheapest months for the inland cities, with Marrakech and the desert baking past 40°C — suited to the heat-tolerant and early risers. The coast is the story now: Agadir and windswept Essaouira are perfect and busy with holidaymakers, so book beach hotels ahead.

August

$ Cheapest inlandPeak heatCoast busy

The other peak-heat month inland, with the same rules: sightsee at dawn, rest at midday, and lean on the coast. The Atlantic resorts are at their busiest with domestic and European holidaymakers, while Marrakech and Fes offer their lowest rates and emptiest sights for those who can take the heat.

September

$$$ Prime valueEasing heatGreat all-round

The heat loosens its grip, especially late in the month, making the cities and the desert a pleasure again while the coast stays warm. Crowds and prices are still gentle before the autumn climb. A smart-value month for combining Marrakech, Fes and a Sahara excursion in comfort.

October

$$$ Prime valueWarm & comfortableSweet spot

Arguably the best all-round month: warm, comfortable weather across the whole country, ideal desert conditions, and crowds and prices below the spring and holiday peaks. Everything is open and pleasant, from the imperial cities to the dunes. Book ahead — plenty of travellers have the same idea.

November

$$$ ShoulderCool & clearDesert ideal

Cooling and clear, with comfortable city days, crisp desert nights and the first rains in the north. Excellent for Marrakech, Fes and the Sahara, and still mild on the coast. One of the calmer, better-value months before the December holidays bring the winter rush.

December

$$ → $$$$ SplitCoolChristmas peak

A month of two halves. Early December is cool, clear and reasonably priced, fine for the cities and the coast, with snow gathering on the Atlas. The Christmas–New Year fortnight is the sharpest peak of the year — lovely weather in Marrakech but packed riads and top rates — so book far ahead or aim for the calmer first half.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern splits between the interior and the coast.

High summer inland, and midwinter away from the holidays

$ Rock bottomJul–Aug in the citiesBeat the heat early

For Marrakech, Fes and the desert, July and August are the cheapest weeks, when the inland heat thins the crowds and hotels and riads drop their rates. The catch is the temperature, so build early starts and shaded midday breaks into the plan. Midwinter, apart from the Christmas–New Year peak, is the other value window for the cities and Atlantic sun.

Want reasonable weather and a deal? Target the shoulder edges: late September to October and March. You get comfortable sightseeing and a pleasant desert at well below spring-peak and holiday prices. As a rule, midweek flights beat weekends, and booking a desert tour or a riad a few weeks out lands the best rooms and rates.

Beating the Heat & the Crowds

Morocco's busy and brutal periods are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the heat to sidestep

Christmas & NYEasterJun–Aug inlandRamadan rhythm

The Christmas–New Year fortnight and Easter are the crowd-and-price crunches in the cities and the desert. On the weather side, June to August in Marrakech, Fes and the Sahara is genuinely punishing at midday. During Ramadan the daytime pace slows and some cafés keep shorter hours, though sights and resorts run as normal. None of these need ruin a trip — they just reward planning.

To have the great sights closer to yourself, come in the shoulder months — October to November and March — and start early wherever you are: the souks, the tanneries and the desert are coolest and quietest in the morning. Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to Morocco.

What is the best month to visit Morocco?

April, May, October and November are the strongest all-round months. Days are warm and comfortable rather than scorching, evenings are pleasant, and everything from Marrakech and Fes to the Sahara and the Atlas is enjoyable. April and October in particular hit the sweet spot of good weather, open sights and crowds and prices below the sharp holiday peaks.

What is the cheapest time to visit Morocco?

For the inland cities and the desert, high summer — roughly July and August — is cheapest, because the heat in Marrakech, Fes and the Sahara thins the crowds. Midwinter is also good value away from the Christmas and New Year peak. The Atlantic coast at Agadir and Essaouira is the exception, staying busy and mild through summer, though shoulder months there still beat peak-season rates.

When should I avoid Morocco because of heat or crowds?

Avoid Marrakech, Fes and especially the Sahara at midday in June, July and August, when inland highs regularly top 38 to 40°C. For crowds and the highest prices, the Christmas–New Year fortnight and the Easter period are the sharp peaks in the cities and the desert. During Ramadan the daytime rhythm changes, though tourist sites and resorts keep running.

Is the Atlantic coast good to visit year-round?

Largely yes. Agadir, Essaouira and Casablanca enjoy mild, temperate weather almost all year, cooled by the Atlantic. Agadir is the sunniest and best for a winter-sun beach break, while breezy Essaouira is a summer favourite for windsurfers and anyone escaping the inland heat. Sea temperatures stay cool year-round, so the coast is more about sunshine and sea air than warm swimming.

What about visiting Morocco during Ramadan?

Morocco is welcoming during Ramadan, but the rhythm changes: some cafés and smaller restaurants keep shorter daytime hours, the pace slows during the day, and towns come alive after the sunset iftar. The dates shift about eleven days earlier each year on the Western calendar. Tourist sites, riads, tour operators and coastal resorts continue to operate; just plan meals and timings with a little extra flexibility.
