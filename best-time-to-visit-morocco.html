When to Go to Morocco

The Best Time to Visit Morocco

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: the shoulder seasons — roughly March–May and September–November — are the sweet spot. You get warm, dry days ideal for the cities, the desert and the mountains, without the fierce summer heat inland. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the heat and crowds, and the best timing for Marrakech, the Sahara, the Atlas and the coast.

Morocco is a year-round destination, but its geography — desert, mountains and two coasts — means the weather you meet depends as much on where you go as when. A golden April in Marrakech, with roses in the gardens and snow still on the Atlas, is a different country from Marrakech in August, when the afternoon heat drives everyone to the pool. Choosing your dates and your route together is the trick. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$$ Prime shoulderWarm & greenBest all-roundBook ahead

Many travelers' favorite season. Days are warm and comfortable across the cities, the Sahara and the coast, wildflowers and roses are out, and the Atlas still wears its snow. Everything is open and at its best — which also makes it popular, so the top riads book up and prices firm. Watch for the occasional hot, dusty chergui wind.

Summer — June to August

$ Low inlandHot inlandCoast & cheapEarly starts

Inland is fierce: Marrakech and Fez regularly top 38–42°C, and the desert is punishing by day, so sightseeing is a dawn-and-dusk affair with the pool in between. The upside is low inland prices and, crucially, the Atlantic coast — Essaouira and Agadir stay breezy and mild, and Moroccan families flock there. Plan a coast-heavy summer trip.

Autumn — September to November

$$$ Prime shoulderWarm & settledGreat value earlyIdeal desert

The mirror of spring, and just as good. The summer heat eases through September into comfortable, settled weather perfect for the imperial cities and the desert, while the sea stays warm on the coast. Crowds thin after the European summer and prices soften early in the season before firming toward the holidays. A superb all-round time to travel.

Winter — December to February

$$ ValueMild daysCold nightsAtlas snow

An underrated season and good value outside the holidays. City and coastal days are mild and often sunny (mid-teens to low 20s°C), though desert and mountain nights are cold and the High Atlas is snowy — you can even ski at Oukaïmeden. Pack warm layers for evenings. Sidestep the Christmas–New Year spike and it's a quiet, affordable time for Marrakech and Fez.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$$ ValueMild days, cold nightsSki season

Cool and often sunny by day in Marrakech and on the coast, cold after dark and in the desert, with snow and skiing in the High Atlas. Quiet and good value once the New Year holiday passes. A fine month for the cities if you pack for chilly evenings.

February

$$ ValueAlmond blossomCool

Similar to January but with the almond blossom coming out and the first hint of spring. Still low season and low prices, still cold at night in the south and mountains. A quiet, affordable time for Marrakech, Fez and the coast.

March

$$$ Shoulder beginsWarmingGreen

Spring arrives: warm, pleasant days, green landscapes and comfortable sightseeing, with the Atlas still snow-capped. One of the loveliest and best-value windows before the Easter surge. Excellent for combining the cities with the desert.

April

$$$ PrimeWarm & brightEaster peak

Arguably the best all-round month: warm, dry and beautiful everywhere. The catch is Easter, which brings a short, sharp surge in crowds and rates. Outside the holiday, superb weather and mood for the whole country.

May

$$$ PrimeWarm & settledEverything open

Warm, stable and gorgeous, with the desert still comfortable and the coast warming. One of the top months for a full Marrakech–desert–Atlas circuit. Popular, so book riads ahead, but well worth it.

June

$$ Value buildsHot inlandCoast great

The heat builds inland toward summer levels, so plan early starts in Marrakech and the south. The coast is lovely and the crowds are lighter than the shoulder peaks, so early June can be a real sweet spot for value and weather combined.

July

$ Cheapest inlandVery hotCoast & pools

Hot and cheap inland; the cities are best at dawn and after dark, with the pool for the middle of the day. This is the coast's moment — Essaouira and Agadir are cool and busy with Moroccan holidaymakers. Skip the desert midday.

August

$ Cheapest inlandPeak heatCoast busy

The other peak-heat, low-price inland month; same playbook — dawn sightseeing, midday shade and pools, and lean on the coast. The Atlantic resorts are at their busiest with local holidays. Doable inland if you plan around the heat.

September

$$$ PrimeEasing heatWarm seas

The heat loosens, especially late in the month, while the sea stays warm — a superb time for the cities, the desert and the coast alike. Crowds thin after the European summer and prices are still soft early on. One of the best all-round months.

October

$$$ Prime valueWarm & settledSweet spot

Warm, comfortable and settled from the cities to the Sahara, with the coast still swimmable. Everything is open and pleasant and the light is beautiful. A real sweet spot for weather and value — book ahead, as many travelers agree.

November

$$$ ShoulderMild & clearPrime desert

Mild, clear and lovely, and one of the best months for the desert, with warm days and cool nights. Cooler in the cities and mountains as winter approaches. Great value before the Christmas rush and ideal for a cities-and-Sahara trip.

December

A month of two halves. Early December is cool, quiet and reasonable, good for city breaks. The two weeks around Christmas and New Year are a sharp peak — Marrakech is a festive-season favorite — so book far ahead or aim for the calmer first fortnight.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear once you split inland from the coast.

High summer inland, and mid-winter — outside the holidays

$ Rock bottomJul–Aug inlandDeep winterSkip Christmas fortnight

Riads and city hotels in Marrakech and Fez are cheapest in July and August, when the heat thins the crowds, and again in deep winter (January–February) — with the carve-out being the roughly two-week Christmas–New Year holiday, when Marrakech prices jump. The trade-off in summer is the temperature; in winter, cold nights.

Want comfortable weather and a deal? Target the shoulder edges: March and late October to November, avoiding Easter and the Christmas fortnight. As a rule, midweek flights beat weekends, and booking sought-after riads two to three months out lands the best rooms and rates.

Beating the Heat & the Crowds

Morocco's busy and brutal periods are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks and the heat to sidestep

Christmas & NYEasterJul–Aug inlandChergui wind

The Christmas–New Year fortnight and Easter are the crowd-and-price peaks, especially in Marrakech. On the weather side, July and August inland — Marrakech, Fez and the Sahara — is genuinely hot, and a chergui (a hot, dusty desert wind) can blow up for a day or two, mostly in spring and summer. None of these need spoil a trip; they just reward planning.

To have the sights and souks a little quieter, come in the shoulder months — March, late October and November — and start early wherever you are. For where to base yourself, see our guide to where to stay in Marrakech.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to Morocco.

What is the best month to visit Morocco?

April, May, October and November are the strongest all-round months. You get warm, dry days that are comfortable for the imperial cities, the Sahara and the Atlas, with cooler evenings and thinner crowds than the summer and holiday peaks. March and early June are close behind.

What is the cheapest time to visit Morocco?

High summer (July and August) is cheapest inland in Marrakech and Fez, because the heat is intense, and mid-winter (excluding the Christmas–New Year holidays) is also good value. The most expensive periods are the spring and autumn shoulder seasons and the Christmas–New Year and Easter holidays, when the best riads book up.

When should I avoid Morocco because of heat or crowds?

Avoid Marrakech, Fez and the Sahara in July and August, when inland highs regularly top 38–42°C and the desert is punishing by day. The Christmas–New Year fortnight and Easter are the busiest and priciest windows. Spring can occasionally bring a hot, dusty chergui wind for a day or two.

When is the best time for the Sahara desert?

October to April is the desert season — warm days and cool-to-cold nights, ideal for camel treks and desert camps at Merzouga and Zagora. Avoid June to August, when daytime desert heat is extreme. Winter nights can be genuinely cold, so pack warm layers even if the days are sunny.

Can you ski in Morocco?

Yes. The High Atlas resort of Oukaïmeden, about a two-hour drive from Marrakech, usually has snow and lifts running from roughly January to March. It's a novelty rather than a world-class ski destination, but you can genuinely ski in the morning and be back among Marrakech's palms by evening.
