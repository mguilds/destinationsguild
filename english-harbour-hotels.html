English Harbour

Where to Stay in English Harbour

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

English Harbour is the most interesting place to sleep in Antigua and the one least like the rest of the Caribbean. It is a deep, almost landlocked inlet on the south coast that the Royal Navy used as its hurricane shelter, and the Georgian dockyard it built round the head of the water is still a working yard — chandlers, sail lofts, crews and all — inscribed by UNESCO in 2016. Over the hill is Falmouth Harbour, where the big charter yachts lie. Between the two you have the best restaurants on the island, the liveliest evenings outside Carnival, and the view from Shirley Heights above. What you do not have is a long beach: the sand here comes in small pockets.

The areas, and which one suits you

Several distinct places go under this name, and they are not interchangeable.

Nelson’s Dockyard

The restored Georgian yard at the head of the harbour: stone pillars, the Admiral’s House, the museum, and yachts tied up where men-of-war once were. A handful of places to stay are inside or on the edge of the dockyard itself, which is a genuinely unusual thing to be able to do.

Falmouth Harbour

Over the low hill to the west, a wider bay and the island’s superyacht quay, busy from December to April. More apartments and self-catering here than hotels, several good restaurants round the water, and a short walk or a very short drive to the dockyard.

Shirley Heights and the ridge

The high ground between the two harbours, with the old gun batteries, the lookout and the finest view in Antigua. A few villas and apartments up here trade a beach for that view and a breeze; you will want a car.

Pigeon Point and the small beaches

The nearest proper swimming to the harbours is Pigeon Point, a short, calm, sheltered beach on the Falmouth side, with Galleon Beach across the water from the dockyard. Both are small. If a long stretch of sand is the point of the holiday, stay in the north-west instead.

What English Harbour is not

It is not a beach resort and it is not quiet in high season. It is a harbour town with a sailing season, which means a lively winter and a very sleepy September. It is also hilly in places, in a way most of Antigua is not.

Getting about

St John’s is about forty-five minutes by road and the airport about forty. Minibuses run to the capital from Falmouth. Taxi fares are set by zone — agree the figure first. A car is more useful here than anywhere else on the island, both for the beaches and for getting up to the ridge.

What your money is worth in Antigua
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Antigua and Barbuda uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged to the US dollar at EC$2.70 — a rate that has held for many years, so a price of EC$27 is about ten US dollars and no app is needed. US dollars are taken almost everywhere, but change often comes back in Eastern Caribbean dollars and paying in local money is usually very slightly better. Cards are fine in hotels, supermarkets and the larger restaurants; keep cash for minibuses, beach bars and the market. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills, so the final total sits above the headline rate — ask what is included when you book, and at an all-inclusive ask exactly what the word covers here, because it varies a great deal from one resort to the next.

Admiral’s Inn

Inside Nelson’s DockyardGeorgian buildingHistoric

The inn’s own website is brief, and we repeat only what it states: that it occupies a secluded section of historic Nelson’s Dockyard at English Harbour on the south coast of Antigua, that it comprises three buildings including a three-storey Georgian brick structure, and that it guarantees its lowest rate to guests who book direct. The site gives its name inconsistently, as both Admiral’s Inn and Admirals’ Inn. It publishes no room count, no restaurant detail and no rates on the page we were able to read, so ask for all three when you enquire. Staying inside the dockyard walls is the attraction and it is a real one.

South Point Antigua

Falmouth / English HarbourSelf-catering suitesNot a hotel

Worth knowing about, and worth being clear about what it is: South Point describes itself not as a hotel but as ocean suites and vacation rentals — privately owned waterfront self-catering suites let for short stays, at Yacht Club Drive, English Harbour, in the parish of Saint Paul. Its own site calls it “a refreshing alternative to the traditional hotel experience” and “just a handful of luxury suites”, in two types: a one-bedroom suite sleeping two and a two-bedroom penthouse sleeping four, each with a full kitchen. There is one restaurant on site, MAIA, open for dinner daily. No total unit count is published. If you want daily housekeeping and a front desk, this is not that; if you want a kitchen and the harbour outside the window, it is.

Travelling in your later years

An honest assessment of this corner of the island.

English Harbour is the one part of Antigua where we would ask you to think about the ground. The harbours themselves are flat and walkable, and the dockyard is level and well surfaced, but the land either side rises quickly — the road up to Shirley Heights is steep, and some of the apartments and villas on the slopes involve steps and a climb. If stairs are a problem, stay down by the water and ask the property how many there are before you book.

What you get in return is the most rewarding base on the island: a World Heritage site you can walk round in an afternoon, a dozen good restaurants within a short taxi ride, something to watch on the water every day of the sailing season, and evenings with some life in them. For a traveller who would rather look at boats and buildings than lie on sand, nothing else in Antigua comes close.

Two practical notes. The beaches here are small, so if swimming every morning matters, check how far your room actually is from Pigeon Point or Galleon Beach. And the hospital and the airport are both about forty minutes away, which is further than from the northern coasts — not a problem, but worth knowing.

Questions about staying in English Harbour

Is English Harbour worth staying in rather than visiting?

If the history and the boats interest you at all, yes. Most visitors see the dockyard for two hours off a tour bus or a ship and leave. Staying here gives you the harbour in the early morning before anyone arrives, dinner at a restaurant that is not a hotel dining room, and the walk up to Shirley Heights at the right hour. It is the one part of Antigua with a life of its own.

What about the beach?

Modest, and that is the honest answer. Pigeon Point on the Falmouth side is a short, calm, sheltered beach, and Galleon Beach sits across the water from the dockyard. Both are pleasant and both are small. If you want to walk out of your room onto a long stretch of sand, stay at Dickenson Bay and come here for a day.

Is it busy?

It has two quite different seasons. From December to April the harbours are full of yachts, the restaurants are busy and there is something happening most evenings, and around Sailing Week in late April it is extremely busy and rooms are scarce and dear. In September much of it closes and the place is almost asleep.

Do I need a car?

More here than anywhere else on the island. The two harbours are walkable from each other if you do not mind a hill, and minibuses run to St John’s, but the beaches, the ridge and the restaurants are spread out and taxis add up over a week. A car for the stay is the easier answer.

Can I stay inside Nelson’s Dockyard?

Yes — a small number of properties occupy the historic buildings in and around the yard itself, which is an unusual thing to be able to do at a World Heritage site. They are small, they book early, and they are not large modern resorts. If that appeals, enquire well ahead and ask specifically what the room includes.

Is Shirley Heights worth the trip up?

It is the best view in Antigua: both harbours below you, the south coast running away east and west, and Montserrat and Guadeloupe on the horizon on a clear day. There is a long-running barbecue with live music up there on Sunday evenings. Check the current days and the entry charge before you set out, because both have changed over the years.
