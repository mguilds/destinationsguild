Best Time to Visit Panama

Panama has two seasons and two coasts, and they do not line up. This is the month-by-month picture, including the weeks when the wettest place in the country is also the brightest.

Month by month

The temperature barely moves at sea level — it is hot every month of the year. What changes is the rain, the price, and which coast is having the better week.

January

Dry season at its most dependable on the Pacific side: sun, a steady breeze off the water and low humidity by Panamanian standards. Peak prices in the capital and on the Pacific beaches. Bocas del Toro, on the Caribbean side, can be wetter than the capital this month.

February

The driest, brightest stretch of the year, and the month of Carnival — four days before Ash Wednesday, with the biggest celebrations in Las Tablas on the Azuero peninsula. Rooms there go months ahead. The best chance all year of a clear sunrise from Barú.

March

Hot, dry and very good, with the Carnival crowd gone and the sea calm on the Pacific. Prime diving and fishing weather. Boquete is dry and breezy. One of the better all-round months if you want the whole country at once.

April

The dry season ends during this month, usually in the second half, and the first heavy afternoon showers arrive. Rates come off their peak before the rain is properly established, which is what makes late April one of our two picks.

May

The green season begins in earnest: hot, humid, with a downpour most afternoons that clears by evening. Everything turns green, the rivers and waterfalls fill, and rooms are noticeably cheaper. Humpback whales begin arriving in Pacific waters.

June

Wet and warm, and the start of the best whale-watching months in the Gulf of Chiriquí and around the Pearl Islands. No hurricanes reach Panama, which separates this green season from the Caribbean’s. Good value everywhere.

July

Often a short drier spell in the middle of the wet season — locally called the veranillo, the little summer — though it cannot be relied on. Whales are offshore. Bocas del Toro is frequently at its best while the Pacific side is wet.

August

Wet, green and quiet, with low rates and few visitors. Rain comes in heavy afternoon bursts rather than all day. Surf is up on the Pacific breaks. A good month for coffee country, where the cloud forest is at its most dramatic.

September

The wettest stretch on the Pacific side — and, in the same weeks, often the calmest and clearest on the Caribbean side, which makes it a strong month for Bocas del Toro and the San Blas. Whale season is ending. The lowest prices of the year.

October

Much like September: very wet in the capital and the west, frequently fine in Bocas. Independence celebrations begin to build towards November. Cheapest flights and rooms, and the fewest other visitors you will ever share the canal with.

November

The wettest month in many places, and also the national one: a run of independence and patriotic holidays fills the first half with parades and drum bands. Panama City is busy with domestic travel around them. The rain begins to ease late in the month.

December

The season turns, usually around the middle of the month: the rain stops, the trade winds pick up and the dry season begins. The second half is festive, expensive and busy, with Panamanian families on the move and the beaches full.

The dry season and the green season

Dry: mid-December to mid-April

Panamanians call it verano, summer, although it falls in the northern winter. Trade winds blow, humidity drops, the Pacific flattens and the sun is dependable. This is the high season: the capital’s hotels are full of business as well as holiday traffic, the beach resorts west of the city are busy at weekends, and prices are at their highest around Christmas, New Year and Carnival.

Green: May to November

Hot and humid, with cloud building through the day and rain most afternoons — often torrential for an hour, then over. Mornings are frequently clear, which is why a green-season trip works if you do your sightseeing early. Rates drop sharply, the forest is at its best, and no hurricane is going to arrive.

The two coasts do not agree

This is the thing most visitors do not know. The Caribbean side keeps its own calendar: wetter across the year, less sharply seasonal, and with a reliable-ish bright spell in September and October, exactly when the Pacific side is at its wettest. If your trip is built around Bocas del Toro or the San Blas, the Pacific calendar is the wrong one to plan by.

Events worth planning around

Carnival — the four days before Ash Wednesday

Moveable, tied to Easter. Las Tablas on the Azuero peninsula holds the famous one, split between two rival sides of the town, with parades, water trucks and all-night music. The country effectively closes. Book early or plan around it, but do not plan to turn up and find a room.

The independence season — early November

A dense run of national holidays through the first half of November, with parades, drum corps and school bands in every town. It is the best time to see the country being itself, and the worst time to need a bus seat or a hotel room at short notice.

Whale season — June to October

Humpbacks in Pacific waters, with the peak in the middle of that window. Boats go from the Pearl Islands, Boca Chica and Santa Catalina.

The coffee harvest — roughly November to March

Boquete’s farms are picking and processing through the dry season, which is when a farm tour shows you the whole chain rather than an empty mill.

Travelling in your later years

If heat and humidity are hard on you, the dry season is the easier half of the year and January is its gentlest month. Better still, go up: Boquete at about 1,200 metres is cool enough to want a jumper after dark, and it is the one part of Panama where the climate asks nothing of you.

In the green season, plan the day around the morning. Tours, the canal and anything outdoors are best before noon; the afternoon downpour is a good reason to be somewhere with a roof. Cobbles in Casco Viejo become slick in the rain, and the small boats to the islands are rougher and wetter in the wet months, which matters more than the rain itself if you are unsteady boarding.

The weeks we would pick

Late April. The dry season has just broken, the heavy rain is not yet established, the high-season rates have come off and the country is green again. Our first choice for value against reliability.

March, if the budget allows. The most dependable weather of the year without the Carnival and Christmas peaks.

September or October, for Bocas del Toro. Counter-intuitive and correct: the Caribbean side is frequently calm and clear while the Pacific is at its wettest, and these are the cheapest weeks of the year. Build the trip around the islands rather than the capital.

Questions about when to visit Panama

What is the single best month to visit Panama?

If you want one answer: March. The dry season is well established, the Carnival rush is over, the Pacific is calm for diving and fishing, and the highlands are clear. It is not the cheapest month — that is September or October — but it is the one that asks the least of your luck.

Is the green season actually a bad time to go?

No, and it is badly named in English. Rain in the green season usually means a heavy downpour in the afternoon that clears, not a grey day. Mornings are frequently bright. What you get in exchange is lower rates, green landscape, full waterfalls, fewer people and, from June, whales. What you lose is reliability: a wet week can be genuinely wet.

Does Panama have a hurricane season?

Not in any practical sense. Panama lies too far south for hurricanes to form over it or track across it, which is why it is treated differently from the islands to the north. Heavy rain, thunderstorms and rough seas on the Caribbean coast are the real weather risks here, not a named storm.

When can I see whales?

Humpbacks are in Panama’s Pacific waters from about June to October, with the heart of the season in July, August and September. They come from both hemispheres, which is part of what makes the Gulf of Chiriquí and the Pearl Islands notable. Trips go from Santa Catalina, Boca Chica and the islands off Panama City.

When is Carnival in Panama?

The four days before Ash Wednesday, so the dates move with Easter each year. The largest celebration is not in the capital but in Las Tablas, on the Azuero peninsula, where the rivalry between two sides of the town is the whole point. Accommodation anywhere near it is booked far ahead and the country largely stops.

Is the weather the same across the whole country?

No, and planning as though it is will cost you. The Pacific side has a clear dry season and a clear wet one. The Caribbean side — Bocas del Toro, Colón, Guna Yala — is wetter overall, less seasonal, and often at its best in September and October when the Pacific is at its worst. The highlands around Boquete are cool and misty most of the year.
