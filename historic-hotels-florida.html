Historic Hotels of Florida

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

12 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 12 in Florida, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1897

Belleview Inn

Belleair, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 2018

Railroad magnate Henry B. Plant built the Belleview Hotel near Clearwater to fill seats on his new lines, opening it in 1897 as Florida's largest wooden building, with peaked gables, wide verandas and its own orchestra. His son Morton painted it white, earning it the name "White Queen of the Gulf," and added Donald Ross golf courses. As the Belleview-Biltmore it drew Babe Ruth, Thomas Edison and Henry Ford. Saved from demolition, the historic core was rotated a quarter turn and shifted 300 feet.

Close by

  • Clearwater Beach's white sand
  • Clearwater Marine Aquarium, home of Winter and Hope
  • Caladesi Island State Park's mangroves

Est. 1913

The Gasparilla Inn

Boca Grande, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 2007

Peter Bradley, who headed the American Agricultural Chemical Company, built this inn to draw passengers to his phosphate railroad, the Charlotte Harbor and Northern. Work began in 1910, stalled when the first plans proved too small, and resumed under architect Francis J. Kennard, who framed it in wood with Queen Anne and Shingle-style touches recalling New England seaside resorts. It opened in 1913. Barron Collier later bought it, and in 1961 a local group headed by Bayard Sharp took over. Anglers founded the Pelican Club here.

Close by

  • Port Boca Grande Lighthouse & Museum, built 1890
  • Banyan Street's century-old banyan trees
  • Boca Grande's bike path along the old railroad

Est. 1925

The Vinoy Resort & Golf Club, Autograph Collection

St. Petersburg, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 1991

Pennsylvania businessman Aymer Vinoy Laughner won a bet with golfer Walter Hagen and spent part of the winnings on land overlooking Tampa Bay. Architect Henry L. Taylor and builder George A. Miller raised the Mediterranean Revival Vinoy Park Hotel in a record ten months, opening it in 1925; Babe Ruth and Calvin Coolidge stayed. In 1942 it was leased to the Army Air Force as barracks. By the 1970s it was a cut-rate boarding house, and it closed in 1975. Restored and enlarged, it reopened in 1992.

Close by

  • The Dalí Museum on the waterfront
  • Chihuly Collection at the Morean Arts Center
  • St. Petersburg's Museum of Fine Arts

Est. 1926

Colony Hotel & Cabana Club

Delray Beach, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 1999

A group around Albert T. Repp spent some $350,000 on this Mediterranean Revival hotel, opened in 1926 as the Alterep during Delray Beach's boom years, but it soon closed. Newlyweds George and Agnes Boughton, stopping in town on their honeymoon trip, found it for sale and, with George's father Charlie, bought it for $50,000, renaming it The Colony. George later added the oceanfront Cabana Club and bought a Kennebunkport, Maine resort he also called The Colony. The 1926 switchboard and staff-run elevator survive.

Close by

  • Atlantic Avenue's cafés and galleries
  • Morikami Museum and Japanese Gardens
  • Wakodahatchee Wetlands boardwalk

Est. 1926

La Concha Key West, Autograph Collection

Key West, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 1991

Developer Carl Aubuchon, partnering with Jefferson B. Browne's Florida Keys Realty Company, opened this six-story Duval Street hotel in 1926 as Key West's first upscale lodging, with marble floors, private baths, an electric elevator and nearly fireproof steel beams. Rooms cost three dollars a night. The Depression and the 1935 hurricane emptied it, but guests returned after World War II. Tennessee Williams stayed here while finishing A Streetcar Named Desire, Ernest Hemingway began To Have and Have Not here, and Harry Truman visited.

Close by

  • The Ernest Hemingway Home & Museum
  • Sunset Celebration at Mallory Square
  • Sloppy Joe's, a Hemingway haunt

Est. 1928

Casa Faena

Miami Beach, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 2015

Architect Martin L. Hampton, a South Carolinian who settled in Miami in 1914 after traveling widely in Spain, designed this Collins Avenue building, which opened in 1928 as El Paraiso Apartments: 18 flats plus two hotel rooms. Hampton, who also drew Miami Beach City Hall, gave it a Mediterranean Revival look. During World War II the U.S. Army Air Forces leased it, like many Miami Beach buildings, and it later shifted to lodging before being restored as Casa Faena.

Close by

  • The Miami Beach Boardwalk along the Atlantic
  • The Faena Theater's cabaret shows
  • Wynwood Walls street murals

Est. 1928

The Don CeSar

St. Pete Beach, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 1989

Thomas Rowe bought 80 acres on the beach in 1924 and began building his "Pink Palace" a year later, naming it for the gallant Don Cesar in the light opera Maritana. It opened in 1928 three times over budget, drawing F. Scott Fitzgerald, Lou Gehrig and Al Capone, and the New York Yankees' spring training deal helped it through the Depression. The Army bought it in 1942 as a hospital for airmen. After a "Save the Don" campaign, William Bowman restored it from 1972 and reopened it a year later.

Close by

  • Pass-a-Grille Beach and the Merry Pier
  • Gulf Beaches Historical Museum
  • Sunken Gardens in St. Petersburg

Est. 1935

The Savoy Hotel & Beach Club

Miami Beach, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 2020

Opened in 1935 as the Savoy Plaza at Ocean Drive's south end, this hotel came from the wave of smaller Art Deco buildings that rose on Miami Beach after the 1926 hurricane and the stock market crash. Architect V.M. Neillenbogen gave it a modest, streamlined Deco look. As many neighbors closed over the decades, the Savoy carried on, and in 2012 it was joined to the next-door Hotel Arlington, a 1930s building by architect Albert Anis, to make one property.

Close by

  • Art Deco walking tours of Miami Beach
  • Española Way, the 1925 artists' colony
  • Vizcaya Museum & Gardens

Est. 1937

The Lodge at Wakulla Springs

Wakulla Springs, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 2016

Financier Edward Ball, brother-in-law and adviser to Alfred I. du Pont, bought 4,000 acres around Wakulla Springs in 1931 and opened this Mediterranean Revival lodge in 1937, with heart cypress, a hand-painted lobby ceiling and a marble soda fountain counter. In 1939 he hired swimming coach Newt Perry as manager, and Hollywood followed: Tarzan's Secret Treasure with Johnny Weissmuller, scenes from Creature from the Black Lagoon, and Airport '77's underwater shots. After Ball died in 1981, the lodge passed to the State of Florida.

Close by

  • Jungle cruises on the Wakulla River springs
  • Mission San Luis de Apalachee in Tallahassee
  • The Tallahassee Museum's nature trails

Est. 1939

The National Hotel

Miami Beach, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 2010

Architect Roy France, a Midwesterner who moved to Miami Beach after a 1931 Florida trip and went on to shape much of its skyline, designed this 14-story Art Deco tower, built in 1939 and opened in 1940. His guiding idea: give guests sunlight and sea air. Socialites and celebrities came in the 1950s, and South Beach revivalists in the 1970s. A tile mosaic in the restaurant copies Tamara de Lempicka's 1920s painting "Girl with Gloves," and a marble compass by the pool points toward Cuba's National Hotel.

Close by

  • Lincoln Road's shops and cafés
  • The Bass contemporary art museum
  • Ocean Drive's Art Deco strip

Est. 1964

Mission Resort + Club

Howey-in-the-Hills, Florida · Historic Hotels member since 2023

Citrus grower William J. Howey bought about 60,000 acres near Orlando in 1916, founded Howey-in-the-Hills as his own town, and built the Chain-O-Lakes Country Club, its course laid out by Chicago designer George O'Neil. The Depression and a hard freeze left it run down. In 1964 Nick Beucher, a former Morton Salt salesman turned livestock man, spotted the club for sale in the Wall Street Journal and bought it. Drawing on a 1,400-mile horseback ride across Mexico in 1939, he opened the Spanish Colonial-style Mission Inn in 1970.

Close by

  • The Howey Mansion, built 1925 to 1927
  • Lakeside Mount Dora and its galleries
  • Lakeridge Winery & Vineyards

Est.

Casa Monica Resort & Spa

St. Augustine, Florida

Boston abolitionist Franklin W. Smith, an early champion of poured concrete, built this Moorish Revival hotel and opened it as the Casa Monica on New Year's Day 1888. Within four months he sold it to Henry Flagler for $325,000, who renamed it the Cordova and in 1902 bridged it to his Hotel Alcazar. The Depression closed it in 1932. In 1968 it became the St. Johns County Courthouse, and in 1997 Richard C. Kessler bought it and brought it back as a hotel.

Close by

  • The Lightner Museum in the old Hotel Alcazar
  • Flagler College, once the Ponce de Leon Hotel
  • Fountain of Youth Archaeological Park
