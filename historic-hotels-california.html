Historic Hotels of California

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

36 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 36 in California, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1875

Palace Hotel

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2010

No hotel on earth was bigger than the Palace when it opened in 1875, and nothing in San Francisco had been so grand. The 1906 earthquake destroyed it, and it was rebuilt and reopened in 1909. Its Garden Court sits under a glass ceiling of more than 72,000 pieces of glass. For the reopening the hotel commissioned Maxfield Parrish's painting The Pied Piper of Hamelin, in which the artist painted himself as the Piper. Green Goddess dressing was created here in 1923.

Est. 1876

The Mission Inn Hotel & Spa

Riverside, California · Historic Hotels member since 1996

It began as Glenwood Cottage, a two-story adobe boarding house kept by civil engineer Christopher Columbus Miller, who came to Riverside to build its water system. His son Frank Miller bought it for $5,000 in 1880 and spent decades enlarging it, until by 1931 the inn filled an entire city block, with domes, a bell tower, flying buttresses and an open-air rotunda. Miller's daughter Allis Hutchings ran it after his death until 1956. It is a National Historic Landmark.

Est. 1884

Napa River Inn

Napa, California · Historic Hotels member since 2004

Captain Albert Hatt built a grain mill and warehouse on the Napa River in 1884, storing farm goods and wine for local vineyards. The Hatt Building anchored downtown Napa for generations and now holds the inn, with a Victorian-style lobby of tall columns, a brass chandelier and a fireplace with a brass grate. Staff and guests say Hatt's son Albert Jr. and his wife Margaret still walk the halls. Its spa now occupies what was once the town police station.

Est. 1885

Casa Madrona Hotel & Spa

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2024

Local businessman William G. Barrett built this Sausalito house in 1885, mixing Victorian lines with the porticos and verandas of a Tuscan villa. After the Barretts moved out in 1906 it became a way station for travelers, and it has welcomed guests ever since. Pink Floyd stayed here on their first American tour in 1967, and Dick Van Dyke is among the names in its guest book.

Est. 1888

Hotel del Coronado

San Diego, California · Historic Hotels member since 2007

Elisha Babcock and H.L. Story built The Del in 1888, dreaming of a beach resort people across the West would talk about. The Queen Anne hotel with its red-roofed turret is one of the largest wooden buildings in America and a National Historic Landmark. Its Crown Room has a 33-foot ceiling of Oregon sugar pine fitted without nails, under crown-shaped chandeliers designed by Wizard of Oz author L. Frank Baum.

Est. 1893

Fairmont Heritage Place - Ghirardelli Square

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2016

Italian immigrant Domenico Ghirardelli came west in the Gold Rush and by 1893 had bought a whole city block, the old Pioneer Woolen Mill. Architect William S. Mooser remade it as a chocolate works, and the 15-foot Ghirardelli sign, raised in 1923, guided sailors on the bay. When the factory moved out in the 1960s, William and Lurline Matson Roth saved the buildings. The clock tower building has held these suites since 2008.

  • The Powell Street cable car

Est. 1894

The Lodge at the Presidio

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2018

The Army built the Montgomery Street Barracks at the Presidio between 1895 and 1897, to house artillery, cavalry and infantry soldiers. The red brick building opened as a 42-room hotel in 2018, close under the Golden Gate Bridge. The renovation honors the 1897 floor plan: the wallpaper marks which walls are new, and contrasting wood in the lobby floor shows the old layout. Photographs and objects tell of the Ohlone people and the Army post.

  • Fort Point National Historic Site
  • The Golden Gate Bridge

Est. 1901

Cavallo Point

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2009

Fort Baker went up in the early 1900s in the Marin Headlands to help guard San Francisco, and it stayed an Army post through World War II. Its 24 Colonial Revival buildings, with columns and wraparound porches, ring a 10-acre parade ground at the foot of the Golden Gate Bridge. The post officially became parkland in 2002, and the restored officers' quarters opened to guests as Cavallo Point in 2008.

Est. 1903

Inn at the Presidio

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2011

This red brick Georgian Revival building went up in 1903 as Pershing Hall, home to the Army's unmarried officers at the Presidio. Its wings are named for officers who once lived there, and the old mess hall is now a shared dining room and lounge. In 2012 it became the first hotel to open inside the Presidio, which is now a national park site of some 1,500 acres, with white-trimmed porches on two levels.

Est. 1905

Hayes Mansion San Jose, Curio Collection by Hilton

San Jose, California · Historic Hotels member since 2019

In 1903 Mary Hayes Chynoweth asked architect George Page to design one great house for three households: her own and those of her sons, Everis and Jay Orly Hayes. The Mediterranean Revival mansion was built with the newest comforts of the day, including fire safety systems, its own power plant and even its own train station. The family sold it in the 1950s and it stood empty until the City of San Jose bought and restored it in the 1990s.

Est. 1907

Fairmont San Francisco

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2001

Sisters Theresa Fair Oelrichs and Virginia Fair Vanderbilt set out in 1903 to build a grand hotel on Nob Hill. After the 1906 earthquake, Julia Morgan, the first woman licensed as an architect in California, restored the Beaux-Arts building. In 1945 delegates drafted the United Nations Charter in its Garden Room, honored by the flags at the entrance. Tony Bennett first sang "I Left My Heart in San Francisco" here.

Hotel Shattuck Plaza

Berkeley, California · Historic Hotels member since 2025

Architect Benjamin McDougall designed this Mission-style hotel, which opened as the Shattuck Hotel in 1910. It honors Francis K. Shattuck, a state assemblyman and early leader of Berkeley who had wanted a grand hotel for the town but died before it could be built. An annex came in 1914 and a covered arcade in 1926. The original chandelier still hangs in the restaurant, and the hotel is on the National Register of Historic Places.

Claremont Resort & Club

Berkeley, California · Historic Hotels member since 2016

The hillside between Berkeley and Oakland once held the Thornburg family's grand estate, which locals called "the castle." The Claremont opened on the site in 1915, and by the early 1930s its white Tudor Revival front had earned it the name "White Castle on the Hill." A restoration marked its 100th birthday in 2015. A lounge off the lobby honors Sir Edmund Hillary, and the pool deck appeared in the film Mrs. Doubtfire.

Est. 1919

Hotel El Roblar

Ojai, California · Historic Hotels member since 2026

Ohio native Edward Drummond Libbey arrived in Ojai in 1908 and fell for its mountains and citrus groves. He led a plan to remake the village, and part of it was a Spanish Colonial Revival hotel he named El Roblar, Spanish for oak grove. It opened in 1919. A mural around the lobby tells the valley's story, from the Chumash people to Libbey, Beatrice Wood and Jiddu Krishnamurti, and a carved wall in the ballroom shows Ojai's farms.

Est. 1921

Fairmont Miramar - Hotel & Bungalows

Santa Monica, California · Historic Hotels member since 2018

This hotel sits on the bluffs above Santa Monica Beach, and its oldest living resident is a tree. In the 1880s Georgina Jones, wife of the city's founder John Percival Jones, planted a Moreton Bay fig in the rose garden of the mansion here, and it still shades the courtyard. The six-story Palisades Wing followed in 1924, built for long beach stays, and soon drew celebrities and politicians. The poolside bungalows came in the 1930s.

Ojai Valley Inn

Ojai, California · Historic Hotels member since 1991

In 1923 glass magnate Edward Drummond Libbey started building a country club here, with a golf course by George C. Thomas Jr. and a Spanish Colonial Revival clubhouse by architect Wallace Neff. A two-story inn joined it in 1934, and Frank Capra filmed parts of Lost Horizon on the grounds. The War Department took the site over in World War II. Afterward hotelier Don Burger rebuilt it as a resort, drawing Clark Gable, Judy Garland and Ronald Reagan.

The Biltmore Los Angeles

Los Angeles, California · Historic Hotels member since 2024

The Biltmore opened on Pershing Square in 1923, blending Mediterranean Revival and Beaux-Arts styles under frescoed ceilings by muralist John B. Smeraldi. The Academy Awards were born in a meeting here, and the hotel hosted eight Oscar ceremonies in Hollywood's Golden Age. During Prohibition Al Capone and Bugsy Siegel drank in its speakeasy. In 1960 John F. Kennedy and Lyndon B. Johnson ran rival campaigns from its rooms during the Democratic National Convention. The tiled indoor pool dates from 1926.

Benbow Inn

Garberville, California · Historic Hotels member since 2014

The Benbow family hired architect Albert Farr, who had designed Jack London's Wolf House, to build this Tudor Revival inn on the Eel River. It opened as Hotel Benbow in July 1926, just as the new Redwood Highway brought motorists north, and it quickly became a favored stop. Clark Gable, Spencer Tracy, Eleanor Roosevelt and President Herbert Hoover all stayed. Some guest rooms still keep doors hand-painted by Eva Benbow.

Fairmont Breakers Long Beach

Long Beach, California · Historic Hotels member since 2024

The Breakers Hotel opened in 1926 and drew Clark Gable, Cary Grant, Rita Hayworth and Babe Ruth to its Art Deco Sky Room. On May 31, 1928, Charles Lindbergh, lost in fog, steered by the glowing "B" on its roof and landed safely nearby. In World War II the Sky Room served as a lookout post. The hotel closed in the early 1960s, and a restoration from 2017 to 2024 brought it back.

Harbor Court Hotel

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2025

This red-brick building near the foot of Market Street went up in 1926 to house servicemen coming home. Its Spanish Colonial touches, arched entries, carved terracotta and clay tile roof, have looked out on nearly a century of change on the bay. From its doorstep San Franciscans watched the Bay Bridge rise and Alcatraz become a prison in the 1930s, and later saw the old piers brought back to life.

Hotel Casa del Mar

Santa Monica, California · Historic Hotels member since 2018

In 1926 businessmen E.A. and Tifford Harter launched Casa del Mar as a private beach club, and it was an instant hit, with around 2,000 members at its peak. Los Angeles architect Charles F. Plummer gave it an Italian Renaissance Revival look, with painted ceilings and bronze statues. Theda Bara was among the Hollywood stars who came to see and be seen. Now listed on the National Register, it came back as a hotel in 1997.

InterContinental Mark Hopkins San Francisco

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2011

Railroad baron Mark Hopkins, one of San Francisco's Big Four, built his Nob Hill mansion here in 1878; the 1906 earthquake destroyed it. In 1925 the site was bought by George D. Smith, a mining engineer, who opened this Renaissance Revival hotel on December 4, 1926. It went on to host presidents, heads of state and royalty, and in 1945 it entertained delegates who founded the United Nations. Maynard Dixon and Frank Van Sloun painted the murals in the Room of the Dons.

La Valencia Hotel

La Jolla/San Diego, California · Historic Hotels member since 1989

La Valencia opened in 1926 in the heart of La Jolla, and its 11-story tower with a gold and blue dome became the village landmark. It began white with a red tile roof; in the late 1950s, so the story goes, the owners came home from Waikiki and painted it pink, earning it the name the Pink Lady of La Jolla. Mary Pickford, Gregory Peck, Ginger Rogers and John Lennon were guests. San Diego named it a historic landmark in 1987.

Omni San Francisco

San Francisco, California · Historic Hotels member since 2010

A granite building from 1906 once stood at the corner of California and Montgomery Streets. In 1926 it came down for the 17-story Financial Center Building, a Renaissance Revival bank tower designed by San Francisco architect Frederick H. Meyer with Albin R. Johnson. When it became a hotel, the 1926 drawings guided the restoration of its stone and brick front. Its two-story lobby, with marble from Italy and crystal chandeliers from Austria, still recalls its banking days.

Sofia Hotel

San Diego, California · Historic Hotels member since 2008

In the 1920s touring cars carried Americans across the West, and the Pickwick Stages line, which kept much of its fleet in San Diego, built a hotel and terminal downtown. Architect Wilber Peugh designed the Gothic Revival Pickwick Hotel, which opened in 1926 with a crenellated roofline and terra cotta trim. No earlier San Diego hotel had a private bath in every room, and it was sold with the slogan "A Room and a Bath for Two and a Half."

Fairmont Sonoma Mission Inn & Spa

Sonoma, California · Historic Hotels member since 2014

Native people held these mineral hot springs sacred long before Captain H.E. Boyes, a young Englishman, bought the land in 1895. Drilling a well, he struck water at 112 degrees, and by 1900 his Boyes Hot Springs Hotel was welcoming travelers who came to bathe. That hotel was destroyed in 1923, and the inn reopened in Spanish Colonial Revival style in 1927. The lobby still has its Saltillo tile floors and grand wood-burning fireplace.

The Hollywood Roosevelt

Los Angeles, California · Historic Hotels member since 2016

Douglas Fairbanks, Mary Pickford, Louis B. Mayer and Sid Grauman built this Spanish Colonial Revival hotel across from Grauman's Chinese Theatre, and it opened in 1927. In 1929 it hosted the very first Academy Awards. Clark Gable and Carole Lombard met in a 12th-floor penthouse, Shirley Temple practiced tap steps with Bill "Bojangles" Robinson by the front door, and Marilyn Monroe lived here in the 1940s. David Hockney later painted the Tropicana Pool.

The Inn at Death Valley

Death Valley, California · Historic Hotels member since 2012

The Pacific Coast Borax Company built the Furnace Creek Inn in 1927 to give its new Death Valley Railroad something to carry once the borax trade slowed. Los Angeles architect Albert C. Martin designed it in the Spanish Mission style, with a red tile roof and stucco walls, and landscape architect Daniel Hull laid out its palm gardens around a spring-fed pool. In Hollywood's Golden Age, film stars rode the train out to this desert hideaway.

The Queen Mary

Long Beach, California · Historic Hotels member since 2024

The Cunard Line began building this liner at Clydebank in Scotland in 1930, stopped when the Depression hit, and finished it with a government loan. Named for Queen Mary, wife of King George V, the 1,019-foot ship entered service in 1936, paneled in woods from more than 50 kinds of trees across the British Empire. She carried film stars, royalty and Winston Churchill, and ferried soldiers in World War II. She now rests permanently at Long Beach.

The Eden

Santa Monica, California · Historic Hotels member since 2025

Architect William E. Foster designed this gleaming white building on the Santa Monica bluffs, and it opened in 1940 as the Shangri-La Apartment Hotel under the motto "America's New Design for Living." Its Streamline Moderne lines, with geometric pillars and a stepped roofline, echo the ships that once docked in Santa Monica Harbor. It has appeared in films, welcomed Elizabeth Taylor and even saw secret wartime work. It reopened as The Eden in 2025.

Est. 1946

Alisal Ranch

Solvang, California · Historic Hotels member since 2021

The Chumash called this valley Jojoqui. In 1854 the Alisal belonged to Ulpiano Yndart, a Basque immigrant who raised cattle here before the great floods and drought of the 1860s. Charles "Pete" Jackson Jr. later turned the 10,500-acre ranch into a guest ranch, and Hollywood came riding in. Doris Day was a regular, and Clark Gable married Lady Silvia in the ranch library. Gregory Peck and Ava Gardner also stayed.

The Lafayette Hotel and Club

San Diego, California · Historic Hotels member since 2023

Local entrepreneur Larry Imig built this hotel in the wake of San Diego's World War II boom, and its Colonial Revival and Neoclassical look was rare in the city. Olympic swimmer and Tarzan star Johnny Weissmuller designed its pool. Bob Hope owned one of the penthouses, and Conrad Hilton personally owned the hotel and used it as his base for the San Diego Chargers. A scene in Top Gun was filmed in its Mississippi Room ballroom.

Surf & Sand, Laguna Beach

Laguna Beach, California · Historic Hotels member since 2021

Surf & Sand opened on the sand at Laguna Beach in 1948, built for the crowds of travelers who poured into town after World War II. When President Richard Nixon came home to San Clemente, the White House press corps stayed here, Tom Brokaw and Diane Sawyer among them, and Nixon himself once stayed while his house was being built. Peter Ustinov, Joe Namath and the Reverend Billy Graham were drawn here too.

Est. 1963

Rancho Bernardo Inn

San Diego, California · Historic Hotels member since 2021

This land was once part of the vast Rancho San Bernardo of Mexican California. In the 1920s it passed to rancher George Daley, and after his death in 1957 his nephews Donald and Lawrence Daley joined partners to build the planned community of Rancho Bernardo. Its Mediterranean Revival inn opened in 1963. Bob Hope and Bing Crosby were guests, the PGA's San Diego Open came in 1964, and the LPGA played here from 1978 to 1980.

Omni La Costa Resort

Carlsbad, California · Historic Hotels member since 2014

Television producer Merv Adelson came upon this land near Batiquitos Lagoon on a horseback ride in 1963 and, with partners Irwin Molasky and Allard Roen, built a Mission Revival resort that opened in 1965. Dick Wilson laid out the golf course, which hosted the Tournament of Champions from 1969 to 1998. Frank Sinatra, Dean Martin and Bob Hope played the course, Johnny Carson and Charlton Heston played tennis, and Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis and Richard Nixon visited the spa.

Fairmont Century Plaza

Los Angeles, California · Historic Hotels member since 2022

In 1961 developer William Zeckendorf and ALCOA bought land from 20th Century Fox, which was cutting back after costly flops such as Cleopatra. Architect Minoru Yamasaki designed the hotel, which opened in 1966 with a gala hosted by Bob Hope and Andy Williams. It later hosted the Emmy Awards and two Grammy Awards ceremonies. Sonny and Cher recorded their first live album here, and it was among the first hotels to offer color television.
