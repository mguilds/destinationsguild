Saranda

Where to Stay in Saranda

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Saranda sits in a horseshoe bay on the Ionian with Corfu a short way across the water, and it is the capital of the Albanian Riviera in the practical sense: the place with the hotels, the ferries, the buses and the supermarkets, from which everything in the far south is reachable. It is not a pretty town — it grew very fast and upward after 1991, and the concrete shows — but the setting is lovely, the promenade round the bay is one of the better evening walks in Albania, and Butrint, Ksamil and the Blue Eye are all inside half an hour. Most southern trips use it as a base for three or four nights.

The promenade and the bay front

Most centralLevel walkingEvening life

The road round the horseshoe of the bay is the centre of Saranda in every sense: the hotels with the balconies and the view, the restaurants, the ice-cream places and the xhiro — the evening walk that half the town takes. It is flat, which in this town is a real consideration, and everything a visitor needs is on it or a street back. Rooms facing the water cost more and are usually worth it. The cost is noise at the busier end in high summer, so ask which way the room faces.

The hill above the bay

Best viewsQuieterSteep walk

Saranda climbs steeply behind the front, and the streets up the hill hold a great many apartments and small hotels with views over the whole bay to Corfu that the front cannot match. They are also appreciably cheaper. The catch is the gradient: these are proper climbs on streets that in places become steps, and what looks like two hundred metres on a map is a hard five minutes back up from dinner. Worth it if your knees are good or you will use taxis; otherwise stay on the flat.

North of town: the quieter beaches

CalmestBeach on the doorstepTaxi to town

Round the northern headland the coast road runs past a string of small bays — the Mango and Pulebardha side of town, and further out towards Kakome — where the water is clearer than in the bay and the development is thinner. Resort hotels and apartments here are quieter than anything in town and some have their own stretch of beach. You will want a car or will use taxis for dinner, which is the trade-off.

The port and ferry end

For the Corfu crossingTransportWorking quarter

The southern arm of the bay holds the passenger port where the Corfu ferries come and go, with hotels and apartments around it at generally lower prices than the middle of the front. If you are arriving late from Corfu or leaving early for it this end saves you a journey, and it is only a ten- or fifteen-minute walk round to the centre. Sailings and timetables are seasonal and change, so confirm your crossing with the operator rather than taking a time from any website.

Out along the Ksamil and Butrint road

For a carBest for familiesShort drive in

South of town the road runs toward Ksamil and then Butrint, and the first few kilometres of it carry larger hotels and apartment complexes with pools, aimed at families and usually better value per room than the front. You are a ten-minute drive from Saranda and about the same from the Ksamil beaches, which suits a car-based trip very well and suits a car-free one poorly. Check what transport the property actually runs before relying on it.

Getting around Saranda

On foot, and the gradient

The promenade and the centre are flat and walkable end to end in about twenty minutes. Everything behind the front is steep, and Saranda is a town where choosing a hotel by its contour line matters more than in most places. If stairs are a problem, stay on the bay road itself.

Buses, furgons and taxis

Buses and furgon minibuses run up the coast to Himare and Vlore, inland to Gjirokaster, and north to Tirana, and down to Ksamil and Butrint frequently in season. Departures are from the area around the centre and the schedules are seasonal and informal rather than published, so ask at your hotel on the day. Taxis are plentiful and cheap for the short hops to Ksamil, Butrint and the Blue Eye.

The Corfu ferry

Passenger ferries cross between Saranda and Corfu and are the most useful way into the south of Albania for a lot of visitors, with a crossing of well under two hours on the faster boats. Services are seasonal, vary by operator and sell out in August; book ahead for August dates and confirm times with the operator rather than from a third-party page. Remember that going to Corfu means crossing into the Schengen area and back.

Day trips worth the effort

Butrint is about half an hour south and is the best single thing in the region. The Blue Eye spring is inland on the Gjirokaster road, with a short walk in from the car park. Ksamil’s beaches are fifteen to twenty minutes south. Gjirokaster is around an hour and a half inland and makes a long but entirely feasible day.

Driving and parking

A car is a real advantage for the Riviera and the Butrint road, and a liability in the middle of Saranda in August, where parking on the front is scarce. If you are hiring, ask the hotel directly whether it has parking rather than assuming, and be ready for narrow steep streets if you are staying up the hill.

When to come

Saranda’s season is the Ionian season. June and September are the best weeks of the year here: warm sea, full services, no crush and prices off the peak. July and especially August are hot and very busy, with the Albanian diaspora home and every room taken — book months ahead for August or go elsewhere. May and early October are lovely, with swimming comfortable for most people and the town calm. November to March the beach trade largely shuts, the weather is mild and wet, and the town is quiet and very cheap; Butrint in winter with nobody there is a real pleasure, but do not come for the sea.

Getting in: what applies, and what does not

Albania is not in the European Union and not in the Schengen area, so it runs its own border. United States citizens may enter as tourists without a visa and may stay up to one year without a residence permit; your passport must be valid for at least three months from arrival and carry a blank page for the stamp. Most other Western nationalities get ninety days visa-free — check your own. The United States rates Albania Level 2, exercise increased caution, in an advisory issued on 26 August 2026 on account of crime; read the current text at travel.state.gov, because the level and wording change.

If your trip also crosses into Greece, Italy or anywhere else in the Schengen area, the following applies to that crossing and not to Albania:

Europe’s Entry/Exit System is now in force. Since 10 April 2026 it has operated at every Schengen border crossing and passport stamps have been discontinued. On your first crossing you will have your fingerprints taken and your photograph made at the border, which adds a few minutes; after that the check is quicker. There is nothing to apply for and nothing to pay.

ETIAS - the travel authorisation you may have read about, expected to cost around EUR 20 and last three years - is not in force yet. No applications are being taken, and the European Union has not announced the exact start date; it has said it will give several months’ notice before the system goes live. It has been postponed repeatedly and no start date is confirmed, so treat it as coming rather than cancelled, and ignore any website offering to sell you one today - nobody can issue one yet. When it does begin there will be six months in which you can travel without one, then six months in which only first-time arrivals are exempt.

What this means for your trip right now: you do not need ETIAS. You do need a passport issued within the last ten years and valid for at least three months beyond the date you plan to leave.

Saranda — questions

Which part of Saranda should I stay in?

Stay on the promenade round the bay if you want to walk everywhere, want the view, and would rather not climb — this suits most visitors. Stay up the hill for better views and lower prices if a steep walk is no trouble. Stay north of town or out on the Ksamil road if you have a car and want quiet or a pool. Stay near the port if you are arriving or leaving on the Corfu ferry at an awkward hour.

Is Saranda a good base for the south?

It is the base, and that is the main reason to stay there. Butrint is half an hour away, Ksamil fifteen to twenty minutes, the Blue Eye is on the inland road, Himare is up the coast and Gjirokaster is about an hour and a half. It has the hotels, the ferries, the bus connections and the shops that the villages along the Riviera do not. If your trip is the far south, three or four nights here plus a night or two up the coast works very well.

Is the town itself attractive?

Honestly, not especially. Saranda grew very fast after 1991 and much of it is unplanned concrete stacked up a hillside. What it has is the setting — the horseshoe bay, the water, Corfu across the strait — and a genuinely good evening promenade. People who arrive expecting a Greek island village are disappointed; people who treat it as a comfortable, cheap and well-connected base are not.

Where do I swim in Saranda?

There is beach in town on the bay, which is convenient and busy and not the clearest water on this coast. Better swimming is round the northern headland at the smaller bays, or south at Ksamil, where the water and the sand are the reason people come to this part of Albania at all. In August all of it is crowded, so go early.

Do I need a car?

Not strictly: taxis to Ksamil, Butrint and the Blue Eye are cheap, and buses and furgons run up the coast and inland. But a car transforms the Riviera, which is a road rather than a destination, and makes the beaches north of town and the villages between here and Vlore properly reachable. If you stay out of town, you need one.

How do I get to Saranda?

Three usual ways: the ferry from Corfu, which is the quickest route from Greece and much the most pleasant arrival; by road from Tirana, which takes most of a day; or by road from Gjirokaster, about an hour and a half, if you are coming through the interior. Ferry and coach timetables are seasonal and change, so confirm them with the operator for your dates.

Is it noisy?

The busy middle of the promenade is, in July and August, until late. The rest of the front is not, and the hill and the northern bays are quiet. Outside high summer the whole town is calm. If you are booking a front room for August, ask the hotel which way it faces and whether there is a bar below it.
