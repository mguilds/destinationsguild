Before you go

Glacier rewards the traveler who plans around one road and one short season.

Glacier covers more than a million acres along the Continental Divide, with Canada's Waterton Lakes National Park across the border to the north. Most visits center on the Going-to-the-Sun Road, about 50 miles from West Glacier past Lake McDonald, up over Logan Pass at 6,646 feet and down to St. Mary Lake on the east side. The other main areas, Many Glacier, Two Medicine and the remote North Fork, are reached by separate roads from outside the park.

The season is short. The full Sun Road usually does not open until late June at the earliest, and in a heavy snow year not until July. Its high section typically closes around the third Monday in October, sooner if winter comes early. If you want to drive over Logan Pass, plan your trip for July, August or early September.

Glacier's access rules have changed almost every year. From 2021 to 2025 the park used vehicle reservations for the busiest areas. In 2026 it dropped them and tried a ticketed shuttle and a three-hour parking limit at Logan Pass instead. The park had not announced its 2027 plan when we checked, so look at the park's website before you book anything.

Two practical points. RVs and trailers are limited on the Sun Road: nothing longer than 21 feet or wider than 8 feet may drive between Avalanche Creek and Rising Sun. And cell service is unreliable throughout the park, so print or download your tickets and reservations before you arrive.

2026 access rules: the park required no vehicle reservations in 2026, including at Many Glacier, Two Medicine and the North Fork, but warned that cars may be temporarily turned away when those areas fill up (visitors with lodging, camping, boat-tour or other reservations are still let in). From July 1 through Labor Day, private vehicles at Logan Pass were limited to three hours of parking, and longer hikes from the pass, such as the Highline Trail, required a ticket on the park shuttle, sold on Recreation.gov. Rules for 2027 had not been announced when we checked.

Right now (winter 2026–27): the high part of the Sun Road is closed for the season. The road from West Glacier to Lake McDonald Lodge is usually plowed and open, weather permitting, and the unplowed road beyond is used for skiing and snowshoeing. Park lodges are closed for winter.

Construction: the Two Medicine Road rehabilitation and water-system work closed the Two Medicine Campground for 2026; the road work is scheduled to finish in late 2027 and may run into 2028. The Inside North Fork Road is closed from the Polebridge Ranger Station toward Logging Creek for water-system work that began in October 2026, with restoration expected by summer 2027. Water-line work at St. Mary Campground runs from fall 2026 into spring 2027, with partial campground closures.

Visitors from outside the U.S.: since January 1, 2026, Glacier is one of 11 national parks that charge non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older an extra $100 per person on top of the entrance fee. The America the Beautiful pass costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for nonresidents, and a pass covers that extra fee.

Best things to do in Glacier National Park

Six ways to spend your days in Glacier.

Drive

Going-to-the-Sun Road

Fifty miles of cliffside road, waterfalls and long views, built in the 1930s. Allow a full day with stops, start early, and drive it both ways if you can; the views are different in each direction.

Alpine

Logan Pass

The top of the road on the Continental Divide, with wildflower meadows and mountain goats in summer. The boardwalk-and-trail route toward Hidden Lake Overlook starts behind the visitor center, but much of it is steep for older legs.

Lake

Lake McDonald & Apgar

The park's largest lake, famous for its colored stones under clear water. Apgar Village at the foot of the lake has a visitor center, shops and boat rentals, and the historic Lake McDonald Lodge sits on the eastern shore.

Easy walk

Trail of the Cedars

A level loop of boardwalk and pavement through old cedars and hemlocks beside Avalanche Gorge, one of the easiest walks in the park. In 2026 the park shuttle did not stop here, so plan to drive.

Valley

Many Glacier

Many people's favorite corner of Glacier, with the grand old Many Glacier Hotel on Swiftcurrent Lake, boat tours that shorten the hike toward Grinnell Glacier, and some of the best bear and moose watching in the park.

Tours

Red bus & boat tours

Let someone else drive. The historic red buses run narrated tours of the Sun Road, Sun Tours offers tours focused on Blackfeet culture, and boat cruises run on Lake McDonald, Swiftcurrent and St. Mary lakes.

From the Guild

If you are not comfortable on narrow mountain roads, book a seat on one of the red bus tours over Logan Pass. The driver handles the drop-offs, the guide tells the story, and the roll-back canvas tops let you look straight up at the peaks. Reserve early; summer tours fill.

When to visit Glacier

July through early September is prime time. The Sun Road is normally open end to end, the wildflowers peak at Logan Pass in July and August, and the lodges, boat tours and red buses are all running. It is also when Glacier is busiest, so get to popular trailheads and parking areas early in the morning.

Late May and June are green and quiet in the valleys, but the high road is usually still being plowed, and many trails are under snow. You can drive the lower Sun Road from West Glacier to Avalanche, visit Lake McDonald and, as snow clears, Many Glacier and Two Medicine. Before the road opens to cars, sections are often open to hikers and bicyclists.

Late September and October bring golden larches, cool days and fewer people, but services close through September and early snow can shut the high road for days at a time. From November to April the park is a winter landscape: the road is open only near West Glacier, and the entrance fee drops to $25 per vehicle.

How to get to Glacier National Park

By car

From the west, US Highway 2 runs from Kalispell to West Glacier and the West Entrance, about 33 miles. From the east, US 89 runs north from Great Falls through Browning to St. Mary, about 125 miles. Many Glacier and Two Medicine are reached from the east side, outside the park, and the remote North Fork by rough, largely gravel roads to Polebridge.

RV drivers: leave the motorhome or trailer at camp to cross the Sun Road; anything over 21 feet long or 8 feet wide is not allowed between Avalanche Creek and Rising Sun. US Highway 2 along the park's southern edge is the RV route between the west and east sides.

By air / train / shuttle

Glacier Park International Airport near Kalispell is about 30 miles from the West Entrance and has rental cars. Missoula and Great Falls are farther away.

Amtrak's Empire Builder stops at West Glacier all year and at East Glacier Park in summer. In summer Glacier National Park Lodges runs a fee shuttle, by reservation, from the West Glacier depot to Apgar and Lake McDonald Lodge. The park's own shuttle on the Sun Road ran by ticket only in 2026, and all park shuttles are wheelchair accessible.

Lodges inside the park

Most lodging inside the park is run by Glacier National Park Lodges (Xanterra), the park's concessioner: the Lake McDonald Lodge, a Swiss-chalet-style lodge on the lake; the Many Glacier Hotel on Swiftcurrent Lake; the Rising Sun Motor Inn near St. Mary Lake; and the Village Inn Motel at Apgar. The company also runs the Swiftcurrent Motor Inn in Many Glacier, which was closed for construction in 2025, so confirm it is open before planning on it.

For hikers, the two historic backcountry chalets, Granite Park Chalet and Sperry Chalet, are reached only on foot and are run by Belton Chalets under a new contract that began in 2026.

The lodges are open in summer only and fill early, especially at Many Glacier and Lake McDonald. Book directly with the concessioner as soon as you know your dates, and if you need an accessible room, ask for one by name when you reserve.

RV travel & camping at Glacier

Thirteen campgrounds, most of them reservation-only, no hookups, and modest site lengths.

Glacier has 13 frontcountry campgrounds, and eight of them, Apgar, Avalanche, Fish Creek, Many Glacier, Rising Sun, St. Mary, Sprague Creek and Two Medicine, must be reserved ahead on Recreation.gov. Some sites open for booking months ahead and some only a few days ahead. The other five, Bowman Lake, Cut Bank, Kintla Lake, Logging Creek and Quartz Creek, are first-come, first-served and fill early in summer.

Site lengths are the thing to watch. The park lists the longest sites as: Apgar, 25 sites up to 40 feet; St. Mary, 3 sites up to 40 feet and 22 up to 35 feet; Fish Creek, 18 sites up to 35 feet; Many Glacier, 13 sites up to 35 feet; and Avalanche, up to 26 feet. Sprague Creek allows nothing over 21 feet, and the remote North Fork and Cut Bank campgrounds are best for small rigs only. Check the limit for your exact site when you book.

Park campgrounds have no hookups. Two Medicine Campground was closed for construction in 2026, and partial closures are expected at St. Mary through spring 2027. In winter, a loop at Apgar stays open for primitive camping. For full hookups and longer rigs, there are private RV parks around West Glacier, Columbia Falls, Whitefish and Kalispell, and on the east side near St. Mary and East Glacier.

Fishing in Glacier

Good news for anglers: no fishing licence is needed inside Glacier. A Montana licence is required to fish the Middle Fork of the Flathead from park land or bridges, and Lower Two Medicine Lake follows Blackfeet Nation rules, so check theirs before fishing there.

Streams are generally open from the third Saturday in May through November 30, and lakes are open all year, with some seasonal closures to protect spawning cutthroat. Use artificial flies and lures only (bait is allowed in two small areas), one rod per person, no lead and no felt-soled boots. All native fish, including bull trout and westslope cutthroat, must be released. Every boat needs an aquatic invasive species inspection before it launches.

Accessible travel at Glacier

What is available for visitors with limited mobility, and the passes that help.

All of Glacier's park shuttles are wheelchair accessible, which makes the Sun Road one of the more reachable mountain drives in the West. Much of the scenery is seen from the car and from roadside pullouts, and the Trail of the Cedars near Avalanche is a level boardwalk-and-paved loop.

The park offers large-print brochures, braille handouts and audio-described videos. Ranger programs that are mobility accessible are marked at visitor centers, and the evening programs at the Apgar, Fish Creek, Many Glacier and Rising Sun campground amphitheaters, the Lake McDonald Lodge auditorium, the Many Glacier Hotel auditorium (use the lower-level entrance) and the St. Mary Visitor Center auditorium are held in accessible venues.

Logan Pass is high and the weather changes fast, so bring a warm layer even in August. When booking a lodge room, ask Glacier National Park Lodges specifically about accessible rooms, since the historic buildings vary.

Park passes that save you money. U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability can get the free lifetime Access Pass. Visitors 62 and over can buy the Senior Pass ($80 for life, or $20 a year). Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass. Each covers entrance fees at every national park.

Traveling on dialysis? The nearest dialysis clinic is in Kalispell, about 33 miles southwest of the West Entrance. Book your treatment at a clinic there several weeks before you go; the large dialysis companies run travel desks that will arrange it with your home unit.

Glacier National Park FAQ

Do I need a vehicle reservation for Glacier National Park?

Not in 2026. The park required no vehicle reservations anywhere, but used a ticketed shuttle and a three-hour parking limit at Logan Pass from July 1 to Labor Day. The park had not announced its 2027 rules when we checked.

When does the Going-to-the-Sun Road open?

The full road usually opens in late June or early July, depending on snow, and the high section typically closes around the third Monday in October. The lower west end near West Glacier is open all year.

Can I drive an RV on the Going-to-the-Sun Road?

Only if it is 21 feet long or less (including bumpers) and 8 feet wide or less (including mirrors). Longer vehicles and combinations are not allowed between Avalanche Creek and Rising Sun.

How much is the Glacier entrance fee?

$35 per private vehicle for seven days in summer and $25 in winter (November 1 to April 30). A Glacier annual pass is $70.

Do I need a fishing licence in Glacier?

No licence is needed inside the park. A Montana licence is needed to fish the Middle Fork of the Flathead from park land, and Lower Two Medicine Lake follows Blackfeet Nation rules.

Which lodges are inside Glacier National Park?

Lake McDonald Lodge, Many Glacier Hotel, Rising Sun Motor Inn, Village Inn Motel at Apgar and Swiftcurrent Motor Inn, run by Glacier National Park Lodges, plus the hike-in Granite Park and Sperry chalets.

Are there RV hookups in Glacier's campgrounds?

No. Park campgrounds have no hookups. Private RV parks with hookups are in the towns around the park.

When is the best time to visit Glacier?

Mid-July to early September, when the Going-to-the-Sun Road is fully open and most trails are clear of snow.
