Where to Stay in Seattle — Pike Place, the Waterfront, Queen Anne & Ballard

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Seattle packs a great deal into a small, hilly centre. Pike Place Market, the waterfront piers, the shopping streets and the Space Needle are all within a walk or a short ride of each other, and the light rail runs straight in from the airport. Where you sleep decides how much you walk uphill. Downtown and the market are the easy first-visit choice, the waterfront gives you the water, Belltown and Queen Anne are livelier and close to Seattle Center, First Hill is calm, and Ballard is a neighbourhood in its own right. This page sorts the areas and names nine hotels we checked on their own websites today.

Choosing Your Area in Seattle

Six bases, each suited to a different trip.

Downtown and Pike Place Market

The heart of the city: Pike Place Market above the water, the retail core around Westlake, theatres and the Seattle Art Museum. Sound Transit’s 1 Line light rail stops at Westlake and Symphony stations, right in the middle of it.

Stay here for a first visit, especially without a car. Expect city-centre prices and city-centre bustle.

The Waterfront

The piers along Elliott Bay below the market. Visit Seattle, the city’s official visitor bureau, notes the summer and autumn crowds that pack the piers, steps from the downtown core.

Stay here for views across Elliott Bay towards the Olympic Mountains.

Belltown

Just north of downtown, between the market and Seattle Center. Visit Seattle describes it as a downtown-adjacent neighbourhood of buzzy restaurants, bars and galleries.

Stay here for an evening scene and an easy walk both to the market and to the Space Needle.

First Hill

On the slope east of downtown, a quieter district of older buildings looking down over the city.

Stay here for calm and character, accepting a walk downhill to the centre and uphill back.

Queen Anne and Seattle Center

Visit Seattle describes Queen Anne as a hilly neighbourhood just north of downtown, with the attraction-heavy Seattle Center — home of the Space Needle — in Lower Queen Anne, and historic houses, quaint shopping streets and quiet parks up on the hill.

Stay here for concerts and events at Seattle Center, and for a slightly more relaxed base than downtown.

Ballard

A neighbourhood northwest of the centre. Visit Seattle points to its working waterfront and Scandinavian seafaring heritage, with lively restaurants and boutiques, and says it glows in summer and autumn.

Stay here to live like a local, on a repeat visit or a longer stay. It is a drive or bus ride from downtown.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Seattle details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Seattle

The airport is Seattle–Tacoma International (Sea-Tac), south of the city. Sound Transit’s 1 Line light rail runs from SeaTac/Airport station north through downtown, stopping at Stadium, Pioneer Square, Symphony and Westlake stations on its way to Capitol Hill, the U District and beyond (read 25 November 2026). Most downtown hotels are a short walk from Symphony or Westlake. Sailing to Alaska? See our Alaska Cruises guide.

Getting around

Downtown, Pike Place Market, the waterfront and Belltown are close enough to walk, though the streets climb steeply away from the water. Seattle Center and Lower Queen Anne are a longer walk or a short ride north. Ballard is best reached by car or bus. If you bring a car, ask your hotel about parking before you book.

When to go

Visit Seattle singles out summer and autumn, with long days and crisp evenings, as the season the waterfront and neighbourhoods like Ballard come alive. Winter is quieter and greyer, but the market, museums and restaurants carry on. We have not printed monthly temperatures because we did not read an official climate source for this page.

Seattle Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in Seattle?

Downtown near Pike Place Market for a first visit and easy walking. The waterfront for views of Elliott Bay. Belltown for restaurants and bars. Lower Queen Anne for Seattle Center and the Space Needle. First Hill for quiet. Ballard for a neighbourhood stay away from downtown.

How do I get from Sea-Tac airport to downtown Seattle?

Sound Transit’s 1 Line light rail runs from SeaTac/Airport station into downtown, stopping at Pioneer Square, Symphony and Westlake stations. Most downtown hotels are a short walk from Symphony or Westlake.

Do I need a car in Seattle?

Usually not if you stay downtown, near the market, in Belltown or in Lower Queen Anne. A car is more useful for Ballard and for day trips out of the city. Ask your hotel about parking before you book.

Where should I stay near the Space Needle?

In Lower Queen Anne or Belltown. The MarQueen Hotel and The Mediterranean Inn, both listed above, are in Lower Queen Anne near Seattle Center, and the Kimpton Palladian is in Belltown.

Is Seattle a cruise port?

Yes. Many Alaska cruises sail the Inside Passage round trip from Seattle; our Alaska Cruises guide explains the routes. Stay downtown or on the waterfront the night before you sail.
