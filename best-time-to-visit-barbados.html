Barbados, month by month

Best Time to Visit Barbados

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Barbados does not really have seasons in the northern sense: the air sits between the low eighties and the high eighties every month of the year, and the sea never drops out of the high seventies. What changes is the rain, the wind and the price of a bed. The island has a dry half from about the middle of December to the end of May, and a wetter, warmer, cheaper and much quieter half from June to November, which is also the Atlantic hurricane season. Below is the year as it actually feels, month by month, with what is open, what is on and what it costs.

The short answer

Four honest recommendations, depending on what you are optimising for.

For certain sunshine: February

Usually the driest month of the year, with a steady trade wind and day after day of clear weather. It is also the fullest and the dearest month, and the hotels on the west coast will want booking a long way ahead. If the weather has to be right, this is the month that delivers.

For the best of everything: late April, or early December

Our own pick. Both are shoulder weeks with dry-season weather, a sea at summer temperature and prices well below the January-to-March peak. Late April also catches the Oistins Fish Festival at Easter; early December catches the island before the Christmas rush.

For the lowest price: September and October

The wettest and quietest weeks of the year, inside the hurricane season, with some small hotels closed. Rooms can be half the winter rate. A genuine bargain for flexible travellers with proper insurance, and a gamble for anyone with one week and fixed expectations.

For the island at full volume: July into August

Crop Over. Weeks of calypso, fetes and markets ending with Grand Kadooment on the first Monday in August. Hot, humid, loud and tremendous, and the one fortnight to avoid if what you want is a quiet beach.

Barbados month by month

Air and sea figures are long-run monthly averages, rounded — a guide to what the month is like, not a forecast for your week.

January

83°F air · 79°F sea

Dry, breezy and busy. The trade wind blows all month, rain is rare and brief, and the island is full of visitors escaping the northern winter. Prices are at or near their peak and the better hotels want booking months ahead. One of the two or three most reliable months for weather in the whole Caribbean.

February

84°F air · 79°F sea

Usually the driest month of the year, and the busiest. If you want guaranteed sunshine this is it, and you will pay for it. The Holetown Festival fills the west coast with street parades and food stalls in the middle of the month, commemorating the first English landing there in 1627.

March

85°F air · 79°F sea

Dry, hot and still high season, with spring-break crowds on the south coast and cruise ships in Bridgetown most days. The sea is calm on the west, lively on the south. Rooms begin to ease in the last week or two as the northern winter loosens its grip.

April

86°F air · 80°F sea

Our favourite month of the first half of the year. The dry weather holds, the high-season crowds thin out after Easter, and prices come down with them. The Oistins Fish Festival runs over the Easter weekend and is the south coast at its best: boat races, fish-boning competitions, music and a great deal of eating.

May

87°F air · 82°F sea

The quiet month, and a bargain. The first proper showers arrive, usually short and in the afternoon, the humidity climbs a little, and the island empties of visitors. The sea is warmer than it has been all year. If you want the west coast at half price with weather that is still mostly dry, come in May.

June

87°F air · 83°F sea

The wet season and the hurricane season both begin, officially on 1 June. In practice June is often a fine month with brief heavy showers between long sunny spells. Crop Over opens with the ceremonial delivery of the last canes, and the fetes begin. Low prices, warm sea, some rain.

July

86°F air · 83°F sea

Hot, humid and full of music. Crop Over builds all month towards Grand Kadooment, and the island is busy with Bajans home from abroad rather than with winter sun-seekers. Rain comes in bursts. This is also the month in which Hurricane Beryl struck the island in 2024, which is worth remembering when you buy your insurance.

August

87°F air · 84°F sea

The warmest sea of the year, the wettest stretch beginning, and Grand Kadooment on the first Monday of the month, when the costumed bands cross the island and nothing else happens at all. A wonderful fortnight for a party and a poor one for a quiet beach.

September

87°F air · 84°F sea

The quietest month on the island and statistically the most active of the hurricane season across the Atlantic. Some smaller hotels and restaurants close for a few weeks. Rooms are at their cheapest. For a traveller with flexible dates, good insurance and no need to be anywhere, it can be a remarkable value.

October

86°F air · 84°F sea

Usually the wettest month of the year, in short tropical downpours rather than grey days, with hot still air between them. The island is green and empty and cheap. Cruise ships begin returning towards the end of the month and the hotels start reopening.

November

85°F air · 83°F sea

A month of two halves. The first is wet and quiet; by the end the rain is easing, the hurricane season closes on the thirtieth, the cruise season is under way and the island is in its National Season, building to Independence and Republic Day on 30 November. Prices rise through the month.

December

84°F air · 81°F sea

The first half of December is the best-value fortnight in the Barbadian year: dry-season weather arriving, the sea still at its summer warmth, Christmas crowds not yet landed and rooms at shoulder prices. From about the twentieth that reverses completely and the island goes to its dearest rates of all.

Rain, wind and hurricanes

The rain here is tropical rain. In the wet half of the year it arrives as a heavy shower that crosses the island, soaks everything for twenty minutes and leaves the sun behind it; the idea of a grey day that never lifts is largely a northern one, though October can produce them. February and March are the driest months, October and November the wettest. The east and the higher inland parishes catch more rain than the leeward west coast, which is one reason the hotels are where they are.

The trade wind is the thing visitors underestimate. It blows almost constantly from the east, which is what makes eighty-seven degrees feel pleasant on a terrace and what makes the south coast breezy and the east coast wild. It also means you will burn without noticing the heat. The wind drops in the late summer, which is when the humidity becomes a presence.

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November, and most of the region’s activity falls between the middle of August and the middle of October. Barbados sits about a hundred miles east of the main island chain and well to the south, so the overwhelming majority of storms track north of it; this is a real advantage over islands further up the arc, and it is why the island is often described as being outside the hurricane belt. It is not a guarantee. Hurricane Beryl passed close in July 2024 and did serious damage to the fishing boats at Bridgetown. Our advice is unexciting and holds every year: travel in those months if the price suits you, buy insurance that covers weather disruption, and watch the forecast in the week before you fly.

Events worth timing a trip around

Holetown Festival · February

A week of street parades, stalls and music along the west coast, marking the first English landing at Holetown in 1627. It is the liveliest the Platinum Coast gets, and it happens in the driest, sunniest part of the year.

Oistins Fish Festival · Easter weekend

The south coast fishing town’s own festival: boat races, fish-boning and fish-scaling competitions, dancing and an enormous amount of grilled fish. A local, unpolished, thoroughly good-natured weekend, and our favourite single event on the island.

Crop Over · mid-June to the first Monday in August

The island’s carnival, grown out of the celebration that ended the sugar harvest. Weeks of calypso tents, fetes and markets, the Pic-o-de-Crop finals, and then Grand Kadooment, when the costumed bands cross the island and the whole country takes the day off.

Independence and Republic Day · 30 November

Barbados became independent on 30 November 1966 and a republic on the same date in 2021, so the end of November is the island’s National Season: parades, craft markets, church services and a great deal of blue and gold. The weather is usually turning fine again by then, and prices have not yet reached their peak.

Prices and what is open

The season moves the price of a room more than anything else on this island.

The winter season

Roughly the middle of December to the middle of April, with the peak from Christmas to the end of March. Everything is open, every boat sails, and the west coast hotels charge their highest rates of the year. Christmas and New Year, and the weeks around them, are dearer again and need booking many months ahead.

The shoulders

Late April and May, and the first half of December. Dry-season weather with low-season crowds, everything still open, and rates a third below the peak. If you can only go once and want the best of both, go now.

The low season

June to the middle of November. Warmest sea, greenest island, emptiest beaches and the lowest prices of the year. Some small hotels and restaurants close for a few weeks in September and October for their annual refit, so check that your choice is actually open before you book the flight.

Cruise season

Late October to mid-April, heaviest from December to March. Bridgetown is a turnaround port as well as a port of call, so on a busy winter morning there may be several large ships in and the town is crowded until mid-afternoon. Our Caribbean cruising guide has the regional picture.

Timing it in your later years

The heat and the humidity, rather than the rain, are what decide the right month.

The dry season is the kind one

From January to April the humidity is low and the trade wind steady, which makes eighty-five degrees comfortable in the shade. From August to October the wind drops and the humidity rises, and the same temperature is much harder work. If heat is a consideration, travel in the first four months of the year.

Late April is the gentlest fortnight

Dry weather, a warm sea, the winter crowds gone home, hotels still fully staffed and open, and prices down. If somebody asked us to choose two weeks on this island for an unhurried couple, it would be the second half of April.

Think twice about September

Not because of storms — because it is the most humid month, the wind is lightest, and the most likely weeks for a small hotel to be shut for its annual refit. The savings are real but so is the discomfort.

Book the west coast for calm water

Whatever month you choose, the leeward west coast gives the flattest sea and the easiest entry, and in the winter months it is calmer still. The south coast is breezier all year, which is lively rather than restful.

Best time to visit Barbados — questions

What is the best month to visit Barbados?

For reliable weather, February: it is usually the driest month of the year, with a steady trade wind and almost no rain. For the best combination of weather, sea and price, we would pick the second half of April or the first half of December, when the dry season is either just loosening or just arriving, the sea is warm, and a room costs a good deal less than it does in the middle of the winter season. There is no bad month for swimming here — the sea does not drop below about 79°F at any point in the year.

When is the cheapest time to go to Barbados?

September and October, by a wide margin, with August and early November not far behind. Those are the wettest and least busy weeks, they fall inside the hurricane season, and some small hotels and restaurants close for part of them. In exchange you can pay half of what the same room costs in February. If your dates are flexible and your travel insurance covers weather disruption, it is a real bargain rather than a trap.

When is hurricane season in Barbados?

The Atlantic hurricane season officially runs from 1 June to 30 November, and the most active weeks across the region are usually from the middle of August to the middle of October. Barbados sits further east and further south than most of the island chain, so the great majority of storms pass to the north of it — but not all of them. Hurricane Beryl struck in July 2024 and badly damaged the fishing fleet at Bridgetown. Travel in those months is sensible with insurance that covers weather disruption, and worth watching the forecast for in the final week.

How warm is the sea in Barbados?

Warm all year. The long-run averages run from about 79°F in the depths of January, February and March to about 84°F from August to October. The west coast is calm and clear, the south coast breezier with a shore break, and the east coast is open Atlantic and not for swimming at all, whatever the temperature says.

When is Crop Over, and should I plan around it?

Crop Over, the old sugar-harvest festival that is now the island's carnival, runs from about the middle of June to the first Monday in August, when Grand Kadooment closes it with the costumed bands crossing the island. If that is what you have come for, book accommodation early, because Bajans living abroad come home for it. If you have come for a quiet beach, choose a different fortnight: the island is loud, full and in no mood to be restful.

Is the rainy season a reason not to go?

Not on its own. The wet season from June to November does not look like a European wet season: it is mostly brilliant sunshine interrupted by heavy showers that pass in twenty minutes, and the island is at its greenest. October is the wettest month and the one where a whole grey day is actually possible. What you are really trading is a slightly lower chance of perfect weather against prices a third to a half lower and beaches you have to yourself.

When is cruise season in Barbados?

Roughly from the end of October to the middle of April, with the heaviest traffic from December to March. Bridgetown is a turnaround port as well as a port of call, so in high season there can be several large ships in the Deep Water Harbour at once and the town is noticeably busier on those mornings. If you are staying on the island and want Bridgetown quiet, go on a day when fewer ships are in, or go late in the afternoon.
