Nepal Travel Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

A country the size of a middling American state with eight of the world’s fourteen highest mountains along its northern edge — and, in the three square miles of the Kathmandu Valley, more carved wood, brick and bronze than almost anywhere on earth. Nepal gives you the Himalaya from a hotel terrace if that is all you want, trails of three days or three weeks if it is not, and down in the hot flat Terai a jungle full of rhino and the plain where the Buddha was born. This is the Guild’s chapter on it.

Why visit Nepal

Four reasons the Guild sends travellers here.

The mountains

Eight of the fourteen peaks over eight thousand metres stand in or on the border of Nepal, including Everest. You do not have to climb anything: on a clear October morning the whole wall of them is visible from a hotel terrace in Pokhara, and from a ridge above Kathmandu.

The valley cities

Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur were three rival kingdoms within a few miles of each other, and each built a palace square to outdo the others. The result is a density of carved wood, brick and bronze that has no equivalent anywhere.

Walking

Nepal is the best country in the world for walking, and not only at altitude. There are trails of three weeks and trails of three days, teahouses the whole way on the popular routes, and hill country at two thousand metres that asks nothing of your lungs.

The other Nepal

Down in the Terai the country is flat, hot and full of rhino and tiger, and at Lumbini the Buddha was born on a plain two dozen countries have since built monasteries on. Most visitors miss both.

The regions of Nepal

Nepal runs in bands from the hot plain to the high Himalaya, and each band is a different country. Towns with a Guild guide are marked; the rest link straight to hotels through our Trip.com partner while their chapters are written.

How the country divides up

The Kathmandu Valley

A bowl at about 1,400 metres holding three cities that were once rival kingdoms. Kathmandu has the old Durbar Square, the hilltop stupa of Swayambhunath, the Hindu cremation ghats at Pashupatinath and, out to the northeast, the great Tibetan stupa at Boudhanath. Patan, across the river, is the valley’s craft city. Bhaktapur, east of both, is the best preserved and the one worth sleeping in.

Pokhara and the Annapurnas

Seven hours west by road, Pokhara sits on a lake with the Annapurna wall and the fin of Machhapuchhre standing over it. It is the resting place of Nepali travel and the gateway to the Annapurna trails, from the short Ghorepani and Poon Hill circuits to the three-week Circuit itself.

The Everest side

East of Kathmandu, the Khumbu is reached by the short, weather-dependent flight to Lukla and then on foot: up the Dudh Koshi to Namche Bazar, the Sherpa capital at about 3,440 metres, and on toward Everest Base Camp. It is a fortnight’s walking at altitude, not a side trip, and it is built around acclimatisation days.

The Terai

Along the Indian border the country drops to a flat, hot, fertile plain. Chitwan National Park holds one of the great populations of greater one-horned rhino, with tigers, gaur and crocodile alongside them. West of it, Lumbini marks the Buddha’s birthplace, now a four-kilometre park of monasteries built by Buddhist countries from around the world.

Top things to do in Nepal

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Sunrise on the Annapurnas from Sarangkot

Up the ridge above Pokhara before dawn, with cloud lying in the lake basin below and Machhapuchhre catching the first light before anything else does.

The evening circuit at Boudhanath

Join the slow clockwise walk around the great stupa at dusk, past butter lamps and murmured mantras. The most atmospheric hour in the Kathmandu Valley, and it costs nothing.

Bhaktapur after the buses leave

Stay the night in the old city and have Durbar Square, Taumadhi and Pottery Square to yourself at dusk and again at six the next morning.

A canoe on the Rapti at first light

A dugout drifting down the river on the edge of Chitwan while the mist lifts — crocodiles on the bank, kingfishers, and rhino coming down to drink.

The monastic zone at Lumbini

Two dozen countries, each building a monastery in its own architecture along one quiet canal on a hot plain. Ride it on a bicycle in the cool of the morning.

A teahouse trek in the middle hills

Not everything is Everest. Ghorepani, Poon Hill and the ridges above Pokhara give you rhododendron forest, villages and a Himalayan dawn in under a week.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Nepal has two clear seasons for visitors and two shoulders. October and November are the best weeks of the year: the monsoon has scrubbed the air, the mountains are out day after day, the trails are dry and the country is in festival. March to April is the second season, warmer and with the rhododendron forests in flower, though haze builds as spring goes on.

December to February is cold, often beautifully clear, quiet and cheap, with the high passes shut and a winter haze that can settle over the Kathmandu Valley for days. The monsoon, June to September, brings heavy rain, landslides on mountain roads, leeches on the trails and mountains hidden for a week at a stretch — though Upper Mustang, in the rain shadow behind the Himalaya, is at its best then.

Two festivals move with the lunar calendar and are worth knowing before you fix dates. Dashain, the longest holiday of the year, falls around September or October and empties the cities as the country travels home. Tihar, the festival of lights, follows a fortnight or so later. See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Nepal for weather, trails, crowds and the festivals through the year.

Getting there

There are no direct flights from North America. Almost everyone connects through Doha, Dubai, Istanbul, Delhi, Bangkok, Singapore, Hong Kong or Seoul into Tribhuvan International Airport (KTM) at Kathmandu, which remains the country’s main gateway and is small for the traffic it handles. Allow more time there than the distances suggest.

Two newer international airports have opened and neither has yet replaced Kathmandu. Gautam Buddha International Airport at Bhairahawa, near Lumbini, opened in 2022, and Pokhara International Airport opened in 2023; Pokhara’s first scheduled daily international service, a link to Dubai, began in September 2026. Check live schedules rather than assuming an international connection into either.

Inside the country, roads are slow and mountainous — Kathmandu to Pokhara is most of a day — and domestic flights save a great deal of time. One thing travellers should know and weigh for themselves: Nepal’s airlines have been on the European Union’s air safety list since 2013 and remained on it through 2026, while the government worked toward being removed. Most visitors still fly internally. We would rather you heard it from us than from nobody.

Most visitors, including United States passport holders, can obtain a visa on arrival at Tribhuvan and at the main land borders, with an official online form available in advance. Rules and fees change; confirm them with Nepal’s own immigration authority, and use only the official government site.

Nepal travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Nepal?

October and November, without much argument: the monsoon has washed the air clean, the mountains are out, the trails are dry and the two great festivals fall in this stretch. March and April are the second season — warmer, with rhododendron forests in flower, though the air hazes as spring goes on. December to February is cold and often brilliantly clear, with high passes closed. The monsoon, June to September, hides the mountains for days at a time and makes flying unreliable.

Do Americans need a visa for Nepal?

Most visitors, United States passport holders included, obtain a tourist visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu and at the main land borders, and an official online form can be filled in beforehand to shorten the queue. Fees, permitted lengths of stay and the list of nationalities who must apply in advance all change from time to time, so confirm the current requirements with Nepal’s own immigration authority before you book, and use only the official government website — there are a great many lookalike sites charging for nothing.

Do you have to trek to enjoy Nepal?

No, and a good many visitors never put on a boot. The Kathmandu Valley cities, Pokhara and its lake, Chitwan and Lumbini make a full and varied two-week trip with no walking harder than a town square. The mountains are visible from terraces, ridges and a short flight. Trekking is the thing Nepal is most famous for; it is not the entrance fee.

How many days do you need in Nepal?

Two weeks without a trek: three or four days in the Kathmandu Valley including a night in Bhaktapur, three in Pokhara, three in Chitwan, two at Lumbini and the travel between them. Add a week to ten days if you want a teahouse trek, and more for Everest Base Camp or the Annapurna Circuit. The roads are slow — count travel days as travel days, not sightseeing days.

Is Nepal a good country for older travellers?

Yes, with planning. The great sights of the valley, Pokhara and the Terai are reached by road and seen on the flat or in a vehicle, and a car with a driver costs very little. What asks more are the surfaces — temple steps, brick lanes and guest-house staircases built like ladders — and altitude, which is not an issue in Kathmandu at 1,400 metres but becomes one above about 2,500. Say what you need when you book, and choose hotels with lifts or ground-floor rooms where it matters.

Is altitude sickness a real risk in Nepal?

Yes, above roughly 2,500 metres, and it is the one health matter worth taking seriously here. It has nothing to do with fitness, it affects the young and strong as readily as anyone, and the only reliable answers are ascending slowly, building in rest days and going down if symptoms appear. Any itinerary that flies you straight to altitude and marches you uphill the same day is badly designed. Discuss a trekking route with a doctor and a licensed agency rather than taking it from a schedule that looks efficient.
