Best Time to Visit Jamaica — Month by Month

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Written in August 2026, after Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) — the hurricane-season advice below reflects where Jamaica actually stands now.

Jamaica has one weather calendar, which makes it simpler than most of the Caribbean: a long dry season from December to April, and two rainy peaks — a modest one in May and June and a much bigger one from September to November. Temperatures barely move all year. What changes is the rain, the price, the crowds and the risk.

The short version: January to April is the best weather and the safest bet. Late April to early June is the best value. September and October are cheap for a reason. We set out below exactly what that reason is, because this is an island that has just lived through the strongest hurricane in its recorded history and our readers deserve the plain figures rather than reassurance.

The Short Answer

If you read nothing else on this page, read this.

January to April is the best weather, and February and March are the best of that

$$$ Peak but worth itDriest monthsLowest humidityNo storm risk

These are the months when Jamaica is simply, reliably good: warm bright days in the low eighties, cool evenings, low humidity, a sea at eighty-one degrees and no hurricane risk at all. February and March are the driest of the four. Everything is open and nothing is a gamble.

The catch is price and occupancy. Christmas and New Year can cost double and sell out months ahead, and March brings American spring break to the Montego Bay and Negril resorts. Our own pick for the sweet spot between weather and money is the second half of January: the holiday premium has gone and spring break has not begun.

Late April to early June is the best value of the year

$$ ValueRates fall after EasterStill very good weatherSea 82–83°F

Once Easter and spring break have passed, rates drop hard while the weather stays essentially dry-season into May. The first rains arrive in May and June, but they are usually a heavy afternoon shower rather than a lost day, and the countryside is at its greenest. You are also still ahead of the serious hurricane window.

If you want February’s holiday at something much closer to September’s price, this is how to get it. It is the window we would book ourselves.

And the months to avoid, if you have the choice

September & OctoberWettestPeak storm riskCheapest for a reason

September is the statistical heart of hurricane season — Gilbert struck on 12 September 1988. October is the wettest month of the year and is when Hurricane Melissa came ashore in 2025. Some hotels close for refurbishment in both.

The prices are genuinely tempting and, for an experienced and well-insured traveller with flexible plans, the gamble can pay. But we would not send a first-time visitor to Jamaica in either month, and we would not book a once-in-a-decade trip then.

Month by Month

Temperatures barely move in Jamaica — highs sit between 82 and 91°F all year, and the sea never drops below 81°F. It is the rain, the price and the risk that change.

January

$$$ PeakDry & brightHighs 82°FSea 81°FBest value after the 6th

The dry season proper. Bright, warm days around eighty-two degrees, cool comfortable evenings, low humidity and essentially no storm risk. The first week is still holiday-priced and full.

The second half of January is our connoisseur’s pick. The Christmas premium has gone, spring break has not started, and you get the best weather of the year at fair rather than eye-watering prices. Book it in September.

February

$$$ PeakDriest monthLowest humiditySea 81°FBook early

Arguably the finest month in Jamaica: the driest of the year, the lowest humidity, reliable sun and the calmest sea. Everything is open, everything works, and nothing is a gamble.

It is priced accordingly and the good resorts fill months ahead. If you want certainty above all else — a wedding, an anniversary, a first visit — this is the month to pay for.

March

$$$ PeakDrySpring breakSea 81°FBusy

As dry and bright as February, with a degree more warmth. The one thing to plan around is American spring break, which fills the Montego Bay and Negril resorts and changes the atmosphere at the livelier properties considerably.

If a quiet week matters to you, favour the smaller hotels — Jamaica Inn, Charela Inn, Round Hill, the Portland boutiques — or the couples-only resorts, which are insulated from it entirely.

April

$$ ValueExcellent value after EasterStill dryCarnivalSea 82°F

The turn. Up to Easter it is peak season; after Easter, rates fall noticeably while the weather stays essentially dry-season. That post-Easter fortnight is one of the two best-value windows of the year.

Kingston Carnival falls after Easter — 8 to 14 April in 2026, road march on Sunday the 12th — and is worth building a Kingston stop around if the music is the draw.

May

$$ ValueFirst rainsQuiet & greenSea 83°FLow storm risk

The first of Jamaica’s two rainy peaks arrives, but do not overstate it: May rain is usually a heavy afternoon shower rather than a washout, and the mornings stay bright. The countryside turns spectacularly green.

Prices are low, the resorts are quiet, the sea is warm, and the serious hurricane window is still ahead. For value with genuinely good weather, May is hard to beat.

June

$$ ValueSeason opens 1 JuneWarm seaQuietWatch the forecast

Hurricane season opens on the 1st, but June risk is materially lower than August through October — with the honest caveat that Hurricane Beryl passed close by as a Category 4 on 3 July 2024, so ‘early season’ is not the same as ‘no season’.

Rain eases from the May peak, the sea is at eighty-three degrees, and prices are still in value territory. Book a flexible rate and buy insurance before you travel.

July

$$ MixedDrier lullHot & humidReggae SumfestFamily travel

A relative dry lull between the two rainy peaks, and one of the better summer months for weather. It is hot and sticky, though — the humidity rather than the temperature is what tires people.

Reggae Sumfest normally takes over Montego Bay for a week in July. In 2026 it was reduced to a single night at Plantation Cove in St Ann on the 18th because of storm damage to the western hotels; 2027 is provisionally back in Montego Bay, 11 to 17 July.

August

$ LowHottest & stickiestRisk climbing hardIndependence weekSea 84°F

The hottest and most humid month, and the point at which hurricane risk starts climbing steeply. From mid-August onward you are in the genuine peak of the season.

Emancipation Day on the 1st and Independence Day on the 6th make the biggest national celebration of the year — the Grand Gala in Kingston, events island-wide, and busy domestic travel. Wonderful if that is what you have come for; busy if it is not.

September

$ LowestPeak storm riskWetCheapest of the yearSome closures

The statistical heart of hurricane season and one of the two cheapest months. Hurricane Gilbert struck on 12 September 1988. Some hotels close for refurbishment.

If you are experienced, flexible, insured and happy to accept the odds, the value is genuine. If this is a once-in-a-decade trip, choose another month. We would not send a first-time visitor to Jamaica in September.

October

$ LowestWettest monthPeak riskMelissa struck 28 OctNot for a first trip

The wettest month of the year at six to seven inches of rain, and the month Hurricane Melissa came ashore in 2025. Prices are at their floor for exactly that reason.

National Heroes’ Day falls on the 19th. Our honest advice is that October is for repeat visitors with flexible plans and good insurance, and for nobody else.

November

$$ ValueSeason ends 30 NovDrying outQuietImproving fast

The rain eases through the month and the risk falls away as the season closes on the 30th. The second half of November is a quietly excellent time to travel: improving weather, low prices, empty beaches and everything reopening for the winter.

American Thanksgiving week is the one exception — it fills quickly and prices spike for those few days.

December

$$$$ Peak from mid-monthDry season returnsChristmas is the peakBook far ahead

The first ten days or so are a genuine bargain: dry-season weather has returned but the holiday rush has not started. It is one of the best-kept secrets in Caribbean travel.

From roughly the 18th everything changes. Christmas and New Year are the most expensive and most fully booked days of the Jamaican year, with rates that can double, and they are also the liveliest and most joyful. Book by the previous spring.

Hurricane Season, Honestly

The dates, and the part of them that actually matters

1 June – 30 NovemberReal peak: mid-Aug to OctOctober is wettestNot the whole season

The Atlantic season runs from 1 June to 30 November, but treating all six months as equally risky is misleading. The genuine peak for Jamaica is mid-August through October, and October carries both the highest rainfall of the year and the sharpest end of the risk.

June and July are materially lower risk — but not zero. Hurricane Beryl passed fifteen to twenty-five miles south of Jamaica as a Category 4 on 3 and 4 July 2024, killing four people, cutting power to four hundred thousand and doing some two hundred million dollars of damage. It was an extraordinarily early major hurricane, and it is the reason we will not tell you that early season is safe season.

Hurricane Melissa, 28 October 2025 — the figures

Category 5185 mph winds45 deathsUS$8.8bn damageStrongest on record

Melissa made landfall near New Hope in Westmoreland as a Category 5 with 185 mph sustained winds and a central pressure of 897 millibars. It was the strongest hurricane ever recorded to strike Jamaica, surpassing Gilbert in 1988, and it ties with Dorian and the 1935 Labor Day hurricane as the strongest Atlantic landfall by wind speed.

Forty-five people died in Jamaica and the damage came to roughly US$8.8 billion — the costliest event in the country’s history. Westmoreland and St Elizabeth were worst hit, with Falmouth, Black River, Mandeville and Montego Bay all badly damaged. Ocho Rios, Portland and Kingston were largely spared.

We give you these numbers not to frighten you but because a guide that glosses over them is not worth reading. This is what the peak of the season can do.

What to do about it if you travel between August and October

Flexible rateInsure before namingMedical evacuation coverSpare day

Book a flexible or refundable rate. Buy travel insurance before a storm is named — once a system has a name, cover for it is generally excluded. Make sure the policy includes medical evacuation, because an air ambulance to the United States starts around thirty thousand dollars and Jamaican private hospitals require payment before admission.

Leave a spare day in the itinerary, and note that you generally get several days’ warning: airports close and reopen, and airlines waive change fees when a storm is tracking. The 2026 season has so far been remarkably quiet — one named storm as of mid-August, and NOAA forecast a below-normal year of eight to fourteen named storms. But a quiet season still only needs one.

Where Jamaica Stands Now

A practical note on which parts of the island are trading normally in 2026, because it should influence when and where you book.

Open, and essentially back to normal

NegrilOcho RiosKingstonPort Antonio

Jamaica’s tourism sector reopened officially on 15 December 2025, and within eight weeks more than seventy per cent of the industry was operating. Airports and cruise ports never stopped. Dunn’s River Falls and Mystic Mountain reopened within weeks, Rick’s Cafe by mid-December. Over eighty thousand hotel workers are back at work.

Negril has rebounded strongly, with Seven Mile Beach renourished and new raised boardwalks installed. Ocho Rios was largely spared — Jamaica Inn stayed open right through the storm. Kingston and Portland saw only tropical-storm conditions and were reported entirely unaffected.

Montego Bay is the exception, and a significant one. The Hyatt Zilara and Ziva pair, both Secrets properties, Breathless, Dreams Rose Hall and Jewel Grande are all closed into 2027; Sandals Montego Bay and Sandals Caribbean Cay reopen on 18 December 2026. What is open there — Half Moon, Round Hill, the Iberostar and RIU properties, S Hotel — is genuinely good, but the choice is far narrower than the brochures suggest.

The south coast around Black River and St Elizabeth is the slowest to return and is not a sensible first-trip choice this year.

Our practical advice for 2026 and into 2027: shift your default from Montego Bay toward Negril or Ocho Rios, and whichever you choose, confirm directly with the property that your room category is open and that no construction is happening beside it. Reopening trackers published online go stale within weeks.

Best Time by Region

The calendar is broadly the same island-wide, but the character of each region shifts with it.

The north coast — Montego Bay, Ocho Rios

Wetter than the southBest Dec–AprCruise crowds Dec–Apr

Montego Bay averages about a thousand one hundred and thirty-five millimetres of rain a year, noticeably more than Kingston. December to April is the reliable window. Bear in mind that the cruise season also peaks then, so Ocho Rios town and Dunn’s River Falls are at their most crowded in exactly the months the weather is best — go to the falls before nine or after half past three.

Negril and the west

Best sunsetsDec–Apr driestFlat beach year-round

The same dry-season calendar, with the advantage that Seven Mile Beach is sheltered and usable in almost any weather. Sunset on the West End is the local ritual and is best from December through April when the evenings are clearest. See our Negril guide.

Kingston and the Blue Mountains

Hotter, drierOnly ~885mm rainMountains cool at night

Kingston sits on the drier southern side and gets around eight hundred and eighty-five millimetres of rain a year against Montego Bay’s eleven hundred, with humidity around seventy per cent rather than eighty. It runs a degree or two hotter but feels considerably more comfortable in summer. The Blue Mountains above it are cool enough at night to want a sweater in any month. See our Kingston guide.

Portland and the east

Greenest & wettestRain in every monthBest Feb–Apr

Portland is the wettest parish in Jamaica, which is precisely why it is the greenest and why the Rio Grande and Reach Falls exist at all. Expect some rain in any month; February to April is the driest stretch and the best time to plan rafting and the Blue Lagoon. See our Port Antonio guide.

The Cheapest Time to Go

The genuine bargains, ranked

1. Sept–Oct2. Late Apr–early Jun3. Second half of Nov4. First 10 days of Dec

September and October are the outright cheapest, and carry the highest risk and the most rain. Late April to early June is the best risk-adjusted value on the calendar and the one we would actually book. The second half of November is quietly excellent — the season is closing, the rain is easing, prices are still low and everything is reopening for winter. And the first ten days of December may be the best-kept secret in Caribbean travel: full dry-season weather, and the holiday rush has not started.

The months to pay peak prices for, if certainty matters more than money, are February and March.

Events & Public Holidays

Worth planning around, in both directions.

The dates that fill the island

Christmas & New YearEmancipation 1 AugIndependence 6 AugEaster

Christmas and New Year is the busiest and most expensive stretch of the year, and also the most joyful — book by the previous spring. Emancipation Day (1 August) and Independence Day (6 August) together form the biggest national celebration, with the Grand Gala in Kingston and heavy domestic travel all week. Easter moves each year — Good Friday fell on 3 April in 2026 — and the fortnight after it is when prices drop.

Other public holidays in 2026: Ash Wednesday 18 February, Labour Day 23 May (observed Monday the 25th), National Heroes’ Day 19 October, and Boxing Day 26 December.

The music, and one important change

Reggae SumfestCarnivalMoved for 2026

Reggae Sumfest normally runs for a week in Montego Bay each July and is the biggest reggae festival in the world. Because of Hurricane Melissa’s damage to the western region’s hotels, the 2026 edition was cut to a single night — ‘A Taste of Reggae Sumfest’, Saturday 18 July, at Plantation Cove in St Ann. Organisers stress that Montego Bay remains its home and the move is temporary; 2027 is provisionally 11 to 17 July back in Montego Bay. Confirm before you build a trip around it.

Carnival in Jamaica is Kingston-based and falls after Easter — 8 to 14 April in 2026, with the road march on Sunday the 12th.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best month to visit Jamaica?

February or March. They are the driest months of the year, the humidity is at its lowest, the sea is around eighty-one degrees, and the hurricane risk is nil. They are also expensive and busy, so book early. If you want that weather at a materially better price, the second half of January — after the Christmas premium has gone and before the spring-break rush — is the connoisseur's choice, and late April to early June is the outright best value of the year.

When is hurricane season in Jamaica, and how bad is it really?

The Atlantic season runs 1 June to 30 November, and the genuine peak of risk for Jamaica is mid-August through October. We are not going to soften that, because Jamaica's two worst modern storms both landed inside it: Gilbert on 12 September 1988, and Hurricane Melissa on 28 October 2025 — a Category 5 with 185 mph winds, the strongest hurricane ever recorded to strike the island. October is also the wettest month of the year, with six to seven inches of rain. June and July carry materially lower risk but not zero: Hurricane Beryl passed close by as a Category 4 on 3 July 2024. The 2026 season has so far been remarkably quiet — one named storm as of mid-August, and NOAA forecast a below-normal year — but a quiet season still only needs one storm to matter to your week.

When is Jamaica cheapest?

September and October, comfortably — and they are cheap for exactly the reason you would expect. Those are the wettest months and the historical peak of storm risk, and a lot of hotels use the window for refurbishment. If you are experienced, flexible, insured and happy to gamble, the value is real. If this is a once-in-a-decade trip, do not book it then. The honest sweet spot is late April to early June: rates drop hard after Easter and spring break, the weather is still very good, the sea is warm, and you are ahead of the serious hurricane window.

Is Jamaica open, and are the resorts rebuilt after Hurricane Melissa?

Jamaica is open — the tourism sector officially reopened on 15 December 2025, airports and cruise ports never stopped, and more than eighty thousand hotel workers are back at work. But the recovery is uneven, and where you are going matters. Negril, Ocho Rios, Kingston and Port Antonio are trading essentially normally; Kingston and the whole east were barely touched. Montego Bay is the exception: a large share of its best-known all-inclusives — the Hyatt Zilara and Ziva pair, both Secrets properties, Breathless, Dreams Rose Hall and Jewel Grande — remain closed into 2027, and Sandals Montego Bay does not reopen until 18 December 2026. Book by specific property name and confirm directly with the hotel.

How hot and humid does Jamaica get?

Less extreme than people fear, but the humidity is the real story rather than the temperature. On the north coast at Montego Bay, expect highs of 72 to 86°F in winter and 77 to 91°F in summer, with humidity around seventy-six to eighty per cent. Kingston, on the drier southern side, runs a degree or two warmer and noticeably less humid. The sea sits around 81°F early in the year and rises to 84°F from August through November, so it is genuinely swimmable all year round. For an older traveller the comfort issue in July and August is the combination of heat and humidity, not the thermometer reading — plan mornings out and afternoons in the shade.

What are the big events and holidays to plan around?

Christmas and New Year is the peak of the peak: highest prices, fullest resorts, and the liveliest time on the island. Emancipation Day (1 August) and Independence Day (6 August) together make the biggest national celebration of the year, with the Grand Gala in Kingston and busy domestic travel all week. Carnival is Kingston-based and falls after Easter — 8 to 14 April in 2026, with the road march on Sunday the 12th. Reggae Sumfest normally runs for a week in Montego Bay each July, but because of Melissa's damage to the western hotels the 2026 edition was cut to a single night at Plantation Cove in St Ann on 18 July. Organisers say Montego Bay remains its home and 2027 is provisionally 11 to 17 July — confirm before you build a trip around it.
