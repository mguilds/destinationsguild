Saltwater Fishing in Southern California — Surf, Piers, Half-Day Boats & the Deep Sea

From the free fishing on a public pier to a week at sea after tuna: Point Conception to the Mexican border, with the landings, the fares, the months and the rules that are in force right now.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the California Department of Fish and Wildlife and each landing, 4 November 2026

Southern California has the most fishing for the least trouble of any coast in the country. Its harbours run a fleet of working sportfishing boats that sail most days of the year. You turn up, pay about the price of a good dinner, rent a rod, and someone else finds the fish. And if that is too much, there is a public pier in most beach towns where anyone may drop a line with no licence at all.

This guide covers the coast below Point Conception by the way you want to fish it: the surf, the piers, the half-day boats and the deep sea. It is part of our California fishing guide. If you are starting from Orange County with a youngster, read a fishing day from Laguna Beach first. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

Surf fishing — a rod, a sand crab and a rising tide

The cheapest serious fishing here. A licence is needed on the sand from age 16.

How it works

What swims there. Surfperch, peaking September to May; yellowfin croaker from June to September; spotfin croaker and California corbina from July to September; California halibut from June to October. Those are CDFW’s own peak months.

How people fish it. CDFW recommends a 7- to 9-foot rod with 6- to 12-pound line and light bait or Carolina-rigged grubs, fished in the early morning or an hour before dusk, on an incoming high tide in mild to moderate surf.

Where. California State Parks names Bolsa Chica State Beach as a great place for surf fishing, for perch, corbina and croaker; Huntington State Beach, as long as no swimmers are near; San Clemente State Beach for bass, croaker, corbina and barred perch; and lists fishing at Doheny and San Onofre, where storm damage has brought closures, so check first.

Grunion. On certain spring and summer nights these small silver fish ride the waves onto the sand to spawn. You catch them with your bare hands, which is the point. It is the best free show on the coast for a child.

Confirmed with CDFW, 4 November 2026 — Grunion: limit 30; “No appliances of any kind may be used to take grunion, and no holes may be dug in the beach to entrap them.” It is “unlawful to take grunion from April 1 through June 30.” Surfperch: 20 in total, not more than 10 of one species. California corbina fall under the general limit of “No more than 20 finfish in combination of all species with not more than 10 of any one species.” Run dates: CDFW grunion page. Croaker limits other than corbina: check the booklet; we did not confirm them.

The pier rules

Confirmed with CDFW, 4 November 2026 — 2026 regulations: a licence is needed from age 16 “except for persons angling from a public pier in ocean or bay waters” (the Ocean booklet says “fishing from a public pier”). On a pier, “no person shall use more than two rods and lines, two hand lines, or two nets, traps or other appliances used to take crabs.” Jetties, breakwaters, promenades and sea walls are not public piers. Bag limits and sizes still apply. Lobster report cards are needed on a pier too. CDFW says it cannot guarantee any pier will be open.

Eating what you catch. The state’s health office (OEHHA) says “DO NOT EAT” white croaker from Santa Monica Beach to Seal Beach Pier, for all groups of people. Read the advisories for the pier you are on.

What a typical day looks like now

Counts are posted on the landings’ sites, and they are the best free guide there is. Newport, afternoon half-day, 2 November 2026: 41 anglers, 36 calico, 9 sand bass, 5 sheephead. Dana Point half-day, 3 November: 40 anglers, 11 bonito, 5 sheephead, 280 bass released. San Diego’s New Seaforth, 3 November: 120 yellowfin and 25 skipjack on the morning half-day, which is a very good day indeed and not one to expect.

Tips. Arrive 30 minutes to an hour before sailing. Outside alcohol and coolers are usually barred. Bring layers. Take seasickness remedies before you board, not after. Tip the crew; they will untangle your line all day.

Deep sea — three-quarter days, overnights and long range

The step up: further out, bigger fish, more money, and a real chance of being seasick.

Three-quarter and full days

Ten hours or so, to the islands and offshore banks, for yellowtail, rockfish in season and, in summer, tuna. Examples we read: Pierpoint’s Enterprise to Catalina, $95, 6 a.m. to 4 p.m.; Fisherman’s Landing’s Tomahawk, $155.25; Oceanside, $105; Channel Islands, $130–$145, to the islands; Santa Barbara Landing, about $145–$166.

Overnights

You sleep on board and wake on the fishing grounds. Pierpoint’s Toronado was listed at $180, $220 for tuna overnights; Ventura’s Endeavor at $289.80; Channel Islands at $280–$325. Dana Wharf, Davey’s Locker, Newport Landing, Long Beach and 22nd Street also list overnights.

Long range, from San Diego

Point Loma, H&M and Fisherman’s list multi-day and long-range boats, Point Loma of up to 16 days, in season for bluefin, yellowfin and the big fish of Mexican waters; Seaforth lists trips of up to two and a half days. On 3 November 2026 Fisherman’s Landing reported the Pacific Queen home with 149 yellowfin and 61 skipjack.

Crossing into Mexico. Many of these trips fish Mexican waters. H&M Landing lists a Mexican permit at about $20–$30. Ask your landing exactly what you need; we could not confirm the current Mexican rules for party boats from a Mexican government source. Keeping more than one day’s limit needs a Declaration for Multi-Day Fishing Trip, which your boat will handle.

Confirmed with CDFW and NOAA, 4 November 2026 — 2026 Ocean regulations: bluefin tuna, 2 a day, in addition to the 20-fish limit, “regardless of where taken”. Yellowfin and other tunas, 10, counted toward the 20. Albacore, 10 south of 34°27′ N. Skipjack, no limit. Yellowtail, Ten, 24-inch fork length, but “Five fish less than twenty-four inches” allowed. White seabass, 3, or 1 south of Point Conception between 15 March and 15 June, 28-inch minimum. Giant sea bass “May not be taken off California.”

Protected areas — know them before you cast

The rule that catches visitors out. Fishing inside a State Marine Reserve is against the law, from shore or boat.

The ones you are most likely to meet

Laguna Beach is the big one: no take from Abalone Point to Table Rock. See our Laguna guide. Point Vicente on the Palos Verdes Peninsula prohibits all fishing. Point Dume at Malibu has a reserve with no take. At Catalina, Casino Point and Blue Cavern Onshore are no-take, Long Point is a reserve, and Lover’s Cove allows hook and line from the Cabrillo Mole only. Batiquitos Lagoon in San Diego County is no-take.

Boat crews know these lines. If you are on your own, use CDFW’s interactive map.

Point Loma and Mission Bay for San Diego; Dana Point and Newport Beach for Orange County; San Pedro and Long Beach for Los Angeles; Oxnard, Ventura and Santa Barbara for the Channel Islands. A 6 a.m. boat is an early start from anywhere else, given Los Angeles traffic.

Questions

Do I need a licence to fish a pier in Southern California?

No, not on a public pier in ocean or bay waters, at any age. Every other rule still applies, you are limited to two rods, and a lobster report card is still required. Jetties and breakwaters are not public piers, so a licence is needed there from age 16.

What does a half-day fishing boat cost in Southern California?

The adult fares we read on 4 November 2026 ran from about $60 at Pierpoint in Long Beach to about $90 at San Diego and Dana Point, plus rod rental of about $15 to $25 and a one-day licence at $21.09. Fares change by boat and day, and some landings add a card or fuel charge.

When is the best time for yellowtail and tuna off Southern California?

Summer and early autumn. Dana Wharf’s month list puts bluefin and yellowtail from June, and yellowfin and dorado from July, tailing off in October. Bluefin are open all year, with a limit of 2 a day. In a warm year they stay later: on 3 November 2026 San Diego half-day boats were catching yellowfin.

Can I keep rockfish from a boat in Southern California right now?

Only some, and only in deep water. From 1 October to 31 December 2026, boats may take shelf and slope rockfish and lingcod seaward of the 50-fathom line, and nearshore rockfish, cabezon and greenlings are closed. From 1 January to 31 March groundfish is closed to boats. Shore anglers are exempt from these seasons.

Is there a limit on calico bass and sand bass?

Yes. Five in any combination of kelp, barred sand and spotted sand bass, with no more than 4 barred sand bass, and a 14-inch minimum length. Many of the bass on a half-day boat are released for that reason.

Is the Ocean Beach Pier open?

No. San Diego’s Ocean Beach Pier has been closed since October 2023, and the city plans to replace it. Oceanside and Malibu piers were partly open in November 2026, and the Gaviota Pier has been closed for years.

When can you catch grunion?

Grunion may be taken from 1 July to 31 March, by hand only, with no holes dug and no gear, and a limit of 30. April to June is closed, though you may watch. A licence is needed from 16. CDFW publishes the expected run dates.

What we could not check today, and will not pretend to have

The part of a fishing page most sites leave out.

Five honest gaps

Pier status. Few piers publish whether they are open today. Where the table says not confirmed, phone the city.

Two landings we left out. Marina del Rey Sportfishing’s website would not open safely on 4 November, and LA Harbor Sportfishing’s own counts stop in June 2025. Boats may well be running from both; we simply could not confirm them by our usual standard. No sportfishing boat is sailing from Malibu Pier.

Mexican paperwork. Ask the landing. The only official Mexican guide we could open is old and written for private boats.

White seabass alternate length. CDFW’s own pages print different figures for the measure of a cut fish. The total-length minimum of 28 inches is clear; if you are filleting at sea, ask the crew.

A warm autumn. The late tuna of November 2026 are not a normal year. We report them as counts, not as a promise.
