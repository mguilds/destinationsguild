Where to Stay in Holguín & Guardalavaca

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Cuba’s holiday coast, and the strangest case on the island. The beaches at Guardalavaca, Playa Esmeralda and Playa Pesquero are the best in the east and were built up for package tourism — and every single resort on them is barred to American travellers. Behind the sand is a quiet provincial capital, the colonial port of Gibara, and the bay where Columbus first stepped ashore in Cuba.

Before you book anything

This page carries no booking buttons, and that is deliberate. Our booking partner sells no accommodation in Cuba at all — we checked, city by city, and the searches returned properties in other countries entirely. We will not point a button at the wrong country to earn a commission, so this is a guide to where to stay rather than a shop. Book a casa particular directly, or through an agency that specialises in Cuba.

American readers — this coast needs the plain version. U.S. law does not permit tourism to Cuba, and travel must fall within an OFAC licence category. Under the widely used Support for the Cuban People category you are expected to stay in privately owned casas particulares rather than state hotels. The State Department’s Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List separately bars U.S. persons from lodging at, paying for lodging at, or booking a listed property, wherever the booking is made.

Holguín is the province where that bites hardest, because the whole point of the place is its resorts. We read the list as published in the Federal Register on 14 July 2025: the Holguín section runs to twenty-five entries and covers every beach resort in the province — Brisas Guardalavaca, Club Amigo Atlántico Guardalavaca and the Atlántico at Guardalavaca; Paradisus Río de Oro, its Royal Service wing and Sol Río de Luna y Mares at Playa Esmeralda; Hotel Playa Pesquero, Hotel Playa Costa Verde, Blau Costa Verde and the Iberostar Selection Holguín at Playa Pesquero; Memories Holguín, Sol Turquesa Beach and Hotel Holguín at Playa Yuraguanal. In the city, Hotel Pernik, Caballeriza and Villa El Bosque; inland and along the coast, Villa Mirador de Mayabe, Villa Don Lino, Villa Cayo Naranjo, Villa Cayo Saetía, Villa Pinares de Mayarí, Brisas Covarrubias at Puerto Padre and Hotel Miraflores at Moa. For an American traveller there is no lawful hotel in this province, on the beach or off it. A casa particular is not the thrifty option here; it is the only option. The list is revised from time to time, so check it yourself before you book. See the full Cuba chapter, and confirm the current position with OFAC and the State Department.

The places, one by one

Holguín province is really two places that happen to share a name: a string of resort beaches along the north coast, and a working inland city an hour away. Between them sit the small towns — Rafael Freyre, Banes, Gibara — which is where a traveller who cannot use the resorts will actually end up sleeping, and is no bad thing.

GuardalavacaTHE MAIN BEACH

The oldest and busiest of the three beaches, and the only one with a village attached — which is exactly why it is the one to choose.

  • A place, not an enclave Casas, paladares and shops outside the gates
  • Good swimming Sheltered, shallow, reef within reach
  • Diving and boats Operators work from the beach road
  • The resorts are closed to you All three on the prohibited list
Playa EsmeraldaTHE PRETTIEST

Two curved coves under low cliffs, four kilometres west of Guardalavaca. The best-looking beach in eastern Cuba and the emptiest of life.

  • The best sand Two sheltered coves, clear water
  • Bahía de Naranjo The bay and park are next door
  • Nothing outside the gates Built as an enclave; no village
  • Both hotels listed and emptied Melíá walked away in June 2026
Playa Pesquero & Playa YuraguanalTHE FAR END

The longest and quietest stretch, further west again, built late and built big. Lovely sand, and a long way from a shop.

  • Space The longest beach on the coast
  • Very quiet Few people, even in season
  • Isolated Taxi for everything, including dinner
  • Seven resorts, all listed And several dark since the closures
Holguín cityTHE CAPITAL

A city of squares an hour inland, with the airport and the bus station. Not pretty in the way Trinidad is pretty, and useful in a way Trinidad is not.

  • Casas and food Plenty of both, and cheap
  • The airport Sleep here before an early flight
  • Parque Calixto García The centre of city life
  • Little for a long stay One night is usually enough
GibaraTHE OLD PORT

Thirty-five kilometres north of the city, a quiet nineteenth-century port on a bright bay. The most characterful bed in the province.

  • Real character Colonial streets, a working harbour
  • Casas with views Over the bay and the hills
  • Two small beaches Enough for a swim, not a week
  • Off the transport grid Taxi in and out; little passing traffic

The kind of stay to look for

On a coast designed around all-inclusive resorts, the traveller who cannot use them has to plan differently — and ends up with the better holiday.

A casa in the village

Guardalavaca village and the roads inland at Rafael Freyre have private rooms, often with breakfast and dinner cooked by the family. This is the lawful stay for an American, and the one that puts money into private hands rather than the state’s.

A casa in Gibara

If you want somewhere to come back to in the evening rather than a beach to lie on, stay in Gibara and take taxis to the sand. Old houses, sea air, and a town that is awake after dark.

A night in the city

Book the city for the night before or after your flight. Holguín’s casas are the cheapest in the province and the airport is minutes away, which removes the worst risk on this coast — a dawn taxi that does not come.

Half board, agreed in advance

There is very little to eat at Playa Esmeralda or Playa Pesquero outside the resorts. Agree meals with your casa when you book, and treat a restaurant as a bonus rather than a plan.

Getting there, and getting about

Nearly everyone arrives by air. Frank País International (HOG), a few kilometres south of Holguín, was built for the charter traffic to these beaches and is the gateway for the whole province; it is roughly an hour from the airport to Guardalavaca by taxi. Coming overland from Havana is the better part of a day and a night, and the eastern run is the one most affected by the fuel shortage — schedules through 2026 have been unsettled, with routes suspended, refuelling stops added and frequencies cut, so book flexible where you can and reconfirm before you fly.

Within the province, taxis are the practical answer and should be agreed as a price before you get in. There is no useful public transport between the beaches, and the three of them are strung out along twenty-odd kilometres of coast road. If you plan to move between Guardalavaca, Gibara and the city more than once, it is usually cheaper to hire a driver for the day than to pay three separate fares, and your casa will know one.

Things worth doing

More than the beaches, and most of it within an hour of them.

Where Columbus landed

Christopher Columbus first stepped ashore in Cuba on 28 October 1492, at Playa Blanca in the Bahía de Bariay, about twenty kilometres east of Gibara. A small monument on the hillside marks the spot above a pale-sand beach. Gibarans will tell you the hill he described in his log is their own Silla de Gibara; the hill he meant is generally taken to be El Yunque, away at Baracoa. Go for the bay rather than the argument.

Chorro de Maíta

Inland from Guardalavaca, an excavated pre-Columbian burial ground under cover, with a site museum built over it and a reconstructed Taíno village across the road. It is the most substantial indigenous site open to visitors in Cuba, and a useful corrective to a chapter of the island’s story that is mostly told through what came after.

Gibara, and the city’s hill

Give Gibara a half day at least: the harbour, the squares, a swim off one of the two small beaches, and lunch looking at the water. The town is known out of all proportion to its size for its film festival. In Holguín itself, a long staircase climbs the Loma de la Cruz from the end of Calle Maceo to a cross on the summit and a view over the rooftops to the hills — go early, before the heat.

Birán

South-east of the city, the farm at Birán where Fidel Castro was born is preserved as a museum — the house, the outbuildings and the school. Whatever you make of the subject, it is an unusually concrete piece of twentieth-century history, and it explains a good deal about the man that the official version does not set out to.

Practicalities

The 2026 conditions apply here as everywhere on the island, and on an isolated coast they bite harder. Expect power cuts; the resorts ran generators, a casa may not. Expect water to be stored in a tank rather than piped continuously. Fuel is short, which is why taxis cost what they cost and why a driver may cancel. Bring what you cannot buy — medicines above all, but also sun cream, insect repellent and batteries, none of which can be relied on in a shop.

Carry cash. Cards drawn on U.S. banks do not work in Cuba at all, and other foreign cards work unreliably; readers arriving with no cash have been genuinely stuck. Euros and Canadian dollars are the easiest to change, and much of the private economy quietly prices in hard currency. Agree a price before a taxi moves and before a meal is cooked, and do not assume a posted rate is the rate you will be offered.

A last and particular warning for this province: do not assume a hotel brand you recognise means a hotel you may use, or even a hotel that is open. Several of the names on the beaches belong to international chains that have left the island, and the properties are on the U.S. prohibited list regardless of whose sign is on the gate.

Holguín & Guardalavaca — FAQ

Can Americans stay in the resorts at Guardalavaca or Playa Esmeralda?

No. Reading the State Department’s Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List as published in the Federal Register on 14 July 2025, the Holguín province section runs to twenty-five entries and takes in every beach resort on this coast — Brisas Guardalavaca, Club Amigo Atlántico Guardalavaca and the Atlántico at Guardalavaca itself; Paradisus Río de Oro, its Royal Service wing and Sol Río de Luna y Mares at Playa Esmeralda; Hotel Playa Pesquero, Hotel Playa Costa Verde, Blau Costa Verde and the Iberostar Selection Holguín at Playa Pesquero; Memories Holguín, Sol Turquesa Beach and Hotel Holguín at Playa Yuraguanal. Hotel Pernik, Caballeriza and Villa El Bosque in the city are on it too, as are Villa Mirador de Mayabe, Villa Don Lino, Villa Cayo Saetía and Villa Pinares de Mayarí. For an American traveller there is no lawful hotel in this province. A casa particular is the lawful stay, and under Support for the Cuban People it is the stay the licence expects anyway. The list is revised, so check it yourself before you book.

Guardalavaca, Playa Esmeralda or Playa Pesquero — which beach?

Guardalavaca if you want somewhere with a village attached. It is the oldest and busiest of the three, the sand is good, and there are casas, paladares and a dive shop within walking distance, which matters a great deal when the resorts are closed to you. Playa Esmeralda is the prettier beach — a pair of curved coves backed by low cliffs — but it was built as a resort enclave and has almost nothing outside the gates. Playa Pesquero is the longest and emptiest, and the furthest from anywhere. On this coast the honest advice is to base yourself at Guardalavaca or in a casa in Rafael Freyre and visit the other two for the day.

Is Holguín city worth a night?

One, and chiefly as a staging post. Holguín is a working provincial capital rather than a colonial set piece — a city of squares, with Parque Calixto García at its centre and a staircase climbing the Loma de la Cruz to a cross and a view over the rooftops. It has none of Trinidad’s prettiness or Santiago’s intensity. What it does have is casas, food, a bus station and the airport, and it is the natural pause between the beach and the rest of the island. If your flight is early, sleep here rather than at the coast.

How do I get to Holguín?

By air, almost certainly. Frank País International (HOG), a few kilometres south of the city, is the gateway for the whole beach coast and has long taken the charter traffic from Canada and Europe. It is a long way east by road — the better part of a day and a night from Havana — and domestic schedules have been unsettled through 2026 by the fuel shortage, with routes suspended, refuelling stops added and frequencies cut. Book flexible where you can and reconfirm before you fly. From the airport it is about an hour to Guardalavaca by taxi, and taxis are the practical way to move around the province.

Is Gibara worth the trip from the beach?

Yes, and it is the best half day on this coast. Gibara is a quiet fishing port thirty-five kilometres north of Holguín, founded in 1827 and once the main north-coast port of Oriente, spread around a bright bay below rugged hills. There are two small scoops of sand for a swim, a handful of casas if you want to stay, and a film festival that has given the town a reputation out of all proportion to its size. It is the opposite of a resort strip, and after a few days behind a fence that is exactly what you will want.

What happened to the Melíá hotels at Playa Esmeralda?

Melíá left them. On 3 June 2026 the Mallorcan chain announced it was immediately ending its management of fifteen hotels in Cuba linked to the military conglomerate GAESA, in the face of tightened U.S. sanctions, and both of the Playa Esmeralda properties were on that list — Paradisus Río de Oro and Sol Río de Luna y Mares. The company said the impact would be limited because nearly all of the fifteen had already closed in the tourism collapse. Iberostar had stepped back from the island before it. So the name over the door at Playa Esmeralda has changed or gone dark, and in any case all three properties there remain on the Prohibited Accommodations List. Do not assume a brand you recognise is still running a Cuban hotel.
