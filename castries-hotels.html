Castries

Where to Stay in Castries

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Castries is the capital, the harbour and the working heart of Saint Lucia — a town of markets, minibuses and ships rather than hotels. Almost nobody sleeps in the centre itself. What people mean by staying in Castries is the coast immediately either side of it: Vigie and the small airport on the north side of the harbour, and then the string of sheltered bays running north towards Gros Islet — Choc Bay, Labrelotte Bay, Bois d’Orange — where several of the larger resorts have a beach to themselves. You get the shortest drive on the island to the centre of things, a real town five minutes away, and a quieter evening than Rodney Bay.

The areas, and which one suits you

Four distinct places go under the name Castries, and they are not interchangeable.

Downtown and the harbour

The covered market, Derek Walcott Square, the cathedral and the two cruise berths at Pointe Seraphine and La Place Carenage. It is busy by day, closed up by evening, and there are very few places for a visitor to stay. Come here for the market on a Saturday morning, not for a bed.

Vigie and the airport peninsula

The headland on the north side of the harbour, with George F. L. Charles airport and Vigie Beach along one side of it. Convenient if you are flying out on a regional connection or want to be able to walk into town, and the beach is long and shaded in parts. The trade-off is aircraft noise.

Choc Bay and Bois d’Orange

A few minutes north of town on the coast road: calm water, a shallow entry, a scattering of hotels and apartments, and prices below the headline resorts. A good middle ground if you want a beach, a supermarket and an easy taxi both to town and to Rodney Bay.

Labrelotte Bay

A private-feeling bay about fifteen minutes north of Castries, almost entirely given over to one large hillside resort. If you want to arrive, stop and have everything on site — with Castries and Rodney Bay both a short drive away when you want them — this is the spot. The hillside means steps or a buggy.

What Castries is not

It is not a resort strip and it is not pretty in the postcard sense. The town is a port that works for a living. Treat it as the convenient, central, slightly cheaper base it is, and go to Soufrière for scenery.

Getting about

Minibuses leave the centre for the north and the south all day and cost very little. Taxi fares are set by zone — agree the figure before you set off. The drive to Rodney Bay is about twenty-five minutes, to Soufrière about an hour and a quarter, and to the international airport at Vieux Fort about ninety minutes.

What your money is worth in Castries
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Saint Lucia uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged at EC$2.70 to the US dollar, so a price of EC$27 is about ten US dollars. US dollars are accepted in most hotels and taxis, and change often comes back in local money. Castries is the cheapest place on the island to eat and shop: the market, the bakeries and the small cook-shops near the waterfront feed the town, not the tourists, and are priced accordingly. Supermarkets in the northern suburbs are well stocked and useful if you have a kitchen. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills — ask what is included when you book.

Windjammer Landing Resort & Residences

Labrelotte BayAll-inclusive availableHillside

Windjammer Landing sits on its own bay at Labrelotte Bay, with a Castries post box address, and its own site describes it as spread over sixty acres of the island’s north-west coast with a long stretch of sand at the bottom. Accommodation runs from guest rooms of roughly four to six hundred square feet up to multi-bedroom hilltop villas with their own pools, along with a separate residences collection, and the resort offers all-inclusive stays as well as room-only. It is built up a hillside, which is where the views come from and also where the steps do, so ask about the buggy service and the walk to the beach when you book.

Travelling in your later years

Castries is the flattest and most convenient base on the island, with one caveat.

The town itself is level, the coast road north is easy, and being twenty minutes from the market, the hospital and the regional airport is worth a great deal if you would rather not spend hours in a car. The caveat is that several of the hotels on this stretch are built into hillsides above their bays, with steps or buggies between the room, the restaurant and the sand. Before booking, ask how many steps there are between the room and the dining room, whether there is a lift or a buggy, and how far it is to the water. The flat-ground options are along Choc Bay and at Vigie. Arriving at the international airport at Vieux Fort means a ninety-minute drive north, so if that is a long day for you, consider a first night in the south or a regional flight into Castries instead.

Questions about staying in Castries

Is Castries a good place to stay, or just a port?

Both, honestly. The downtown has almost nothing for a visitor to sleep in and shuts early, but the coast immediately north of it - Vigie, Choc Bay, Bois d'Orange, Labrelotte Bay - has beaches, hotels and supermarkets, and puts you within half an hour of nearly everything in the north of the island. It is the practical base rather than the beautiful one. If scenery is what you came for, split your week with a few nights near Soufriere.

How far is Castries from the airport?

It depends which airport your ticket means. George F. L. Charles airport is on the Vigie peninsula at the edge of town, a few minutes from the northern hotels, and handles regional flights. Hewanorra International at Vieux Fort, which takes the long-haul flights, is at the far southern tip of the island and about ninety minutes away by road on a winding route. Check which code is on your booking before you arrange a transfer.

Can you walk into town from the hotels?

From Vigie, yes - it is a walk of fifteen or twenty minutes around the harbour, and plenty of people do it in the cooler part of the day. From Choc Bay and further north it is too far and too hot; take a minibus from the main road or a taxi, both of which are quick and cheap. Within the downtown everything a visitor wants is within a few blocks of the two cruise berths.

Is Castries Market worth it?

Yes, and it costs nothing. The covered market and the streets around it are at their fullest on Saturday morning: fruit, root vegetables, spices, baskets, hot food and a good deal of noise. It is the most ordinary and most alive hour you will spend on the island, and it is five minutes from the ships. Go early, and carry small local notes.

Where is the beach in Castries?

Vigie Beach runs along the airport peninsula and is long, calm and partly shaded. North of town, Choc Bay and Labrelotte Bay have sheltered sand with easy entry, and Reduit Beach at Rodney Bay - the best on the island - is twenty-five minutes away. The harbour itself is a working port, not a swimming spot.

Is it safe to walk around Castries?

The centre is busy and ordinary by day and the usual town sense applies: keep valuables out of sight, use a taxi after dark rather than walking unlit streets, and agree the fare first. The quiet streets around the harbour empty out in the evening once the ships have gone, and most visitors eat in the northern suburbs or at their hotel rather than downtown at night.
