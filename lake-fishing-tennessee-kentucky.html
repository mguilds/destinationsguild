Lake Fishing in Tennessee & Kentucky — Six Great Lakes and the Towns Beside Them

Kentucky Lake, Lake Barkley, Dale Hollow, Norris, Chickamauga and Lake Cumberland: what swims in each, the 2026 rules from both states, which licence you need where the lakes cross the state line, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency and Kentucky Fish and Wildlife, 11 November 2026

Tennessee and Kentucky share some of the biggest man-made lakes in the East. The Tennessee and Cumberland rivers were dammed by the TVA and the Army Corps of Engineers, and what they left behind is hundreds of thousands of acres of water: crappie in the flooded timber, largemouth along the ledges, smallmouth on rocky points, stripers in deep cold water and catfish everywhere. Three of the six lakes on this page cross the state line, and that is where most visiting anglers get confused about licences. We explain it plainly below.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, bass anglers towing a boat, crappie fishermen and anyone who wants a lake, a cabin and perhaps a fish for supper. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 11 November 2026 from the agency that sets it — the Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency (TWRA) and the Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources — and is dated. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

Before you go: which licence, where the lakes cross the line

Kentucky Lake and Dale Hollow sit on both sides of the border. Lake Barkley does too, but it is not covered by the shared licence.

The reciprocal agreement, in plain words

Kentucky Lake: a licence from either state lets you fish the middle of the lake, from Eggner’s Ferry Bridge (US 68) in Kentucky south to the Governor Ned McWherter Bridge (US 79) in Tennessee, with its bays and creeks — except the Blood River embayment on the Kentucky side, which needs a Kentucky licence.

Dale Hollow: only the Wolf River arm is shared. The rest of the lake is Tennessee water, and you need a Tennessee licence for it.

Lake Barkley: not shared. Fish the Kentucky part on a Kentucky licence and the Tennessee part on a Tennessee licence.

Whose rules? In a boat, the rules of the state that licensed you. On the bank, the rules of the state you are standing in.

Confirmed with TWRA and Kentucky Fish and Wildlife, 11 November 2026 — Kentucky Fish and Wildlife: “Kentucky anglers licensed in Kentucky may fish all Tennessee waters on the portion of Kentucky Lake from Eggner’s Ferry Bridge (U.S. 68 and KY 80) in Kentucky south to the Governor Ned McWhorter Bridge (U.S. 79 and TN 76) in Tennessee without buying a Tennessee fishing license”; “Blood River is not included in the bordering waters zone and will require a Kentucky non-resident fishing license to fish.” TWRA’s 2026-27 regulations: Dale Hollow “Reciprocal waters are the Wolf River arm of the lake”; “Anglers must obey the regulations of the state in which they are licensed unless they are fishing from the bank.” Kentucky Fish and Wildlife announced on 17 October 2025 that the agreements were back in place from 16 October 2025. Neither agency lists Lake Barkley.

These are warm southern lakes with no ice season. Spring is the big time; autumn is the quiet, beautiful one.

Kentucky Lake — the crappie capital, across two states

The Tennessee River, dammed at Gilbertsville, Kentucky: about 160,000 acres, two-thirds of it in Tennessee.

Kentucky Lake, from Gilbertsville to Paris Landing

What swims there. Largemouth bass and crappie, above all — TWRA calls both “abundant”. Kentucky Fish and Wildlife’s 2026 forecast adds that smallmouth fishing is “the best it has been in 20 years at least” and that white bass fishing “hasn’t been this good since the 1980’s”. Sauger, stripers, catfish and redear sunfish too.

How people fish it. Crappie on brush piles and stake beds in spring and autumn; bass on the main-lake ledges and in the creek bays; catfish in the channel. It is big water — wind on the open lake can be rough for a small boat.

Keeping a fish. Crappie must be 10 inches, on both sides of the line. Largemouth and smallmouth must be 15 inches on both sides. Tennessee allows 20 crappie a day here; Kentucky’s statewide crappie limit is also 20.

Silver carp. Invasive carp are thick in Kentucky and Barkley lakes, and they jump. TWRA says moving live carp is illegal in Tennessee.

Confirmed with TWRA and Kentucky Fish and Wildlife, 11 November 2026 — TWRA 2026-27, Kentucky Lake: “Black Bass: Five (5) per day in combination”; “Largemouth/Smallmouth Bass: 15 inch minimum length limit”; “Crappie (all species): 20 per day in combination, 10 inch minimum length limit”; “Sauger: 10 per day, 15 inch minimum length limit”; “Paddlefish: Two (2) per day; season is open from April 24 through May 31.” Kentucky guide, Kentucky and Barkley lakes: “Crappie: 10-inch minimum size limit”; “Largemouth and Smallmouth Bass: 15-inch minimum size limit.” TWRA invasive carp page: “Silver Carp are most abundant in Kentucky Reservoir”; “It is illegal to move live carp in Tennessee.”

Nearest towns: Gilbertsville and Grand Rivers at the Kentucky end; Paris and Buchanan (Paris Landing State Park) on the Tennessee side.

Lake Barkley — the quieter twin

The Cumberland River, running parallel to Kentucky Lake with the Land Between the Lakes in between, joined to it by a canal at Grand Rivers.

Lake Barkley, from Kuttawa to Dover

What swims there. Largemouth bass, crappie and big blue catfish. TWRA calls Barkley “a well-kept secret among anglers”, in Kentucky Lake’s shadow. Kentucky Fish and Wildlife’s 2026 forecast reports “good numbers of 10- to 20-pounders with the occasional 50+ pounder” among the blue catfish.

How people fish it. Much like Kentucky Lake — crappie on cover, bass in the bays and on the ledges, catfish in the river channel — with fewer boats. Most of the lake, about 47,500 acres, is in Kentucky; about 10,350 acres are in Tennessee.

Licence. Barkley is not reciprocal water: fish the side your licence covers.

Confirmed with TWRA and Kentucky Fish and Wildlife, 11 November 2026 — TWRA 2026-27, Barkley (Tennessee portion): same bass, crappie (“20 per day in combination, 10 inch minimum length limit”), sauger and striper limits as Kentucky Lake, and “The dewatering areas are closed to fishing five (5) days prior to and during the late waterfowl season.” Kentucky guide: crappie 10-inch minimum and largemouth and smallmouth 15-inch minimum on Kentucky and Barkley lakes and their connecting canal. Catfish: Tennessee allows only one catfish over 34 inches a day; Kentucky allows one blue or flathead of 35 inches and over a day, and one channel catfish of 28 inches and over.

Nearest towns: Kuttawa, Eddyville and Cadiz in Kentucky; Dover in Tennessee.

Dale Hollow Lake — the world-record smallmouth lake

The Obey River, dammed near Celina, Tennessee: 27,700 acres of clear, deep water with 620 miles of shoreline, reaching into Kentucky.

Dale Hollow, from Celina, Byrdstown and Burkesville

What swims there. Smallmouth bass first. TWRA: “Dale Hollow has long been home to the famous state and world record smallmouth bass caught in 1955.” Kentucky Fish and Wildlife’s 2026 forecast reports “good numbers of fish across all sizes, with fish over 3 pounds available”, and walleye “with many fish over 20 inches”. Largemouth, crappie, rainbow and brown trout, and muskellunge too.

How people fish it. Light line and patience, in very clear water. Houseboats and pontoons from the marinas, and long wooded shores.

Keeping a fish. Both states use the same smallmouth rule: only two smallmouth a day, and fish between 16 and 21 inches go back — one may be under 16 inches and one over 21. Tennessee allows no more than four rods per angler here.

Eating the fish. Tennessee’s 2026 advisory lists a precautionary mercury advisory for walleye and black bass from the Tennessee part of the lake: children and pregnant or nursing women should not eat them, and everyone else should limit them to about one meal a month.

Confirmed with TWRA, Kentucky Fish and Wildlife and TDEC, 11 November 2026 — TWRA 2026-27, Dale Hollow: “Black Bass: Five (5) per day in combination, only two may be Smallmouth Bass”; “Smallmouth Bass: Two (2) per day, 16–21 inch PLR. One (1) fish may be under 16 inches and one (1) may be over 21 inches”; “Largemouth Bass: 15 inch minimum length limit”; “Crappie (all species): 15 per day in combination, 10 inch minimum length limit”; “Walleye: Five (5) per day, 16 inch minimum length limit”; “Muskellunge: One (1) per day, 50 inch minimum length limit”; “Trout: Seven (7) per day”; “No more than 4 rods and reels per angler may be used.” Kentucky guide, Dale Hollow: “Smallmouth Bass: 16- to 21-inch protective slot limit”; “Crappie: 10-inch minimum size limit, 15-fish daily limit”; “Walleye: 16-inch minimum size limit, 5-fish daily limit.” TDEC, Posted Streams, Rivers and Reservoirs 2026 (rev. August 2026): Dale Hollow, mercury, “Precautionary advisory for walleye and black bass.”

Nearest towns: Celina, at the dam; Byrdstown, at the eastern end; Burkesville, on the Kentucky side.

Norris Lake — smallmouth, walleye and big stripers in the hills

A TVA lake on the Clinch and Powell rivers north of Knoxville: 34,200 acres of deep, clear mountain water.

Norris, from LaFollette and Rocky Top

What swims there. TWRA: “Norris is noted for its Smallmouth Bass fishing” and “contains one of the better Walleye populations in the region.” Striped bass grow huge here — the lake gave Tennessee a 49.5-pound state-record striper in 1978 — and TWRA stocks crappie every year. Largemouth, spotted bass and muskellunge too.

How people fish it. Trolling and live bait for stripers and walleye; finesse fishing for smallmouth on the rocky banks; crappie on brush. Many marinas, and a state park at the dam.

Rules that change with the season. Smallmouth: one a day at 18 inches from 1 June to 15 October; five a day at 15 inches the rest of the year. Stripers: two a day at 15 inches from 1 April to 31 October; one a day at 36 inches from 1 November to 31 March — the winter rule is in force now. Doakes Pond, by Highway 63, is closed to fishing.

Eating the fish. TDEC’s 2026 advisory lists a precautionary mercury advisory for largemouth, smallmouth, striped bass and sauger in the Clinch River arm, from the Powell River up to Indian Creek.

Confirmed with TWRA and TDEC, 11 November 2026 — TWRA 2026-27, Norris: “Largemouth Bass: 14 inch minimum length limit”; smallmouth “June 1–Oct. 15: One (1) per day, 18 inch minimum length limit” and “Oct. 16–May 31: Five (5) per day, 15 inch minimum length limit”; “Crappie (all species): 10 per day in combination, 10 inch minimum length limit”; striped bass “April 1–Oct. 31: Two (2) per day, 15 inch minimum length limit” and “Nov. 1–March 31: One (1) per day, 36 inch minimum length limit”; “Walleye/Sauger: Five (5) per day in combination, 15 inch minimum length limit”; “Muskellunge: One (1) per day, 36 inch minimum length limit.” Exceptions page: Doakes Pond “Closed to fishing.” TWRA Norris page: “Norris yielded a 49.5 lb state record striped bass in April 1978.”

Nearest towns: LaFollette, on the northern shore; Rocky Top (the town once called Lake City), near Norris Dam State Park.

Chickamauga Lake — big largemouth on Chattanooga’s doorstep

The Tennessee River above Chickamauga Dam, from Chattanooga north past Dayton: 36,200 acres and 810 miles of shoreline.

Chickamauga, from Chattanooga, Harrison and Dayton

What swims there. Largemouth bass are the reason most people come, and TWRA notes the lake “has been ranked nationally by media sources as a top destination for crappie fishing.” Smallmouth, spotted bass, stripers, walleye, sauger, bluegill, redear and catfish too.

How people fish it. Grass beds and shallow flats in spring, ledges in summer; crappie on brush. A city lake at one end and a country lake at the other, with a state park at Harrison Bay.

Keeping a fish. Largemouth must be 15 inches, five black bass a day. Smallmouth and spotted bass have no minimum, but only one of them over 16 inches a day. The Hiwassee Refuge is closed from 15 November to the end of February, except the wildlife viewing area; fishing is allowed in the main river channel.

Confirmed with TWRA, 11 November 2026 — TWRA 2026-27, Chickamauga: “Black Bass: Five (5) fish per day in combination”; “Largemouth: 15 inch minimum length limit”; smallmouth, spotted, Alabama bass and their hybrids “no minimum length limit”, “Only one (1) may be over 16 inches per day”; “Crappie (all species): 15 per day in combination, 10 inch minimum length limit”; “Hiwassee Refuge is closed to all forms of use and trespass from Nov. 15 through the last day of Feb., except the wildlife viewing area.” TWRA Chickamauga page (29 September 2026): “The current regulation for largemouth bass at Chickamauga is 5 fish/15-inch minimum per day.”

Nearest towns: Chattanooga, by the dam; Harrison, by Harrison Bay State Park; Dayton, at the upper end.

Lake Cumberland — stripers in deep blue water

The Cumberland River behind Wolf Creek Dam in south-central Kentucky — long, deep and clear, with steep wooded shores.

Lake Cumberland, from Jamestown, Russell Springs and Somerset

What swims there. Kentucky Fish and Wildlife: “well known for it’s Smallmouth, Largemouth, Striper and Walleye Population.” Its 2026 forecast reports walleye in “good numbers” in the 17- to 20-inch range.

How people fish it. Stripers on live bait and trolled lures, often at night in summer and near the surface in the cool months; bass on the points and bluffs; walleye deep. Houseboats are a way of life here.

Keeping a fish. Stripers: two a day, 22 inches or longer. Smallmouth 18 inches, largemouth 15, crappie 10. Lake sturgeon go straight back.

Confirmed with Kentucky Fish and Wildlife, 11 November 2026 — Kentucky Fishing & Boating Guide, March 2026 – February 2027, Lake Cumberland: “Crappie: 10-inch minimum size limit”; “Lake Sturgeon: Catch and release only”; “Largemouth Bass: 15-inch minimum size limit”; “Smallmouth Bass: 18-inch minimum size limit”; “Striped Bass: 22-inch minimum size limit, 2-fish daily limit.” Walleye follow the statewide rule: six a day, 14 inches. Kentucky Fish and Wildlife’s consumption advice: “All Kentucky waters are under an advisory for mercury.”

Nearest towns: Jamestown and Russell Springs on the western half; Somerset to the east.

The licences and the limits, plainly

Two states, two licences, two sets of rules. Children fish free in both — up to 12 in Tennessee, up to 15 in Kentucky.

Tennessee (TWRA)

Who needs one. No licence for ages 12 and under. Ages 13 to 15 need a junior licence; 16 and over need an adult one.

For a visitor. Non-resident fishing, not including trout: 3-day $20, 10-day $30, annual $49. Including trout: 3-day $40, 10-day $61, annual $98. Annual licences run 365 days from the day you buy.

For a Tennessean. TWRA sells no fishing-only annual licence for adults; the basic annual is the Combination Hunt/Fish at $33 (ages 16–64), and trout need a $21 supplement. A one-day fishing licence (no trout) is $6.

Prices are going up. In August 2026 the Tennessee Fish and Wildlife Commission approved increases “most in the 27-29 percent range” (the Combination from $33 to $42), still to be approved by a legislative committee. We could not find an effective date. Check the price when you buy.

Confirmed with TWRA, 11 November 2026 — licence pages: “No license is required for ages 12 and under”; “anglers ages 13 to 15 are required to have a Junior Fish/Hunt license”; “Annual licenses and permits are valid 365 days from the date of purchase unless otherwise noted.” Fees as listed above; processing fees are added. TWRA news, 21 August 2026, on the new licence structure.

Kentucky (Kentucky Fish and Wildlife)

Who needs one. Everyone 16 and over. Residents and visitors aged 15 and younger fish free.

For a visitor. Non-resident 1-day $15.86, 7-day $37.00, annual $58.14. A trout permit ($10.57) is needed only to keep trout.

For a Kentuckian. Annual $24.31, 1-day $7.40, three-year $58.14.

The licence year runs 1 March to the last day of February. Free Fishing Days are the first Saturday and Sunday in June (6–7 June in 2026; the 2027 dates were not yet published).

Confirmed with Kentucky Fish and Wildlife, 11 November 2026 — fee page: “Kentucky’s license year begins March 1 and continues through the last day of February”; licence page: “Resident and nonresident youth ages 15 and younger are not required to purchase licenses or permits to fish”; guide: “Unless license exempt, anglers who intend to keep trout must have a trout permit.” Prices change. Buy from the agency or a licensed agent.

Questions

Do I need a Tennessee or a Kentucky licence to fish Kentucky Lake?

On the shared middle of the lake, one licence from either state will do. Under the reciprocal agreement published by both TWRA and Kentucky Fish and Wildlife, anglers licensed in either state may fish Kentucky Lake between Eggner’s Ferry Bridge (US 68 / KY 80) in Kentucky and the Governor Ned McWherter Bridge (US 79 / TN 76) in Tennessee, including its embayments and tributaries, except the Blood River embayment in Kentucky. In a boat you follow the rules of the state that licensed you; from the bank you follow the rules of the state you are standing in. Outside that zone you need the licence of the state you are in.

Does the reciprocal licence cover Lake Barkley?

No. Neither TWRA nor Kentucky Fish and Wildlife lists Lake Barkley as reciprocal water. The agreements cover Kentucky Lake between the two bridges, the Wolf River arm of Dale Hollow, and a stretch of the Big South Fork. On Barkley, buy the licence of the state whose water you fish.

What are the smallmouth bass rules on Dale Hollow Lake?

Both states use the same rule. Five black bass a day in combination, of which only two may be smallmouth. Smallmouth between 16 and 21 inches are protected: of your two, one may be under 16 inches and one over 21. Largemouth must be 15 inches. That is the 2026-27 TWRA guide and Kentucky Fish and Wildlife’s March 2026 to February 2027 guide.

What is the striped bass limit on Lake Cumberland?

Two a day, and each must be at least 22 inches long. Kentucky Fish and Wildlife’s March 2026 to February 2027 guide also sets an 18-inch minimum for smallmouth and 15 inches for largemouth on Lake Cumberland, a 10-inch minimum for crappie, and catch-and-release only for lake sturgeon.

Can I keep a striped bass on Norris Lake in winter?

Only a big one. TWRA’s 2026-27 rule for Norris is two stripers a day at 15 inches from 1 April to 31 October, and from 1 November to 31 March one a day that must be at least 36 inches. Smallmouth on Norris are one a day at 18 inches from 1 June to 15 October, and five a day at 15 inches the rest of the year.

Five honest gaps

Lake levels and boat ramps. These lakes are drawn down every winter, and some ramps are short of water until spring. We did not find a 2026 list of ramp closures from TVA or the Army Corps of Engineers. Check TVA (Kentucky, Norris, Chickamauga) or the Corps (Barkley, Dale Hollow, Cumberland) before you tow a boat.

Kentucky’s state resort parks. Kenlake, Lake Barkley, Dale Hollow Lake and Lake Cumberland state resort parks all sit on these lakes, but their pages would not load properly for us today, so we have not described their lodges or marinas. Check Kentucky State Parks directly.

Fishing guides. Every lake has guides, but we could not find one today with a website of its own that we could open. We will add them at the same standard.

Tennessee’s new prices and 2027 free days. Tennessee’s licence increase has no effective date yet, and neither state had published its 2027 free fishing days.

Park entry and launch fees. We did not confirm them. Check with each park or marina.
