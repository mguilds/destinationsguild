Abu Dhabi’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Abu Dhabi — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Abu Dhabi is calmer than Dubai, cheaper for the same standard of room, and arranged across a main island and a string of smaller ones. The decision is really between four places: the Corniche and the island centre for the city itself, Saadiyat for the museums and the best beaches, Yas for the theme parks and the Grand Prix circuit, and the inland business districts for value. The Corniche & the island centre

The heart of the city and the easiest base for seeing it. The Corniche is an eight-kilometre landscaped seafront with a cycle path, public beaches and cafés, and behind it sits the dense grid of the old downtown with the souks, the restaurants and the bus station. The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is about fifteen minutes away by taxi. This is the walkable Abu Dhabi.

Emirates Palace Mandarin Oriental, Abu Dhabi

$$$$ LuxuryA landmark in its own right1.3 km private beachVast grounds

Built as a statement and still the most photographed hotel in the capital: a kilometre of domed sandstone at the west end of the Corniche, with its own marina, a long private beach and gardens you need a buggy to cross. Mandarin Oriental took over the management and the service is now as considered as the architecture. Rooms are enormous. Best for a traveller who wants the grandest address in Abu Dhabi and does not mind that the scale is the point.

Al Maryah Island & the business city

A small island just off the centre, redeveloped as the financial district, with a mall, a waterfront promenade and a handful of serious hotels. It is quiet in the evening and at the weekend, five minutes from the old downtown by taxi, and the best place in the capital to eat well.

Rosewood Abu Dhabi

$$$$ LuxuryAl Maryah IslandRooftop pool and viewsExcellent restaurants

The pick of the business-district hotels and, for many travellers, the best all-round luxury hotel in the city — more interesting than a chain tower and far more central than the island resorts. Rooms look across the water to the skyline, the rooftop pool is superb at dusk, and the restaurants draw residents rather than only guests. Best for a stay that mixes the city, good food and a pool.

Saadiyat Island — the museums and the best beaches

Ten minutes from the centre and a different world: a low-rise island of beach resorts, golf and the cultural district, with the finest natural beach in the Emirates — soft white sand where hawksbill turtles nest, which is why building near it is restricted. Louvre Abu Dhabi is here. It is the best combination in the country of a genuine beach and genuine sightseeing.

The St. Regis Saadiyat Island Resort, Abu Dhabi

$$$$ LuxuryOn Saadiyat beachGolf alongsideFamily facilities

A large, formal, Mediterranean-styled resort set directly on the Saadiyat sand, with a long beach, several pools, a golf course next door and the cultural district a few minutes’ drive away. Rooms are big and most have a sea view and a balcony. It handles families and couples equally well. Best for a beach week with the Louvre and the Grand Mosque on the itinerary.

Yas Island

The entertainment island, next to the airport: the Formula 1 circuit, Ferrari World, Yas Waterworld, SeaWorld and a mall, with the hotels among them. Excellent for a family or a race weekend, and a long way from the Corniche, the mosque and the museums — about half an hour each way. Rates spike enormously for the Grand Prix.

The hotel that straddles the Yas Marina Circuit, under a curved shell of coloured lights that changes through the evening — a few rooms genuinely overlook the track. Loud, stylish and busy, with the theme parks and the mall within walking distance and the airport ten minutes away. Best for a race weekend or a family doing the parks; poor for anyone whose plan is the old city.

Value & the airport

Abu Dhabi is already cheaper than Dubai for the same class of hotel, and the inland business districts take it further. You give up the sea view and gain a reliable room for roughly what a bad one costs on the coast. Both of these are a short taxi ride from the centre and very close to the airport.

Premier Inn Abu Dhabi Capital Centre

$ BudgetBy the exhibition centrePoolConsistent rooms

Beside the national exhibition centre, about fifteen minutes from the Corniche and ten from the airport. It is exactly what the name promises: a clean, quiet, properly air-conditioned room with a decent bed and a small pool, at a price nothing on the seafront can match. Best for a short stay, a conference, or a family counting the cost of a week.

Where to stay in Abu Dhabi — questions

Abu Dhabi or Dubai — which should I stay in?

Abu Dhabi for museums, the Grand Mosque, better beaches and better value; Dubai for the spectacle, the shopping and the sheer choice of restaurants and hotels. They are roughly ninety minutes apart by road, so if you have a week you can reasonably see both from a single base, though two bases save a lot of driving. For a short first trip most people pick Dubai; travellers on a second visit, and those travelling with an interest in art or architecture, often prefer Abu Dhabi and are surprised by how much they like it.

Which area is best for seeing the Grand Mosque and the Louvre?

They are at opposite ends of the city, so the honest answer is the centre, which is between them. From a Corniche or city-centre hotel the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is about fifteen minutes by taxi and Louvre Abu Dhabi on Saadiyat about twenty. Staying on Saadiyat puts the Louvre within walking or shuttle distance and leaves the mosque a half-hour drive. Staying on Yas leaves both of them a long way off.

Is Saadiyat worth it for the beach?

If the beach matters to you, yes — it is the best natural beach in the country, wide and soft and pale, and it is protected because hawksbill turtles nest on it between roughly March and June. Hotels on it are expensive and the island is deliberately low-density, so there is little to walk to in the evening beyond your own resort. There are public access points to the beach for a modest fee if you are staying elsewhere.

Do I need a car in Abu Dhabi?

Less than you might think, but more than in Dubai. There is no metro, so a car is the only way to move freely between the island centre, Saadiyat, Yas and the mosque without relying on taxis. Taxis are, however, metered, abundant and cheap, and the city's buses are inexpensive and cover the main island well. For a stay in the centre, taxis are usually the cheaper and simpler answer; for a stay on Yas or Saadiyat with plans across the city, a hire car earns its keep.

When do hotel rates spike?

Around the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix in late November, when Yas Island in particular can go to several times its normal rate and the whole city tightens, and over Christmas and New Year. National Day on 2 December is busy too. The cheapest months are July and August, when the heat is severe. Our best time to visit page covers the year in detail.

Is there anywhere to stay near the desert?

Yes, inland and to the south, where the dunes rise towards the Empty Quarter around the Liwa oases, and at Al Ain near the Omani border. Both are a long drive from the city — well over two hours to Liwa — and our booking partner does not list hotels in either place, so we have not put a name to one here. If a desert night is the point of your trip, the easier options are the conservation reserves within reach of Dubai, or the Al Wadi desert reserve in Ras Al Khaimah.
