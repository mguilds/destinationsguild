When to Go to the United Kingdom

The Best Time to Visit the United Kingdom

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Short answer: the shoulder seasons — roughly May–June and September–October — are the sweet spot. You get the mildest, driest weather Britain offers and long daylight without the summer-holiday crowds and peak prices. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the crowds, and the best timing for London, Scotland, and the coast.

The United Kingdom is a year-round destination, but it is not the same destination year-round. The country you meet on a long June evening — light until ten o'clock, gardens in full flush, hills a startling green — is a very different place from a raw January afternoon, when the sun gives up before four and the moors turn to slate. British weather is famously changeable in any month, so pack layers whenever you come; but the odds of warmth, dry days, and long light shift enormously across the calendar. Choosing your dates well is the single biggest lever you have over the trip. Here is how to pull it.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$ ShoulderMild & bloomingBest for gardensFewer crowds

The country greens up, daffodils then bluebells arrive, and daylight stretches fast — from around 12 hours in March to 16 by late May. Temperatures climb from the low 50s°F (about 12°C) to the mid-60s°F (around 18°C). Late April and May are prime: gardens peak, lambs dot the fields, and crowds haven't yet surged. Watch two spikes — Easter and the late-May bank holidays — and pack for showers. March can still be cool and wet, but it's cheap.

Summer — June to August

$$$$ PeakWarm & busyFestivals & coastBook early

High season, and it feels like it. June is the pick of the three — the longest days of the year (16–17 hours of light, more in Scotland), warm weather in the low 70s°F (around 21–23°C), and festival season underway — before the school holidays from mid-July push crowds and prices to their annual peak. This is the time for the coast, the countryside, and the Scottish Highlands, plus the great festivals: Glastonbury, Wimbledon, and the Edinburgh Fringe. If summer is your only window, book flights and hotels months ahead.

Autumn — September to November

$$ ShoulderMellow & goldenHarvest & colorGreat value

Many travelers' favorite season. September holds much of summer's warmth and light with the peak crowds ebbing once schools go back — one of the best all-round months of the year. October turns golden and mellow, superb for country houses, woodland walks, and city breaks at soft prices. Rain and wind build through November as the clocks go back and the light fades early, but the cities stay lively and rates fall.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold & short daysFestive & quietCheapest

The bargain season, and a characterful one for the right trip: crowd-free museums and galleries, Christmas markets and pantomimes, cozy pubs, and a real chance of the Northern Lights in northern Scotland. Days are short — as few as 7–8 hours of light — cold, and often wet, and many rural attractions and gardens close or run reduced hours. Sidestep the two-week Christmas–New Year spike and January and February are the cheapest weeks in Britain.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ CheapestCold & darkQuiet cities

The quietest, cheapest month once the New Year holiday passes. Cold, damp, and short on daylight, but ideal for near-empty museums, theatre, winter sales, and cozy pubs in London and Edinburgh. Northern Scotland offers Northern Lights potential on clear nights. Book cities, not the countryside.

February

$ CheapColdSnowdrops

Still low season and low prices, with the first snowdrops and, late in the month, early daffodils in the south. Half-term week lifts family attractions briefly. Cold and often the driest of the winter months in the east. A great time for a quiet, affordable city break.

March

$$ Value shoulderCool, variableSpring begins

Shoulder season opens and the clocks spring forward, adding evening light. Weather is variable — mild days, cool nights, plenty of showers — but crowds are thin and prices low. Daffodils carpet parks and gardens. If Easter falls in late March, expect a short surge around it.

April

$$ ShoulderMild & greenEaster peak

One of the loveliest months: blossom, bluebells in the woods, and comfortable sightseeing weather in the low 60s°F (about 15°C). The catch is Easter and school holidays, which bring domestic crowds and higher rates. Outside those dates, superb value and mood — if you don't mind the odd April shower.

May

$$$ PrimeMild & settledGardens peak

Arguably the best all-round month. Often the driest and sunniest, with long days, gardens at their peak (the Chelsea Flower Show is the highlight), and everything open before the summer crush. Two bank holidays bring domestic mini-breaks, so book those weekends ahead — but this is prime time and worth it.

June

$$$ PrimeWarm, long daysFestivals

The longest days of the year — light past 9pm, and past 10 in Scotland — with warm, generally settled weather and the countryside at its greenest. Festival season begins with Glastonbury, and Wimbledon starts late in the month. Crowds are climbing but the school-holiday peak hasn't hit. An excellent, well-rounded month.

July

$$$$ PeakWarmestBusy

Warm, long, and lively — but crowded and pricey once the school summer holidays begin mid-month. Best for the coast, festivals, and outdoor events, harder work at the busiest honeypots. Wimbledon runs into early July. Reserve accommodation, trains, and headline attractions well in advance.

August

$$$$ PeakWarm & crowdedEdinburgh Fringe

The busiest month, with schools off nationwide and the biggest crowds and prices of the year. The Edinburgh festivals take over Scotland's capital all month — thrilling, but the city is packed and rooms scarce and dear, so book far ahead. Warm and often the wettest of the summer, especially in the west and north. Doable, but plan around the crowds.

September

$$$ PrimeWarm & mellowCrowds fade

The other star month. Summer warmth and light linger while the crowds thin and prices ease once schools go back in the first week. Comfortable for cities and countryside alike, with harvest festivals and the first autumn color. A superb time to combine London with a countryside or Scotland leg.

October

$$ Best valueGolden & coolAutumn color

Mellow, golden, and one of the best-value months of the year. Cool but often crisp and bright, with spectacular autumn foliage in parks, woods, and the Lake District. Low crowds and soft prices. The clocks go back late in the month and rain increases, but the cities are at their calm, atmospheric best.

November

$ Low seasonCool & wetBonfire Night

Low season returns: cool, often wet and grey, and short on daylight. But prices are low, attractions quiet, and the mood cozy. Bonfire Night (November 5) brings fireworks nationwide, and Christmas lights and markets switch on toward month's end. Rewarding for city breaks if you don't mind a coat and an umbrella.

December

$ → $$$ SplitFestiveShortest days

A month of two halves. Early December is quiet and cheap, with Christmas markets, festive lights, and pantomimes in full swing on the year's shortest days (barely 7–8 hours of light). The stretch around Christmas and New Year is a sharp peak — magical, with Edinburgh's Hogmanay a highlight, but book early and expect higher rates.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

November to March — with January and February the lowest

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSkip Christmas fortnight

Transatlantic airfares and hotel rates bottom out in the dark months. January and February are the cheapest weeks of the year for both, with November and early December close behind — the one carve-out being the roughly two-week Christmas–New Year holiday, when prices briefly jump. You'll trade daylight and warmth for the savings, but for city breaks, museums, and theatre the value is unbeatable.

Want milder weather and longer days and a deal? Target the edges of shoulder season: late March to April (avoiding Easter and school holidays) and late October. You get comfortable days and open attractions at a fraction of the July–August cost. As a rule, midweek flights and stays beat weekends, and booking two to three months out lands the best fares.

When to Avoid the Crowds

The busy periods in the UK are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks to sidestep

July & AugustSchool holidaysEasterChristmas & NY

July and August are the busiest, priciest stretch, driven by the school summer holidays from mid-July. London, Edinburgh, and the honeypots — the Cotswolds, Lake District, Cornwall, the Scottish Highlands — are at their fullest, and the Edinburgh festivals fill Scotland's capital for all of August. Easter week and the Christmas–New Year fortnight are shorter, sharper spikes, concentrated in the marquee cities and around big events.

To have the big sights closer to yourself, come in the shoulder months — May, June, September, October — or in winter. Whenever you go, the crowd-beating tactics are the same: pre-book timed tickets for the Tower of London, Westminster Abbey, Edinburgh Castle, and Stonehenge, and visit at opening or late in the day. Best Time by Region

Britain is compact but varied — the ideal month depends on where you're headed.

The Cities — London, Edinburgh, Bath, York

Best: May–Jun, Sep–OctValue: Nov–FebAvoid: Aug in Edinburgh

Cities are the UK's all-weather option — museums, galleries, theatre, and pubs carry you through rain — so they reward visits year-round. Spring and autumn are ideal for comfort and value; winter is quiet and cheap if you can take the dark and cold. The one caveat is Edinburgh in August, when the festivals bring crowds and premium rates. See where to stay in London and Edinburgh.

The Countryside & Coast — Cotswolds, Lake District, Cornwall

Best: late May–Jun, SepSeason: Easter–OctQuiet: winter

Rural and coastal Britain runs on a softer season — roughly Easter through October, when gardens, country houses, ferries, and seaside towns are fully open. The sweet spots are late May to June and September: long days, settled weather, and lighter crowds than the July–August school-holiday crush. Cornwall and the south coast catch the most sun and the warmest sea; winter shutters many rural attractions.

Scotland & the Highlands

Best: May–Jun, SepAurora: Oct–MarMidges: Jun–Aug

Scotland is at its finest in late spring and early autumn. May and June bring the longest days — light until nearly 11pm in the far north — the driest weather, and few of the biting midges that plague the Highlands and west coast from June through August. September is glorious and quieter as the midges fade. Winter is short-dayed and cold, but that's when the Northern Lights can appear over the northern sky. Time festival trips for August's Edinburgh Fringe.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a British trip.

What is the best month to visit the United Kingdom?

May, June, and September are the strongest all-round months. You get the mildest, driest weather of the year, long daylight, gardens and countryside at their best, and noticeably thinner crowds and lower prices than July and August. May and June bring blossom and up to 16–17 hours of daylight; September keeps the summer warmth as the crowds fade. April and October are close behind and even cheaper.

What is the cheapest time to visit the United Kingdom?

November through March is the cheapest window, with January and February the lowest of all for both flights and hotels — the two weeks around Christmas and New Year excepted. If you want longer days and milder weather with a deal, target the edges of the shoulder season: late March to April and late October.

When should I avoid the UK because of crowds?

July and August are the most crowded and expensive months, coinciding with the school summer holidays. London, Edinburgh, and honeypot spots like the Cotswolds, Lake District, and Cornwall are at their fullest, and the Edinburgh festivals in August fill that city and lift its prices for a month. Easter week and the Christmas–New Year period are also short, sharp peaks.

Is the United Kingdom worth visiting in winter?

Yes, for the right trip. Winter is the cheapest and quietest time for London and the big cities, with Christmas markets, pantomimes, and festive lights, plus a real chance of the Northern Lights in northern Scotland. The trade-offs are cold, wet weather and very short days — as little as 7 hours of daylight in December — and many rural attractions, gardens, and coastal towns close or run reduced hours.

What is shoulder season in the UK and why is it best?

Shoulder season is late spring (May–June) and early autumn (September–October), the stretches on either side of the summer school holidays. It's widely considered the best time to visit the UK because you get the mildest, driest weather and long daylight without the peak-season crowds and prices — the sweet spot for combining cities with the countryside, coast, and Scotland.
