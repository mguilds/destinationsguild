Where to Stay in Punta Cana

Where to Stay in Punta Cana — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Punta Cana is thirty-odd miles of white sand on the eastern tip of the Dominican Republic, and it is the most concentrated stretch of all-inclusive resorts anywhere in the Caribbean. That abundance is the problem as much as the promise: the resorts look alike in photographs and are anything but alike in practice. What separates them is which beach you wake up on and whether you can walk anywhere at all.

Four areas matter. Bávaro is the long central beach and has the widest choice at every price. Los Corales and El Cortecito, at Bávaro’s northern end, are the only part of Punta Cana where you can stroll to an independent restaurant, a pharmacy and an ATM — which for many of our readers is worth more than a fourth swimming pool. Cap Cana is a gated luxury enclave on the calm, reef-sheltered Juanillo beach. Uvero Alto is quieter, greener and forty-five minutes further from the airport, with nothing whatsoever outside the gates.

A word on hurricanes and seaweed, plainly. Atlantic hurricane season runs 1 June to 30 November, with the sharp end from mid-August to mid-October. A direct hit on this coast is a once-in-several-years event, not an annual one, and you get three to five days’ warning — but if you travel in that window, book a flexible rate and buy travel insurance before a storm is named. The more likely nuisance is sargassum seaweed, which lands on these east-facing beaches from roughly June to August. Cap Cana and Juanillo are noticeably cleaner; December to April is clearest of all.

What You Need to Know Before You Book

The four things that decide whether a Punta Cana holiday is relaxing or exasperating.

What ‘all-inclusive’ actually covers — and what it does not

Read this firstExtras add up

Normally included: all meals, buffet and à la carte; unlimited local-brand spirits, beer and house wine by the glass; soft drinks; non-motorised watersports such as kayaks, pedal boats and snorkel gear; pools, loungers and towels; the kids’ and teens’ clubs; nightly entertainment; and the gym. At the better brands, twenty-four-hour room service, minibar refills and Wi-Fi are in as well.

Almost never included: motorised watersports — jet skis, parasailing, scuba certification — anywhere at all. Premium and imported liquor at mid-tier resorts, which pour local rum and generic spirits unless you have bought a club upgrade. Spa treatments, even at hotels with ‘Spa’ in the name. Golf, bottled wine, private dinners, cabanas and Bali beds, babysitting and the separate water parks. Wi-Fi is chargeable at some of the large Spanish-chain resorts, which regularly catches American guests out.

À la carte restaurants are usually covered by your rate but capped by number of visits, and they require reservations made on the day you arrive. Dress codes are enforced — long trousers and closed shoes for men at dinner — so pack accordingly.

Tax, service and tips. American-style nightly resort fees are not the norm here. The eighteen per cent ITBIS tax and a ten per cent service charge are normally already inside an all-inclusive rate, but confirm, because some quoted rates are pre-tax. The legal service charge often does not reach the staff, so cash tipping in small US dollar bills is both expected and effective: a dollar or two per drink or bag, five to ten dollars a day for housekeeping, ten to twenty for a good à la carte dinner. Bring singles and fives from home.

Where you can actually walk — and where you cannot

Los Corales walkableCap Cana is not

Genuinely walkable: only El Cortecito and Los Corales, at the northern end of Bávaro. Beach, pharmacy, cash machines, bakeries, mini-markets and a couple of dozen independent restaurants sit within a five- to ten-minute stroll on paved, lit paths, with the tourist police visible. If being able to walk to dinner matters to you, book here and nowhere else.

Partly walkable: Punta Cana Village and Downtown Punta Cana are pleasant to wander once you are there, but you need a car or a ride to reach them.

Resort bubble only: Cap Cana, Uvero Alto, Arena Gorda, Macao and Cabeza de Toro. Cap Cana’s distances are measured in kilometres; Uvero Alto is genuinely isolated. In all of these, every outing is a booked transfer, and that is not a criticism — it is simply the deal, and worth knowing before you sign.

Taxis, Uber or a rental car?

Don’t rentBook the airport transfer

For a resort holiday, do not rent a car. Driving here is assertive, signage inconsistent, night driving poor and the motorbikes unpredictable; once you add compulsory local insurance and fuel, the cost usually exceeds simply being driven. The Dominican Republic has one of the highest road-fatality rates in the Americas, and the official advice for visitors is to hire a driver rather than drive yourself.

Uber works in Punta Cana and is the cheapest way to go out for the evening — roughly six to eight dollars for a short hop, fifteen to twenty-five for a longer one. One catch that matters if walking is difficult: because of taxi-syndicate agreements, many resorts will not let Uber collect at the main entrance, so expect a short walk to the gate. Uber cannot officially collect at Punta Cana airport at all — pre-book a private transfer for arrival.

Taxis are syndicate-run at fixed zone rates with no meters, and they are not cheap: around twenty dollars for a short trip, thirty to forty from the airport to Bávaro, sixty or seventy from Bávaro out to Uvero Alto. Agree the fare before you get in. The local guaguas are a dollar or two but crowded and unairconditioned; we would not send our readers on one.

When to come, and what the sea will look like

Best: Dec–AprMarch driestSargassum Jun–Aug

December to April is the dry season and the reason Punta Cana fills up in winter, with March the driest month of all and February to April the kindest combination of dry air, lower humidity and no storm risk. Highs sit around 81–86°F all year; it is the rain, not the heat, that varies.

September is the wettest month and the statistical peak of hurricane season, which is why it is also the cheapest week of the year. Rain in season usually falls as a short, heavy afternoon shower rather than an all-day washout, so a green-season morning is often perfect.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions Guild readers ask most about staying in Punta Cana.

Which part of Punta Cana should I stay in?

For the widest choice at every price, Bávaro. For the calmest, most sheltered swimming beach and the most polished resorts, Cap Cana and Playa Juanillo. For peace and green space, Uvero Alto — accepting a forty-five to sixty-minute airport transfer and nothing outside the gates. And if you want to be able to walk to an independent restaurant, a pharmacy and a cash machine, stay in Los Corales or El Cortecito, which is the only genuinely walkable part of Punta Cana.

What does all-inclusive really cover in Punta Cana?

Meals, local-brand drinks, non-motorised watersports, pools, entertainment and the clubs are standard. What is not included almost anywhere: motorised watersports, spa treatments, premium and imported spirits, golf, bottled wine, private dinners and cabanas. À la carte restaurants are usually covered but capped by number of visits and need reserving on your arrival day. Wi-Fi is chargeable at some large resorts. The 18% ITBIS tax and 10% service charge are normally inside the rate — confirm, as some quotes are pre-tax.

Should I rent a car in Punta Cana?

No, for almost every visitor. Driving is assertive, night driving is poor, and the Dominican Republic has one of the highest road-fatality rates in the Americas; official guidance for visitors is to hire a driver rather than drive. Pre-book a private transfer from the airport — Uber is not permitted to collect at Punta Cana airport — then use Uber or resort taxis for evenings out. Note that many resorts will not let Uber collect at the main gate, so expect a short walk.

When is the seaweed worst in Punta Cana?

Sargassum builds from March to May and peaks roughly June to August on these east-facing beaches, tapering through the autumn. December to February is the clearest stretch. Within the area, Cap Cana and Playa Juanillo are noticeably cleaner thanks to reef shelter, while Bávaro faces due east and takes the heaviest landings. Big resorts rake their beaches every morning, and the water is usually clear a few hundred metres offshore even on a bad week.

Is Punta Cana a good choice for travellers over 55?

Yes, and it is one of the easiest Caribbean destinations to manage. Flights from the United States are short and direct, resorts are geared to being looked after, and the adults-only choice is the widest in the country. Two practical cautions: the largest resorts involve a great deal of walking, so ask for a room near the lobby or beach if that matters; and if being able to stroll to a restaurant is important to you, book in Los Corales rather than a gated enclave.

Do I need a visa for the Dominican Republic?

Citizens of the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and the EU do not need a visa for tourism and are normally admitted for thirty days. Everyone must, however, complete the free government E-Ticket online before flying — it covers both arrival and departure and produces a QR code for check-in. File it only at the official migracion.gob.do site; a number of look-alike sites charge for what is free. Carry a passport with at least six months’ validity to be safe.
