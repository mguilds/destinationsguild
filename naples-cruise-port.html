🗺️Harbor map — where everything is

Naples is one of the great walk-off ports. You tie up at Stazione Marittima, five minutes from the castle and ten from the royal palace, with the ferries to Capri leaving from the next pier along. Pompeii is forty minutes away by a train that costs a few euros.

🚌Getting around — metro, funiculars & taxis

Most of what you want is on foot. Beyond that, Municipio metro station is a few minutes from the terminal on line 1 — modern, with lifts, and one stop from Toledo, a few from the museum, and straight to Garibaldi for the Pompeii train. A single ticket is around a euro and a half and a day ticket about four and a half; validate a paper ticket in the machine or you will be fined.

Taxis are white, metered, and have official fixed fares for common runs — the port to the airport is one of them. Ask for the tariffa predeterminata before you set off, and use the rank rather than anyone who approaches you in the terminal. There is no UberX in Italy; the local apps simply hail a licensed taxi. The three funiculars up to the Vomero hill are part of the same ticket and give you the postcard view over the bay.

⚓Stepping off the ship — straight into the city

Walk out of Stazione Marittima and Castel Nuovo is five minutes away, Piazza del Plebiscito twelve, and the Galleria Umberto I with its glass dome about ten. The port apron is flat and easy; the climb only begins when you head north into the old town.

Twenty minutes on foot takes you into Spaccanapoli — the dead-straight Greek street that slices the historic centre in half, hung with washing, lined with churches and workshops, and the best free thing to do in Naples. If your ship is berthed at the far northern quay, a shuttle brings you to the main terminal first.

🌋What to do with 6–8 hours ashore

  • Pompeii — metro to Garibaldi, then the Circumvesuviana toward Sorrento to Pompei Scavi–Villa dei Misteri (not Pompei Santuario). About forty minutes and a few euros; the gate is two minutes from the platform. Allow three hours on site. Site tickets are timed and carry your name — book ahead.
  • Herculaneum — half the walking, better preserved, twenty minutes on the same line. The best choice on a short call or a hot day.
  • The Archaeological Museum — the mosaics and bronzes that were taken from Pompeii. Do this and the ruins make sense.
  • Capri — hydrofoil from Molo Beverello, three minutes’ walk from the ship, about fifty minutes each way. Wonderful, and tight: only on a long call.
  • Vesuvius — timed tickets sold online only, a bus up from Pompeii, then a twenty-minute climb on loose gravel. Pompeii and Vesuvius together need a ten-hour call or a tour.
  • Naples itself — Spaccanapoli, the Veiled Christ at the Cappella Sansevero (book ahead), underground Naples, and pizza. No trains, no stress.
Book before you sail: Vesuvius (online only, timed, no ticket office at the gate), the Cappella Sansevero (every visit pre-booked), and Pompeii (timed slots, name on the ticket, a daily cap). Everything else in the city you can buy at the door.
New to cruising? Three quick things for Naples: you walk straight off into the city; the Pompeii train is cheap but crowded, so keep your bag in front of you; and cross the road at a steady, predictable pace — the traffic flows around you, and dithering is what gets people hurt.

👕What to wear & the weather

Summer here is high twenties Celsius (mid eighties Fahrenheit) with very little rain, and Pompeii has almost no shade — go early or late, take water, and wear a hat. Sturdy closed shoes: Pompeii is ancient basalt with cart ruts and high kerbs, and the Vesuvius path is loose volcanic gravel where sandals are genuinely dangerous. Churches expect shoulders and knees covered.

💵Money, ATMs & tipping

Euro, and Naples runs on cash more than most Italian cities — small pizzerias, coffee bars and street-food stalls may not take a card, and some places set a minimum. Carry €50–100. Use bank ATMs rather than the standalone machines, and decline any offer to charge you in dollars — always choose euros.

The coperto is a small per-person cover charge printed on the menu, not a tip. Tipping is not expected: round up, or five to ten per cent for good service. Coffee at the bar is drunk standing, costs about a euro, and you do not tip for it — in many bars you pay at the till first and take the receipt to the barman.

📶Staying connected

Wifi in cafés and the terminal. An eSIM is worth it for the day if you are heading to Pompeii or Capri — live train and ferry times are exactly what you need when the clock matters.

🛍️What to buy — and what to skip

Worth it: a hand-painted Neapolitan nativity figure from the workshops on Via San Gregorio Armeno, coffee from a proper roaster, limoncello from the coast, dried pasta from a Gragnano maker. Skip: "designer" bags from a pavement seller, and anything sold to you at a traffic light.

⚠️Scams & hassles to dodge

The traffic is the real hazard, not crime. Scooters treat lights as suggestions. Cross decisively, at a steady pace, making eye contact — the traffic flows around a predictable pedestrian and panics around a hesitant one.

  • Pickpockets around Piazza Garibaldi and the central station, in the Pignasecca market, and on the Circumvesuviana to Pompeii, which is a known working ground. Bag on your lap, in front, zipped.
  • Scooter bag-snatching — walk with your bag on the wall side of the pavement, not the road side.
  • Taxi touts in the terminal offering a "special price to Pompeii". Use the official rank and ask for the fixed tariff where one exists; agree any longer run in writing before you get in.
  • "Free" bracelets and clipboard petitions — the clipboard hides the hand in your bag.
  • "The site is closed / the entrance has moved" from a helpful stranger outside Pompeii. It isn’t. Walk to the official gate.

🩹Health, water & sun

Tap water is safe and the public fountains around the centre are fine to refill from. Pharmacies show a green cross and the pharmacist will deal with minor complaints directly; a duty rota in the window covers nights. Heat and dehydration at Pompeii are the commonest problem of the day.

♿Accessibility & families

The port and the walk to Piazza Municipio, Castel Nuovo and Piazza del Plebiscito are flat and step-free, and Municipio metro has lifts. The Circumvesuviana is largely not step-free — Garibaldi is reached by stairs and the carriages have steps up — so Pompeii by train is hard going for a wheelchair. Pompeii itself does have a barrier-free route of about two miles, entered at Piazza Anfiteatro rather than Porta Marina, with free entry for a disabled visitor and a companion and loan wheelchairs available. Families do well here: pizza, the castle, the funicular and a lot of noise.

⭐Best excursions, ranked

  • Pompeii with a guide — the site is enormous and largely unlabelled; a guide is the difference between rubble and a city.
  • Herculaneum — smaller, richer, less walking, and far fewer people.
  • The Amalfi Coast — only through the ship. Ninety minutes to two hours each way on a hairpin road that jams solid in summer; independent travellers get caught out every season.

🍕Where to eat — pizza, and the rest

This is the birthplace of pizza and the standard is absurd. A margherita costs a few euros almost everywhere.

  • L’Antica Pizzeria da Michele — the famous one, near Forcella. Margherita or marinara, that is the menu. Take a number and wait; the queue is the experience.
  • Gino e Toto Sorbillo, Via dei Tribunali — no reservations, an hour at peak, and worth it.
  • Antica Pizzeria Di Matteo, also on Via dei Tribunali — the street window sells you a folded margherita for a few euros with no queue at all. The cruiser’s answer.
  • Sfogliatella — the shell-shaped ricotta pastry, crisp (riccia) or soft (frolla). There is a famous counter inside the Galleria Umberto I, ten minutes from the ship.

Drink the coffee standing at the bar, and if someone tells you about the caffè sospeso — the coffee you pay for and a stranger drinks — that is a real Naples tradition, not a story for tourists.

🧮Ship excursion or do-it-yourself?

Naples city is a fine do-it-yourself day: you walk off, everything is close, and the metro is easy. Pompeii independently is very doable if you book your site ticket and leave early. Buy through the ship for Vesuvius combined with Pompeii, for the Amalfi Coast, and for Capri on a short call — those are the ones where a strike, a jam or a cancelled sailing turns into a missed ship.

⏰Don’t miss the ship

The Circumvesuviana is the risk. The line to Pompeii is prone to delays and to strikes, which are announced in advance — check on the morning if you can. Take the train back that gets you in two to three hours before all-aboard, not the last one that theoretically works, and keep enough cash for an emergency taxi from Pompeii. For Capri, buy your return the moment you land and never rely on the last sailing: summer boats sell out and get cancelled for wind.

🇮🇹Emergency numbers & staying safe

In Italy, dial 112 for police, ambulance or fire — free from any phone, English available.
Nearest hospital to the port: Ospedale Loreto Mare, on the waterfront about 1.5 km east of the terminal.
The one cruisers forget — your ship’s Port Agent: your line lists a local agent in each day’s programme. Write that number down before you step off. If a train strands you at Pompeii, call the agent immediately.
Also save: your cruise line’s 24/7 emergency number, and keep a phone photo of your passport, ship and stateroom.

🏨Stay the night before — or after — you sail

Naples rewards an extra night more than almost any port on this coast — and the airport is fifteen minutes from the terminal.

❓Good to know — quick answers

Can I walk into Naples from the cruise ship?
Yes — this is one of the easiest walk-off ports in the Mediterranean. Castel Nuovo is about five minutes from Stazione Marittima, Piazza del Plebiscito twelve to fifteen, and the historic centre around twenty. Only the far northern berth needs a shuttle to the main terminal first.

What is the cheapest way to Pompeii, and which station do I get off at?
Metro line 1 from Municipio to Garibaldi, then the Circumvesuviana toward Sorrento — a few euros and about forty minutes. Get off at Pompei Scavi–Villa dei Misteri, not Pompei Santuario, which is the modern town. The gate is two minutes from the platform.

Is Capri doable on a port day?
On a long call, yes, but tightly. The hydrofoil from Molo Beverello is about fifty minutes each way and the pier is three minutes from your ship. Buy your return the moment you arrive, take a boat that lands you at least two hours before all-aboard, and never bank on the last sailing.

What do I need to book before I sail?
Three things: Mount Vesuvius, which is sold online only with timed slots; the Cappella Sansevero, where every visit must be pre-booked; and Pompeii, which has timed entry, a daily cap and your name on the ticket. Everything else you can buy at the door.

Is Naples dangerous?
Violent crime against visitors is not the issue — traffic and opportunistic theft are. Cross roads at a steady, predictable pace, keep a zipped bag in front of you around the central station, the Pignasecca market and on the Pompeii train, and use only official white taxis from the rank.
