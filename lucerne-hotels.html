Where to Stay in Lucerne

Where to Stay in Lucerne — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

If you are making one base for a first trip to Switzerland, make it Lucerne. It is forty to fifty minutes from Zurich by direct train, the old town is compact and almost entirely flat, the lake steamers leave from the quay opposite the station, and two of the country’s great mountain excursions — Rigi and Pilatus — begin at the door. Where to sleep comes down to three strips: the lakefront along the Schweizerhofquai and Haldenstrasse, which is level, wide and quiet; the old town on the Reuss, which is beautiful, cobbled and noisier at weekends; and the station quarter, which is plain but superbly convenient for day trips. Below are the hotels we would send our own family to, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Flat ground, but watch the cobbles

Both riverbanks, the whole lakefront and the station quarter are level — you can cross the entire old town in fifteen minutes without meeting a hill. The surface is the difficulty, not the gradient: the old town lanes and squares are cobbled throughout, which is hard on wheeled luggage and unsteady footing. Both covered wooden bridges have gentle timber ramps and are easy to cross. Anything described as Musegg, Gütsch or Wesemlin involves a genuine climb.

The boats and the station are the same place

The lake steamer piers sit directly opposite the railway station, so boat and train connect without a walk worth mentioning. The fleet includes five historic paddle steamers, and a lake trip is a highlight in its own right rather than merely transport — you buy a ticket, walk on, and sit on deck or in an Art Nouveau saloon. Rail passes and the Half Fare Card are accepted.

Rigi is the gentler mountain; Pilatus is the famous one

Pilatus is the headline trip — boat to Alpnachstad, then the steepest cogwheel railway in the world, down by cable car. Note the cogwheel section only runs from about May to November. Rigi is the kinder day and runs year-round from Vitznau or Arth-Goldau, with mineral baths at Rigi Kaltbad if you want to break the journey. Both are entirely seated; neither requires walking.

Two weeks to plan around

Lucerne Festival fills the concert hall and the hotels through August and into September, and Fasnacht — carnival — takes over the whole city for six days before Ash Wednesday, beginning with an explosion at five in the morning. Both are spectacular and neither is restful. July and August also bring shoulder-to-shoulder crowds on the Chapel Bridge by mid-morning.

Luxury

Lucerne’s grand hotels stand in a row on the lake, on dead-level ground five to twelve minutes from the Chapel Bridge.

Hotel Schweizerhof Luzern

$$$$ LuxuryLakefrontFamily-run since 1861Wheelchair accessiblePets welcome

Lucerne’s grande dame, run by the same family for six generations, with a hundred and one rooms all renovated since 2014. The rooms are traditional and plush rather than fashionable, air-conditioned, and many look over the water. For an older traveller the location is the best in town and it is not close: dead flat, about five minutes on the level to the Chapel Bridge, five to the station, and right by the boat piers. The hotel is listed as wheelchair accessible with lifts throughout. There is a sauna and treatments but no swimming pool.

Boutique

One on the river in the old town, one a quiet design house by the station — both small enough to feel personal.

Hotel des Balances

$$$ UpscaleOld Town riversidePainted historic façadeRiver terracePets welcome

Fifty-six rooms in one of the most photographed buildings in Switzerland, on the Weinmarkt with a terrace directly over the river. The rooms have been fully modernised and are calm and comfortable; there is a lift, underground parking and a well-regarded restaurant. It is a two-minute flat walk to the Chapel Bridge and about ten to the station. Two honest cautions: it sits in the pedestrian old town, so taxis drop nearby rather than at the door, and rooms facing the square catch summer evening noise.

Budget & Value

Lucerne is not cheap, but these two are honest value in positions you would otherwise pay a great deal for.

Hotel Waldstätterhof

$$ Value100 m from the station91 roomsBreakfast includedDogs welcome for a small nightly fee

The best-value practical choice in the city: ninety-one rooms in a twin-towered building about a hundred metres — three minutes — from the station exit, with breakfast included and a front desk staffed around the clock. The rooms are plain but properly maintained. From the door it is a flat five to eight minutes over the Reuss to the Chapel Bridge, with no hill at any point. Family-friendly

Rooms that take more than two people without splitting the party — the usual Swiss difficulty, solved.

Hotel Continental Park

$$$ Mid-rangeStation quarterFamily suites and triples67 air-conditioned roomsTwo restaurants

A dependable four-star just behind the station with a dedicated family suite room type and triple rooms, which solves the usual Swiss problem of a family being split across two doubles. Sixty-seven air-conditioned rooms, two restaurants, and a flat two- to three-minute walk to the trains — genuinely useful when you are coming back tired from Pilatus or Rigi. About ten level minutes to the Chapel Bridge. It is a working city street rather than a scenic one, so ask for a room on the courtyard side.

Adults-only & quiet

Lucerne has essentially one genuine adults-only hotel, and we would rather name it and stop than pad the list with a guess. The second entry below is a quiet, couples-minded castle — clearly labelled, because it does not exclude children.

Boutique Hotel Weisses Kreuz – Adult only Hotel

$$–$$$ Mid-rangeGenuinely adults-only, 18+Old Town on the Reuss90 m from the Chapel BridgeRoof terrace

Twenty-one rooms in a historic old-town house about ninety metres from the Chapel Bridge and roughly ten flat minutes from the station. This is a real age policy rather than a marketing label — eighteen and over — so it stays genuinely quiet. There is a lift, a rooftop terrace and a restaurant. Two things an older traveller should know before booking: there is no air conditioning, which bites in a July or August heatwave, and the lanes outside carry bar noise at weekends. Ask for a room off the street.

$$$$ UpscaleNot adults-only — quiet and romanticHilltop above the cityFree funicular37 rooms

We are labelling this one plainly: it is not adults-only, but it is the most romantic and the quietest address in Lucerne. There is no pool, and step-free access inside the castle is worth confirming directly before you book.

Pet-friendly

Two hotels with pet arrangements written down rather than merely tolerated. Confirm the current fee with the hotel before you book.

Hotel des Alpes

$$–$$$ Mid-rangeOld Town on the ReussChapel Bridge viewsLift confirmedPets welcome

Possibly the best view for the money in Lucerne: it stands on the riverbank with the Chapel Bridge, the lake and Pilatus in front of it, and its restaurant terrace sits right on the water. Forty-five rooms, a confirmed lift, reception around the clock and pets explicitly welcome. There are no hills at all — you step out onto the flat quay, with the station about ten minutes away over the bridge. The trade-off is the price of that address: the quay is busy, and riverside rooms hear the crowds until late.

Spa & pool

Hotel swimming pools are genuinely scarce in central Lucerne. Here is the one in town that has a real one, and the resort worth the boat ride.

Grand Hotel National Luzern

$$$$ LuxuryLakefrontIndoor pool and saunaSix restaurants and barsNine minutes to the Chapel Bridge

The one hotel in the city centre we could confirm has an actual swimming pool, on a spa floor with a sauna alongside. It is a grand hotel of 1870 sitting directly on the lake, with sixty-two rooms, three apartments and an unusual six restaurants and bars, including Indian and Chinese kitchens. The position is excellent and completely flat: about nine minutes to the Chapel Bridge and ten to the station and the concert hall. Lifts throughout. Book pool times ahead during festival weeks.

Lucerne Hotels — common questions

Which part of Lucerne should I stay in?

For an older traveller, the lakefront along the Schweizerhofquai and Haldenstrasse is the strongest choice: completely level, wide pavements, benches, the boat piers right there and a five- to twelve-minute flat walk to the Chapel Bridge. The old town is beautiful and mostly flat but cobbled throughout, and the bars around the Weinmarkt and the Rathausquai run loud on Friday and Saturday nights, so ask for a courtyard room. The station quarter is plain but unbeatable for day trips.

How do I get to Lucerne from Zurich airport?

Take the direct train. It runs from the station beneath the airport terminal to Lucerne in about seventy minutes with no change, for roughly 31 francs at full fare. From Zurich's main station the direct journey is forty to fifty minutes and around 27 francs. Trains run frequently through the day, platforms are step-free, and there are luggage racks by the doors. You do not need to reserve a seat on Swiss domestic services.

Is Lucerne walkable for older travellers?

Yes, and it is one of the easiest towns in Switzerland for anyone with limited stamina. The centre is small enough to cross in fifteen minutes and it is essentially flat along both riverbanks and the whole lakefront. The one real difficulty is the surface: cobblestones throughout the old town lanes and squares. The two covered wooden bridges have gentle timber ramps and are easy. Avoid the Musegg Wall towers if stairs are a problem, as they involve a climb and narrow internal staircases with no lift.

Pilatus or Rigi — which mountain should I choose?

Rigi is the gentler day and, for many older visitors, the better one. It runs year-round, reached by boat to Vitznau and a cogwheel train, or fastest by train to Arth-Goldau, and there are mineral baths at Rigi Kaltbad to break the journey. Pilatus is the more dramatic trip, with the steepest cogwheel railway in the world, but that cogwheel section only runs from about May to November. Both are entirely seated and neither requires walking.

When is the best time to visit Lucerne?

May, June, late September and early October are the sweet spot: mild weather, the mountain railways running, the lake at its best and noticeably fewer coach groups. July and August are the crowded months, when the Chapel Bridge is shoulder-to-shoulder by mid-morning and hotel rates peak. Two dates to plan around are the Lucerne Festival, which fills the hotels in August and September, and carnival in the six days before Ash Wednesday, which is spectacular but very loud.

Does Switzerland use the euro?

No. Switzerland uses the Swiss franc, despite being surrounded by the eurozone. Some tourist businesses will take euro notes, but the rate they offer is poor and your change comes back in francs. Pay by card, which is accepted virtually everywhere including boats and mountain railways, or withdraw francs from a cash machine. Tipping is not expected because service is included by law, though rounding up a restaurant bill is normal courtesy.
