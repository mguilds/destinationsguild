Dar es Salaam — The Harbour City and the Door to Zanzibar

Where to Stay in Dar es Salaam — Area by Area

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Most visitors treat Dar es Salaam as a night either side of somewhere else — a safari, or the boat to Zanzibar — and never decide where in it to sleep. The districts are not interchangeable, though. The peninsula, the harbour front and the working centre offer genuinely different evenings, different drives to the airport, and different risks.

This is also a city where the honest word is worth more than the enthusiastic one. Official advice names the specific streets and beaches where visitors have been robbed, and it names some of the same places we would otherwise send you to stay. We would rather print that than leave it out.

Masaki and Oysterbay — where most visitors end up, with a caveat

The peninsula north-east of the centre holds most of the city’s better rooms, the restaurants, the supermarkets and the consulates, and it is where a first-time visitor will be most comfortable. It is also, specifically, an area where official advice says tourists and residents have been targeted by armed criminals — particularly at Coco Beach and along Toure Drive. Both of those things are true at once. Stay here, and treat the seafront walk at dusk as something you do not do.

Kivukoni and the harbour front — for the ferry and the museums

The old government quarter at the tip of the harbour is where the National Museum, the botanical gardens and the Zanzibar ferry terminal are, and a couple of the larger business hotels look straight down the channel. If your plan is a night in Dar and a morning boat to Zanzibar, this is the sensible place to be: you can walk to the terminal rather than cross the city in rush hour.

The city centre — and the honest warning about it

Kariakoo and the streets inland from the harbour are the working city, the cheapest rooms and the real markets. They are also named in official advice as part of the city centre where tourists and residents have been targeted. We are not going to tell you this is a relaxing place to sleep. If you stay here, move by car after dark, leave the watch and the camera in the room, and do not carry a bag on your street-side shoulder.

Along the northern beaches — Kunduchi and Mbezi

Half an hour or so north of the centre, the beach hotels at Kunduchi and Mbezi sell a resort day rather than a city one: pools, sand, and a long drive to anything else. They suit a family with an awkward flight time or anyone who wants to decompress before a safari, and they are a poor choice if you came to see the city.

Near the airport — for a short layover and nothing else

Julius Nyerere International is close to the city by African capital standards, but the traffic on Nyerere Road can turn fifteen minutes into an hour. If you land late and fly out early, a room near the terminal saves a genuinely miserable morning. For anything longer than a night, stay on the peninsula instead.

Before you book

Government travel insurance is compulsory

Every visitor to mainland Tanzania and to Zanzibar, residents aside, must hold inbound travel insurance provided by the Tanzanian government — and you must hold it even if you already have a policy of your own from home. You buy it online beforehand or at your port of entry, it covers the length of your stay up to 92 days, and you only buy it for your first port of entry. Arriving in Zanzibar first means buying from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation; arriving on the mainland first means the National Insurance Corporation. You may be refused entry without it. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements for Tanzania, still current at 22 November 2026.

Get the e-visa before you fly

You need a tourist or business visa, and you apply for an e-visa online before you travel — it is no longer possible to get one from the Tanzanian High Commission in London. A single-entry visa is available on arrival at the main ports of entry if you meet the requirements, and you may be asked to show your return or onward ticket; a multiple-entry visa cannot be had on arrival. Your passport needs an expiry date at least six months after you arrive and at least one blank page.

Health screening at the border

Tanzania’s Ministry of Health announced heightened screening on 17 October for travellers arriving from high-risk regions and affected countries, at every point of entry, and every traveller has to complete a Travellers Health Surveillance Form before departure or on arrival. You also pass through mpox screening — body temperature checks — at all points of entry. Check the current list of affected countries before you fly.

Your phone, and what you photograph

This one catches visitors out. It is illegal in Tanzania to take or share photographs or video of protests and violence, and the security services may ask to inspect your phone and other devices. The police have said it is an offence to hold or share images that “cause panic or degrade a person’s dignity”. Keep away from anything that could be read as political, online as much as in public. Source: UK Foreign Office safety and security advice for Tanzania, updated 30 October 2026.

Demonstrations, and a change in the mood

The general election of 29 October 2025 was followed by protests and a security response that caused deaths and injuries in many parts of mainland Tanzania, and further demonstrations are possible at any time. Official advice also notes an increase in anti-foreigner rhetoric, and that the authorities have limited internet access during and after protests before now. Write down your airline, insurer and embassy numbers rather than keeping them only online, and leave any crowd you come across.

Malaria, and where the hospitals are

Malaria is a risk across Tanzania, and dengue, cholera and mpox are all named in official health advice. Get travel health advice at least eight weeks before you fly and ask about your whole itinerary rather than one city. Medical facilities are limited, particularly outside Dar es Salaam, so insurance that covers medical evacuation is not a luxury here. The emergency number is 112.

Questions readers actually ask

Which area should I stay in for a first visit to Dar es Salaam?


Masaki or Oysterbay, on the peninsula north-east of the centre. That is where the better rooms, the restaurants and the supermarkets are, and where short trips do not need a long drive. Be aware that official advice names this same peninsula, and Coco Beach and Toure Drive in particular, as places where tourists have been targeted by armed criminals, so you take the ordinary precautions: a car after dark, nothing valuable on show, and no seafront walk at dusk.

Is Dar es Salaam safe for tourists?


There is a risk of armed crime, including armed robbery, throughout Tanzania, and in Dar es Salaam official advice names the city centre, the Masaki and Oysterbay peninsula, Coco Beach especially, and Toure Drive as places where tourists and residents have been targeted. Bag snatching by criminals on motorbikes or from cars is a known tactic. None of that makes the city one to skip, but it does mean you move by car at night, you do not wander, and you walk facing the traffic so you can see what is coming.

How do I get from Dar es Salaam to Zanzibar?


By fast ferry from the terminal at Kivukoni, which takes about two hours, or by a short flight from Julius Nyerere airport. If you are taking the ferry, sleep near the harbour the night before rather than on the peninsula, because crossing the city in the morning rush can take longer than the crossing itself. Note that Tanzania's compulsory government travel insurance is bought for your first port of entry only, so if you fly into the mainland first you do not buy it again for Zanzibar.

Do I need the government travel insurance if I already have my own policy?


Yes. Official advice is explicit that all visitors to mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar, residents excepted, must hold the Tanzanian government's inbound travel insurance even if a personal policy is already in place. You can buy it online before you travel or at your port of entry, it lasts the length of your stay up to 92 days, and you may be refused entry without it. Buy it for your first port of entry only.

Do I need a car in Dar es Salaam?


Not your own. The traffic is heavy, parking is awkward and driving yourself after dark is not a good idea. Ride apps work in the areas visitors stay in, and your hotel will arrange a car and driver for the day if you are going further. Hire a vehicle with a driver for any trip out of the city.
