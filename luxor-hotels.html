Where to Stay in Luxor

Where to Stay in Luxor — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Luxor is the world’s greatest open-air museum, split by the Nile. The East Bank is the lively side — the corniche, the souk, and Karnak and Luxor temples floodlit at night. The quieter West Bank, across the river among the sugarcane fields, sits at the doorstep of the Valley of the Kings and the tombs. Common questions about where to stay in Luxor

Which side of the Nile should I stay on in Luxor?

Most first-time visitors stay on the East Bank, along the corniche, close to Luxor Temple, Karnak, the souk, restaurants and the train station. The West Bank is quieter and more rural, right by the Valley of the Kings and the tombs, and suits travelers who want calm, character and easy early starts at the monuments. A short ferry or bridge crossing links the two.

Is Luxor a good place for a Nile-view hotel?

Yes — a Nile-view room is one of Luxor’s great pleasures, from the historic Winter Palace to the riverside resorts along the corniche. Rooms facing the river cost a little more but the sunsets over the West Bank hills are worth it. When booking, confirm you are getting a river-view rather than a city-view room.

How many nights should I spend in Luxor?

Two to three nights lets you see the East Bank (Karnak and Luxor temples) and the West Bank (Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut’s temple and the Colossi of Memnon) without rushing. Many travelers also join a Nile cruise between Luxor and Aswan, spending a night or two in town at each end.

Is Luxor safe for travelers?

Luxor is one of Egypt’s most visited towns and is generally safe, with a visible tourist-police presence around the monuments. Take normal precautions against petty theft, and expect firm-but-friendly haggling with taxi drivers and souvenir sellers. Agree fares and prices before you set off.
