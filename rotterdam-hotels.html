The areas, and why Rotterdam is the easy one to walk

Rotterdam is the easiest large Dutch city to get around on foot, and the reason is unhappy: the medieval centre was destroyed in May 1940, and what replaced it was built for the twentieth century — wide, flat, straight streets with generous modern pavements and almost no cobbles, canal steps or humpbacked bridges. Anyone expecting Amsterdam’s picturesque tangle will be surprised; anyone with tired knees will be relieved. The metro and an extensive tram network converge on Rotterdam Centraal, and you can tap in with a contactless card. The honest caution is distance — “flat and walkable” still means real distances, and the walk across to the south bank is a long, exposed, windy span.

Centrum / Weena, by Centraal

The practical choice, and the one we would push most older visitors toward. The big full-service hotels are effectively at the station: you arrive, walk a few flat minutes, and never wrestle luggage. Slightly corporate, quiet in the evenings.

Maritime District (Leuvehaven)

The sweet spot between convenience and interest, with the Maritime Museum, the Markthal, the Cube Houses and Blaak market on foot and a metro stop at the door. Best for a first visit.

Kop van Zuid / Wilhelminapier

The south bank — new towers, river views and the finest modern architecture in the country. Atmospheric and calm at night, but you commit to a metro ride or the water taxi every time you want the centre.

Witte de Withstraat

The eating-and-drinking street, dense with bars, galleries and restaurants — excellent if you want to walk out of your door into dinner, genuinely noisy on weekend nights, so ask for a room at the back.

Luxury

Rotterdam’s most comfortable large-scale stays — and if you are arriving by train, the second of these is the single easiest luxury address in the city.

Haven Hotel Rotterdam, Curio Collection by Hilton

Rotterdam’s most comfortable grand-scale stay — a waterfront hotel wrapped around a wellness floor, with a heated pool, sauna and steam room, and upper rooms with harbour views. Boutique

Two waterfront conversions on the south bank, both national monuments, both with real character — and one with the best story of any hotel building in the country.

Suite Hotel Pincoffs

$$$ BoutiqueKop van Zuid, on the water17 suites1879 customs house

A genuinely small hotel — seventeen suites in a listed 1879 customs house on the Entrepothaven — run with an attention that big hotels cannot manage. Rooms are large and individually laid out, organic coffee and tea are included, and the feel is more well-kept private house than hotel. For older travellers it is quiet and residential, well away from nightlife noise; it sits on the south bank at Kop van Zuid, so it is a short metro hop plus a walk from Centraal, and a converted monument of this age is worth a call about lift access before you book.

Hotel New York

$$$ BoutiqueKop van Zuid, Wilhelminapier1901 Holland America HQLift · water taxi

The most storied building in Rotterdam to sleep in — the 1901 head office of the Holland America Line, from whose quay generations of emigrants sailed for New York, now a hotel with two turret rooms and a permanently busy cafe-restaurant. For older travellers there is a lift, and — the real charm — a water taxi runs from the door across the Maas to the centre, sparing you the walk over the Erasmus Bridge; dogs are not accepted here.

Budget & Value

A reliable waterfront chain hotel, and the cheapest genuinely interesting bed in the city — inside the tilted Cube Houses.

$ BudgetWijnhaven, on the waterAccessible roomsDogs accepted

A straightforward, well-located chain hotel on the water between the Cube Houses and the Maas — nothing decorative, but reliably clean, air-conditioned and cheap, with a decent buffet breakfast and a waterside terrace. Family-friendly

One central hotel with pool access and connecting rooms, and one out by the excellent zoo — the single best thing in Rotterdam for children.

A large, reliable family-shaped hotel physically connected to the Haven Hotel next door, which is how guests reach the indoor pool and spa. Easy day trips from Rotterdam

Three of the best short trips in the Netherlands, all from Rotterdam Centraal.

Delft — ten minutes away

The easiest day trip in the country: direct trains take ten to twelve minutes and run constantly, so you need no plan at all. You get the old Holland that Rotterdam lost — canals, the great market square, Vermeer’s town, the Nieuwe Kerk and the blue-and-white pottery. Do mind the cobbles and the step-up thresholds; this is where the walking gets harder, not easier.

Kinderdijk windmills — by water

Nineteen authentic eighteenth-century mills in a working drainage landscape, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The best approach is the shuttle boat from the Willemskade, about half an hour — but it is a weekend service, so check the days before you build a trip around it. The site itself is flat, open and windy, with long level paths and no shade: good easy walking.

Gouda — twenty minutes away

Direct trains in eighteen to twenty-one minutes to a compact market town with a spectacular Gothic town hall and the stained glass of the Sint-Janskerk; the cheese market runs on summer Thursday mornings. An easy half day.

Rotterdam — common questions

Is Rotterdam worth visiting, or should I just do Amsterdam?

Rotterdam is well worth it, and it is a genuine contrast rather than more of the same. Because the old centre was destroyed in 1940 and rebuilt, it is the most modern and architecturally bold city in the country — the Cube Houses, the Markthal, the Erasmus Bridge, a real working harbour — and it is flat, wide and the easiest large Dutch city to walk in. It also makes an excellent base for Delft, Gouda and the Kinderdijk windmills, all a short train ride away. Many visitors pair a few Amsterdam nights with a few in Rotterdam.

Where should an older traveller stay in Rotterdam?

By Rotterdam Centraal, in the Centrum or Weena area, if ease is the priority: the big full-service hotels are effectively at the station, so you arrive and walk a few flat, cobble-free minutes with your luggage. The Maritime District around Leuvehaven is the best all-round choice, with the main sights on foot and a metro stop at the door. The south bank (Kop van Zuid) has the most dramatic hotels and river views, but you commit to a metro ride or the water taxi each time you want the centre.

Is Rotterdam easy to get around?

Very. It is flat, modern and wide, with almost none of the cobbles, canal steps or humpbacked bridges that make the older Dutch cities hard underfoot. The metro and tram network converges on Rotterdam Centraal and you can tap a contactless card to ride. The one honest caution is distance — the city is spread out, so “walkable” still means real walking, and crossing to the south bank on foot is a long, exposed span with river wind that is genuinely cold outside high summer.

How do I get to the Kinderdijk windmills?

The nicest way is by water. A shuttle boat leaves from the Willemskade in Rotterdam and reaches Kinderdijk in about half an hour — but it is a tourist service that runs mainly Friday to Sunday, so check the days before you plan a trip around it. A year-round waterbus route also works with a change onto a small ferry. Once there, the site is flat, open and windy, with long level paths and no shade, which makes for good easy walking.

Does Rotterdam get crowded?

Far less than Amsterdam — it is a working city rather than a tourist one, so it never gets uncomfortably packed. Hotel rates do spike around big events, though, because the same rooms serve the port’s business trade: the summer jazz festival and carnival in July, the spring marathon, and the film festival in late January and early February. The wise windows are May to June and September into early October: mild, long daylight, terraces open and rooms noticeably cheaper than in high summer.
