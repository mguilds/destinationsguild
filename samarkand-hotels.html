Where to Stay in Samarkand — the Old Town or the Resort Park

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Samarkand gives you a genuine choice, and it is the only city in Uzbekistan that does. You can sleep in the old town among the monuments — the Registan, the Gur-e-Amir where Timur is buried, the tiled lane of tombs at Shah-i-Zinda — in a small hotel on a residential street, and walk. Or you can stay out at Silk Road Samarkand, the large purpose-built resort park by the rowing channel on the edge of town, where the international names are. Neither is wrong; they are different holidays. Every hotel below was opened on Trip.com’s own listing for Samarkand and its street address checked before we described it.

Samarkand questions, answered

Should you stay in the old town or at Silk Road Samarkand?

The old town if the monuments are the point of the trip, the resort park if comfort and quiet are. Staying in the old town means you can walk to the Registan in the early morning and again after dark when it is lit and nearly empty, which is the thing most people remember about Samarkand. Silk Road Samarkand is a handsome, modern, landscaped place with the international brands in it, but it is a park on the edge of the city and you will be taking a car in and out every day.

How many nights does Samarkand need?

Two nights as a minimum, three if you want to see it properly. The Registan, the Gur-e-Amir and Shah-i-Zinda can be covered in a long day, but that is the hurried version. A second full day gives you the Ulugbek observatory, the Afrasiab museum, the Siyob bazaar and time to go back to the Registan at an hour when the light is better.

Is Samarkand walkable?

The monument core is, if you are staying in it. The Registan, Bibi-Khanym, the Siyob bazaar and Shah-i-Zinda sit roughly along one axis and the walk between them is pleasant in spring and autumn. In July and August it is not: afternoon highs in Samarkand average around 98°F (37°C), and you will want a car between sites and your sightseeing done early and late.

Is the fast train the best way to arrive?

For most visitors, yes. The high-speed line runs Tashkent–Samarkand–Bukhara, the station is a short ride from both the old town and the resort park, and it is far more pleasant than the road. Book ahead in the shoulder seasons. We do not print timetables or fares here because they move; the railway’s own site has the current ones.

Which monuments are worth paying to go inside?

The Registan, Shah-i-Zinda and the Gur-e-Amir, without question. Bibi-Khanym is impressive from the outside and partly a twentieth-century reconstruction inside. If you only have one evening, spend it at the Registan.
