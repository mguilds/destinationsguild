Where to Stay in Ho Chi Minh City

Where to Stay in Ho Chi Minh City — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Saigon — almost nobody who lives there calls it anything else — is hotter, louder and more forward-leaning than Hanoi, and the pleasure of it is largely a matter of where you plant yourself. District 1 holds the Opera House, the cathedral, the Central Post Office and the river promenade, and for a first or second visit it is simply the right answer. Across the river, Thao Dien is green, low-rise and unhurried, with restored villas and garden pools, at the cost of twenty to thirty-five unpredictable minutes in traffic. Below are the city’s most reliable hotels by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Choosing Your Neighbourhood

Where you sleep shapes the whole trip. Here is the honest lay of the land.

District 1, Ben Thanh or Thao Dien?

Within District 1 the sub-area matters more than the district. The Dong Khoi and Lam Son Square corridor is the classic choice, putting the Opera House, Notre-Dame Cathedral, the Central Post Office and the river walk within a flat ten minutes; Ben Thanh, a block or two west, is a little noisier and noticeably cheaper for the same convenience. Thao Dien, formerly District 2 and reached over the Saigon River, is the quiet, leafy option — ideal if you want gardens, boutique villas and a slower pace, and content to plan trips into town rather than wander out. One administrative note that confuses returning visitors: Vietnam abolished the district tier of local government in July 2025, so hotel addresses now read as wards — Sai Gon, Ben Thanh, Tan Dinh, Thao Dien — but every taxi driver and booking site still says ‘District 1’. The historic state-owned houses — the Continental, the Majestic and the Rex — are all open and trading, and are worth a drink or a meal even if you sleep elsewhere; they trade on atmosphere rather than pampering.

Luxury

Saigon’s top tier divides cleanly between restrained international polish and full-throated ornamental excess. Both are within a few minutes of the Opera House.

Park Hyatt Saigon

$$$$ LuxuryLam Son SquareOpera House doorstepXuan Spa

Purpose-built in 2005 in an unabashedly French-colonial idiom, the Park Hyatt faces Lam Son Square with the Opera House as its front-door view — you can walk to a performance in under a minute, which matters more than it sounds when the heat is heavy. Square One is one of the very few hotel restaurants in Vietnam that locals book for themselves, and Opera handles the Italian side. The palm-shaded courtyard pool is quiet and genuinely usable in the afternoon, and Xuan Spa is the city’s most consistently praised. The service is old-school attentive rather than performative: staff remember names and take their pace from yours.

The Reverie Saigon

$$$$ LuxuryNguyen HueItalian interiorsLarge rooms

Occupying the upper floors of a tower on the Nguyen Hue walking street, The Reverie is the most deliberately opulent hotel in Vietnam — Italian furniture, malachite and marble, and a lobby that looks like a Milanese palazzo airlifted into the tropics. It divides opinion and should: if gilt tires you, stay elsewhere; if you enjoy a hotel with conviction, it is a spectacle. The rooms are unusually large for Saigon, with floor-to-ceiling glass over the river or the boulevard, and the bathrooms are enormous — a real consideration for anyone who values space and something solid to hold. The sixth-floor pool terrace is the calm counterweight.

Boutique

The city’s boutique scene runs in two directions — Indochine-revival design hotels in the District 1 core, and genuinely small restored villas across the river — and the two have almost nothing in common but their room count.

Hotel des Arts Saigon - MGallery Collection

$$$ BoutiqueDistrict 11930s stylingRooftop pool

Accor’s MGallery arm conjured a 1930s Saigon that never quite existed and then executed it with real discipline — dark timber, chequerboard floors, a lift lobby full of curiosities, and a serious collection of Vietnamese art through the public rooms. The rooftop bar and infinity pool on the twenty-third floor have one of the two or three best skyline views in the city, and unlike some rooftops it is comfortable in the early evening rather than only after ten. Notre-Dame Cathedral and the Central Post Office are a short, flat walk. Rooms are compact by luxury-tower standards, but the styling is coherent rather than themed.

Villa Song Saigon

$$$ BoutiqueThao DienRiverside villaBoat to District 1

A restored French-era villa on the bank of the Saigon River in leafy Thao Dien, and the antidote to the city’s tower hotels — twenty-odd rooms, a lawn running down to the water, and a pool that faces the river traffic rather than a light well. The hotel runs a private boat down to District 1, far and away the pleasantest way into town and a merciful alternative to the motorbike scrum. The terrace restaurant serves under old trees, and evenings here are genuinely quiet, which is not a phrase one uses often about this city. Best for travellers who want Saigon at arm’s length.

Budget & Value

Saigon is extraordinarily generous between about fifty and a hundred and twenty dollars — rooftop pools, included airport transfers and full breakfasts are normal at that price. Below roughly forty-five dollars quality becomes erratic, so the sweet spot is the upper-value tier rather than the true budget floor.

Silverland Yen Hotel

$$ ValueNear Ben ThanhRooftop poolAirport pickup included

Silverland is a Vietnamese-owned group that has quietly become the most reliable value operator in central Saigon, and Yen is its most likeable house: sixty-odd rooms on a side street just far enough off Le Loi that the traffic noise drops away, with a small rooftop pool and bar for the hour before dinner. Included in the rate are things that usually cost extra — airport pickup, a proper breakfast, and an afternoon tea served with live piano — which adds up to real money over a week. The rooms are modest in size; the service is not. One block from Ben Thanh Market.

A dependable four-star run by the Vietnamese Liberty group, sitting on Pasteur a few minutes’ walk from both the Opera House and the cathedral — about as central as a mid-priced hotel gets in Saigon. The seventeenth-floor rooftop pool and sky bar are the draw, and the rooms are noticeably more spacious than the city’s boutique average, with proper desks and good blackout curtains. An executive lounge gives you somewhere quiet to sit out the midday heat. It is unglamorous in the best sense: consistent, well staffed, sensibly priced, and a sound base for a first visit.

Family-friendly

Saigon is a business and heritage city rather than a resort, so family strength here means space, connecting rooms, several restaurants and a pool for the middle of the day. Check the cot policy specifically — several excellent hotels do not provide them.

Sheraton Saigon Grand Opera Hotel

$$$ FamilyDong KhoiFree cotsTerrace pool

The most practical big-brand choice for three generations travelling together: children up to four stay free in existing bedding, cots are free on request, and rollaway beds are available — which sounds prosaic until you have tried arranging any of it elsewhere in the city. The open-air terrace pool is heated saltwater, and the restaurants run live show kitchens across Vietnamese, European and pan-Asian, so a fussy grandchild and an adventurous grandparent can eat at the same table. The wine bar upstairs, half covered and half open, is where the adults decamp afterwards. Dong Khoi puts the Opera House, the cathedral and the river walk within an easy stroll.

Lotte Hotel Saigon

Adults-only

Ho Chi Minh City has no genuine adults-only hotel, and we will not dress one up. The handful of properties carrying that label are budget short-stay hotels, serviced apartments that exclude minors for licensing reasons, or backpacker hostels. Travellers who want a child-free resort should plan it for the coast — Phu Quoc, Nha Trang, Con Dao or Da Nang — and treat Saigon as a city stop. Pet-friendly

Most international-brand hotels in Saigon refuse animals outright, so the workable options are Vietnamese-run residences and the Wink group. Always confirm the policy in writing when you book rather than relying on a booking-site filter.

Saigon Domaine Luxury Residences

$$ Pet-friendlyBinh QuoiRiverbank gardensApartment-style

A low-rise, garden-wrapped residence on the Saigon River at Thanh Da, an old market-garden peninsula that still feels semi-rural despite being a short drive from the centre. Dogs and cats are accepted on request at no additional charge, which is unusual in a country where most international hotels refuse animals flatly. The fifty units are apartment-style with kitchens, which suits a longer stay, and there is a riverside pool, a sauna and a café-bar looking out at the water traffic. The trade-off is honest: you will rely on taxis to reach District 1, so this suits an unhurried traveller rather than a tight sightseeing list.

Ho Chi Minh City Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Ho Chi Minh City.

What is the best area to stay in Ho Chi Minh City?

District 1, and within it the Dong Khoi and Lam Son Square corridor, which puts the Opera House, Notre-Dame Cathedral, the Central Post Office and the river promenade within a flat ten-minute walk. Ben Thanh is a little cheaper and a little noisier for the same convenience. Choose Thao Dien, across the river, only if you want quiet gardens and a slower pace and are content with a twenty- to thirty-five-minute drive into town.

Are the historic Saigon hotels still open?

Yes. The Hotel Continental Saigon (1880), the Hotel Majestic Saigon (1925) and the Rex Hotel are all open and trading, and all three are worth a drink or a meal for the atmosphere and the history — the Continental’s courtyard, the Majestic’s river-facing rooftop, the Rex’s fifth-floor garden bar. As places to sleep they trade on character rather than pampering, so set expectations accordingly against the newer luxury houses.

Is Saigon a good city for older travellers?

Yes, with two caveats. The heat and humidity are considerable from March onward, so plan mornings out and afternoons in. And crossing a Saigon street is a skill: step off the kerb slowly and walk at a steady, predictable pace, and the scooters will flow around you — do not stop or dart. Staying in District 1 keeps most of the sights within a short walk, and taxis and ride-hailing cars are plentiful and inexpensive.

How much should I expect to pay for a hotel in Saigon?

A well-run four-star with a rooftop pool and breakfast typically runs between fifty and a hundred and twenty dollars a night, which is the sweet spot for most visitors. The city’s luxury hotels sit in the three-hundred-dollar range and above, still a fraction of their equivalents in Tokyo or Singapore. Below about forty-five dollars quality becomes unpredictable, with windowless rooms and surprise charges a documented risk.

Do Saigon hotels allow pets?

Most international-brand hotels do not, and several refuse even service animals, so this is a category to research carefully rather than assume. The two dependable options are Saigon Domaine Luxury Residences on the river, which accepts dogs and cats at no extra charge, and Wink Saigon Centre in District 1, which publishes a clear nightly pet fee. Confirm in writing at the time of booking.
