Fiji Travel Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Fiji is the one Pacific country that is genuinely easy to reach and genuinely easy to travel in, which is why so many people’s first South Pacific trip is this one. More than three hundred islands, about a third of them lived on, scattered over a stretch of ocean the size of a small country. English is an official language, the national greeting is shouted at you by strangers, and the distance from the aircraft door to a boat is shorter than almost anywhere else in the tropics. Most visitors land at Nadi on the dry western side of Viti Levu, sleep near the airport or on Denarau Island, and are out in the Mamanucas or the Yasawas by the following morning. This is the Guild’s chapter on it.

Why visit Fiji

Four reasons the Guild sends travellers here, in the order people tell us about them afterwards.

The islands offshore

The Mamanucas start twenty minutes out of Port Denarau and the Yasawas run north from them for sixty miles. White sand, water you can see your feet through, and resorts from backpacker huts to the kind with their own airstrip. This is the Fiji on the posters, and it is real.

It is unusually easy

English is an official language and spoken everywhere, the catamarans run to a timetable, the resorts are used to first-time visitors, and you can be swimming within a couple of hours of landing. For a long-haul tropical trip, Fiji asks less of a traveller than almost anywhere.

The diving

Fiji is known for soft corals rather than hard ones, and the colour of a Fijian reef wall takes people by surprise. The Somosomo Strait off Taveuni and the lagoon at Beqa are the two names divers arrive with, and the water is warm every month of the year.

The welcome is not a performance

Fijian hospitality has a formality behind it — the kava ceremony, the request to visit a village, the shoulders-covered courtesy — and once you understand the shape of it, it stops looking like a resort routine and starts looking like manners.

The islands of Fiji

Fiji is an archipelago, and the country changes completely from the dry west coast to the wet southeast and again out at the islands. Places with a Guild guide are marked; the rest carry a link to a hotel our Trip.com partner sells there, checked on its own page, while their chapters are written.

How the country divides up

Viti Levu — the dry west and the Coral Coast

The main island carries the international airport, the capital, three quarters of the population and the mountain range that decides the weather. West of that range, Nadi and Denarau sit in the rain shadow among sugar cane, and inland the Sabeto valley has hot springs and mud pools below the Sleeping Giant ridge. South and east, the Queens Road runs along the Coral Coast past Natadola Bay, the Sigatoka Sand Dunes — Fiji’s first national park — and a string of resorts at Korotogo and Korolevu.

Suva, Pacific Harbour & the southeast

On the far side of the range the island turns green and wet. Pacific Harbour is the base for diving Beqa Lagoon and rafting the Navua river. Suva, the capital, has the Fiji Museum, a colonial seafront and the best market in the country, and Colo-i-Suva Forest Park twenty minutes above it. Few holidays include Suva; the ones that do tend to rate it.

The Mamanucas & the Yasawas

Two chains running north and west off Nadi, and between them the reason most people book Fiji. The Mamanucas are closer — a scatter of small islands twenty minutes to an hour out of Port Denarau, with everything from day-trip beaches to over-water villas. The Yasawas run further north in a long volcanic line, are quieter and less developed, and are where the Blue Lagoon is. One catamaran serves both, stopping at resort jetties on its way up and down.

Vanua Levu, Taveuni & the far islands

The second island, Vanua Levu, has Savusavu on a sheltered bay — a yachting and pearl-farming town with hot springs in the main street. Across the strait, Taveuni is called the Garden Island and is the wettest and greenest place in the country: waterfalls, rainforest trails, and the Somosomo Strait running between the two islands with Fiji’s most famous soft-coral diving in it. Beyond them the Lau and Kadavu groups are a different order of remoteness again.

Top things to do in Fiji

The experiences worth building a trip around.

A day out to the Mamanucas

The catamaran leaves Port Denarau in the morning, stops at a string of islands, and has you back by late afternoon. It is the cheapest way to see why people rave about Fiji, and the single best day a Denarau-based trip can buy.

Diving the Somosomo Strait

The Rainbow Reef between Vanua Levu and Taveuni is Fiji’s best-known wall, and the soft coral on it is the reason the country has the reputation it has. Strong currents; go with an operator who knows the tides.

The shark dive at Beqa Lagoon

Run out of Pacific Harbour, and one of the few places in the world set up to put experienced divers on the reef floor with large sharks in numbers. It is a serious dive with a long briefing, not a theme-park ride.

Kava, properly

Yaqona is the social fabric of the country, not a tourist novelty. Accept a bowl with both hands, clap once before and three times after, and drink it in one. It tastes of muddy water and the evening it belongs to is the point.

The Sigatoka Sand Dunes

Fiji’s first national park, where the wind has piled the river sand into dunes behind the Coral Coast. Marked walking trails, archaeology under the sand, and an hour of the country that looks like nowhere else in it.

Colo-i-Suva Forest Park

Twenty minutes above the capital: rainforest, mahogany, marked paths and natural swimming pools with a rope swing. The fastest way in Fiji to get from a city street into a forest.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

May to October is the dry season and the straightforward half of the year: warm rather than hot, much less humid, and reliably sunny on the western side of Viti Levu and out at the islands. July and August are the busiest and dearest, filled by Australian and New Zealand school holidays; May and September give you most of the same weather for less money and fewer people.

November to April is the wet season — hotter, far more humid, and wet in heavy afternoon bursts rather than all day. The islands are green, the sea is warm, the resorts are quiet and the rates are the lowest of the year. It is also the cyclone season, which runs November to April and peaks in January and February; the Fiji Meteorological Service, which runs RSMC Nadi at the airport, has published an above-average outlook for the 2026–27 season. Check met.gov.fj near your own dates, and take insurance that covers weather disruption.

Getting there

Nadi International Airport (NAN) on the west coast of Viti Levu takes almost every international flight and is where a Fiji trip begins. Nausori Airport (SUV) outside Suva handles mostly regional and domestic services. Fiji is a long-haul hop from the US West Coast and Honolulu and a short one from Australia and New Zealand; route maps change, so check current services rather than assuming.

From the terminal, Port Denarau marina is about twenty minutes by road, and that is where the catamarans to the Mamanucas and Yasawas leave from. Allow a night on the mainland at each end: the long-haul arrivals and the island boats are not timed to connect, and trying to make them do so is the commonest way to lose a day of a Fiji holiday.

Fiji publishes a list of visa-exempt nationalities who are issued a visitor’s permit on arrival, and the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand are all on it. The Ministry of Immigration does not print a standard length for that permit on its own page, so check what you are given at the border and confirm the current rules at immigration.gov.fj before you book.

Fiji travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Fiji?

May to October is the dry season and the easiest weather of the year — warmer than it sounds, much less humid, and reliably sunny on the western side of Viti Levu and out at the islands. July and August are the busiest and dearest because of the Australian and New Zealand school holidays; May and September are the sweet spots. November to April is the wet season: hotter, greener, far cheaper and quieter, and also the cyclone season.

Do Americans need a visa for Fiji?

Fiji publishes a list of visa-exempt nationalities whose citizens are issued a visitor's permit on arrival rather than needing a visa in advance, and the United States is on it, along with Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, Germany and Japan among many others. The Ministry of Immigration's own page does not print a standard length for that permit, so do not assume one — check what you are given on arrival. Confirm the current list and the rules at immigration.gov.fj before you book, and remember a permit still comes with conditions about what you may do in Fiji.

When is cyclone season in Fiji, and does it matter?

November to April, with the peak normally January and February. The Fiji Meteorological Service, which runs RSMC Nadi — the regional cyclone centre for the southwest Pacific, based at the airport most visitors fly into — notes that storms have occasionally formed in October and May as well. Its published outlook for the 2026–27 season forecasts above-average activity. It does not make a wet-season trip a bad idea, but it does mean taking insurance that covers weather disruption, preferring bookings you can move, and checking met.gov.fj close to your dates.

How do you get out to the islands?

By catamaran from Port Denarau marina on Denarau Island, about twenty minutes from Nadi airport. Scheduled boats run north through the Mamanucas and on up the Yasawas, dropping guests at resort jetties along the way, and the same marina handles day cruises and most resort transfers. Seaplanes and helicopters serve the further resorts. Check your boat's departure time against your flight before booking a first night — the long-haul arrivals and the island departures do not connect.

How many days do you need in Fiji?

Ten is the comfortable shape: a night near Nadi on arrival, five or six out at an island, and a couple on the mainland — the Coral Coast or Suva — before flying home. With a week, give the islands the middle and keep the mainland to the first and last nights. With less than five nights, do not try to add an island transfer at both ends; stay on Denarau or the Coral Coast and take a day boat out.

What should you know before visiting a Fijian village?

Go with someone who belongs there, or with a guide, rather than wandering in. The custom is sevusevu: you present a gift of kava to the village head, and you are welcomed as a guest rather than a sightseer. Cover your shoulders and knees, take off your hat and sunglasses inside the village, and do not touch anyone's head. Ask before photographing people. None of it is difficult, and getting it right changes the whole tenor of the visit.
