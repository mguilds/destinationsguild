Where to Stay in Bocas del Toro

An archipelago where the roads run out and the water taxis take over. Which island you choose decides almost everything about the trip — the noise, the cost of moving about, and how easily you reach a beach.

Bocas Town, Isla Colón

The main settlement, built on stilts over the water in Caribbean clapboard. Everything arrives and leaves here: the airstrip, the boats, the banks, the restaurants and the bars. Convenient and cheap to move around, and genuinely noisy at night on the main waterfront. Best if you want to walk to dinner rather than book a boat for it.

The rest of Isla Colón

North of town the island opens out to Bluff, Big Creek and the surf beaches, and it is quiet in a way the town never is. You will need a taxi or a bicycle for anything, and the road is rough in the wet months.

Isla Carenero

Five minutes across the water from town by boat, and a different world: no road, a handful of places on the shore, and the lights of Bocas Town visible but not audible. The easiest compromise between quiet and convenient.

Isla Bastimentos

Larger, greener and more spread out, with the village of Old Bank at one end, Red Frog Beach and a national marine park. Boats are the only way in and out, and most places build the transfer into the stay. The choice if you want sand and forest rather than a town.

The outer cays

Scattered places on small islands, reached only by arranged boat, often with their own generator and no shops at all. Wonderful, and not somewhere to be improvising.

Travelling in your later years

Everything here involves a boat, and that is the thing to settle before you book. You step down into an open launch from a dock, sometimes in a swell and sometimes in rain, and there is rarely a handrail. Ask the hotel plainly how you board and whether they will help, and say so if you need a hand — the boatmen here do it all day and are used to it.

Bocas Town itself is flat and short, which is in its favour, but the waterfront walkways are wooden, uneven and slippery when wet. Medical care is basic: there is a small hospital, and anything serious means a flight to the mainland or the capital. Bring your prescriptions with you; the pharmacies are small. Take insurance that will move you.

Questions travellers ask about Bocas del Toro

How do I get to Bocas del Toro?

Either a short flight from Panama City to the airstrip at Bocas Town, or a long drive or overnight bus to Almirante on the mainland and then a water taxi across. The flight is much the easier day; the bus and boat is much the cheaper one.

Which island should I choose?

Bocas Town if you want restaurants, company and no transfer costs. Carenero if you want five minutes of water between you and the music. Bastimentos if you are here for the beach and the forest and are happy to book boats. The outer cays if you want to disappear.

When is the weather best?

Not when you would guess. The Caribbean side keeps its own calendar, and September and October are often calm and clear here while the Pacific side of the country is at its wettest. February and March are usually good too. Our month-by-month guide sets it out.

Do I need cash?

Yes, more than elsewhere in Panama. There are banks and machines in Bocas Town and effectively nothing on the smaller islands, and plenty of small places take cash only. Draw what you need before you leave town.
