Nairobi — A Capital with a National Park on Its Doorstep

Where to Stay in Nairobi — Area by Area

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Nairobi is the front door to Kenya for almost everybody, and most visitors give it one night at the start and one at the end without ever deciding where in it to sleep. That is a pity, because the districts are genuinely different from one another — in what you can walk to, in how long the drive to the airport takes, and in how the evening feels. This page is about choosing between them.

It is also a city where the honest word matters more than the enthusiastic one. There is real violent crime here, official advice names the districts where the risk is highest, and the traffic will eat hours you had planned to spend elsewhere. None of that is a reason to stay away. It is a reason to book the right side of town and to arrange your airport car before you land.

Westlands — the straightforward first choice

Westlands has the deepest supply of rooms in the city across every price level, and it is the district where a visitor with two nights and a long list gets the most done. Restaurants, malls and offices sit close together, so an evening does not require a car for every leg, and the road out to Gigiri and the northern suburbs is short. It is busy, it is modern, and it does not pretend to be charming. For a first visit that is the right trade.

Kilimani and Lavington — quieter, greener, still central

South and west of Westlands, these are leafy residential districts that have filled up with smaller hotels and serviced apartments. You trade some of the restaurant density for jacaranda trees and a quieter night, and you are still fifteen minutes from the centre when the traffic allows. This is the part of town we would point a returning visitor to, or anyone staying more than three nights.

Karen and Langata — for the park, the giraffes and an early flight

Out to the south-west, Karen and Langata are where Nairobi stops feeling like a city. The elephant orphanage, the giraffe centre and the Karen Blixen house are all here, Nairobi National Park runs along the edge, and Wilson Airport — where the small safari planes leave from — is close. If your Kenya trip begins with a light aircraft at dawn, sleeping out here instead of in town can save you an hour of morning traffic and a good deal of anxiety. The cost is that you are a long way from everything else.

Gigiri, Runda and Muthaiga — the diplomatic quarter

North of Westlands, around the United Nations complex and the embassies, this is the quietest and most heavily secured part of the city, with large grounds and a settled feel. It suits anyone here on business with the international organisations, and anyone who wants the calmest possible base. It is residential rather than lively, and you will use a car for every meal you do not eat in the hotel.

The city centre — and the honest warning about it

The Central Business District has the oldest hotels in Nairobi, the railway station and the cheapest rooms, and it is where the city's own working life happens. It is also one of the areas the Foreign Office names where the risk of violent crime is higher, along with Eastleigh and the slum districts. We are not going to tell you the centre is a relaxing place to sleep. If you stay here, do it with your eyes open: move by car after dark, leave the watch at home, and treat the walk back from dinner as something you do not do.

Near the airport — for a short layover and nothing else

There are rooms along Mombasa Road close to Jomo Kenyatta International, and they exist for one purpose: a late landing and an early departure. They are not a base for seeing Nairobi, because getting anywhere from them means the same traffic you were trying to avoid. If you have one night between two flights, this is the sensible choice. If you have two, stay in town.

Before you book

You now need government travel insurance

From 5 October 2026, most visitors staying under twelve months must hold travel health insurance provided by the Kenyan government. If you are applying for an electronic travel authorisation you buy it as part of that application; if you already hold a valid one you buy it through the eCitizen insurance portal. A policy from home does not satisfy this. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements for Kenya, still current at 21 November 2026.

Apply for the eTA well ahead

You must apply online for electronic travel authorisation before you travel, and the Kenyan authorities recommend applying at least two weeks before departure. Your passport needs an expiry date at least six months after you arrive and at least two blank pages. Anyone still holding a valid visa can travel on it until it expires.

The airport road, specifically

Use the Mombasa Road or the Nairobi Expressway between the airport and the city. That is the Foreign Office's own wording, not a general caution. Book the transfer through your hotel in advance.

Altitude, gently

At about 1,800 metres the first day is more tiring than you expect. Nothing dramatic — but if you are arriving off a night flight and your plan is a walking tour, move it to the following morning.

Malaria is a lowland and coastal risk

Nairobi sits high and the risk here is lower than on the coast and in the lowlands, but your trip will almost certainly include somewhere that is not Nairobi. Get travel health advice at least eight weeks before you fly and ask about the whole itinerary, not the capital.

An Ebola case was confirmed in October

On 6 October 2026 the Kenyan Ministry of Health confirmed the country's first imported case of Ebola, and public health measures including contact tracing and border screening have been in place since. Check the Ministry of Health and your own government's travel health advice before you fly, and check whether anywhere you travel on to afterwards has added screening for arrivals from Kenya.

Questions readers actually ask

Which area should I stay in for a first visit to Nairobi?


Westlands for a first visit. It has the widest choice of rooms, it is close to the places most visitors want to reach, and it is one of the few parts of the city where short walks between restaurants are normal. Karen and Langata suit anyone whose plan is the elephant orphanage, the giraffe centre and an early start for a safari flight from Wilson Airport.

How do I get from Jomo Kenyatta airport into the city?


The Foreign Office advice is specific about this: for travel between Jomo Kenyatta International Airport and Nairobi city, use the Mombasa Road or the Nairobi Expressway. Arrange the transfer through your hotel before you land rather than finding a car at the kerb, and allow much more time than the distance suggests, because the traffic is heavy.

Is Nairobi high enough to feel the altitude?


The city sits at roughly 1,800 metres, about 5,900 feet. That is high enough that a first-day walk uphill is harder work than you expect and alcohol goes further than it does at home. It is not high enough to be dangerous for most people. Take the first afternoon slowly, drink more water than you want to, and leave anything strenuous until day two.

Which parts of Nairobi does official advice tell visitors to avoid?


The Foreign Office names Eastleigh, the Central Business District, and Mathare, Kibera and the slum areas as places where the risk of violent crime is higher. It also advises against walking alone in isolated areas even in daylight anywhere in the city. None of that makes Nairobi a city to skip, but it does mean you move by car at night and you do not wander.

Do I need a car in Nairobi?


No, and most visitors should not want one. Traffic is slow, parking is awkward and driving yourself at night is not advised. Ride apps work well in the areas visitors stay in. Hire the vehicle, with a driver, for the days you leave the city.
