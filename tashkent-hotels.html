Where to Stay in Tashkent — the Avenue, the Towers, the Old City

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Tashkent is a capital of four million people rebuilt on a grand scale after the 1966 earthquake, which is why it feels less like an old Silk Road town than the three cities to its west and more like a city of wide avenues, parks and a Soviet-built metro with stations worth riding out of your way to see. Nearly every visitor passes through: it holds the main international airport and the western end of the fast train to Samarkand and Bukhara. Where you sleep comes down to which Tashkent you want — the avenue, the new towers, or the old city near Chorsu. Tashkent questions, answered

Is Tashkent worth more than a night on the way through?

A day and two nights is about right for most people. Tashkent is not the reason anyone flies to Uzbekistan — Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva are — but it has the Khast Imam complex and its old Qur’an manuscript, the Chorsu bazaar under its great blue dome, several good museums, and a metro whose older stations were built as public architecture and are worth a ride in their own right. It is also the easiest place in the country to eat well and to sort out anything practical before you head west.

Where is the best area to stay in Tashkent?

Along or just off Amir Temur Avenue, if it is your first visit. The avenue runs through the middle of the city, the metro follows it, and the parks and restaurants are on it. Tashkent City, the new district of towers to the west, is newer and smarter but is a built-from-scratch quarter rather than a neighbourhood. If you want the older, lower, more domestic Tashkent, stay on the Chorsu and Khast Imam side.

How do you get from Tashkent to Samarkand and Bukhara?

By train, and it is the best thing about travelling in Uzbekistan. A high-speed service runs west from Tashkent to Samarkand and on to Bukhara, with slower trains on the same line. Book ahead in spring and autumn, when seats go early. The railway’s own site and ticket counters have the current timetable and fares; we do not print times here because they change with the season and we would rather you had the real one.

Do you need a car in Tashkent?

No. The metro is cheap, clean and goes where visitors go, and ride-hailing apps work across the city. A car is only worth it for day trips out of town.

Is the airport far from the centre?

No — Tashkent’s international airport is inside the city, a short drive south of the centre rather than an hour out in the fields. That is why an ‘airport hotel’ in Tashkent is still an ordinary city hotel, and why you do not need to move out there the night before unless you want to.
