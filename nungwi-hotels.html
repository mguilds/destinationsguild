Nungwi — The Northern Tip, Where the Water Stays Deep

Where to Stay in Nungwi — Area by Area

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Nungwi and Kendwa sit at the northern tip of Zanzibar, and they are busy for one unglamorous and entirely practical reason: the water off this stretch is deep enough to swim in whatever the tide is doing, which is not true of much of the island’s coast.

Beyond that, the choice is between a lively strip and a quiet one, and they are a few minutes apart. This page is about which is which.

Nungwi village and the west-facing beach — sunset and deep water

The western side of the northern tip is the reason Nungwi filled up: a deep, swimmable channel that does not empty at low tide, which much of Zanzibar’s coast does, and the sunset in front of you. The hotels run shoulder to shoulder along it, from backpacker rooms to serious resorts, and the beach bars are busy into the evening. Lively is the word. If that is what you want, this is the best stretch on the island for it.

The eastern and northern headlands — quieter, a short walk out

Round the point towards Kendwa’s side and along the northern rocks, the properties thin out and the noise with them. You are ten or fifteen minutes’ walk from the bars and the sand is just as good. For couples and for anyone who came for a book and a swim rather than a beach party, this is the better half of Nungwi.

Kendwa, four kilometres south — wider sand, same water

Kendwa has the broadest beach in the north, the same reliable deep water and a calmer feel than Nungwi village, with a famous full-moon party as the exception. Taxis between the two are quick and cheap. Many people who think they want Nungwi actually want Kendwa, and it is worth looking at both before booking.

Before you book

Government travel insurance, and which one to buy

Every visitor to Zanzibar and to mainland Tanzania, residents aside, must hold inbound travel insurance provided by the government — and you must hold it even if you already have a policy of your own. The important detail for Zanzibar: you buy it for your FIRST port of entry only. Fly straight into Zanzibar and you buy from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation, online beforehand or on arrival. Come through Dar es Salaam or Kilimanjaro first and you buy from the National Insurance Corporation instead, and you do not buy again when you reach the island. It covers your stay up to 92 days, and you may be refused entry without it. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements for Tanzania, still current at 22 November 2026.

Get the e-visa before you fly

You need a tourist or business visa, and you apply for an e-visa online before you travel — it is no longer possible to get one from the Tanzanian High Commission in London. A single-entry visa is available on arrival at the main ports of entry if you meet the requirements, and you may be asked to show your return or onward ticket; a multiple-entry visa cannot be had on arrival. Your passport needs an expiry date at least six months after you arrive and at least one blank page.

Health screening at the border

Tanzania’s Ministry of Health announced heightened screening on 17 October for travellers arriving from high-risk regions and affected countries, at every point of entry, and every traveller has to complete a Travellers Health Surveillance Form before departure or on arrival. You also pass through mpox screening — body temperature checks — at all points of entry. Check the current list of affected countries before you fly.

What official advice says about Zanzibar specifically

There is a risk of armed crime across Tanzania, and on Zanzibar official advice records incidents in Stone Town and at hotels and on popular tourist beaches throughout the island — not only in the town. The Foreign Office added guidance on 30 October 2026 about using the security measures your accommodation provides to reduce the risk of burglary. Use the room safe, lock the balcony door, and do not leave a bag on a sunbed. Past elections in Zanzibar have also turned violent.

Dress, and Ramadan

Zanzibar is a mainly Muslim island and the beach resorts are an exception to it rather than the rule. Shoulders and knees covered away from the sand is the courtesy that costs nothing, and in Stone Town it will change how you are treated. During Ramadan many restaurants outside the hotels close during daylight and eating or drinking in the street is poor form. This is the Guild’s own advice, not a legal requirement.

Malaria, and where the hospitals are

Malaria is a risk across Tanzania, and dengue, cholera and mpox are all named in official health advice. Get travel health advice at least eight weeks before you fly and ask about your whole itinerary rather than one city. Medical facilities are limited, particularly outside Dar es Salaam, so insurance that covers medical evacuation is not a luxury here. The emergency number is 112.

Questions readers actually ask

Why Nungwi rather than the east coast of Zanzibar?


Tide. Much of Zanzibar's east coast turns to shallow seagrass flats at low tide, which is fascinating to walk on and no use for swimming, so the resorts there rely on pools for half the day. The channel off Nungwi and Kendwa at the northern tip stays deep enough to swim at any state of the tide. If swimming from the beach matters to you, the north is where to book.

Nungwi or Kendwa?


Kendwa for a wider beach and a quieter evening; Nungwi village for choice, restaurants and a night out. They are four kilometres apart with the same water, and a taxi between them takes minutes, so you are not really giving anything up either way. Couples and families generally prefer Kendwa. Nungwi's west-facing strip is the livelier one, and in high season it is genuinely busy.

How do I get to Nungwi from Zanzibar airport or Stone Town?


By road, about an hour and a half from either, since the airport sits just south of Stone Town. Arrange the transfer through your hotel for a fixed price rather than negotiating at the rank, especially on a late arrival. The usual shape of a week is two nights in Stone Town first and then the north, which also means you do the history before the sand rather than after.

Is the north coast safe?


Broadly yes, with the ordinary coastal-resort caution. Official advice records incidents affecting visitors at hotels and on popular tourist beaches throughout Zanzibar, not just in Stone Town, and in October 2026 it added guidance about using the security measures your accommodation provides against burglary. In practice: the room safe, the balcony door locked, and nothing left on a sunbed while you swim. Walking the beach alone late at night is the thing we would not do.

Do I need to cover up away from the beach?


Yes, and it is worth doing properly. Zanzibar is a mainly Muslim island and the resort strip is the exception rather than the rule. Swimwear belongs on the sand; shoulders and knees covered in the village, and more so in Stone Town. During Ramadan, eating or drinking in the street in daylight is poor form and many non-hotel restaurants close. This is courtesy rather than law, and it changes how you are treated.
