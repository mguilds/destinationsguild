Where to Stay in Vientiane

Where to Stay in Vientiane — Riverfront & Nam Phou

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Vientiane is the quietest capital in Southeast Asia and makes no apology for it. A bend of the Mekong with Thailand on the far bank, a grid of French-planned streets, a golden stupa, a triumphal arch built from borrowed concrete, and a pace that catches visitors off guard after Bangkok or Hanoi. Most people give it a night and wish they had given it two. Stay on the riverfront for the sunset and the night market, around Nam Phou for the old quarter and the restaurants, and out towards Patuxai and That Luang for space and quiet.

Choosing Your Area in Vientiane

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

The riverfront and Chao Anouvong Park

The Mekong promenade is where Vientiane goes in the evening: a long raised park, an aerobics class or three, a night market under the trees, and the sun going down over Thailand on the far bank. Hotels here range from small guest houses on the back lanes to the larger river-facing places.

It is the most atmospheric place to stay and the easiest to walk from. The river itself drops to sandbanks in the dry months, so the view is of a wide pale channel rather than a full river for much of the high season.

Nam Phou and the old quarter

The few blocks inland around the Nam Phou fountain hold the city's French-era shophouses, its best restaurants and bakeries, and most of what visitors actually come to see — Wat Si Saket with its thousands of small Buddhas, Haw Phra Kaew, and the National Museum, all within an easy walk.

This is the Guild's default recommendation for a first stay. You can leave the hotel on foot and reach almost everything, and still be five minutes from the river.

Out towards Patuxai and That Luang

East and north of the centre the streets widen into boulevards, and this is where the larger, newer and generally cheaper hotels sit, along with the Patuxai arch and the golden stupa of Pha That Luang.

Quieter and better value, with more room, but you will take a tuk-tuk to dinner. Worth it if you want space or a pool; less so for a single night.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Vientiane trip goes.

The railway station is a long way out of town

The Laos–China Railway has made Vientiane to Luang Prabang a short ride instead of a long mountain drive, and it is the best way to travel north. The catch is the station: the line's Vientiane terminus is well outside the city centre, not in it. Allow a proper transfer at both ends and do not plan to arrive at the station twenty minutes before departure the way you might at home.

Seats on popular departures sell out, so arrange tickets in advance rather than turning up and hoping.

Vientiane is an eVisa port — three of them, in fact

Three of the nine checkpoints that accept the Lao eVisa are here: Wattay International Airport, the Lao-Thai Friendship Bridge I crossing from Nong Khai in Thailand, and Khamsavath Railway Station. If you are arriving by air, by bridge or by train from China, your eVisa works.

Print the approval letter. Lao immigration requires a printed copy and does not accept it on a phone screen.

The COPE centre, and why it is worth an hour

COPE's visitor centre, attached to the rehabilitation hospital, explains the bombing of Laos and the unexploded ordnance still in the ground, and what is being done about both. It is clear-eyed rather than grim, it is free, and almost everyone who goes says it was the most worthwhile hour they spent in the city.

It also tells you the one thing a visitor needs to know about getting around the countryside: stay on marked paths and never handle metal objects you find on the ground.

Vientiane Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Vientiane.

Where is the best place to stay in Vientiane?

Around Nam Phou and the old quarter for a first visit — the temples, the museum, the restaurants and the river are all within a walk, which is not true of anywhere else in the city. The riverfront itself if you want the promenade and the night market on your doorstep. Out towards Patuxai if you want more room and a lower rate and do not mind a tuk-tuk to dinner.

How long do you need in Vientiane?

Two nights. One is enough to see Pha That Luang, Wat Si Saket, Patuxai and the river at sunset, but it leaves no time for the COPE centre or the Buddha Park out of town, and it turns a relaxed city into a checklist. Most people who stay two nights say they were glad of the second; most who stay one say they wish they had stayed two.

Is Vientiane walkable?

The centre is, comfortably. The old quarter, the riverfront, Wat Si Saket, Haw Phra Kaew and the National Museum sit within about a kilometre of each other on flat ground. Patuxai is a long but manageable walk; Pha That Luang and the Buddha Park are not, and want a tuk-tuk. Pavements are uneven in places, and the midday heat from March to May will shorten anyone's ambitions.

How do you get from Vientiane to Luang Prabang or Vang Vieng?

By train, in most cases. The Laos–China Railway runs north from Vientiane through Vang Vieng to Luang Prabang and has replaced a long day on mountain roads with a short ride, and it is the reason most itineraries now include Vang Vieng at all. Book ahead, because seats sell out. The Vientiane–Boten expressway is the road alternative and is much faster than the old Route 13; there are also short domestic flights to Luang Prabang.
