Granada’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Granada — Best Neighborhoods & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Granada climbs from a lively centre up two great hills — the Alhambra on one side, the white Moorish Albaicín on the other — so where you unpack decides your days: waking inside the monument, lost in Moorish lanes, or steps from the Cathedral and the tapas bars. The centre is walkable, but the hills are steep and cobbled, worth weighing if stairs are a concern. We’ve grouped Granada’s most characterful and well-loved hotels by neighborhood, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can simply choose your dates.

On the Alhambra Hill

To sleep on the wooded hill of the Alhambra is to have the Nasrid palaces, the Generalife gardens and the great views to yourself once the day-trippers leave. It is a short but steep walk or a minibus ride from the centre — magical, a little removed, and the most coveted address in the city.

Parador de Granada

$$$$ LuxuryInside the Alhambra15th-century conventIconic

Spain’s most sought-after parador occupies a 15th-century convent within the walls of the Alhambra itself, steps from the Nasrid palaces and gardens. Book far ahead: nowhere else lets you stay inside the monument and walk the grounds after the crowds leave. Rooms are characterful rather than large. The bucket-list Granada stay.

Alhambra Palace Hotel

$$$$ LuxuryAlhambra hill1910 Moorish-style landmarkCity views

A flamboyant red Moorish-style palace opened in 1910 on the hill just below the Alhambra, this landmark hotel is famous for a terrace with sweeping views over Granada to the Sierra Nevada. Grand, ornate public rooms and a storied guest list define it. For travelers who want history, drama and a view.

The Albaicín — The Moorish Quarter

Facing the Alhambra across a ravine, the UNESCO-listed Albaicín is a white labyrinth of cobbled lanes, carmen gardens and the famous San Nicolás viewpoint. Staying here is pure atmosphere — but the streets are steep and largely car-free, so pack light and wear good shoes.

Palacio de Santa Inés

$$$ BoutiqueAlbaicín16th-century palaceRenaissance frescoes

A tiny boutique hotel in a 16th-century palace deep in the Albaicín, Santa Inés wraps a handful of rooms around a columned courtyard, some with faded Renaissance frescoes and Alhambra views. Steeply cobbled lanes lead to the door. An atmospheric, intimate choice for romantics who don’t mind a walk uphill.

Around the Cathedral & the Centre

The flat heart of the city around Plaza Nueva and the Cathedral is the easiest base: level walking to almost everything, the start of the Alhambra path, and tapas bars where a free plate still comes with your drink. Central, convenient and lively — the practical choice for a first visit.

Gran Vía & the Realejo

The Gran Vía de Colón cuts a straight, handsome line through the centre past the Cathedral, while the old Jewish quarter of the Realejo climbs gently toward the Alhambra with leafy squares and characterful bars. Well connected and central, with a good spread of hotels at every price.

Budget & Value

Granada is famously good value, and its free-tapas culture stretches any budget. Around Puerta Real, the Realejo and the centre you’ll find bright, well-run smaller hotels and guesthouses a short walk from the sights — comfort and character without the marquee price.

Room Mate Leo

$$ Mid-rangePuerta RealDesignGreat value

A fun, design-led Room Mate hotel in the very centre near Puerta Real, Leo offers bold, comfortable rooms — some with little balconies — a generous late breakfast, and warm service at a friendly price. Central and lively. Great value for younger travelers and couples who want style without the splurge.
