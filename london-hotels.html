London’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in London — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

London is a city of villages, and the district you choose colours the entire visit. It sprawls far wider than most European capitals, so the first decision isn’t the hotel — it’s the area you want to walk out into each morning, and how close you want to be to the Tube. We’ve grouped London’s most enduring, well-loved hotels by district and character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can simply pick your dates.

Mayfair & St James’s

The grandest corner of London, wedged between Hyde Park and the shops of Bond Street, Mayfair is the address of the city’s legendary hotels, private clubs, and art galleries. It is elegant, quiet after dark, and superbly central. Book here when the hotel itself is part of the occasion.

Claridge’s

$$$$ LuxuryBrook Street, MayfairArt DecoIconic afternoon tea

The definitive London grand hotel, Claridge’s has hosted royalty and film stars for well over a century behind its shimmering Art Deco facade. Its foyer, afternoon tea, and bars are institutions in their own right. The natural choice for a landmark stay where old-world glamour is the whole point.

The Connaught

$$$$ LuxuryCarlos Place, MayfairMichelin diningAward-winning bar

Discreet and impeccably run, The Connaught pairs a red-brick Edwardian frame with some of the finest food and drink in London, from its Michelin-starred restaurant to a bar routinely ranked among the world’s best. Service here is famously anticipatory. Ideal for travellers who prize refinement and quiet over showiness.

The Ritz London

$$$$ LuxuryPiccadillyGilded & historicGreen Park views

A byword for opulence since 1906, The Ritz overlooks Green Park with its Louis XVI interiors, gilded salons, and the famously formal Palm Court tea. It sits on the seam between Mayfair and St James’s, steps from the parks and Piccadilly. Best for those who want unabashed grandeur and a name that needs no introduction.

Covent Garden & the Strand

The theatrical heart of London, this is the district of the West End stages, the piazza’s street performers, and the river just beyond the Strand. Everything is walkable — theatres, the National Gallery, the South Bank across the Thames. Choose it if you want energy, culture, and the city’s pulse right outside the door.

The Savoy

$$$$ LuxuryThe StrandThames viewsAmerican Bar

A riverside legend on the Strand, The Savoy blends Edwardian and Art Deco splendour with the celebrated American Bar and the light-filled Thames Foyer for tea. Its position bridges Covent Garden and the river, minutes from the theatres. A grand, glamorous base for a first visit that wants to feel like an event.

Warm, modern service sets it apart from the grande dames. A polished pick for culture-lovers who want theatres and the river at their feet.

A Firmdale boutique tucked on a quiet street in the thick of Theatreland, dressed in the group’s signature bold English fabrics and antiques. Knightsbridge & South Kensington

London’s smartest shopping-and-museums quarter runs from Harrods and Knightsbridge west into leafy South Kensington, home to the great museums and elegant garden squares. It is refined, green, and beautifully connected. A calm, upscale base for families, culture-seekers, and anyone who wants Hyde Park on their doorstep.

The Berkeley

$$$$ LuxuryKnightsbridgeRooftop poolContemporary

A Maybourne sibling of Claridge’s and The Connaught, The Berkeley leans more modern, crowned by a rooftop pool with a retractable roof overlooking Hyde Park. Its bars and spa are destinations for Londoners as well as guests. Ideal for travellers who want top-tier luxury with a fresher, design-led edge beside the park.

Understated on the outside, lavish within. A sophisticated choice for guests who favour sleek modern luxury and a serious wellness retreat.

Soho & Fitzrovia

Soho is London at its most restless — a warren of restaurants, bars, and music that runs late, with quieter, media-flecked Fitzrovia just to the north. You are steps from Oxford Street, the West End, and some of the city’s best eating. Choose it for atmosphere, nightlife, and dinner over a picture-perfect address.

The Soho Hotel

$$$ UpscaleSohoPlayful designScreening rooms

A Firmdale flagship on a quiet Soho cul-de-sac, splashed with bold colour and anchored by a huge Botero cat sculpture in the lobby. Its two film-screening rooms and buzzy bar pull in the film and fashion set. Best for design-minded travellers who want a lively, colourful base in the middle of the West End.

A trio of Georgian townhouses on Frith Street, Hazlitt’s is a candlelit warren of four-poster beds, panelled walls, and antiques with a devoted literary following. There is no restaurant and no lift — just character in spades. A romantic, atmospheric choice for travellers who prize history over modern polish.

Another Firmdale gem, this one in media-land Fitzrovia, decorated in a fresh English style with nods to the Bloomsbury Group and a much-loved bar and brasserie. Quieter than Soho proper but a short walk from everything. Ideal for travellers who want boutique warmth and creative energy just north of the bustle.

The City & Shoreditch

East of the centre, the historic City gives way to Shoreditch and Spitalfields, where old markets, street art, and independent bars define London’s creative edge. Weekdays hum with finance, weekends with nightlife and vintage stalls. Choose it for a hipper, less touristy stay with excellent transport and easy access to Tower Bridge.

The Ned

$$$ UpscaleThe CityFormer bankMany restaurants

Set in a monumental former Midland Bank headquarters, The Ned turns a soaring pillared banking hall into a buzzing floor of restaurants beneath its bedrooms and rooftop pool. It is as much a scene as a hotel, minutes from Bank and St Paul’s. Best for travellers who want grand architecture, great dining, and City energy under one roof.

Intimate and richly atmospheric, it is a world away from the corporate chains. A distinctive pick for travellers drawn to period charm and East London’s history.

Budget & Value

London is famously pricey, but a good night’s sleep needn’t cost a fortune — you simply trade square footage or a marquee address for a smart, central room and a short Tube ride. These dependable value picks put you close to the sights so you can spend on museums and meals instead of the nightly rate.

Premier Inn London County Hall

$$ ValueSouth BankFamily-friendlyBy the London Eye

Housed in the grand old County Hall beside the London Eye, this reliable branch of Britain’s best-loved budget chain offers comfy, consistent rooms right on the South Bank. Waterloo station and the river walk are on the doorstep. A no-surprises, well-located choice for families and first-timers watching the budget.
