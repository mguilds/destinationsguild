Bridgetown

Where to Stay in Bridgetown

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Bridgetown is a working capital rather than a resort, and that is the whole point of staying here. You get a UNESCO World Heritage site on the doorstep — the old town and its Georgian garrison were inscribed together in 2011 — the calmest swimming water on the island in Carlisle Bay, the bus hub for everywhere else, and prices well below the west coast. What you do not get is a resort strip. Almost nobody stays in the commercial centre itself; the hotels are strung south along the bay through the Garrison and on towards Hastings, which is where the rest of this page is looking.

Where to base yourself in Bridgetown

Four quite different places to sleep within three miles of Parliament, and they suit very different travellers.

The Garrison and Aquatic Gap

Our pick for most visitors to the capital. The old British garrison, its parade ground now a racecourse, sits on a low rise between the town and the sea, with Pebbles Beach and the calm end of Carlisle Bay below it. You can walk to Parliament in twenty minutes along the bay, swim before breakfast, and read two centuries of hard history on the walls on the way home. Level ground, good buses, and the cruise terminal a short taxi away.

Carlisle Bay and Bay Street

The strip of road between the town and the Garrison, right on the water. This is where the turtles feed and where the two accessible shipwrecks lie, so it is the best address on the island for a snorkeller. It is also a main road, so ask for a room away from the traffic side and expect some night noise at weekends.

Hastings and Rockley

A mile or two further south-east, where Bridgetown blurs into the south coast. More restaurants, more shops, a boardwalk along the sea that is flat, paved and genuinely pleasant to walk, and better value again. A bus into town takes ten minutes and runs late. If you want a town base with somewhere to eat every night, stay here.

The commercial centre

Broad Street, the careenage and the markets. Busy, hot and thoroughly alive by day, and largely shut by eight in the evening. There are a handful of beds here and they are cheap and convenient for an early flight or a ship, but there is no beach and little reason to be here after dark. We would stay by the water and come into town on the bus.

What your money is worth in Barbados
US $1 = 2.00 Bds$
Bds$ 1 = US 50¢
The Barbados dollar is pegged to the US dollar at two to one, so halving the local price gives you the dollar price. US dollars are accepted almost everywhere and change comes back in Barbados dollars. Hotel bills normally carry a nightly government room levy and a service charge on top of the room rate, and restaurants usually add service too — ask what is included when you book rather than at check-out. Keep some cash for buses, rum shops and the fish fry.

Bridgetown in your later years

The bay path is the flattest ground in the capital

From the Garrison along Carlisle Bay towards the town, and the Hastings boardwalk further on, you have a level, paved, largely shaded walk beside the sea. If walking is a consideration, basing yourself along either changes the week for the better.

Carlisle Bay has the gentlest entry to the water

Pebbles Beach and the bay beside it are calm, shallow and sandy with almost no shore break — the easiest place to get in and out of the sea in Barbados, and the reason we recommend the capital to anyone uncertain on their feet.

Ask about the lift and the road

Many of the hotels along Bay Street are older buildings of three or four storeys, and not all have a lift. The same stretch is a main road, so a quiet room and a working lift are both worth asking about by name before you book.

Do the town in the morning

Bridgetown is flat and walkable but it is also hot, and the shade in Broad Street runs out by mid-morning. Go early, take a long lunch, and leave the afternoon for the water.

Nearby, and the rest of the island

Bridgetown is the island’s bus hub, which makes it the easiest base for seeing the rest of Barbados without a car. Oistins and the fish fry are twenty-five minutes down the south coast — our Oistins and south coast guide has the detail — and Holetown and the Platinum Coast are thirty to forty-five minutes north. Bridgetown hotels — questions

Is Bridgetown a good base for a holiday in Barbados?

It is a good base for a particular kind of holiday: one where you want some town around you, the island's history on the doorstep, calm swimming water and a bus to everywhere, at a price well below the west coast. It is not a resort strip, and the commercial centre closes in the evening. Stay along Carlisle Bay or in the Garrison rather than in the middle of town, and you get the best of both.

Where is the beach in Bridgetown?

Carlisle Bay, immediately south-west of the town, with Pebbles Beach at the Garrison end. It is a wide crescent of pale sand with calm, clear, shallow water, and it is the easiest sea to swim in on the island. Two shipwrecks lie close enough inshore to snorkel, and green and hawksbill turtles feed in the bay.

How far is the cruise terminal from the hotels?

The Deep Water Harbour is on the north-west side of the town, roughly a mile and a half from the Garrison hotels and about a mile from the centre. It is a short, cheap taxi ride and a long, hot walk, so take the taxi. If you are joining a ship here, a night ashore first is worth a great deal more than it costs.

Is Bridgetown safe to walk around?

By day, yes, and it is busy and ordinary; normal city sense is all that is needed. At night the commercial centre empties out, and we would take a taxi rather than walk back through it. The Garrison, the bay road and Hastings stay livelier and are the pleasanter places to be after dark.

Can I walk from Bridgetown to the Garrison?

Yes, and it is one of the nicest short walks on the island: about twenty minutes along the bay on level ground, with the sea on one side most of the way. Buses run the same route constantly if the heat defeats you.

Is there much noise at night?

Along Bay Street, some: it is a main road, and at weekends the bars near the bay run late. Ask for a room on the sea side or away from the road. The Garrison itself is quiet, and Hastings is somewhere in between.
