A short history

Utah's canyons and plateaus have been home to Ute, Paiute, Shoshone and Navajo peoples for thousands of years, with ancestral Puebloan sites and rock art still visible near Bears Ears and along the Colorado Plateau. Spanish explorers passed through in the 1700s, but it was Mormon pioneers arriving in 1847, led by Brigham Young into the Salt Lake Valley, who founded the permanent settlements that shaped the territory.

Utah's path to statehood was unusually long, delayed largely over the church's practice of polygamy, which it formally renounced in 1890; statehood finally arrived in 1896 as the 45th state. The transcontinental railroad's completion at Promontory Summit in 1869 opened the territory to trade and migration, while irrigation engineering turned the arid Great Basin into productive farmland.

Today Utah balances a strong Latter-day Saint cultural imprint centred on Salt Lake City with a booming outdoor-recreation economy: five national parks, red-rock canyon country, and some of the driest, lightest powder snow in North America draw skiers to resorts just outside the capital. Mining, tech and a young, fast-growing population round out a state whose landscapes remain its biggest draw.

Bodhi

Utah's five national parks, Zion, Bryce, Arches, Canyonlands, and Capitol Reef, are nicknamed the Mighty Five, and their wild formations come from millions of years of the same red Navajo sandstone eroding in wildly different ways. Salt Lake City was founded in 1847 by Mormon pioneers led by Brigham Young, who famously declared 'this is the right place' upon seeing the valley for the first time.

Nolie

Hike Delicate Arch about ninety minutes before sunset, the sandstone turns a color you won't believe and the crowd thins out fast once the light starts fading. For powder, locals chase Alta and winter-sun after a storm on a weekday morning, midweek is when you actually get fresh tracks instead of a lift line.

When to go

Spring and autumn for the parks; winter for the ski resorts.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Prime ski season, with famously light powder at resorts close to Salt Lake City.

Spring (Mar–May)

Wildflowers and comfortable hiking temperatures return to the Mighty Five parks. Spring break brings late-season skiers to Park City.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Hot in the low desert canyons; start hikes early and carry plenty of water.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Crisp air and thinner crowds make this the best all-round season for park touring.
