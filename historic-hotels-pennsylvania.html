Historic Hotels of Pennsylvania

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

22 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 22 in Pennsylvania, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1806

Omni Bedford Springs Resort & Spa

Bedford, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2014

Bedford doctor John Anderson bought the land around seven mineral springs in 1796 and soon housed patients who came for the waters; by 1806 he had built a stone inn. The resort grew to include one of America's early golf courses and, in 1905, an early spring-fed indoor pool. President James Buchanan used it as his summer office, and thirteen presidents have visited, including Thomas Jefferson, Dwight Eisenhower and Ronald Reagan.

Est. 1858

The Sayre Mansion

Bethlehem, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2013

Robert Heysham Sayre, a former canal lockmaster, moved to Bethlehem in 1854 to help push the Lehigh Valley Railroad north and east, later serving as its chief engineer. He spent four years building this Gothic Revival brick house, finished in 1858, the first in the Fountain Hill neighborhood. He ran much of the railroad's work from the surrounding streets, and the line's headquarters stood just down the road from his home.

Est. 1865

Cork Factory Hotel

Lancaster, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2010

This hotel lives inside an old Lancaster cork works. In 1895 Thomas M. Armstrong, the son of Irish immigrants who began by carving bottle stoppers by hand, bought the plant for his growing cork business, and the site later turned out corkboard and linoleum. Kerr Glass ran the buildings from 1969 to 2000. Today the factory's brick walls, wooden staircases and timber ceilings frame the rooms, and the old smokestack still stands over Urban Place.

The Inn at Leola Village, Est. 1867

Leola, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2001

These buildings began as a Lehman family tobacco farm in Lancaster County, growing leaf for Lancaster's cigar makers. The Bard family later owned it and took in overnight guests long before it was a hotel. After the farm was abandoned and faced demolition, it was saved in 2000, and in 2001 owner Deb Shirk opened an inn, restoring the old farmhouses and outbuildings rather than replacing them.

Est. 1869

Penn Wells Hotel

Wellsboro, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2017

Wellsboro's first tavern, the United States House, stood here from 1816 until fire took it. Albert Pitkin Cone built the Penn Wells Hotel on the site in 1869, and it later ran as the Holiday Inn, the Parkhurst House and Coles House before returning to its first name. Renovated in the 1920s on the Route 6 corridor, it hosted Groucho Marx and Joan Crawford. A lobby flag is made of 1,438 Corning glass Christmas bulbs.

Est. 1870

Glasbern

Fogelsville, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2011

Farmer Melchior Siep took up this Lehigh Valley land in 1787, a decade before Fogelsville existed. The Mohr family built the present farmhouse in 1870, and the place stayed a working farm through most of the twentieth century, until its last farming owner, William Kirschner, died in 1972. The old farmhouse was later divided into guest rooms, and the barns now hold vintage photographs and drawings of the farm's long past across 150 acres.

Est. 1881

Lancaster Arts Hotel

Lancaster, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2008

New York merchant Arnold Falk paid $1,400 for this Harrisburg Avenue lot in 1881 and raised a brick tobacco warehouse on a stone base in three months, with a wall splitting it in two. The halves later joined as the Falk and Rosenbaum Tobacco Warehouse. A tobacco inspection ticket from 1890 found during the conversion gave the restaurant its name, John J. Jeffries, and local art now hangs among the original timbers.

Est. 1887

Eagles Mere Inn

Eagles Mere, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2021

Local builder Albert Canover Little, known to everyone as A.C., bought this lot in the autumn of 1887 and put up a modest boarding house he named the Hotel Lewis. Little and his sons built sixteen cottages around Eagles Mere for the syndicate that was turning the mountain lake into a resort, and many of the workers who raised the big lakeside hotels lodged with him. The grand hotels are gone; Little's small inn is still here.

Close by

  • Eagles Mere Lake, steps from the door
  • Ricketts Glen State Park
  • Forksville Covered Bridge and Country Store

Est. 1890

Ledges Hotel

Hawley, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2013

Irish-born glass cutter John S. O'Connor, who had worked for Christian Dorflinger after the Civil War, built this bluestone factory in 1890 at the foot of the Wallenpaupack falls. Water from the creek powered some 200 cutting frames, and his patterns helped define America's brilliant period of cut glass. The building first became an inn in the 1980s, and furniture in its rooms is made from the trusses of the old Bellemonte Silk Mill.

Est. 1916

Omni William Penn

Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2014

Coke and steel man Henry Clay Frick wanted Pittsburgh to have a hotel to rival Europe's. Built during World War I by architects Janssen and Abbott for about $6 million, the William Penn opened in March 1916. Hotelier Eugene Eppley bought it in 1928 and enlarged it to 1,600 rooms, adding the Urban Room, an Art Deco ballroom by Joseph Urban, the designer for the Metropolitan Opera and the Ziegfeld Follies.

Est. 1922

Genetti Hotel & Suites

Williamsport, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2023

When Williamsport was rich from lumber, local backers raised about $800,000 and hired architect William Lee Stoddart to build a ten-story Beaux-Arts hotel. It opened in 1922 as the Lycoming Hotel, with a ballroom, a mosaic dining room and a three-day opening party. Eleanor Roosevelt was among its guests, and after Little League's World Series came to town in 1947, most of the young players stayed here. The Genetti family later restored it.

Historic Hotel Bethlehem

Bethlehem, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2002

Moravian settlers built the First House of Bethlehem, a log cabin, on this spot in 1741. A store replaced it in 1794 and grew into the Golden Eagle Hotel, which welcomed Mark Twain and Ulysses S. Grant. Steel magnate Charles M. Schwab then raised about $1 million and built today's hotel, finished in 1922. Winston Churchill, John F. Kennedy, Amelia Earhart and the 14th Dalai Lama have all stayed since.

The George Washington Hotel

Washington, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2023

Traffic on the National Road had swamped Washington's small inns, so the Chamber of Commerce sold stock to hundreds of residents, raised more than a million dollars and hired architect William Lee Stoddart. His ten-story Beaux-Arts hotel opened in 1922 with a ballroom and its own movie theater. President John F. Kennedy spoke from that theater in 1962 and stayed to watch the Army-Navy game. A large mural by Malcolm Parcell remains.

Est. 1925

The Yorktowne Hotel, Tapestry Collection by Hilton

York, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2026

After World War I, York's business leaders ran a one-week stock drive for a community hotel, and nearly 1,200 residents put in more than $1.17 million. Architect William Lee Stoddart designed a Renaissance Revival tower at East Market and South Duke streets, and The Yorktowne opened in 1925. It became known as the Cornerstone of York, hosting Hank Aaron, Marian Anderson, Ray Charles, Joe DiMaggio and Ella Fitzgerald. Its brass mail chutes survive.

Est. 1926

Historic Americus Hotel

Allentown, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2022

Albert D. Gomery and a few partners cleared the old American Hotel from this Allentown lot and hired Philadelphia architects Ritter and Shay to build something grander. The Americus opened in 1926 with Spanish and Italian touches meant to honor the sailors of the Age of Discovery, and a ring of shops around its lobby. Its crank-run elevator, mail chutes and green crystal chandeliers survive, and the restored hotel reopened in 2021.

Est. 1927

Silver Birches

Hawley, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2016

George and Rachel Singer started a farm here in 1904. In the early 1920s the power company bought most of it and flooded the valley to make Lake Wallenpaupack, leaving the Singers with lakefront land. They turned what remained of the homestead into a small inn, feeding guests three meals a day of home cooking, and it became one of the region's lake resorts. The restored inn keeps its sunroom, stone-fireplace library and long front porch.

Close by

  • Lake Wallenpaupack, right outside the door
  • Lacawac Sanctuary nature preserve
  • Bethel Woods Center for the Arts, New York

The Settlers Inn at Bingham Park

Hawley, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2010

After Lake Wallenpaupack was created in 1925, the Hawley Chamber of Commerce expected tourists and built a 54-room hotel of native bluestone and chestnut to receive them. The Depression and World War II held up its debut for years. In 1980 Jeanne and Grant Genzlinger bought it and restored it in the Arts and Crafts spirit, with Bradbury & Bradbury wallpaper, Fulper tiles and beams of local chestnut and cherry.

Est. 1928

Skytop Lodge

Skytop, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2000

In 1925 five men, John Stubbs, Charles Thompson, Frederic Smith, Earl Mayne and Samuel Packer, bought 2,500 acres of overgrown farmland on a Pocono plateau to build a grand resort. Architect John Muller designed the stone Dutch Colonial lodge, the Olmsted brothers of Boston laid out the grounds, and Robert White, a founder of the PGA, designed the golf course. Skytop Lodge opened in 1928 above Skytop Lake.

Hotel Warner

West Chester, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2016

Warner Bros. picked West Chester for a 1,650-seat movie palace, and the Chicago firm Rapp & Rapp designed it. The Warner Theater opened in 1930 on a corner once taken up by a horse stable, with shops, a restaurant and a tower carrying its marquee. After years of decline, Brian McFadden's group restored it as a hotel; the old theater lobby and its 1930s Art Deco staircase now greet guests.

Est. 1931

The Nittany Lion Inn of Pennsylvania State University

State College, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2000

Penn State built this Georgian-style inn in 1931 on a private loan of about $350,000, because there were few good rooms near campus. A night in one of its 75 rooms cost about $3.50, and some early guests still grumbled. Staff lived in the building. Eleanor Roosevelt stayed during a 1953 visit, and President Eisenhower, Robert Frost, Louis Armstrong and Danny DeVito have also been guests.

Est. 1933

The Hotel Hershey®

Hershey, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 1991

Milton S. Hershey and his wife Kitty dreamed of a grand hotel on Pat's Hill, and before she died in 1915 she urged him to finish it. During the Great Depression he built it as part of his Great Building Campaign, giving work to people in his company town. The hotel opened in 1933 overlooking the town, and its design drew on ideas the Hersheys gathered on their travels, including a Mediterranean postcard.

Est. 1968

The Lodge at Nemacolin

Farmington, Pennsylvania · Historic Hotels member since 2018

Pittsburgh industrialist Willard F. Rockwell began buying land in the Laurel Highlands in the late 1940s and built a game reserve around it. His friend, architect C.W. Balling, designed this four-story Tudor Revival hunting lodge, finished in 1968. Rockwell sold the property in 1979. In 1987 Joseph A. Hardy III, founder of 84 Lumber, bought it so his daughter Maggie would have somewhere peaceful to fish, and the family enlarged the lodge.
