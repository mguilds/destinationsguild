Munich’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Munich — Best Areas & Hotels

Munich is a city of distinct quarters, and the one you choose sets the tone of the whole trip. The historic core is compact and walkable, ringed by leafy residential districts that are only minutes away by U-Bahn or tram — so the real question isn’t which hotel first, it’s which area you want on your doorstep each morning. We’ve grouped Munich’s most enduring, well-loved hotels by neighborhood and character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can simply choose your dates.

Altstadt & Marienplatz

The old town around Marienplatz is the postcard Munich and the easiest base for a first visit: you step out into the pedestrian heart of the city, within a short walk of the Viktualienmarkt, the Residenz, the Hofbräuhaus, and the great churches. Expect the highest prices and the liveliest streets, but also the shortest walk home after dinner.

Hotel Bayerischer Hof

$$$$ LuxuryPromenadeplatzGrande dameRooftop spa

A family-owned Munich institution on Promenadeplatz since the 1840s, the Bayerischer Hof is the city’s most storied grand hotel, with a rooftop spa and pool, a celebrated cluster of bars and restaurants, and its own cinema and jazz club. Its blend of Bavarian tradition and glossy modern design draws statesmen and stars alike. The definitive splurge for travelers who want the hotel itself to be part of the occasion.

Maxvorstadt & the Museums

Munich’s university and museum quarter, Maxvorstadt wraps around the Kunstareal — the Pinakothek galleries, the Glyptothek, and Königsplatz — and hums with students, bookshops, and cafes. It’s cultured, calmer than the Altstadt, and still an easy walk or one U-Bahn stop from the center, making it a smart base for art lovers and repeat visitors.

Rocco Forte service and understated design set it apart, and the museum quarter and Hauptbahnhof are both a short stroll away. Best for travelers who want modern luxury with a green outlook and gallery-hopping on foot.

Schwabing

Once Munich’s bohemian artists’ quarter, Schwabing is now a leafy, well-heeled neighborhood of tree-lined avenues, cafes, and boutiques stretching up from the university toward the English Garden. It trades big-sight proximity for local charm and green space, and with the U-Bahn running its length, the center is only minutes away — a lovely base for a slower, more residential stay.

Ludwigsvorstadt & the Hauptbahnhof

The district around the main station is Munich’s transport hub and its best-value central address: trains to the airport, the Alps, and Neuschwanstein leave from your doorstep, and Marienplatz is a ten-minute walk or one S-Bahn stop away. It’s busier and less picturesque than the old town, but hard to beat for convenience — and it’s the natural base if you’re taking day trips.

Lehel & Isarvorstadt

Flanking the old town along the Isar river, these two quarters offer a more local Munich. Genteel Lehel, with its museums and quiet streets, sits between the Altstadt and the English Garden; across the center, Isarvorstadt takes in the buzzing Glockenbachviertel, the city’s café and nightlife heart. Both keep you walkably close to the sights while feeling like where Münchners actually live.

Budget & Value

Munich is not a cheap city, but you can stay central without overspending by choosing a smart design-budget hotel or a well-run hostel near the transport hubs. The area around the Hauptbahnhof and Sendlinger Tor packs in the best value, keeping you a short walk or single U-Bahn stop from the sights while you save the difference for beer gardens and day trips.
