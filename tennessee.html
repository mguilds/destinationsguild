A short history

Long before statehood, the Cherokee, Chickasaw and other Indigenous nations inhabited the region that became Tennessee, a history brutally interrupted when the Cherokee were forcibly removed along the Trail of Tears in the 1830s. Tennessee became the 16th U.S. state in 1796, named for a Cherokee town, and later earned the nickname the Volunteer State for the many soldiers who enlisted for the War of 1812 and the Mexican-American War.

Tennessee seceded during the Civil War but was also home to significant Union sentiment in its eastern mountains, and it became the first Confederate state readmitted to the Union in 1866. The 20th century brought profound cultural influence, as Nashville grew into Music City USA through the Grand Ole Opry and country music industry, while Memphis nurtured the blues, soul and rock and roll of Sun Studio, Stax Records and a young Elvis Presley.

Today Tennessee's economy spans music and entertainment, healthcare, automotive manufacturing and logistics, anchored by Nashville and Memphis. The Great Smoky Mountains National Park, straddling the border with North Carolina, is the most-visited national park in the country, drawing millions each year to its misty ridgelines, historic Cades Cove and the family-friendly gateway towns of Gatlinburg and Pigeon Forge.

Bodhi

The Grand Ole Opry started as a simple radio barn dance in 1925 and is now the longest-running live radio show in American history, still broadcasting every week. Memphis earned its 'home of the blues' reputation because Beale Street was where Delta musicians came north to record, turning it into the birthplace of both blues and, later, rock and roll at Sun Studio.

When to go

Spring and autumn; foliage in the Smokies is superb in October.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Cool and occasionally snowy in the Smokies; a quieter, budget-friendly time in Nashville and Memphis.

Spring (Mar–May)

Mild weather and blooming wildflowers make this a lovely time to hike the Smoky Mountains.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Hot and humid, with peak crowds at Dollywood, Gatlinburg and the national park.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Spectacular fall foliage, especially in October, makes this the most popular season in the Smokies.
