Where to Stay in Zurich

Where to Stay in Zurich — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Zurich is the door most Americans come through, and it is worth a night or two at either end of a Swiss trip rather than a whole week. The old town along the Limmat is handsome and compact, the lakefront promenade is one of the pleasantest evening walks in Europe, and the airport train reaches the main station in about eleven minutes. The choice of where to sleep comes down to three areas: the Old Town for atmosphere and the shortest walks, Enge for flat, quiet streets between the lake and the centre, and Seefeld for the lake’s eastern shore. Below are the hotels we would send our own family to, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Good to know before you book

A few plain facts that decide where an older traveller should sleep in Zurich.

CurrencySwiss franc (CHF) — not the euro
AirportZurich Airport (ZRH), 9 km north
Airport to centreTrain, 9–13 minutes, no change
Best monthsMay · September – early October
LanguageGerman; English very widely spoken

The centre is walkable — mind the cobbles

Bahnhofstrasse, the river quays and the lakefront are genuinely flat and smooth, and most hotels on this page are within a ten-minute stroll of the main station. The catch is the old town’s climbing lanes: the steps up to the Lindenhof and the alleys behind the Grossmünster are cobbled, uneven and slippery in rain. Plan the flat routes and let the trams do the climbing — they are frequent, spotless and easy to board.

Take the train from the airport

The railway station sits directly beneath the airport terminal, so it is a short indoor walk with a lift and no shuttle bus. Trains run roughly every five minutes and reach Zurich main station in nine to thirteen minutes for a few francs. A taxi costs around CHF 50 to 75 and is slower in traffic — worth it only with heavy luggage or if walking is difficult.

Take the train onward, too — never the plane

Lucerne is forty to fifty minutes away, Bern about an hour, Geneva under three hours and all of them direct, station to station. There is no case for a domestic flight in a country this size, and Bern has no meaningful commercial airport at all. Swiss platforms are step-free, trains have luggage racks by the doors, and no seat reservation is needed on domestic services — you simply get on.

Where the noise is — and one weekend to avoid

Niederdorfstrasse and Oberdorfstrasse on the east bank are the bar strip and run loud from Thursday to Saturday; ask for a courtyard room. Langstrasse is the city’s nightlife and red-light quarter, and cheap rates there are cheap for a reason. One date to plan around: the Street Parade on the second Saturday of August draws close to a million people, hotels sell out and rates double.

Luxury

Zurich’s grand hotels sit on the lake and the Bahnhofstrasse, on flat ground and a short walk from the station.

Baur au Lac

$$$$ Grand luxuryCity centreOwn lakeside parkFamily-owned since 1844Pets welcome

The old-money grand hotel of Zurich, run by the same family since 1844 and still the address a local names first. It stands in its own small park on the lake, a flat and easy walk from Bahnhofstrasse and about fifteen level minutes from the main station. Service is formal but genuinely warm, and the guests skew older and returning. Lifts throughout and not a hill in sight. One current note worth knowing: the Brasserie is closed for works through much of 2026, so check what is open before you book a stay built around dining in. Dogs get their own beds and menu for a nightly fee.

The Dolder Grand

A castle-like hotel on a wooded hillside above the city, and more a resort than a city hotel. The four thousand square metres of spa are the reason to come: a twenty-five-metre indoor pool, an outdoor pool on a sun terrace, whirlpools and saunas. Boutique

Two historic houses in the old town, both a short flat walk from the station — with one caution about stairs.

Widder Hotel

$$$$ Upscale boutiqueOld TownNine linked medieval houses49 roomsSteps from Bahnhofstrasse

Nine medieval townhouses stitched into a single hotel of forty-nine rooms, so no two rooms are alike and the building is full of original beams and stonework. The location is superb — just off Bahnhofstrasse, in the old town, roughly ten level minutes from the main station. The honest caution for older guests is the layout: because it spans nine joined houses, reaching your room can mean long corridors, changes of level and more than one lift. Ask at booking for a room close to a lift and on a single floor. There is no spa or pool.

Thirty-nine rooms in a genuinely fifteenth-century building with six hundred years of continuous innkeeping behind it, restored with a light modern hand. Budget & Value

Zurich is expensive and there is no pretending otherwise. These two are the honest value in a central position.

The reliable value pick, from a design-led budget chain whose lobby and bar look far more expensive than the room rate. Rooms are small and functional at sixteen to twenty square metres, with good beds and showers rather than tubs. It sits in quiet, well-heeled Enge between the lake and the centre; the main station is fifteen to twenty minutes on foot over entirely flat ground, with trams a couple of minutes away for anyone who would rather not walk it. Family-friendly

Quiet & couples (no true adults-only in Zurich)

We will say this plainly rather than imply otherwise: Zurich has no hotel we could verify that actually excludes children. The “adults-only” filters on the big booking sites are marketing labels here, not age policies. What the city does have is genuinely quiet, couples-minded houses, and these are the two we would choose.

Signau House and Garden

$$$ Quiet boutique villaSeefeldOnly nine roomsWalled garden with a pondLifts installed

A classical villa turned nine-room boutique hotel, and described by the city’s own tourist board as very quiet. The defining feature is a walled garden with a small pond, which is a rare thing this close to the centre. Two of the nine rooms are two-room suites with terraces, and lifts are installed — which matters in a converted villa. It is a little over two kilometres from the main station in smart Seefeld near Bellevue, so a tram or a pleasant lakeside walk into the old town. At nine rooms it books out quickly.

Pet-friendly

Two hotels with pet arrangements written down rather than merely tolerated. Confirm the current fee and any weight limit with the hotel before you book.

The modern, low-key luxury option — glass, stone and warm wood rather than gilt, and quieter than the grand old houses. Dogs are welcomed for a nightly fee and are given a bed, fresh water and house-made treats. Spa & pool

For when the point of the afternoon is a swim and a treatment rather than another museum.

The Dolder Grand

$$$$ Destination spaAdlisberg hillside25 m indoor poolOutdoor pool and sun terraceChildren restricted

The best hotel spa in the city by a wide margin, and the reason many people book the Dolder at all. Four thousand square metres take in a twenty-five-metre indoor pool, an outdoor pool on the sun terrace, whirlpools, saunas and a domed meditation room. A useful detail for older couples wanting calm: children under twelve are excluded from the spa entirely, twelve- to fifteen-year-olds are limited to the pool area, and the textile-free saunas are sixteen and over. Lifts throughout, though the hilltop position means a taxi into town each time.

Zurich Hotels — common questions

Which neighbourhood should I stay in in Zurich?

For a short trip, the west bank of the Old Town or Enge. Both are flat, both are a ten- to twenty-minute level walk from the main station, and both are quiet at night. The Old Town's east bank, around Niederdorf, is central and full of life but noisy from Thursday to Saturday, so ask for a courtyard room. Seefeld on the lake's eastern shore is calm and smart. Avoid Langstrasse, which is the nightlife and red-light quarter, and skip Oerlikon, which is a business district with no atmosphere and a real commute.

Is Zurich a walkable city for older travellers?

Largely yes, with one caution. Bahnhofstrasse, the river quays and the whole lakefront are flat and smoothly paved, and the centre is compact enough that most walks are under fifteen minutes. The difficulty is the Old Town's climbing lanes: the steps up to the Lindenhof and the alleys behind the Grossmunster are cobbled, uneven and slippery when wet. Wear shoes with real grip, plan the flat routes along the river, and use the trams for the hills. They are frequent, clean and easy to board.

How do I get from Zurich airport to the city?

Take the train. The railway station sits directly beneath the airport terminal, so it is a short indoor walk with a lift and no shuttle bus involved. Trains run roughly every five minutes and reach Zurich main station in nine to thirteen minutes for a few francs. A taxi takes ten to twenty minutes depending on traffic and costs around 50 to 75 francs, which is worth it only with heavy luggage or if walking is difficult. The tram takes about thirty-five minutes and has no real advantage.

Does Switzerland use the euro?

No. Switzerland uses the Swiss franc, and this catches many visitors out because the country is surrounded by the eurozone. Some shops and hotels in tourist areas will accept euro notes, but your change comes back in francs at an exchange rate the shop sets, and it is reliably a poor one. Pay by card, which is accepted almost everywhere including ticket machines, or withdraw francs from a bank cash machine. When a card terminal offers to charge you in US dollars, decline and pay in francs.

When is the best time to visit Zurich?

May and September are the comfortable months, with mild temperatures, long days and hotel rates well below the summer peak. July and August are the busiest and most expensive, and Swiss hotels are not reliably air-conditioned. January and February are the cheapest and coldest, and the valley can sit under grey fog for weeks. One date to plan around: the Street Parade on the second Saturday of August draws close to a million people, hotels sell out and rates double.

How many days do you need in Zurich?

One or two nights is the honest answer for most visitors. Zurich is a working financial city rather than a postcard, and a day covers the Old Town, the lakefront and either the Kunsthaus or the National Museum comfortably. It makes an excellent bookend at either end of a Swiss trip. If your time is short, go straight from the airport to Lucerne, which is forty to fifty minutes by direct train, and you will lose very little.
