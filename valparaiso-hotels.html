Valparaíso & Viña del Mar

Where to Stay in Valparaíso & Viña del Mar

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Two towns sit side by side on this coast and they are nothing like each other. Valparaíso is a working port climbing forty-two hills, its old quarters painted every colour and listed by UNESCO, reached by staircases and century-old funicular lifts. Viña del Mar, ten minutes along the shore, is flat, modern and built for the beach. Which you should book depends less on the hotels than on your knees and your plans — so we have set out both, with an honest note on each and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com.

The cerros — Alegre and Concepción

This is why people come to Valparaíso. Two adjoining hills above the old port, laid out in the nineteenth century by British and German merchants, now a UNESCO World Heritage area of painted houses, staircases, murals and small restaurants with enormous views. Almost every hotel worth having is here, usually a converted mansion of eight or twelve rooms. Understand before you book that these are hills: the lanes are steep, often stepped, and a taxi cannot always reach the door. The funicular lifts help, when they are running.

Palacio Astoreca

$$$$ LuxuryCerro AlegreRestaurant and poolRestored mansion

The most polished address on the hill: a 1920s mansion on Cerro Alegre restored into a small luxury hotel, with a well-regarded restaurant, an indoor pool and rooms that keep the high ceilings and the original floors. It is a short, level walk to the best of the murals and the viewpoints, which is rare up here. Best for a traveller who wants the hill without a climb at the end of the evening.

Viña del Mar — the beach half

Ten minutes up the coast by train, and a completely different town: wide avenues, high-rise apartments, a casino, public gardens and actual beaches. Chileans come to Viña to swim and to Valparaíso to look at things. If you are travelling with family, want a lift and a flat pavement, or simply want a beach holiday with a day trip to the murals, stay here instead of on the hills. Note that the Humboldt Current runs up this coast: the sea is beautiful and genuinely cold.

Further up the coast

The built-up coast runs north from Viña through Reñaca and Concón to Quintero, and it is where many Chilean families actually take their summer. It needs a car, and in January and February it is busy.

Hippocampus Resort & Spa

$$$ UpscaleIn Concón, not ValparaísoSpaNeeds a car

We list this one with a plain warning, because its name is often filed under Valparaíso: it is in Concón, about twenty kilometres up the coast past Viña del Mar, not in the city. On its own terms it is a comfortable spa resort near the dunes and the seafood restaurants Concón is known for. Book it if you want the coast and a car; do not book it expecting to walk to the murals.

What to know before you book

Valparaíso is vertical

The old city is forty-two hills and a narrow flat strip along the port. Hotel photographs rarely show the staircase you will climb to reach the door. If stairs, hills or uneven cobbles are a problem, stay in Viña del Mar and visit Valparaíso by train — the ride takes about ten minutes and the station is in the flat part of town.

Where the care is needed

Valparaíso has a real reputation for daylight bag-snatching, concentrated around the port, the bus terminal, Plaza Sotomayor and the less-visited hills. The tourist cerros are patrolled and generally fine. Carry a phone out of sight, leave the good watch at the hotel, and do not wander into unlit hills after dark. This is the honest picture rather than a reason not to come.

Two cities, one decision

Staying in Valparaíso and staying in Viña del Mar give you two different holidays. The metro train between them runs frequently and takes about ten minutes, so whichever you choose, the other is an easy afternoon.

Cruise ships and the summer

Valparaíso is a working port and a cruise call, so the old town can fill suddenly on a ship day, and January and February are the Chilean holidays. Book early for those months and expect the coast to be at its busiest.

Don’t see your perfect fit?

The hills hold dozens of small guesthouses that never make a list like this one, and Viña del Mar has a long row of apartment hotels along the seafront. The full list with live prices is one tap away.

Valparaíso and Viña del Mar — questions

Should I stay in Valparaíso or Viña del Mar?

Valparaíso if you have come for the painted hills, the murals, the staircases and the small restaurants, and you do not mind a climb. Viña del Mar if you want a beach, a flat pavement, a lift and a larger modern hotel — and particularly if you are travelling with children or with anyone who finds steep ground difficult. The two are ten minutes apart by train, so neither choice shuts the other out.

Can I visit as a day trip from Santiago?

Yes, and a great many people do. Buses run from Santiago to both cities through the day and take about ninety minutes to two hours. A day gives you one hill and a late lunch. Two nights is much better: the hills are at their best early in the morning and in the hour before sunset, which a day trip misses entirely.

Are the funiculars running?

Some are and some are not, and which is which changes. Valparaíso’s ascensores are more than a century old, several are heritage-listed, and they go in and out of service for repair. Treat any one of them as a bonus rather than a plan, and never choose a hotel on the assumption that the lift at the bottom of its hill will be working on the day you arrive.

Is the sea warm enough to swim?

Not by most visitors’ standards. The Humboldt Current brings cold water up this coast all year, and even at the height of the Chilean summer in January and February the Pacific here is bracing. Chileans swim; many visitors paddle. The beaches are for walking, sitting and seafood as much as for swimming.
