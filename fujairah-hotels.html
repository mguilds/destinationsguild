Where to Stay in Fujairah — The Other Coast

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Fujairah is the only emirate with no coast on the Arabian Gulf. It sits on the far side of the Hajar mountains facing the Gulf of Oman, and that single fact is the whole reason to come: the water is deeper, cooler and far clearer than on the Dubai side, which makes this the country’s diving and snorkelling coast and its quietest beach stay. Most visitors stay on the northern strip at Al Aqah and Dibba, where the resorts and the reefs are; Fujairah city itself, further south, is a working port with a fort and a very old mosque.

Al Aqah & Dibba — the resort strip

A twenty-kilometre run of coast at the northern end of the emirate, with the mountains dropping almost to the road and the hotels strung along the beaches below. Snoopy Island — a rock formation a short swim offshore that is said to resemble the dog lying down — is the snorkelling site everybody starts with, reachable from the beach. Dive centres operate from most of the hotels. It is about two hours from Dubai and ninety minutes from Sharjah over the mountain road.

The most polished resort on the east coast, set on the Al Aqah beach with the Hajar mountains immediately behind it and a large pool complex between the rooms and the sand. Fujairah city

The emirate’s capital, about forty minutes south of the resort strip: a working container port with a corniche, a restored hilltop fort and, at Al Bidyah just up the coast, the oldest mosque in the country — a small mud-and-stone building with four domes, still in use. There is far less tourism here, which is rather the point.

Where to stay in Fujairah — questions

Why stay in Fujairah rather than Dubai?

For the water and the quiet. Fujairah faces the Gulf of Oman rather than the Arabian Gulf, so the sea is deeper, cooler and much clearer, and the snorkelling and diving are genuinely good rather than merely available. The coast is undeveloped by comparison, the mountains come down almost to the beach, and rates are below the Dubai equivalent. What you give up is everything else: there is very little to do in the evening beyond your hotel, and the shopping, nightlife and sightseeing of the big cities are two hours away.

How do I get there?

By road, over or around the Hajar mountains. It is roughly two hours from Dubai, ninety minutes from Sharjah and about an hour from Ras Al Khaimah, and the mountain roads are good and well worth driving in daylight. There is an airport at Fujairah but it handles almost no passenger traffic, so in practice you fly into Dubai or Sharjah and drive or take a transfer. A hire car is well worth having here.

Is the diving actually good?

By Gulf standards, yes, and it is the best in the Emirates. Visibility is far better than on the Dubai side, the water is cooler, and there are reefs, drop-offs and a few wrecks within a short boat ride, with turtles and reef sharks commonly seen. Snoopy Island at Al Aqah is the easy shore snorkel that everyone does first and is genuinely rewarding with a mask. Visibility varies with the season and with algal blooms, which do occur here, so ask the dive centre what the water has been doing that week.

Is alcohol served in Fujairah?

Yes, in licensed hotels and restaurants, as in most of the country. This catches some visitors out in the other direction: neighbouring Sharjah, which includes the Khor Fakkan exclave on the same coast, is entirely dry, so a drive between the two passes in and out of different rules. Transporting alcohol through Sharjah is an offence, which is worth remembering on the road back to Dubai.

Can I combine Fujairah with Khor Fakkan?

Easily, and it makes a good trip. Khor Fakkan is about half an hour north along the same coast, in a spectacular bay with a landscaped corniche and a waterfall amphitheatre, and it belongs to Sharjah — so it is dry, while the Fujairah resorts a few minutes away are not. Many visitors stay on the Al Aqah strip and drive up for the day. See our Sharjah guide for the hotels there.

When is the best time to come?

November to March, as elsewhere in the country, though the east coast is a little more forgiving in the shoulder months because the sea is cooler and the mountains catch some breeze. Summer is severe here too. The water is at its clearest in the cooler months, which is the other reason divers come then. Our best time to visit page covers the year in full.
