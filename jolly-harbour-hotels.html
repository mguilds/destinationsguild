Jolly Harbour

Where to Stay at Jolly Harbour

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Jolly Harbour is the middle of Antigua’s west coast and the most self-sufficient place to stay on the island — a purpose-built marina village of villas and apartments set around the water, with a supermarket, a chemist, restaurants, a golf course and a long beach beside it. It is not elegant and it does not pretend to be; what it is, is practical, good value and extremely easy, particularly if you want a kitchen and a car rather than a resort. Either side of it, within fifteen or twenty minutes, sit some of the quietest and most expensive bays in the Caribbean, which makes this stretch of coast an unusually wide choice.

The areas, and which one suits you

Several distinct places go under this name, and they are not interchangeable.

The marina village

Villas and apartments round the water in a grid of small canals, with moorings, a supermarket, a pharmacy, a few restaurants and bars and a sports club. Flat as a table, walkable throughout, and far cheaper than a resort for a family or a long stay.

Jolly Beach

The long pale beach on the seaward side of the village — calm, shallow water, a mile or so of sand and plenty of room on it. Public, as all Antiguan beaches are, with bars at one end and almost nobody at the other.

Darkwood and Ffryes, to the south

Two of the best beaches on the west coast, five and ten minutes down the road: Darkwood with a well-known beach bar, Ffryes broad and open with a view south. Little accommodation at either, so people stay at Jolly Harbour and drive.

The quiet bays north and south

Within twenty minutes in each direction are bays with one resort in them and nothing else: Hermitage Bay on its own water to the north, and the south coast at Old Road beyond Ffryes. These are the expensive end of Antigua and quite separate in character from the village.

Valley Church and the inland villages

Behind the coast are ordinary Antiguan villages, cheap places to eat and a scattering of apartments with no sea view and much lower prices. Fine if you have a car and want to spend your money on something other than the room.

Getting about

St John’s is about twenty-five minutes north, the airport about thirty-five, and English Harbour about thirty by the inland road. Minibuses run up the coast road to the capital. This is the part of the island where a hire car earns its keep.

What your money is worth in Antigua
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Antigua and Barbuda uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged to the US dollar at EC$2.70 — a rate that has held for many years, so a price of EC$27 is about ten US dollars and no app is needed. US dollars are taken almost everywhere, but change often comes back in Eastern Caribbean dollars and paying in local money is usually very slightly better. Cards are fine in hotels, supermarkets and the larger restaurants; keep cash for minibuses, beach bars and the market. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills, so the final total sits above the headline rate — ask what is included when you book, and at an all-inclusive ask exactly what the word covers here, because it varies a great deal from one resort to the next.

Hermitage Bay

Its own bay, north of Jolly Harbour32 villasAll-inclusive or B&B

Hermitage Bay is a short drive north of Jolly Harbour and has its own water entirely — its site says it “sits alone on Antigua’s west coast, its namesake bay shared with no one else” — with a published address at Jennings New Extension, in the parish of St Mary. It states thirty-two villas scattered between the hillside and the shore, in six types, up to a three-bedroom Sanctuary Villa, and names three places to eat: The Restaurant, La Petite Maison and The Tree Bar. You can take the all-inclusive plan or stay on bed and breakfast, which is unusual at this end of the market and worth asking about. Villas on the hillside mean a climb or a buggy; ask which you are being given.

Curtain Bluff

Old Road, the south coast72 rooms and suitesMeals and most activities included

Curtain Bluff is not at Jolly Harbour — it is round on the south side of the island at Old Road, about twenty-five minutes away — but it belongs on any list of this coast. Its own site describes it as an award-winning Relais & Châteaux hotel tucked away on a rocky promontory on Antigua’s south side, with a published postal address of P.O. Box 288, Old Road, and seventy-two suites and guest rooms in three groups: beach front rooms, rooms on the bluff and pool suites. It names three restaurants — The Sea Grape, The Tamarind and The Wine Cellar — and states that all meals and nearly all activities are included in the room rate, without using the phrase all-inclusive. It is not adults-only: the site promotes family activities and a children’s club.

Travelling in your later years

An honest assessment of this corner of the island.

The marina village at Jolly Harbour is one of the flattest and most convenient places to stay in the eastern Caribbean. There is no hill, the paths are level, the beach is a short walk with a gentle sandy entry, and there is a supermarket and a pharmacy within the village rather than a single hotel shop — which for a stay of two or three weeks makes a real difference. Many of the apartments are ground floor or have a lift; ask, because not all do.

The self-catering option is worth considering on its own merits. Being able to make your own breakfast, keep your own hours and buy fruit from a shop rather than a menu suits a good many older travellers better than a resort dining room, and it costs a great deal less over a fortnight.

The caution is the same one as everywhere on this coast: you will want a car, or you will be paying for taxis. St John’s is twenty-five minutes and the hospital with it, the airport thirty-five. And if you are looking at the hillside villas at the quieter bays nearby, ask plainly how many steps lie between the room, the restaurant and the sand — those properties are built up a slope even though most of Antigua is not.

Questions about staying in Jolly Harbour

Is Jolly Harbour a good base?

It is the most practical one on the island. You get a long calm beach, a supermarket and a pharmacy on site, restaurants within walking distance, a marina to look at, flat ground throughout and prices well below the resort coasts — and you are twenty-five minutes from the capital and half an hour from the dockyard. What you do not get is charm: it is a modern development, not a village that grew.

Villa, apartment or hotel?

Most of what is available here is self-catering, and for many people that is the attraction — a kitchen, your own hours and a much lower bill over a fortnight. If you want a front desk, daily housekeeping and meals included, the resorts on the bays either side of Jolly Harbour are the alternative, at several times the price.

How is the beach?

Good, and long. Jolly Beach runs about a mile, with calm shallow water, bars at one end and space at the other. The finest beaches on this coast are just south at Darkwood and Ffryes, five and ten minutes by car, and both are worth the short drive.

Do I need a car?

Yes, realistically. You can live inside the village without one, but the point of this coast is that the best beaches, the capital and the south of the island are all a short drive away, and taxi fares mount up over a week. Remember you will need a temporary local driving licence, which the hire company arranges, and that traffic keeps to the left.

Is there a golf course?

Yes, there is a course at Jolly Harbour, which is one of the reasons the village exists in the form it does. Confirm the current green fees and whether clubs can be hired when you book, as arrangements have changed over the years.

What else is within reach?

A great deal, which is the real argument for staying in the middle of the west coast. English Harbour and Shirley Heights are about thirty minutes by the inland road, St John’s twenty-five minutes north, the east coast beaches about forty-five, and boat trips and day charters leave from the marina here.
