Best Time to Visit Mongolia

Researched and compiled by the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Gorkhi-Terelj National Park. Photo: Wikimedia Commons.

Mongolia has a short, generous summer and a long, serious winter, and almost everything a visitor wants to do happens in the first one. The table below is Ulaanbaatar, which sits at about 1,300 metres in a ring of hills; the countryside is colder, windier and more exposed than the city in every month of the year, so read these figures as the mild end of what you will meet.

The seasons, plainly

June to early September — go now

The grass is green, the camps are open, the rivers are fishable and the days are long. July and August are the two wettest months of the year, but the rain arrives as afternoon storms rather than days of grey. This is also the only stretch when the country is comfortably accessible to an ordinary traveller, which is why it carries the crowds, the Naadam festival and the highest prices. Book early.

May and late September — the real shoulders

Thinner crowds, lower prices, sharper nights and a serious chance of a cold snap. Late September can be the best photography of the year, with the larch turning gold in the north. Check that the camps you want are still open; many close as soon as the season turns.

October to April — winter, and it means it

Averages in the capital stay below freezing from November to March and the January night average sits near -11F. Winter travel here is a specialist trip: the festivals are real and the light is extraordinary, but go with an operator who runs winter regularly, and do not treat it as a cheap version of summer.

Questions about timing

What is the best month to visit Mongolia?

July and August, with June and early September close behind. Those are the months when the grass is green, the countryside camps are open, the tracks are passable and Ulaanbaatar is genuinely warm — an average daily high near 76F in July. They are also the months everyone else comes, and the Naadam festival in July fills the capital, so book rooms and domestic flights early.

How cold does Mongolia actually get?

Colder than almost anywhere people live. Averaged over the last ten years, Ulaanbaatar's January day tops out near 10F and its January night sits near -11F, and the countryside is colder still and far more exposed. It is usually reckoned the coldest national capital on earth. The cold is dry rather than damp, which helps, but it is not the kind of cold you improvise clothing for.

Is Mongolia worth visiting in winter?

Yes, but as a deliberate winter trip rather than a cheaper summer one. The Ice Festival on Khuvsgul and the Golden Eagle festivals in the west are winter and early-spring events, the light is extraordinary and you will have the place to yourself. Go with an operator who runs winter trips regularly, expect some camps to be shut, and accept that a vehicle problem in February is a different kind of problem.

When is the Naadam festival?

July. Naadam is the national festival — wrestling, horse racing and archery — and it is a public holiday with the main events in Ulaanbaatar and smaller versions in towns across the country. The exact dates are set nationally, so check them for your own year before you book, and book the room before you book the flight: the capital fills.

Does it rain much in Mongolia?

Very little, and almost all of it falls in summer. Ulaanbaatar averages roughly 86 mm in July and 90 mm in August — the two wettest months of the year — against about 2 mm in January. The rain comes as short afternoon storms rather than all-day grey, so it rarely costs you a day; what it does cost is traction on a dirt track.
