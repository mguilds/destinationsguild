Mombasa — Fort Jesus, Carved Doors and the North Coast Sand

Where to Stay in Mombasa — Area by Area

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Mombasa is two things at once and the choice of where to sleep turns on which one you came for. There is the island — a working port city with four hundred years of Portuguese, Omani and British history layered into a few streets — and there is the ribbon of beach hotels running north and south of it. Most visitors want the beach and a day in the city, and the north coast gives you both.

The practical business of getting between the two is where plans go wrong, so this page is honest about the ferry, the airport and the drive.

Nyali — the closest beach to the city

Just across the creek from the island, Nyali is the nearest thing to a smart suburb on the coast: beach on one side, restaurants and a golf course inland, and Fort Jesus fifteen or twenty minutes away when the bridge traffic is kind. It is the best compromise for anyone who wants sand in the morning and the Old Town in the afternoon.

Bamburi and Shanzu — the main resort strip

Keep going north and the hotels get larger, the gardens get bigger and the beach gets longer. This is the heart of the north coast package trade, and it is where the widest choice of rooms and the most all-inclusive options are. The sea here sits behind a reef, so the water is calm and the swimming depends on the tide — at low water you may be walking out a long way. Ask about tide times for your dates before you commit to a particular hotel if swimming matters to you.

Mtwapa — further out, better value

North again, past the creek, Mtwapa is a livelier, less polished stretch with a mix of smaller hotels and self-catering places and a reputation for a late night. It is better value than Bamburi and further from the city, which is a fair trade if you are staying a week and have transport arranged.

Mombasa island and the Old Town — visit, think twice about sleeping

The island has the city's history and its cheapest beds. It also appears by name in official safety advice: the Foreign Office names Mombasa's Old Town, along with the Likoni ferry area, among the places where the risk of crime is higher. We would see the Old Town properly, in the morning, with a guide — and sleep on the north coast. If you do stay on the island, choose a hotel with a secure entrance and do not walk the lanes after dark.

Near Moi International Airport

The airport sits on the mainland west of the island, close enough that most north coast hotels are a manageable transfer. There is no real reason to sleep beside it unless you have a dawn flight, and if you do, arrange the car the night before rather than at six in the morning.

Before you book

Government travel insurance is now required

Since 5 October 2026 most visitors staying under twelve months must hold Kenyan government travel health insurance, bought with the electronic travel authorisation or afterwards through the eCitizen insurance portal. A policy from home is not accepted in its place. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements, still current at 21 November 2026.

Dress for a Muslim coast

Official advice is explicit: dress conservatively away from the resorts, particularly in Mombasa. It costs nothing and it changes how you are received.

The ferry, and the alternative

The Likoni ferry links the island to the southern resorts and is named in official advice as a higher-risk spot. Cross in daylight and keep your belongings close. For Diani with luggage, flying into Ukunda is the calmer option.

Malaria is a real coastal risk

Unlike the highlands, the coast carries a genuine malaria risk all year. Get travel health advice at least eight weeks before you fly and take what you are prescribed for the whole stay, not just the nights you are inland.

The reef and the tide

Most of the north coast sits behind a reef. That makes the water calm and safe for children and it means the sea retreats a long way at low tide. Neither is a fault; it is just worth knowing before you picture the week.

An Ebola case was confirmed in October

Kenya's Ministry of Health confirmed the country's first imported case of Ebola on 6 October 2026 and public health measures remain in place. Check your own government's travel health advice before flying, and whether countries you travel on to have added screening for arrivals from Kenya.

Questions readers actually ask

North coast or Mombasa island for a first visit?


The north coast. Nyali, Bamburi and Shanzu are where the beach hotels are, the sand is in front of you and you are still only a short drive from Fort Jesus and the Old Town when you want a day in the city. The island itself is the working heart of Mombasa and makes more sense to visit than to sleep in.

Should I use the Likoni ferry to reach the south coast?


It is the normal way and thousands of people cross every day, but be deliberate about it. The Foreign Office names the Likoni ferry and the area around it among the places in Mombasa where the risk of crime is higher. Cross in daylight, keep your bag in front of you, and if you are heading to Diani with luggage, consider flying into Ukunda airstrip instead or arranging a transfer that takes the inland road.

How conservatively should I dress in Mombasa?


The coast is mainly Muslim and the Foreign Office asks visitors to dress conservatively away from the resorts and the hotel grounds, particularly in Mombasa itself. Shoulders and knees covered in the Old Town and at religious sites. Beachwear belongs on the beach, and wearing it in town will draw the wrong sort of attention.

When is the coast at its best?


The drier, clearer stretches are roughly January and February, and again from about July to October. The long rains around April and May are the wettest weeks of the year and some coastal hotels go very quiet or close for refurbishment then. It is hot and humid all year and the sea is warm all year.

Is Mombasa worth more than a beach week?


Yes, if you give the island a proper day. Fort Jesus is a genuine piece of Indian Ocean history and the Old Town's streets and carved doors are the real thing rather than a staged version of it. Go with a guide or with somebody who knows the lanes, and go in the morning.
