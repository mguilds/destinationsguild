Fly Fishing in Argentine Patagonia — Four Provinces, Two Seasons, One Long Drive

From the Limay at Bariloche to the sea-run browns of the Río Grande at the bottom of the continent: the waters, the rules that govern them, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the provincial fishing authorities and Argentina’s National Parks, 5 October 2026

Argentine Patagonia is not one fishery. It is four provinces — Río Negro, Chubut, Santa Cruz and Tierra del Fuego, with Neuquén next door — and they do not share a rulebook, a licence or even an opening day. Bariloche and Esquel fish under one Patagonian licence from November. Santa Cruz opens a month earlier and sells its own. Tierra del Fuego sells its own again, and on its most famous river every trout goes back.

Everything below that is a rule, a date or a price was read from the provinces’ and the National Parks’ own published texts on 5 October 2026, and is dated. Where we could not confirm something from the agency, we say so and send you to them.

The season — upside down, and not the same everywhere

The first thing to get right, because it decides which year you can go.

November to May in the north, October in Santa Cruz, April on the Río Grande

Patagonia fishes through the southern summer. For a northern angler that is the whole point: you leave in January, when your own river is frozen, and fish in shirt sleeves. But the provinces open and close on different days, and the dates are reset by resolution every year.

The best months, plainly. In the north — Bariloche, Esquel, Trevelin — the season opens in November and the lodges fill from December to March. On the Río Grande the weeks the lodges sell run from late December to mid-April; that is the run of sea-run fish, and it closes before the rest of the province. Lago Strobel is a spring and autumn water as much as a summer one, because Santa Cruz opens in October.

Confirmed with the agencies, 5 October 2026 — the shared Patagonian rulebook: “La temporada formal de pesca inicia el 1/11/2025 y finaliza el 1/05/2026” (the season runs 1 November 2025 to 1 May 2026). Argentina’s National Parks: the season in the Patagonian national parks is “de noviembre a mayo de cada año”. Santa Cruz’s Ministerio de la Producción: “Desde este 1.º de octubre quedó oficialmente habilitada la nueva temporada de pesca deportiva continental en Santa Cruz” (2026-27). Tierra del Fuego, Resolución 276/2025: province-wide 1 November 2025 to 1 May 2026, with the Río Grande and its tributaries closing on 19 April 2026. The 2026-27 rulebook for Neuquén, Río Negro and Chubut, and Tierra del Fuego’s 2026-27 resolution, had not been published when we checked. Confirm this season’s dates with the province before you book flights.

The Río Limay — Bariloche’s river

The easiest way into Patagonian fishing: a big, clear river that leaves a lake on the edge of a city with an airport.

Río Limay, below Lago Nahuel Huapi

The Limay drains Lago Nahuel Huapi at Bariloche and runs east as the border between Río Negro and Neuquén. It is a wide, clear river, fished from drift boats and on foot, for brown and rainbow trout. Its upper reaches lie within reach of the national park, and the guides in Bariloche also run to the Manso, the Chimehuín and the Collón Curá on longer days.

What to expect. Bariloche is a proper town with hotels at every price, which makes the Limay the water to choose if not everyone in your party fishes. Expect wind — Patagonian wind is not a figure of speech — and bring a rod that can cast into it.

Flies. We have not yet worked through a Limay hatch calendar with the local shops, and we will not invent one. Ask your guide what is on the water the week you go.

Confirmed with the agencies, 5 October 2026 — the Limay falls under the shared Patagonian rulebook (Resolución 417/2025), which states that in the national parks “son de devolución obligatoria todos los ejemplares de especies nativas” (all native species must be released), and that where release is compulsory only “UN (1) anzuelo simple, sin rebaba o con rebaba aplastada” (one single hook, barbless or with the barb flattened) may be used. The rulebook sets different limits stretch by stretch on the Limay. We could not confirm each stretch’s rule for 2026-27 — check with the Nahuel Huapi National Park office or Río Negro’s Dirección de Pesca before you fish it.

Esquel and Trevelin — the Futaleufú and Chubut’s fly-only streams

Our favourite corner of the map, and the one with the clearest rules.

Río Futaleufú, near Trevelin

The Futaleufú runs out of the lakes of Los Alerces country and past Trevelin towards Chile. It is a big, cold, blue river with a longer season than the rest of the province — and Pacific salmon as well as trout. No motors, and no trolling.

Confirmed with Chubut’s Pesca Continental, 5 October 2026 — the province’s 2025-26 rulebook kept the Futaleufú open to 30 June 2026, two months past the general close, for fly and spinning with a single hook; it bans motors and trolling, allows one salmonid under 35 cm to be kept, and allows two Pacific salmon over 50 cm. That extension is set season by season. Check the current rulebook before planning a May or June trip.

Arroyo Pescado, the Carrileufú and the upper Corcovado

Three waters where Chubut has made the decision for you: fly only, every fish back. Arroyo Pescado is a small, slow, weedy stream on the open steppe east of Esquel — famous for large trout, hard to approach, and fished on foot, because floating it is forbidden. The Carrileufú at Cholila is fly only. The Corcovado, further south, is fly only for trout from the lake to the Arroyo Las Carpas, with a separate salmon fishery below.

Confirmed with Chubut’s Pesca Continental, 5 October 2026 — Arroyo Pescado: “Mosca solamente y devolución obligatoria. Prohibida la flotación en toda su extensión” (fly only and compulsory release; floating prohibited along its whole length). Río Carrileufú: “Mosca solamente. Devolución obligatoria.” Río Corcovado: fly only with compulsory release for trout from its source at Lago Wintter to the confluence with the Arroyo Las Carpas; motor navigation prohibited. From the 2025-26 rulebook. The 2026-27 text had not been published when we checked.

Lago Strobel — the lake of very large rainbows

Remote, windswept, and the one Santa Cruz water every fly fisher has heard of.

Lago Strobel (“Jurassic Lake”), Santa Cruz

A big lake on the high steppe of Santa Cruz, a long way from any town, known for rainbow trout of a size that seems improbable until you see the photographs. Most anglers fish it from a lodge, and the lodge arranges the transfer; this is not a water you drive up to on a whim. The fishing is at the inflowing creeks and along the shore, in wind that rarely stops.

When. Santa Cruz opens on 1 October, a month before the northern provinces. The lodge on the lake sells weeks from October to May.

Confirmed with Santa Cruz’s Ministerio de la Producción, 5 October 2026 — the 2026-27 season opened on 1 October 2026 under an updated rulebook, Disposición N.º 241/26, and permits are sold online through the province’s SIPESQ system. We could not find the lake’s own rules — limits, methods, release — on an official page. Check with Santa Cruz’s Dirección de Pesca Continental before you go. The closing date for 2026-27 was not stated either.

The Río Grande, Tierra del Fuego — sea-run brown trout

The reason many people go to Patagonia at all: brown trout that go to sea and come back very large.

Río Grande and its tributaries

A treeless, wind-scoured river crossing the island of Tierra del Fuego to the Atlantic at the town of Río Grande. Brown trout stocked here in the last century took to the sea, and the fish that return each summer are what the lodges sell: large, silver, and caught mostly on two-handed rods in a gale. It is lodge fishing almost without exception — the water is reached through the estancias — and the lodges sell week-long packages.

The rule that matters most: every trout goes back. That is the law, not a lodge preference.

Confirmed with Tierra del Fuego’s Secretaría de Ambiente, 5 October 2026 — Resolución S.A. 276/2025, for the 2025-26 season: on the Río Grande and its tributaries the Candelaria, Ona, Menéndez, Rasmussen, Moneta and Herminita, “Sólo se permite la pesca con devolución obligatoria de todos los salmónidos” (only fishing with compulsory release of all salmonids is allowed); the river closed on 19 April 2026; and “Queda absolutamente prohibida la pesca nocturna, en todo el ámbito provincial” (night fishing is absolutely prohibited throughout the province). Foreign anglers pay an additional Zona Preferencial fee on the Río Grande and the Menéndez. The 2026-27 resolution had not been published when we checked. Your lodge will normally arrange the licence; ask.

Licences and rules, plainly

Three licences, not one — and a rule about your boots that catches visitors every year.

Which licence, where, and what it cost last season

Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut and the national parks share one Patagonian licence. Bariloche, Esquel, Trevelin, San Martín de los Andes — one licence covers them all.

Santa Cruz sells its own, online through its SIPESQ system, and asks for the request at least three days ahead.

Tierra del Fuego sells its own, with an extra fee for foreign anglers on the Río Grande and the Menéndez.

Leave the felt soles at home. The shared rulebook bans felt-soled wading boots in every water, and asks anglers arriving from abroad to bring new or disinfected gear. The reason is didymo, an invasive alga that rides on wet felt from river to river.

Confirmed with the agencies, 5 October 2026 — shared rulebook (Res. 417/2025), 2025-26: non-resident season permit “PESOS DOSCIENTOS OCHENTA MIL ($ 280.000.-)”, day $50,000, ten days $140,000; resident season $40,000; permits are “personal e intransferible”; “Se prohíbe el uso de calzado de vadeo con suela de fieltro en todos los ambientes”; anglers from abroad must bring “equipos nuevos o desinfectados”; lake bag limit “UN (1) ejemplar por día por pescador entre el 1/12/2025 y el 31/3/2026”. Santa Cruz, 2026-27: non-resident day $90,000, week $300,000, season $450,000. Tierra del Fuego, 2025-26: foreign day $60,000, week $150,000, season $200,000, plus the Zona Preferencial fee. All figures are Argentine pesos. Prices are reset every season and the peso moves fast. Buy from the province or the national park office — Chubut sells through its Pesca Continental site — and confirm the current price when you buy.

Where to base yourself

Five towns, and an honest word about the links.

The towns that serve each water

For the Limay: Bariloche — an airport, every kind of hotel, and plenty for non-fishers. See our Bariloche hotel guide.

For the Futaleufú, Arroyo Pescado and the Chubut streams: Esquel, which has the airport, or Trevelin, twenty-odd kilometres closer to the Futaleufú and quieter.

For Lago Strobel: the lodge on the lake; Gobernador Gregores is the nearest town of any size.

For the Río Grande: the lodges themselves, which are full-board. The town of Río Grande has the airport; many anglers add a night or two in Ushuaia at either end. See our Ushuaia hotel guide.

Questions

When is the fishing season in Argentine Patagonia?

It is the southern summer. The shared Patagonian rulebook for Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut and the national parks set 2025-26 at 1 November 2025 to 1 May 2026, with some named waters running longer. Santa Cruz opens on 1 October; its 2026-27 season opened on 1 October 2026. In Tierra del Fuego the Río Grande closed on 19 April 2026, earlier than the rest of the province. The 2026-27 dates for the other provinces had not been published when we checked on 5 October 2026 — confirm with the province before you book.

Is one fishing licence good for all of Patagonia?

No. One Patagonian licence covers Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut and the region’s national parks. Santa Cruz and Tierra del Fuego each sell their own, so Bariloche plus the Río Grande means two licences. For 2025-26 the shared rulebook priced a non-resident season licence at 280,000 pesos and a day at 50,000; Santa Cruz prices its 2026-27 non-resident season licence at 450,000 pesos. Prices are reset every season.

Is the Río Grande catch and release?

Yes, entirely. Tierra del Fuego’s 2025-26 resolution allows only fishing with compulsory release of all salmonids on the Río Grande and its tributaries, the Candelaria, Ona, Menéndez, Rasmussen, Moneta and Herminita. Night fishing is prohibited throughout the province.

Can I wear felt-soled wading boots?

Not under the shared Patagonian rulebook, which bans them in every water and asks anglers from abroad to bring new or disinfected gear, because of the invasive alga didymo. Pack rubber soles.

Which waters are fly fishing only?

Several in Chubut: Arroyo Pescado (fly only, compulsory release, no floating), the Carrileufú (fly only, compulsory release), and the Corcovado for trout from Lago Wintter to the Arroyo Las Carpas. Wherever release is compulsory under the shared rulebook, use one single hook, barbless or with the barb flattened.
