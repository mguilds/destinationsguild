Historic Hotels of Arizona

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

14 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 14 in Arizona, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1789

Tubac Golf Resort and Spa

Tubac, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2008

The resort's 1789 date goes back to the Spanish land grant made to Don Toribio de Otero by the King of Spain. You can stay in the Otero House, his preserved ranch house from the 1800s, with three fireplaces and a back patio.

Close by

  • Tubac Presidio State Historic Park, Arizona's first state park
  • Tumacácori National Historical Park's mission ruins
  • The Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum

Est. 1890

Bright Angel Lodge & Cabins

Grand Canyon, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2012

The busiest corner of the South Rim, and the easiest way to sleep in history there. The oldest piece is the Buckey O'Neill Cabin, built in the 1890s by the Rough Rider and Arizona judge of that name. The Red Horse Cabin was moved here from a ranch in 1902, and the kachina over the lodge fireplace was named "The Bright Angel" by the architect Mary Colter. The Bright Angel Trail begins a few steps away.

Close by

  • The Bright Angel Trailhead, on foot or on horseback
  • Hermit's Rest, Colter's 1914 rest stop
  • Hopi House and the Desert View Watchtower

Est. 1896

Castle Hot Springs

Morristown, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2024

Arizona's first wellness resort opened in 1896 around natural hot springs in the Bradshaw Mountain foothills, north of Phoenix. Theodore Roosevelt, Zane Grey and the painter Maxfield Parrish came to soak, along with Vanderbilts and Rockefellers. After long years of change it was restored and reopened in 2019 as a small, all-inclusive retreat of about thirty rooms on 1,100 acres, with its own farm.

Close by

  • The spring-fed pools themselves
  • Guided hikes and horseback rides in the Sonoran Desert
  • Produce from the resort's own farm at dinner

Est. 1900

White Stallion Ranch

Tucson, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2016

A working guest ranch in the desert outside Tucson, where part of the original early-1900s adobe wall is still exposed in the buffet room. The main dining room keeps hand-peeled vigas and saguaro-rib ceilings in the old Southwestern way.

Close by

  • Mission San Xavier del Bac, founded 1692
  • The Presidio San Agustín del Tucson Museum
  • Tucson Botanical Gardens

Est. 1905

El Tovar Hotel

Grand Canyon, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2012

Built of native stone and Oregon pine, El Tovar opened in 1905 as a Harvey House, during the railroads' push into the American West. It stands about twenty feet from the rim of the Grand Canyon and is a National Historic Landmark. Theodore Roosevelt, Albert Einstein, Zane Grey and President Bill Clinton are among those who have slept here, and the porches still look straight out over the canyon.

Close by

  • Mary Colter's Lookout Studio on the rim
  • The Grand Canyon Railway from Williams
  • Sunset over the canyon's named temples and buttes

Est. 1922

Phantom Ranch

Grand Canyon, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2012

The only lodging below the rim of the Grand Canyon. The Grand Canyon Transportation Company built these rustic cabins and dormitories in the National Park Service style, and opened them in 1922. Native stone and rough-hewn timber were carried to the canyon floor by mule and by hand. You reach it on foot, by mule or by river, and the night sky down there is as dark as it gets.

Close by

  • The Kaibab Suspension Bridge over the Colorado River
  • Plateau Point
  • Stargazing from the canyon floor

Est. 1927

Hassayampa Inn

Prescott, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 1996

Prescott's grand hotel opened in 1927, designed by the firm of Trost & Trost in a mix of Spanish Colonial and Italian Renaissance Revival. It keeps many original features and sits on the National Register of Historic Places. Clark Gable, Greta Garbo, Will Rogers, Tom Mix and Georgia O'Keeffe are among the guests it lists.

Close by

  • The Sharlot Hall Museum, a few blocks away
  • Shows at the Elks Opera House
  • Boating and the granite dells at Watson Lake

Est. 1928

Hotel San Carlos

Phoenix, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 1991

Downtown Phoenix's Italian Renaissance-style landmark opened in 1928 as the first air-conditioned hotel in the city, with the first passenger elevators in an Arizona high-rise hotel. In Hollywood's Golden Age it hosted Jean Harlow, Ingrid Bergman, Spencer Tracy, Marilyn Monroe and Humphrey Bogart.

Close by

  • Heritage Square's historic homes
  • The Arizona Capitol Museum in the old capitol
  • The Arizona Science Center and planetarium

Est. 1929

Hacienda del Sol Guest Ranch Resort

Tucson, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2009

Hacienda del Sol opened in 1929 as a finishing school, then became a guest ranch and a desert hideaway for Golden Age Hollywood; John Wayne and Katharine Hepburn both stayed. The original 1929 gateway arch and the Spanish Colonial Revival buildings still stand against the Santa Catalina Mountains, and the resort runs its own history tours twice a week.

Close by

  • Sabino Canyon
  • Tohono Chul gardens and nature center
  • Old Tucson and the Fox Theatre

Est. 1929

The Wigwam

Litchfield Park, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2010

Goodyear Tire & Rubber built the Organization House here in 1918 as a retreat for its executives, and Paul Litchfield opened it to guests in 1929. Today The Wigwam spreads across 440 acres in the town that also bears his name, a former Goodyear company town with a walkable historic downtown.

Close by

  • A walking tour of historic Litchfield Park
  • The P.W. Litchfield Heritage Center
  • The Desert Botanical Garden in Phoenix

Est. 1930

Arizona Inn

Tucson, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2026

Congresswoman Isabella Greenway opened this pink Spanish Colonial Revival inn in 1930, and it still feels like a private house. Architect Merritt Starkweather gave it corbel arches and original wood beams. Some of the furniture came from a Tucson woodshop Greenway founded in 1929 to employ disabled World War I veterans during the Depression, and a few of those pieces are still in use.

Close by

  • Mission San Xavier del Bac and the Arizona State Museum
  • Sabino Canyon and Saguaro National Park
  • The restored 1930 Fox Tucson Theatre

Est. 1930

La Posada

Winslow, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2026

Mary Colter's masterpiece opened in 1930 on the Santa Fe Railway and Route 66 in Winslow. Colter believed American architects did not need to copy Europe, and she built La Posada from the materials and traditions of the Southwest. Charles Lindbergh, who designed the town's airport next door, was among its guests. Today the owners, Allan Affeldt and the artist Tina Mion, have filled it with art; Mion's studio is upstairs.

Close by

  • The Affeldt Mion Museum in the old train depot
  • Petrified Forest National Park and Meteor Crater
  • Homolovi State Park

Est. 1935

The Hermosa Inn

Paradise Valley, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2011

The cowboy artist Lon Megargee built this adobe home and studio in Paradise Valley, and it opened to guests in 1935. Behind its carved doors are some of the oldest original adobe walls in the Phoenix area, and Megargee's legacy carries on in the inn's restaurant.

Close by

  • Frank Lloyd Wright's Taliesin West
  • The Heard Museum of American Indian art
  • The Musical Instrument Museum

Est. 1948

Royal Palms Resort and Spa

Phoenix, Arizona · Historic Hotels member since 2000

New York financier Delos Cooke built these Mediterranean-style villas at the foot of Camelback Mountain in 1929. They opened as a hotel in 1948 and drew Groucho Marx, Eva Gabor and the Gish sisters. Resort ambassadors still lead daily history walks through the gardens.

Close by

  • Camelback Mountain's Cholla Trail
  • Old Town Scottsdale
  • Biltmore Fashion Park
