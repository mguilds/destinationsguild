Rio’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Rio de Janeiro — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Rio is a city of beaches with a city attached, and where you sleep shapes the whole trip. Copacabana has the greatest choice of rooms, a two-and-a-half-mile promenade and a metro stop every few blocks. Ipanema and Leblon are calmer, leafier and better for dinner. Botafogo and Flamengo trade the sand for lower prices and quick metro rides, and Santa Teresa, up on its hill, offers the most character of all. We have grouped Rio’s best-run hotels by the kind of stay you are after — with a frank word on which streets are pleasant to walk in the evening and where to take a car — each with a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com.

Choosing Your Neighbourhood — and the Honest Word on Safety

Rio rewards visitors who pick the right base and travel like a local: relaxed, but not careless.

Zona Sul is where you want to be

CopacabanaIpanema & LeblonBotafogo & FlamengoMetro line 1

Almost every hotel worth your money sits in Zona Sul, the ribbon of beach neighbourhoods between the mountains and the Atlantic. Copacabana is the workhorse: flat, well lit, busy from dawn joggers to late-night juice bars, with more hotels in every price bracket than the rest of the city combined and a metro station roughly every ten blocks — the practical choice if you would rather walk than take cars. Ipanema and Leblon are the smart residential end, quieter and greener, with Rio’s best restaurants and a gentler evening street scene; you pay perhaps thirty per cent more for the calm. Botafogo and Flamengo are a few minutes inland, well served by the metro, noticeably cheaper and increasingly full of good small restaurants, though you will be riding rather than walking to the sand.

Santa Teresa is the romantic one — cobbled lanes, artists’ studios, colonial houses and long views over the bay — but it is steep, poorly lit at the edges and reached by car rather than on foot. It suits a return visitor who wants atmosphere over convenience. Barra da Tijuca, out west, has broad clean beaches and big resort hotels, but it is a forty-minute drive from the classic sights and built for cars; we would not put a first-time visitor there.

Walking in the evening: what experienced visitors actually do

Walk the main avenuesCar after dark to LapaPhone in pocketLeave the good watch home

Rio has a reputation, and it is worth meeting it calmly. The reality for most visitors is opportunistic theft rather than anything worse, and it is largely avoidable. On the lit main streets of Copacabana, Ipanema, Leblon, Botafogo and Flamengo — Avenida Atlântica, Rua Barata Ribeiro, Rua Visconde de Pirájá, Rua Dias Ferreira — you will find plenty of people out until eleven or later, and a stroll to dinner is entirely normal. Do what residents do: carry your phone in a pocket rather than in your hand, leave the good watch, the wedding rings and the passport in the hotel safe, take one card and modest cash for the evening, and keep a bag on your lap rather than hooked on a chair back.

Where to take a car instead: the historic centre after about six in the evening, when the offices empty and the streets go quiet; Lapa and Santa Teresa at night, where the fun is real but the walk in and out is not; the beach sand itself after dark, which is unlit and best admired from the promenade; and any street that suddenly feels empty. Ride apps are cheap, plentiful and the local default — order from inside the restaurant and step out when the car arrives, rather than standing on the kerb with the phone up. Ignore anyone who approaches you at the airport offering a taxi, and use the official ranks or an app instead. Finally, favela tours are best booked through an established operator, and never wandered into independently.

Luxury

Rio’s grand hotels face the water on Avenida Atlântica and Avenida Vieira Souto, trading on ocean views, celebrated restaurants and pools you will not want to leave. Book here when the view and the service are the point of the trip.

Copacabana Palace, A Belmond Hotel

$$$$ LuxuryCopacabana1923 landmarkFamous pool

The white Art Deco grande dame that has anchored Copacabana beach since 1923, and still the address in Rio: a long marble lobby, sea-facing balconies across Avenida Atlântica, the most photographed swimming pool in Brazil and a level of service that suits guests who like being looked after properly. Rooms in the original building have the higher ceilings and the romance; the newer wing is quieter. It is expensive, and it is also the one hotel here that is a genuine experience in itself.

Boutique

For character rather than scale, Rio’s smaller design hotels put you in a converted mansion on the Santa Teresa hillside or in a smart tower in up-and-coming Botafogo, usually with a rooftop worth climbing to.

$$$$ Boutique luxurySanta TeresaHillside gardenBay views

A converted coffee-plantation mansion on the Santa Teresa hill, and the most charming address in Rio: reclaimed wood and Brazilian craft in the rooms, a tropical garden, a small pool on a terrace with the city and the bay spread out below, and a spa that uses Amazonian ingredients. It is a taxi ride from the beaches and the airport, which is the trade-off, and utterly peaceful in return. Budget & Value

Copacabana is unusually kind to a modest budget: a block or two back from the beach, clean modern rooms cost a third of the front row, and everything is still a flat walk away.

ibis Copacabana Posto 5

$ BudgetCopacabanaTwo blocks from the sandReliable brand

A dependable, spotless Accor budget hotel in the quieter Posto 5 end of Copacabana, a couple of short blocks from the beach and close to the Cantagalo metro. Rooms are compact and simply furnished, the beds are good, breakfast is included on most rates and the front desk is used to helping visitors with taxis and directions. No frills and no surprises — exactly what you want when the money is better spent on dinner and Sugarloaf tickets.

Family-friendly

With grandchildren in tow you want space, a pool that is genuinely usable, and a beach you can reach without crossing six lanes. These two do all three.

$$$$ ResortLeblon / VidigalPrivate beachThree pools

The only large hotel in Rio with its own stretch of sand directly below the terrace — no road to cross — set on the headland just past Leblon, with three outdoor pools, tennis courts, big family rooms and a lift down to the beach. Adults-only

A word of honesty here. Rio has almost no adults-only hotels of the kind you find in the Caribbean or Mexico; the properties that carry that label locally are by-the-hour motels, and are not what a visiting couple wants. The grown-up Rio experience comes instead from the calm end of the luxury and boutique hotels — a rooftop pool in Ipanema, a garden terrace in Santa Teresa, a quiet high floor above Copacabana. Ask for a high, sea-facing room away from the lifts, and you will have all the peace you came for.

Beachfront — Copacabana, Ipanema & Leblon

One thing to understand about Rio: except at the Sheraton, a public avenue runs between every hotel and the sand. Beachfront here means a front-row room with the ocean filling the window and a signalled crossing to the beach — which is exactly how residents use it too.

Emiliano Rio

$$$$ LuxuryCopacabanaRooftop pool & spaOcean-front rooms

A slim, quietly glamorous Brazilian-owned hotel on Avenida Atlântica, with only a handful of rooms per floor, warm wood-and-stone interiors, one of the best breakfasts in the city and a small rooftop pool and spa that look straight down the beach to Sugarloaf. Service is unusually personal for Rio, and the ocean-front rooms have deep balconies you will actually use. The pick when you want the beach view without the bustle of a big hotel.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when choosing where to stay in Rio de Janeiro.

Which neighbourhood is the best place to stay in Rio de Janeiro?

For most first-time visitors the answer is Zona Sul, the beach strip south of the centre. Copacabana has the widest choice of hotels at every price, a flat beachfront promenade and a metro station roughly every ten blocks, which makes it the easiest base if you like walking. Ipanema and Leblon are quieter, greener and more residential, with better restaurants and calmer streets in the evening, at higher prices. Botafogo and Flamengo sit slightly inland with good metro links and lower rates, and Santa Teresa on its hill is the choice for atmosphere rather than convenience.

Is it safe to walk around Rio de Janeiro in the evening?

In the main tourist neighbourhoods of Zona Sul, yes, with ordinary big-city sense. The lit main avenues of Copacabana, Ipanema, Leblon, Botafogo and Flamengo carry plenty of people until late and are comfortable to stroll. Keep your phone in your pocket rather than in your hand, leave good jewellery and your passport in the hotel safe, and carry only the cash and one card you need for the evening. Take a taxi or a ride app rather than walking after dark to Lapa and Santa Teresa, to and from the historic centre, which empties out after office hours, and along the unlit stretch of beach itself at night.

Which Rio de Janeiro hotels are actually on the beach?

Rio has a road between the hotels and the sand almost everywhere, so beachfront here means a front-row room across Avenida Atlantica in Copacabana or Avenida Vieira Souto in Ipanema, with the beach a signalled crossing away. Copacabana Palace, Emiliano Rio, Fairmont Rio de Janeiro Copacabana and Hilton Copacabana all face the sand on Avenida Atlantica; Hotel Fasano Rio de Janeiro and Sofitel Rio de Janeiro Ipanema face Ipanema beach. When you book, ask specifically for an ocean-front room, as city-view rooms in the same hotels look inland.

When should I book a Rio de Janeiro hotel, and when are prices highest?

Carnival and New Year are the two peaks, and they behave differently from the rest of the year: prices multiply, minimum stays of four or five nights are common, and the best rooms go six to twelve months ahead. Carnival 2027 runs from Friday 5 February to Tuesday 9 February, with the Sambadrome parades on the Sunday and Monday. December through March is high summer and busy throughout. The best value with fine weather comes in the shoulder months of April, May, September and October, when you can often book six to eight weeks out.

Do Rio de Janeiro hotels accept pets?

A growing number do. Fairmont Rio de Janeiro Copacabana and Sheraton Grand Rio Hotel and Resort are the best known of the pet-welcoming addresses in Zona Sul, and several smaller hotels accept small dogs on request. Policies vary by property and change often, so confirm the weight limit, the nightly fee, whether pets may be left alone in the room and which room categories are eligible directly with the hotel before you pay.
