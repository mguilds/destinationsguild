Mountains that rise straight out of the valley floor with no foothills in the way, lakes at their feet and moose in the willows. Grand Teton is compact, easy to drive and one of the most rewarding parks in the West.

Before you go

Grand Teton is smaller and simpler than Yellowstone next door, and that is a good thing.

The park runs about 40 miles from north to south along the east face of the Teton Range. Two roads carry most visitors: the Teton Park Road along the foot of the mountains past Jenny Lake and Signal Mountain, and the highway (US 89/191) on the open valley floor to the east, with its long views of the whole range. Drive one up and the other back and you have seen the heart of the park in a day.

Most people pair Grand Teton with Yellowstone, which begins just to the north, across the narrow John D. Rockefeller, Jr. Memorial Parkway. The two parks charge separate entrance fees, so budget for both or bring an annual pass. The park does not accept cash at its entrance stations or visitor centers, so carry a card.

The best of Grand Teton is close to the road. Oxbow Bend, Schwabacher Landing, the Snake River Overlook and the shore of Jenny Lake are all short walks from a parking area. Wildlife is part of daily life here: moose browse the willows near Moose and Oxbow Bend, bison graze the sagebrush flats, and black and grizzly bears are seen along the roads in summer. Keep your distance and carry bear spray if you hike.

Plan around the season. The Teton Park Road closes to cars from November 1 to April 30 between the Taggart Lake trailhead and Signal Mountain Lodge, and the park's campgrounds and lodges run from roughly May to early October. Several construction projects will also shape a 2027 visit; we list them below.

Grand Teton entrance fees, hours & reservations

The essentials at a glance. Fees and reservation rules change every year, so confirm on the park's official website before you travel.

Entrance fee$35 per private vehicle, good for 7 days; $30 motorcycle; $20 per person on foot or bicycle (16 and older). Grand Teton annual pass $70. No cash accepted.
Open24 hours a day, all year. The Teton Park Road is closed to cars November 1 to April 30 between Taggart Lake and Signal Mountain Lodge.
Vehicle reservationsNone. No timed entry or vehicle reservation is needed.
What does need bookingAll park campgrounds (Recreation.gov, up to six months ahead) and the lodges. Boats need a park permit.
YellowstoneA separate entrance fee is charged at Yellowstone.
Best timeMid-June to mid-September for everything open; late September for fall color and fewer people.
Nearest airportJackson Hole Airport, which sits inside the park a few miles north of Jackson. Idaho Falls is about two hours west.
Size / elevationAbout 310,000 acres. The valley floor sits around 6,400 to 6,800 feet; the Grand Teton rises to 13,775 feet.

Winter (November to April): the Teton Park Road between Taggart Lake and Signal Mountain Lodge closes to cars on November 1 and is groomed for skiing, snowshoeing and walking from mid-December to mid-March. The Moose-Wilson Road closes November 1 until about mid-May. The highways through the park (US 89/191 and US 26/287) stay plowed from Jackson north to Flagg Ranch. Campgrounds and most visitor facilities are closed.

Construction in 2027: work is paused for winter until May 1, 2027. When it resumes, the park says the Taggart Lake trailhead and trails close to all use from May 1, 2027 (Taggart Lake will be reachable only from the Valley Trail to the north); the Moose-Wilson Road closes between the Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve and Moose, and the Death Canyon road and trailhead close, both from May 1 to June 19; and building a new roundabout at the Moose entrance on the Teton Park Road brings delays of about 20 minutes from May 1 to November 15, on top of entrance-station waits. Access to some historic buildings on Mormon Row is limited during work that runs into 2027, and the Cottonwood Creek picnic area is closed.

Visitors from outside the U.S.: since January 1, 2026, Grand Teton is one of 11 national parks that charge non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older an extra $100 per person on top of the entrance fee. The America the Beautiful pass costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for nonresidents, and a pass covers that extra fee.

Sources: the National Park Service website (nps.gov/grte: fees, park roads, operating hours and seasons, road construction, park construction, camping, lodging, fishing and accessibility pages), checked November 2026.

Best things to do in Grand Teton National Park

Six ways to spend your days in Grand Teton.

Lakes

Jenny Lake

The park's most popular spot, with a paved shoreline path near the visitor center. A seasonal shuttle boat crosses the lake to the trail for Hidden Falls and Inspiration Point, which saves about two miles each way.

Views

Oxbow Bend

A slow bend of the Snake River with Mount Moran reflected in it, just east of Jackson Lake Junction. Early morning is best for still water, moose and otters.

History

Mormon Row

Old homestead barns on Antelope Flats with the whole range behind them, one of the most photographed scenes in the West. Expect some construction and limited building access into 2027.

Drive

Teton Park Road & Signal Mountain

The scenic road along the foot of the range, with turnouts at the Mountain View and Cathedral Group overlooks and a side road to the top of Signal Mountain for a view over the whole valley.

Lake

Jackson Lake & Colter Bay

The park's largest lake, with marinas at Colter Bay, Signal Mountain and Leeks, lake cruises and boat rentals in summer, and the easy, paved trail on the Colter Bay headwall.

Quiet

Menors Ferry & the Chapel of the Transfiguration

A short, level walk at Moose past a homesteader's cabin and ferry crossing on the Snake River, and a small log chapel whose window frames the Grand Teton. The chapel is open all year.

From the Guild

Drive the highway north in the morning and the Teton Park Road back south in the late afternoon. The sun lights the face of the range from the east in the morning, and the evening light at Schwabacher Landing and the Snake River Overlook is worth waiting for. Bring binoculars; the moose are often closer than you think.

When to visit Grand Teton

Summer (mid-June to early September) is when all the lodges, campgrounds and boat services are open. Days are warm and dry, nights are cool, and afternoon thunderstorms are common over the peaks. It is also the busiest season, and parking at Jenny Lake and the popular trailheads fills by mid-morning.

May and late September into early October are quieter. In May the Teton Park Road reopens (May 1 in normal years) and lodges begin opening through the month, though snow lingers high up. September brings golden aspens, bugling elk and cool, clear days, and is our pick for older travelers. In 2026 the Craig Thomas Discovery and Visitor Center at Moose ran April 10 to October 31, Colter Bay Visitor Center May 8 to September 30 and Jenny Lake Visitor Center May 15 to October 12.

Winter is cold, snowy and very quiet. The highways stay open, wildlife is easy to see on the valley floor, and the closed Teton Park Road becomes a groomed track for skiers and snowshoers. Almost all services inside the park close, so base yourself in Jackson or Teton Village.

How to get to Grand Teton National Park

By car

From the south, US 89/191 runs north out of the town of Jackson into the park. The Moose entrance on the Teton Park Road is about 12 miles north of town; the Granite Canyon entrance on the Moose-Wilson Road is just north of Teton Village; and the Moran entrance is on the east side, where US 26/287 comes over Togwotee Pass from Dubois. From the north, the highway continues straight through from Yellowstone's South Entrance.

RV and trailer note: the narrow, winding Moose-Wilson Road between Teton Village and Moose is closed to RVs and trailers (the park sets the cutoff at vehicles over about 23 feet). Use the highway instead.

By air / shuttle

Jackson Hole Airport is inside the park, with service from several major hubs in season, and rental cars on site. Idaho Falls, about two hours west, often has lower fares; Bozeman and Salt Lake City are longer drives.

There is no park-wide shuttle. A car is the practical way to see Grand Teton, though commercial tours operate from Jackson and Teton Village, and the paved multi-use pathway is a pleasant way to ride a bicycle between Jackson, Moose and Jenny Lake.

Lodges inside the park

Most of the lodging inside the park is run by the Grand Teton Lodge Company: Jackson Lake Lodge, the full-service hotel with its wall of windows facing Mount Moran (mid-May to early October); Jenny Lake Lodge, 37 upscale cabins near Jenny and String lakes (early June to early October); Colter Bay Village, with log cabins and tent cabins on Jackson Lake (late May to late September); and Headwaters Lodge & Cabins at Flagg Ranch, between the two parks (early June to late September).

Signal Mountain Lodge on the shore of Jackson Lake has lakefront apartments with kitchenettes, log cabins and motel-style rooms, usually from early May to mid-October. Triangle X Ranch is a working dude ranch inside the park, booked by the week. Book directly with each, as early as you can; the park's lodges fill months ahead for July and August.

The park says every lodge except the American Alpine Club's Climbers' Ranch has some accessible units, so ask for one when you reserve.

RV travel & camping at Grand Teton

Two RV parks with full hookups, several campgrounds with electric sites, and every site reserved ahead on Recreation.gov.

All of Grand Teton's campgrounds are reservation-only, booked on Recreation.gov up to six months ahead. Stays are limited to 14 days at most campgrounds (seven at Jenny Lake) and 30 days in the park per year. Campgrounds generally open in May or June and close by early October; check Recreation.gov for each one's dates.

Full hookups: the park has two RV parks with full hookups, Colter Bay RV Park on Jackson Lake (112 sites, most of them pull-throughs) and the Headwaters Campground & RV Park at Flagg Ranch, which also has camper cabins and showers.

Electric sites: Gros Ventre near the south end of the park (more than 300 sites, 45-foot limit, dump station), Colter Bay Campground (about 350 sites, 45-foot limit, dump station) and Signal Mountain (81 sites, 30-foot limit, dump station) each have a number of electric sites. Lizard Creek on the north end of Jackson Lake takes rigs up to 30 feet, and Jenny Lake is tents only.

If you want a long stay with full hookups and could not get a park site, Jackson and the surrounding valley have private RV parks. Remember that RVs and trailers are not allowed on the Moose-Wilson Road.

Fishing in Grand Teton

Unlike Yellowstone, Grand Teton uses state rules: you need a Wyoming fishing licence, which you can buy online or in the park at Flagg Ranch, Colter Bay Marina and Signal Mountain Lodge. Lakes in the park are open all year. The limit in lakes is six trout a day, no more than three of them cutthroat; in streams it is three trout. Only one cutthroat over 12 inches may be kept in either.

Some waters close seasonally to protect wildlife, including a stretch of the Snake River from Moran to Menors Ferry and the Buffalo Fork from December 15 to March 31, and Snake River cutthroat caught from November 1 to March 31 must be released. Read the park's fishing page for the full list before you go.

Every boat, canoe, kayak and paddle board needs a Grand Teton boat permit, and boats must stop for an aquatic invasive species inspection; motorized boats also need a Wyoming invasive species decal. See our Wyoming lake fishing guide and our fly fishing library for more on the region.

Accessible travel at Grand Teton

What is available for visitors with limited mobility, and the passes that help.

Much of Grand Teton can be enjoyed from the car, and the park's overlooks and wayside exhibits have curb cuts and designated parking. The park lists several paved, gently graded paths with good views: the Colter Bay headwall, the Jackson Lake Dam overlook, the Menors Ferry Historic District and the Jenny Lake shore trail.

The Craig Thomas Discovery and Visitor Center at Moose and the Jenny Lake and Colter Bay visitor centers have accessible parking, entrances and restrooms, with TDD service. Accessible restaurants are at Flagg Ranch, Leeks Marina, Colter Bay, Signal Mountain Lodge, Jackson Lake Lodge and Jenny Lake Lodge.

For campers, Gros Ventre, Jenny Lake and Colter Bay are on fairly level ground, while Signal Mountain and Lizard Creek are hilly. Headwaters at Flagg Ranch has accessible campsites and RV sites. The park publishes an accessibility brochure, including a large-print version.

Park passes that save you money. U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability can get the free lifetime Access Pass. Visitors 62 and over can buy the Senior Pass ($80 for life, or $20 a year). Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass. Each covers entrance fees at every national park.

Traveling on dialysis? The nearest dialysis clinics we found are in Idaho Falls, Idaho, about 90 miles west of Jackson over Teton Pass. Book your treatment at a clinic there several weeks before you go; the large dialysis companies run travel desks that will arrange it with your home unit.

Grand Teton National Park FAQ

How much does it cost to enter Grand Teton?

$35 per private vehicle for seven days, $30 per motorcycle, or $20 per person on foot or bicycle (16 and older). A Grand Teton annual pass is $70. The park does not accept cash.

Does my Grand Teton pass get me into Yellowstone?

No. Yellowstone charges its own entrance fee. An America the Beautiful pass covers both parks.

Do I need a reservation to enter Grand Teton?

No. There is no timed entry or vehicle reservation. Campgrounds and lodges do need reservations.

Is the Teton Park Road open in winter?

No. It is closed to cars from November 1 to April 30 between Taggart Lake and Signal Mountain Lodge, and is groomed for skiing and snowshoeing in mid-winter. The highways through the park stay open.

Where can I get RV hookups in Grand Teton?

Colter Bay RV Park and the Headwaters Campground and RV Park at Flagg Ranch have full hookups. Gros Ventre, Colter Bay and Signal Mountain campgrounds have some electric sites. All are reserved on Recreation.gov.

Can I drive an RV on the Moose-Wilson Road?

No. RVs and trailers are not allowed on the Moose-Wilson Road. Use the highway between Jackson and Moose instead.

Do I need a fishing licence in Grand Teton?

Yes. Grand Teton requires a Wyoming state fishing licence, sold online and at Flagg Ranch, Colter Bay Marina and Signal Mountain Lodge.

Will construction affect a 2027 visit?

Yes. The Taggart Lake trailhead and trails close from May 1, 2027, the Moose-Wilson Road and Death Canyon close May 1 to June 19, and roundabout work at Moose causes delays from May 1 to November 15.
