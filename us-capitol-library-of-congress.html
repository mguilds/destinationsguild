The Capitol and Capitol Hill

Where Congress has met since 1800, and the building whose dome stands for the country.

The United States Capitol is home to both houses of Congress, the Senate and the House of Representatives. The cast-iron dome was finished during the Civil War, and President Lincoln insisted the work go on as a sign that the Union would carry on.

All visitors now come in through the Capitol Visitor Center, built underground below the East Front so it doesn't spoil the view. Its great central space is Emancipation Hall, named in honor of the enslaved laborers who helped build the Capitol. Across First Street stand the Supreme Court and the Library of Congress, the largest library in the world. A tunnel lets you walk from one to the other without going back through security.

Capitol tours & hours

The tours are free and guided. Book ahead, especially in spring and summer.

OpenMonday – Saturday, 8:30 a.m. – 4:30 p.m.
ClosedSundays, Thanksgiving, Christmas Day, New Year's Day and Inauguration Day
CostFree. Guided tours only, with no self-guided tours
ReservationsOnline at visitthecapitol.gov, or ask your senator's or representative's office about a staff-led tour
Walk-insSeated on the next open tour. Arrive by 2:30 p.m.
EntranceCapitol Visitor Center, below the East Front, First Street between Constitution and Independence Avenues

What the tour covers: the Crypt beneath the Rotunda, the great Rotunda under the dome, and National Statuary Hall, the old House chamber. The route can change. Tours in Spanish and Mandarin are also offered. Security: every visitor is screened, so allow extra time and check the prohibited-items list at visitthecapitol.gov before you go.

Sources: U.S. Capitol Visitor Center (visitthecapitol.gov) and Library of Congress (loc.gov), checked November 2026.

Watching Congress in session

The Capitol tour does not take you into the House or Senate chambers. To watch from the public galleries, you need a gallery pass:

Senate gallery: passes come from your senators' offices. Once you are through security, you can also ask for one at the Senate Appointment Desk on the upper level of the Visitor Center.
House gallery: passes come from your representative's office.

The galleries are open when each chamber is in session. Sessions run on Congress's own calendar, so check the House and Senate schedules before you plan your day around it.

From the Guild

Call your representative's office a few weeks before your trip and ask for both a staff-led tour and gallery passes. Constituents often get a more personal visit, and the staff will tell you whether Congress is sitting while you're in town.

The Library of Congress

The Thomas Jefferson Building may be the most beautiful room in Washington. Entry is free, but you need a ticket.

OpenTue, Wed, Fri, Sat 10 a.m. – 5 p.m. · Thu 10 a.m. – 8 p.m.
ClosedSundays, Mondays and some federal holidays
TicketsFree timed entry, required. Reserve at loc.gov up to 30 days ahead
Same-dayTickets released online daily at 9 a.m. Eastern
Last entry30 minutes before closing
AddressThomas Jefferson Building, 10 First Street SE, Washington, D.C. 20540

The tunnel: from the Capitol Visitor Center, take the tunnel from the upper level near the House Appointment Desk straight into the Jefferson Building, Tuesday through Saturday. You still need your Library ticket. Expect longer security lines from March through July.

Getting there

By Metro

Capitol South (Blue, Orange and Silver lines) is the closest station, a few minutes' walk from both the Visitor Center and the Library. Union Station (Red line) is about ten minutes away on foot.

On foot and by bus

The Capitol sits at the east end of the National Mall. Parking on Capitol Hill is very limited, so most visitors use the Metro or a hop-on hop-off bus, which stops nearby.

Accessibility

All Library of Congress buildings are fully accessible. If you use a wheelchair, enter the Jefferson Building at the driveway level below the Grand Staircase on First Street SE. You can ask for accommodations at least five days ahead at ADA@loc.gov or (202) 707-6362. For the Capitol, contact the Visitor Center through visitthecapitol.gov before your trip to arrange wheelchairs or other help on the tour.

U.S. Capitol & Library of Congress FAQ

How do you get a tour of the U.S. Capitol?

Tours of the Capitol are free and guided; there are no self-guided tours. Reserve online at visitthecapitol.gov, or ask your senator's or representative's office about a staff-led tour. Walk-ins are seated on the next available tour, and the Capitol Visitor Center recommends arriving by 2:30 p.m.

What are the Capitol Visitor Center's hours?

The Capitol Visitor Center is open Monday through Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. It is closed on Sundays, Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Day, New Year's Day and Inauguration Day.

Can you watch Congress in session?

Yes, but the Capitol tour does not include the House or Senate galleries. You need a gallery pass from your representative's office for the House, or from your senators' offices for the Senate. Senate passes can also be picked up at the Senate Appointment Desk on the upper level of the Capitol Visitor Center after security.

Do you need tickets for the Library of Congress?

Yes. Entry to the Thomas Jefferson Building is free, but every visitor needs a free timed-entry ticket. Reserve at loc.gov up to 30 days ahead; a batch of same-day tickets is released online at 9 a.m. Eastern each day.

What are the Library of Congress's hours?

The Thomas Jefferson Building is open Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Thursday from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. It is closed on Sundays, Mondays and some federal holidays.

Is there a tunnel from the Capitol to the Library of Congress?

Yes. A tunnel links the Capitol Visitor Center with the Thomas Jefferson Building. Its entrance is on the upper level of the Visitor Center, near the House Appointment Desk. Because the Library is closed on Sundays and Mondays, plan to use it Tuesday through Saturday.

What is the closest Metro station to the U.S. Capitol?

Capitol South (Blue, Orange and Silver lines) is the closest station to the Capitol Visitor Center and the Library of Congress. Union Station (Red line) is about a 10-minute walk away.
