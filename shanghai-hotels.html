Shanghai’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Shanghai — Best Hotels by Style

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Shanghai wears many faces — the grand riverfront hotels of the Bund, the leafy lanes of the Former French Concession, and the glittering towers of Pudong across the water. Rather than sort the city by neighborhood alone, we’ve grouped its most dependable, well-run hotels by the kind of trip you’re planning — from full-dress luxury to honest value, family stays to grown-up retreats. Luxury

Shanghai does grand hotels as well as any city on earth, and the Bund is where the legends line up — marble, river views and service polished to a shine. Book here when you want the hotel to be part of the story, with the historic waterfront and the Pudong skyline right outside the glass.

The Peninsula Shanghai

$$$$ LuxuryThe BundRiver viewsArt Deco grandeur

The last great building raised on the Bund, The Peninsula pairs 1920s Art Deco glamour with some of the largest, most beautifully finished rooms in the city, many looking straight across the Huangpu to Pudong. A superb spa, a rooftop bar and famously attentive service make it a benchmark for Shanghai luxury. Best for a landmark stay where location and craftsmanship both matter.

Boutique

Budget & Value

Shanghai stays — quick questions

A few honest answers to help you settle on a base before you book.

What is the best area to stay in Shanghai?

For a first visit, base yourself around the Bund and People’s Square on the Puxi (west) side — you are walking distance from the riverfront promenade, the old lanes, shopping on Nanjing Road, and a metro ride from everything else. The Former French Concession is the choice for tree-lined charm, cafés and boutiques, while Lujiazui in Pudong suits travelers who want soaring skyline hotels and the Disney and expo districts within easy reach.

Is Shanghai walkable?

Central Shanghai is very walkable in pockets — the Bund, the Former French Concession and the old town reward strolling — but the city is enormous, so you will lean on the metro for longer hops. The subway is clean, cheap, signed in English and easy to use, and taxis and ride-hailing are inexpensive. Sidewalks are wide and generally flat, which helps if you prefer an unhurried pace.

When is the best time to visit Shanghai?

Spring (late March to May) and autumn (September to early November) are the most comfortable, with mild days and lighter humidity. Summers are hot, humid and prone to rain, while winters are damp and chilly rather than freezing. Avoid the national holiday around the first week of October if you can, when domestic crowds and prices peak.
