Where to Stay in Santa Catalina

A fishing village at the end of a long road that turned into a surf town and then into the doorway to Coiba. Small, simple and better for it — but you should know what it is before you book.

The village and the main street

One road down to the water, with the dive shops, the boat landing, a scatter of restaurants and most of the simpler places to stay. Walkable end to end in ten minutes. This is where the boats leave from and where you will eat.

Playa El Estero

Over the hill east of the village, reached by a rough track or a short walk at low tide, with a long beach and the beginner surf. Quieter and prettier, and a genuine trudge back and forth with luggage or after dark.

Travelling in your later years

Be honest with yourself about this one. Santa Catalina is a village: the roads are rough, a good deal of the walking is on sand, loose stone or an unlit track, and some places have no air conditioning, intermittent power or no hot water. It is wonderful, and it is not a soft landing.

The reason most people come — Coiba — is an hour and a half each way in an open boat across open water, boarded from the beach. That is a hard day if the sea is up, and the operators will tell you honestly if it is. There is no hospital here; David and Santiago are hours away by road. If you want the Pacific without the roughness, the resort coast near Playa Blanca is the easier choice.

Questions travellers ask about Santa Catalina

What is Coiba?

A large island off this coast that was a penal colony for most of the twentieth century, which left its forest and reef almost untouched. It is now a national park and a World Heritage site, and the diving and snorkelling around it are the best in Panama. Trips go daily from the village, weather allowing.

Is Santa Catalina only for surfers?

No, though the surf came first and the right-hand point break is well known. Most visitors now come for Coiba. There is beginner surf at El Estero and serious water at the point, and plenty of people here do neither and simply go out on boats.

How do I get there?

It is a long way down a side road: drive or bus to Soná and then on to the village, which is several hours from Panama City and a couple from David. There is no airstrip. Allow a whole day for the journey and do not plan to arrive after dark.

What should I bring?

Cash, first of all — banking in the village is limited and many places do not take cards. Then sun cover for long open-boat days, something for motion sickness if you are prone to it, and a torch. Shops are small and stock what they stock.
