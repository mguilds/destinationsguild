Where to Stay in Washington DC — Downtown, Capitol Hill, Georgetown & Beyond

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Washington is a walking city with a very good subway, and the neighbourhood you sleep in decides how many of the monuments you can reach on foot. Downtown and Penn Quarter put you a short walk from the White House, the National Mall and the Smithsonian. Capitol Hill sits beside the Capitol and Union Station. Georgetown, Foggy Bottom, Dupont Circle and Adams Morgan trade a few more minutes of travel for a neighbourhood feel. This page sorts the areas and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in Washington DC

Six parts of the city, each suited to a different trip.

Downtown, by the White House

The city’s official visitors bureau, Destination DC, calls Downtown the centre of the city because it is close to the main attractions, and notes that the White House at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue sits on its border. Shops, restaurants and museums, and the CityCenterDC shopping streets. Stay here for a first visit and for walking to the Mall.

Penn Quarter and Chinatown

North of Pennsylvania Avenue NW. The bureau describes it as “as hopping at night as during the day”, with the National Portrait Gallery and the American Art Museum in one building, Ford’s Theatre, and theatres that fill up every evening; it draws sports fans too. Lively, central and handy for museums; ask for a quiet room if you sleep lightly. See our Chinatown guide and Ford’s Theatre guide.

Capitol Hill

Home of the U.S. Capitol, the Supreme Court and the Library of Congress, with, in the bureau’s words, 19th-century rowhouses, a market and a lively dining scene. Union Station, where Amtrak trains arrive, is close by. Good for travellers coming by train and for anyone with business on the Hill. See our U.S. Capitol and Library of Congress guide.

Georgetown

The historic neighbourhood where M Street meets Wisconsin Avenue. The bureau points to its shops, restaurants and hotels, its 18th- and 19th-century houses and Georgetown Waterfront Park. Charming and walkable, a little further from the Mall; ideal for a second visit or a couple’s weekend.

Foggy Bottom and the West End

A riverside neighbourhood beside Georgetown. The bureau names the Kennedy Center, the State Department and George Washington University, and calls the West End a hotel- and restaurant-rich area next to Georgetown. A practical middle ground between the monuments and Georgetown.

Dupont Circle and Adams Morgan

North of Downtown. The bureau describes Dupont Circle as a cosmopolitan neighbourhood of bistros, bars, boutiques, museums and embassies around its fountain, and Adams Morgan as a 24-hour neighbourhood known for nightlife, international dining and vintage shops along 18th Street. For repeat visitors and travellers who want to live like locals and take the Metro to the sights.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Washington details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Washington

Destination DC notes that three airports serve the capital, among them Washington Dulles International. Ronald Reagan Washington National and Dulles are both run by the Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority, and both airports’ own websites list the Metrorail among their ground transport options, alongside taxis and ride apps.

By train, the bureau says Amtrak arrives at Union Station, within walking distance of landmarks and with its own Metro station, taxis and rideshare.

Getting around

The bureau’s advice is that you do not need a car: Washington is a walking town, and it says 58 per cent of commuter trips in the city are made on foot, by bike or by public transport. If you do drive, it warns of a complicated street plan of diagonal avenues, circles and one-way streets, rush hours of roughly 5–10 AM and 3–7 PM, and recommends checking your hotel’s parking when you book.

When to go

Spring is cherry-blossom season. The National Park Service says peak bloom is most likely between the last week of March and the first week of April, and that it varies every year with the weather; in 2026 it fell on 26 March. Hotels near the Mall fill early in that window, so book as soon as your dates are fixed.

To tour the U.S. Capitol, the bureau says you need a reservation; visits to the House and Senate galleries are arranged through your own senator or representative.

Washington DC Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in Washington DC for a first visit?

Downtown or Penn Quarter, for walking to the White House, the National Mall and the Smithsonian museums. Capitol Hill if you arrive by train at Union Station or have business at the Capitol. Georgetown, Foggy Bottom, Dupont Circle or Adams Morgan for a neighbourhood feel a little further out.

Do I need a car in Washington DC?

No. The official visitors bureau calls Washington a walking town and says cars are not needed for the best experience. The Metro, walking and ride apps cover the sights. If you drive, check your hotel’s parking when you book.

When do the cherry blossoms bloom in Washington?

The National Park Service says peak bloom is most likely between the last week of March and the first week of April, but it changes every year with the weather. Book early: hotels near the Mall fill quickly in that window.

Which airport should I fly into for Washington DC?

Three airports serve the capital. Ronald Reagan Washington National and Washington Dulles International both list the Metrorail among their ground transport options on their own websites. Compare fares and how far each is from your hotel before you book.

Can I tour the U.S. Capitol?

Yes. The official visitors bureau says tours run from the Capitol Visitor Center and need a reservation, and that visits to the House and Senate galleries are arranged through your own senator or representative.
