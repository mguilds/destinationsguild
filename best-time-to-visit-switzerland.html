Best Time to Visit Switzerland

Best Time to Visit Switzerland

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

The short answer is September, or the first half of June. Both give you full mountain access, comfortable temperatures, thinner crowds and moderate prices, and September in particular is the wisest month for anyone who would rather not queue in the heat. The longer answer matters more, though, because Switzerland closes down twice a year in a way that catches visitors out — and a cable car that is not running will ruin a carefully planned day far more thoroughly than a rain shower. Here is the whole year, plainly.

Why September wins

It is the month with the fewest compromises. The walking weather is excellent, the valleys are turning gold, the crowds have thinned after the European school holidays, the afternoon thunderstorms that plague July and August have eased, and summit visibility is the clearest of the year. Crucially, everything is still open — the maintenance closures do not begin until the middle of October.

Why early June is the close second

Nearly everything has opened for the summer by the first or second week, the alpine meadows are in flower, snow still caps the peaks so the photographs look the way you hoped, and the days are at their longest. The crowds arrive later in the month. The one caution is that a handful of the highest lifts open only in mid-June, so check the specific ones you want.

Summer is not bad — it is crowded and expensive

July and August are warm, everything runs and the high trails are all accessible. What you trade is money and elbow room. Rates peak, Jungfraujoch queues run thirty to sixty minutes by mid-morning, and the buses up to Grindelwald can be standing-room only. Valley towns reach the high eighties, and Swiss hotels are not reliably air-conditioned. Take the first departure of the day and the difference is remarkable.

The wiser trip for crowd-averse travellers

If you are choosing between a fortnight in August and a fortnight in September, take September without hesitating. If your dates are fixed in summer, base yourself somewhere flat and well-connected — Lucerne or Interlaken — start each mountain day on the earliest train, and be back down by early afternoon when the crowds and the storms both arrive.

The two closure windows — the fact most visitors miss

This is the single most important thing on the page. Swiss mountains are not open all year in the way an American national park is.

Roughly mid-April to mid-June

The spring maintenance window. Low-altitude ski areas close, summer services have not started, and resort villages go quiet — hotels, restaurants and shops in places like Wengen, Mürren, Grindelwald and Zermatt may simply be shut for several weeks. In 2026 the Wengen and Grindelwald Männlichen cableways were closed from 7 April to 22 May, and the Grütschalp route up to Mürren was closed for reconstruction from mid-April well into July.

Roughly mid-October to early December

The autumn maintenance window, and the one that surprises people who booked an October trip for the foliage. Grindelwald First closed from 26 October to 27 November 2026; the Männlichen cableways from 26 October into December; the Eiger Express for a fortnight in November; Zermatt’s Sunnegga and Blauherd lifts take a six-to-eight week break from early October. The Glacier Express does not run at all between 11 October and 4 December 2026.

What stays open regardless

The Jungfraujoch tunnel railway runs every single day of the year. The Gornergrat above Zermatt, Matterhorn Glacier Paradise via Trockener Steg, Titlis, Stoos and Rigi run year-round with only short maintenance breaks — the Gornergrat pauses for about a fortnight in early November 2026. Rigi is a particularly rewarding winter mountain because it frequently sits above the fog covering the lakes.

And if you are driving: the high passes

The Furka, Grimsel, Susten, Nufenen and similar alpine road passes are closed by snow from roughly late October until late May or June. There is no fixed national opening date and summer storms can close them at short notice. In 2026 the Gotthard reopened on 8 May, the Furka and Grimsel on 29 May, and the Susten only on 12 June. The rail alternatives — car-carrying trains and the base tunnels — run year-round.

One rule covers all of it: before you book flights for a trip between mid-October and mid-June, check the operating dates of the specific lift or railway your plans depend on, on that operator’s own website. The dates move every year.

Month by month

Weather, crowds, prices and what is actually open.

January

Cold, cheap, and often grey at valley level — but the peaks above are frequently in brilliant sun while Zurich and Bern sit under fog for weeks. Ski season in full swing. Cities are quiet and hotel rates are at their lowest of the year. A fine month for a city break and a poor one for mountain views unless you go up.

February

The most expensive month in the ski resorts, thanks to Swiss and European half-term holidays. Book far ahead or avoid. Lucerne’s carnival takes over the city for six days before Ash Wednesday — spectacular, very loud, and not a restful time to be there. Bright, cold and clear at altitude.

March

The sweet spot of winter: better weather, longer days, and prices easing from about mid-month. Still full ski conditions high up. For a non-skier it is still a hard sell, because most summer viewpoints, hiking trails and scenic lifts remain shut.

April

The classic trap, and the month we would steer you away from. Low-altitude ski areas are closing, summer mountain services have not opened, and resort villages go quiet. It is the cheapest and emptiest time of year for a reason. If you must come in April, stay in the cities.

May

Improving steadily. Spring flowers from mid-month, and many cogwheel railways and cable cars have their opening dates in late May. Snow lingers above 2,000 metres until late in the month. Good value, but you must check every single lift you are counting on before you commit.

June

Early to mid June is arguably the best window of the whole year. Nearly everything is open, the meadows are in flower, snow still caps the peaks, the days are at their longest, and the crowds have not yet arrived. Comfortable temperatures and long light evenings.

July

Peak on every axis: maximum crowds, maximum prices, and Jungfraujoch queues of thirty to sixty minutes by mid-morning. Valleys reach the high eighties Fahrenheit and Swiss hotels are not reliably air-conditioned. Afternoon thunderstorms are frequent in the Bernese Oberland. Go early in the day or not at all.

August

As busy as July, with one extra date to plan around: Zurich’s Street Parade on the second Saturday draws close to a million people, hotels sell out and rates double. Crowds ease noticeably in the last week as European schools go back. Ticino in the south is genuinely hot, into the nineties.

September

Possibly the best overall month, and our recommendation for this readership. Excellent walking, foliage turning gold, thinning crowds, fewer afternoon storms and the clearest summit visibility of the year. Everything is still open. Lucerne Festival fills that city’s hotels, so book ahead there.

October

One of the quietest months, and the autumn colour in the alpine valleys and vineyards is superb — but this is when the shutters start coming down. Many mountain railways and cable cars close for annual maintenance from mid-month, first snow arrives at altitude, and the Glacier Express stops entirely from 11 October.

November

Avoid, unless you only want cities and Christmas markets. Ski resorts have not opened, summer mountain facilities are closed, and the lowlands are grey and often fogbound. It is the cheapest month of the year and it earns that price honestly. The Gornergrat railway also pauses for a fortnight in early November 2026.

December

Christmas markets run from late November to the 24th, and the ski season opens in earnest in early December. The week between Christmas and New Year is among the most crowded and expensive of the entire year. The Glacier Express resumes winter service on 5 December.

Best time to visit Switzerland — common questions

What is the single best month to visit Switzerland?

September, for most travellers and particularly for anyone wanting to avoid both crowds and heat. You get excellent walking weather, autumn colour coming into the valleys, thinning crowds, fewer afternoon thunderstorms and the clearest summit visibility of the year, with everything still open. Early to mid June is a close second: almost everything is running, the meadows are in flower, snow still caps the peaks and the days are at their longest.

Which months should I avoid?

November and April, if the mountains are why you are coming. Both fall inside the annual maintenance windows when resort villages and mountain lifts shut down: in November the ski season has not started and the summer facilities have closed, and the lowlands are grey and often fogbound; in April the low ski areas are closing while summer services have not yet opened. Both are perfectly fine for cities, Christmas markets and museums.

What exactly closes between the seasons?

Swiss resort villages and mountain lifts effectively close twice a year for maintenance, roughly mid-April to mid-June and roughly mid-October to early December. In those windows, hotels, restaurants and shops in villages such as Wengen, Murren, Grindelwald and Zermatt may simply be shut, and the cable car you built your day around may not run. Always check the specific lift's own website for its operating dates before you book flights.

What runs all year round?

The Jungfraujoch tunnel railway runs every day of the year without exception. The Gornergrat above Zermatt, Matterhorn Glacier Paradise, Titlis, Stoos and Rigi also run year-round with only short maintenance breaks - the Gornergrat pauses for about a fortnight in early November 2026. Rigi is a particularly good winter mountain because it often sits above the fog that settles over the lakes below.

Is July and August really that bad?

They are not bad, they are simply crowded and expensive. Everything is open, the weather is warm and the high trails are all accessible. What you trade is money and elbow room: rates peak, Jungfraujoch queues run thirty to sixty minutes by mid-morning, and Grindelwald buses can be standing-room only. Valley towns reach the high eighties Fahrenheit and Swiss hotels are not reliably air-conditioned. Take the first departures of the day.

When is it cheapest?

November is the cheapest month, followed by January and February in the cities and April in the mountains. The honest catch is that cheap months are cheap because much of what you came to see is closed. If you want value with good conditions, aim instead for late May to early June or late September to mid-October, when rates sit well below the summer peak but the lifts and railways are still running.
