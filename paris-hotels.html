Paris’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Paris — Best Neighborhoods & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Paris is a city of arrondissements, and the number on your door quietly sets the mood of the whole trip. Nearly every quarter is walkable and stitched together by the Métro, so the first choice isn’t which hotel — it’s which neighborhood you want to wake up in. We’ve grouped Paris’s most enduring, well-loved hotels by area and character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can simply choose your dates.

Louvre, Tuileries & Opéra

The Right Bank’s grand center — the Louvre, the Tuileries gardens, the arcaded Rue de Rivoli, and the gilded Palais Garnier — is the postcard Paris and a superb first-visit base. You wake steps from world-class museums and the great department stores, walk almost everywhere, and pay handsomely for the privilege.

Le Meurice

$$$$ LuxuryRue de RivoliPalace hotelFacing the Tuileries

A Dorchester Collection palace on Rue de Rivoli, gazing straight across at the Tuileries gardens with the Louvre a short stroll away. The interiors nod to Versailles, and its dining rooms have long been among the city’s most storied. It suits travelers who want classic French grandeur in the very center of the Right Bank.

Hôtel du Louvre

A 19th-century landmark on Place André Malraux, wedged between the Louvre, the Palais Royal, and the Opéra district. Its Haussmannian bones and corner café put you at a genuine crossroads of the city, museums and Métro lines all within a block. A strong, walk-everywhere choice for first-timers who want a marquee location without a full palace tariff.

Le Marais

The Marais is Paris at its most characterful — medieval lanes and Renaissance mansions, the arcaded Place des Vosges, independent galleries, and some of the best small shops and cafés in the city. Largely spared by Haussmann, it feels historic and lived-in at once, and it’s a favorite for travelers who want atmosphere and browsing over big boulevards.

Le Pavillon de la Reine

Tucked behind a leafy courtyard off the Place des Vosges, this ivy-clad retreat feels like a private mansion in the heart of the Marais. Antique-filled rooms, a quiet spa, and the calm of a garden setting make it a hushed escape from the lanes just outside. Ideal for couples who want intimacy and old-Paris charm over a grand lobby.

Saint-Germain-des-Prés & the Latin Quarter

The Left Bank is the Paris of literary cafés, bouquinistes along the Seine, and the Sorbonne’s student streets. Saint-Germain brings polished galleries and the legendary Café de Flore and Les Deux Magots; the Latin Quarter, just east, is livelier and more affordable. Together they make an atmospheric, walkable base for a classic Paris stay.

Hôtel Lutetia

$$$$ LuxuryBoulevard RaspailArt Deco landmarkSpa

The Left Bank’s only historic palace hotel, a 1910 Art Nouveau-meets-Deco landmark reborn after a meticulous restoration. Its brasserie, jazz bar, and large spa anchor Saint-Germain social life, while the facade remains a neighborhood icon. Best for travelers who want grand-hotel polish without crossing to the Right Bank.

Champs-Élysées & the Golden Triangle

The 8th arrondissement’s “Golden Triangle” — bounded by the Champs-Élysées, Avenue Montaigne, and Avenue George V — is the home of haute couture and the city’s greatest palace hotels. This is old-world glamour distilled: flagship boutiques, Michelin dining, and rooftops with the Eiffel Tower in view. Book here when the address is part of the occasion.

Just off the Champs-Élysées, the George V is famous for its extravagant floral displays and a trio of celebrated restaurants. Marble, tapestries, and a serene courtyard set a tone of unhurried opulence steps from Avenue Montaigne’s couture houses. The definitive Paris palace for travelers who want service and spectacle in equal measure.

Montmartre

On its hill above the city, Montmartre keeps a village feel — winding stairs, the white domes of Sacré-Cœur, artists’ squares, and vineyards that predate the tourists. It’s more residential and further from the central sights, but few quarters are as romantic. Choose it for atmosphere, views, and quieter mornings away from the crowds.

The seclusion makes it feel less like a hotel than a friend’s eccentric, art-filled townhouse. A distinctive, romantic hideaway for travelers who want privacy and Montmartre magic.

Budget & Value

Paris rewards thrifty travelers too — you simply trade a marquee address for a livelier fringe or a smaller room. The northeastern quarters around the Canal Saint-Martin, Belleville, and the Grands Boulevards offer the best value and excellent transport, freeing your budget for museums, markets, and long café afternoons.

It’s well run and well connected, near the Gare du Nord and lively nightlife. Best for solo travelers and groups who want character and low rates over a central address.
