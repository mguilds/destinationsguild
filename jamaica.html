An island the size of Connecticut with more character than continents twice its size — seven miles of flat white sand at Negril, the terraced falls at Dunn’s River, jerk smoke drifting off the roadside at Boston Bay, coffee grown in mountains that touch seven thousand feet, and the music that changed the world coming out of a small house on Hope Road. It is also a country still putting itself back together after the worst storm in its recorded history, and this guide tells you plainly what that means for your booking.

Read this before you book: Jamaica after Hurricane Melissa

On 28 October 2025 Hurricane Melissa came ashore near New Hope in Westmoreland as a Category 5 storm with 185 mph winds — the strongest hurricane ever recorded to strike Jamaica, ahead of Gilbert in 1988. Forty-five people died and the damage came to roughly US$8.8 billion, the costliest event in the country’s history.

Jamaica reopened to visitors on 15 December 2025, and it is genuinely open. Airports and cruise ports never stopped. Dunn’s River Falls, Mystic Mountain and Rick’s Cafe were back within weeks. More than eighty thousand tourism workers are back at work. But the recovery is uneven, and the honest picture as of August 2026 is this:

  • Negril, Ocho Rios, Kingston and Port Antonio are trading essentially normally. Kingston and the whole eastern end of the island were barely touched.
  • Montego Bay is the exception. A great many of the resorts an American reader will already have heard of are still shut — the Hyatt Zilara and Ziva pair, Secrets, Breathless, Dreams Rose Hall and Jewel Grande are all closed into 2027, and Sandals Montego Bay does not reopen until 18 December 2026. What is open there is very good, but the choice is much narrower than the brochures suggest.
  • The south coast around Black River and St Elizabeth is the slowest to come back and is not a sensible first-trip choice this year.

Our practical advice: book by specific property name and specific dates, and telephone or email the hotel to confirm that your room category is open and that no construction is going on next to it. Reopening trackers published online go stale within weeks. This is not a reason to avoid Jamaica — it is a reason to choose your hotel deliberately, which is what the five guides below are for.

Why visit Jamaica

Three and a half hours from Miami, and unlike anywhere else in the Caribbean.

The beaches earn their reputation

Seven Mile Beach at Negril is a genuine seven miles of flat, pale, walkable sand — you can stroll from bar to bar at the water’s edge without a single step. Doctor’s Cave in Montego Bay, Frenchman’s Cove in Portland and the private crescent at Jamaica Inn are each, in their own way, as good as the Caribbean gets.

All-inclusive was invented here

The modern all-inclusive resort was effectively born in Jamaica, and the island still does it better and in more variety than anywhere else — couples-only, adults-only, family, budget, and a handful of small properties that pointedly refuse to do it at all.

A culture that travelled the world

Reggae, ska, dancehall, jerk, Blue Mountain coffee, Red Stripe and rum. The Bob Marley Museum is the actual house he lived in. Boston Bay is the actual place jerk cooking comes from. Very little here is staged for visitors.

Mountains behind the beach

Most Caribbean islands are flat and dry. Jamaica has a spine of green mountains rising past seven thousand feet, a rainforest coast in the east, waterfalls you can climb, and a river you can ride down on a bamboo raft. You do not have to spend the whole week on a lounger.

How the country divides up

The resort coast

Montego Bay, Negril and Ocho Rios form a north- and west-facing arc that holds almost every all-inclusive in the country. Montego Bay has the airport and the only genuinely walkable hotel strip. Negril has the flattest and best beach. Ocho Rios has the most to do within half an hour of the front door, and the cruise ships to prove it.

Kingston & the mountains

Kingston is a working capital, not a resort town, and that is the point of it: the Bob Marley Museum, the National Gallery, Devon House, and a restaurant scene the north coast cannot touch. Behind it the Blue Mountains rise to 7,402 feet, cool enough at night to need a sweater, and the coffee estates above Irish Town make an easy, lovely half day.

The east

Port Antonio and the parish of Portland are the Jamaica that development passed by. There are no all-inclusive resorts here at all — the hotels are small, individual and often eccentric — and the scenery is the best on the island: the Blue Lagoon, Frenchman’s Cove where a cold river meets the warm sea, Reach Falls, and a bamboo raft down the Rio Grande. It is two to two and a half hours from Kingston airport and five hours from Montego Bay, which is exactly why it stayed quiet.

The south coast

Treasure Beach, Black River and the Pelican Bar are the slow, unpolished, fishing-village side of Jamaica. It is lovely — but St Elizabeth took the full force of Hurricane Melissa and is still rebuilding, so we would not send a first-time visitor there in 2026.

Top things to do in Jamaica

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Walk Seven Mile Beach at Negril

Seven miles of flat, pale sand with no steps, no hills and no roads to cross — you can walk from beach bar to beach bar at the water’s edge. Most people walk a mile or two and take a route taxi back. New raised boardwalks went in as part of the post-storm rebuild.

Climb Dunn’s River Falls

Six hundred feet of terraced limestone that you climb in the water, hand in hand in a human chain, with guides who have done it ten thousand times. Go before nine in the morning or after half past three — on a two-ship cruise day the middle of the day is bedlam.

Raft the Rio Grande

Two to three hours lying back on a thirty-foot bamboo raft while a captain poles you down a green valley — an Errol Flynn invention from the 1940s and still the loveliest slow afternoon in Jamaica. Getting on and off needs a hand up; the ride itself asks nothing of you but a hat.

The Bob Marley Museum, Kingston

Not a themed attraction — the actual house at 56 Hope Road where he lived and recorded, with the bullet holes still in the wall. Guided tour only, about an hour, and the guides are genuinely good. Pair it with Devon House and an ice cream.

Jerk at Boston Bay

The birthplace of jerk cooking, twenty minutes east of Port Antonio: working roadside pits, pimento wood smoke, and pork or chicken chopped onto paper in front of you. Informal, smoky, cash only, and worth the drive.

Blue Mountain coffee, above Kingston

A working estate tour at Craighton or thereabouts, an hour up a winding road into cool air and cloud. Ask for the tasting-led version rather than the full farm walk if the slopes are a concern — the view alone is worth the trip.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

January to April is the dry season and the classic window — warm, bright, low humidity, and the lowest storm risk of the year. February and March are the driest months. Christmas and New Year are the peak of the peak, sell out months ahead and can cost double.

Late April to early June is the honest value sweet spot: the Easter and spring-break premium has gone, the weather is still very good, the sea is at eighty-one degrees, and you are ahead of the serious hurricane risk. September and October are the cheapest months and the wettest — October averages more than six inches of rain and is the historical peak of storm risk.

Getting there & getting around

Sangster International (MBJ) at Montego Bay handles more than seventy per cent of arrivals and is the airport for Montego Bay, Negril (about an hour and fifty minutes on the coast road) and Ocho Rios (about two hours). Norman Manley (KIN) at Kingston is right for the capital, the Blue Mountains and Port Antonio, and now reaches Ocho Rios in about an hour on the tolled North–South Highway. Ian Fleming (OCJ) near Ocho Rios takes private and small regional aircraft only.

We would not suggest hiring a car. Jamaica drives on the left, rural roads are potholed and unlit, and driving at night is specifically warned against. Use a pre-booked private transfer on arrival, licensed red-plate JUTA taxis locally with the fare agreed in advance, and your hotel’s driver for excursions. The Knutsford Express coach links the main towns comfortably.

Tap water is safe to drink. Buy travel insurance that includes medical evacuation — an air ambulance to the United States starts around thirty thousand dollars, and most Jamaican hospitals ask for payment upfront.

Jamaica travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Jamaica?

January to April is the sweet spot: dry, sunny, warm days in the low eighties, and the lowest chance of a storm all year. February and March are the driest months of all. If you want the same weather for noticeably less money, come between late April and early June, after the Easter and spring-break rush and before the serious hurricane risk begins. September and October are the cheapest months in Jamaica, and they are cheap for a reason — they are the wettest and the riskiest.

Is Jamaica safe after Hurricane Melissa?

Yes, and it is open — but book by property name, not by resort area. Hurricane Melissa came ashore in Westmoreland on 28 October 2025 as a Category 5, the strongest storm ever recorded to strike Jamaica. The tourism industry reopened on 15 December 2025 and airports, cruise ports and most attractions were working within weeks. Recovery has been very uneven, though. Negril, Ocho Rios, Kingston and Port Antonio are trading normally. Montego Bay is not: a large share of its best-known all-inclusives, including the whole Hyatt Inclusive Collection there, remain shut into 2027, and Sandals Montego Bay does not reopen until 18 December 2026. Confirm directly with your hotel that your room category is open and that there is no construction beside it.

Which part of Jamaica should I stay in?

For a flat, walkable beach holiday, Negril — Seven Mile Beach is genuinely level sand from end to end, which matters more than anything else if steps are a problem. For the widest choice of all-inclusives and excursions, Ocho Rios. For the shortest airport transfer, Montego Bay, whose Hip Strip is the one place in Jamaica you can walk out of your hotel to restaurants and a beach. For music, food, art and the Blue Mountains, Kingston. For quiet, green, unspoiled Jamaica with almost no resort development, Port Antonio.

What does ‘all-inclusive’ really cover in Jamaica?

At Sandals, Beaches and Couples it genuinely covers everything: all meals, premium spirits, watersports, scuba at most properties, airport transfers on some packages, and tips — tipping is actively discouraged. At the big Spanish and family brands, RIU, Iberostar, Bahia Principe and Moon Palace, meals and domestic drinks are in, but spa treatments, premium wines, some à la carte restaurants and off-property excursions are extra. And several of Jamaica's finest hotels — Half Moon, Round Hill, Jamaica Inn, Trident — are not all-inclusive at all. They run on room-only or bed-and-breakfast rates and the dining bill is separate, which surprises people. Always read the plan before you compare prices.

Should I hire a car in Jamaica?

For most travellers over fifty-five, no. Jamaica drives on the left, the roads outside the main highways have deep potholes and no lighting, local driving is assertive, and the US State Department specifically warns against driving at night and notes that rental vehicles have been targeted. Ambulance cover is limited and most hospitals want payment upfront. Use a pre-booked private transfer from the airport, licensed red-plate JUTA taxis locally with the fare agreed before you set off, and your hotel's driver for day trips. The Knutsford Express coach is a comfortable, air-conditioned way to move between the main towns.

Do I need a visa, and is there a form to fill in?

US, Canadian and UK citizens do not need a visa; Americans are generally admitted for up to six months. Most western European nationals get ninety days, some thirty. Everyone must complete the free C5 Immigration and Customs declaration online before arrival — the only official site is enterjamaica.com, it takes about ten minutes, and it is completely free. Be careful: several look-alike sites charge between five and thirty-five dollars for this free form. Carry at least six months of passport validity because airlines commonly insist on it, and confirm the current rules with the official Jamaican authority before you travel.
