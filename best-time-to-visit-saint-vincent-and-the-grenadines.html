Best Time to Visit Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

The short version: come between January and May if you want the weather you have been imagining, and come in the first half of December or in late April and May if you want that weather without the February prices. The long version is below, month by month, with the honest word on the wet half of the year — which is not the write-off people assume — and on the hurricane season, which in this country is not an abstraction. Hurricane Beryl came ashore on 1 July 2024.

The short answer

January to May is the dry season and the reliable half of the year: steady sun, a working trade wind off the Atlantic, lower humidity, calmer crossings to the Grenadines and the highest room rates of the year. June to November is hotter, stickier and wetter, and it is also the Atlantic hurricane season, 1 June to 30 November. What the wet half actually looks like is heavy showers that arrive, empty themselves and move on, not days of grey — and there is a real argument for it, because the islands are greener, the sea is warmest and a room costs a good deal less.

Our own two picks are the edges. The first half of December has dry-season weather and pre-Christmas prices. Late April into May has the same weather with the February crowds gone. If you have a fixed week and no flexibility, February and March are the safest bet and you will pay for the certainty.

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines month by month

Temperatures barely move here — daytime highs sit in the mid to high eighties Fahrenheit almost all year and the sea is warm enough to swim in every month. What changes is rain, wind, sea state and price.

January

Dry, bright and breezy, with the trade wind at its most dependable. One of the two or three best months to be here and priced accordingly. Crossings to Bequia are usually comfortable. Book early: this is high season and the small hotels are genuinely small.

February

The driest, clearest, most expensive stretch of the year. If you want a guarantee rather than a good chance, this is the month to buy it. Everything is open, every boat is running, and nothing is cheap.

March

Still firmly dry season and a shade less pressed than February. Excellent sailing and snorkelling conditions in the Grenadines. Easter sometimes falls at the end of the month and brings a local surge with it, so check the calendar before you book.

April

The last full dry month and, in its second half, one of the best-value weeks of the year: the weather has not broken and the high-season rates have. Easter is the exception and is busy and dear wherever it lands.

May

The season turns during May. Early May is often still dry and quiet and good value; by the end of the month the afternoon showers have started and the humidity has climbed. A fair trade if you like an empty beach.

June

Hot, humid, green and much cheaper. The hurricane season opens on 1 June and the second half of the month is when Vincy Mas builds, which fills Kingstown and the rooms near it. Showers rather than washouts.

July

Carnival finishes at the turn of the month and Kingstown is at its loudest and best for it. Otherwise this is the wet season proper and the start of the busier end of the storm season — Beryl made landfall on 1 July 2024. Insure for weather disruption.

August

Hot and wet, with the warmest sea of the year and the lowest prices outside September. A good month for diving if you do not mind the humidity. It is also the heart of the hurricane season; watch the forecast in the week before you fly.

September

The quietest and cheapest month, the statistical peak of the Atlantic hurricane season, and the month when some small properties and restaurants simply close. If you come now, come with flexible flights and good insurance, and expect to have the place to yourself.

October

Still wet and still inside the storm season, but the islands are at their greenest and Independence Day on 27 October brings flags, music and a national holiday. Prices stay low until the last week or so.

November

A month of two halves. The rain eases, the hurricane season closes on 30 November, and the last two weeks are often very good weather at shoulder prices. One of the better value months if you can take the risk of the first half.

December

The first half is the best-value dry-season week of the year: the weather has settled, the boats are all running and the Christmas rates have not started. From about the third week prices go to their annual peak and stay there into the New Year.

Rain, wind and hurricanes

The pattern is simple enough to plan around. The north-east trade wind blows across these islands most of the year, which is why the windward coasts are rough and the leeward coasts are calm, and why a hotel on the sheltered side feels like a different country from one on the exposed side. Rain is heaviest from about June to November, falls as short hard showers rather than all-day drizzle, and is heavier on the mountainous main island than out in the low, dry Grenadines — Union Island and Canouan get noticeably less rain than Saint Vincent in the same week.

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November and the country sits inside the belt, not at the edge of it. Hurricane Beryl crossed the Southern Grenadines on 1 July 2024 and hit Union Island, Canouan, Mayreau and Petit Saint Vincent hardest; rebuilding on Union Island was still under way more than two years later. We say that plainly because the honest advice follows from it: if you travel between June and November, buy insurance that covers weather disruption, book flights you can move, and watch the forecast in the week before you go rather than the month before.

The other weather question people ask here is about the volcano rather than the sky. La Soufriere, at the north end of Saint Vincent, erupted explosively in April 2021. When we checked in November 2026 the University of the West Indies Seismic Research Centre listed it at alert level Green, its lowest. That is the page to check before you travel, and it is not a seasonal matter — a volcano does not keep a calendar.

Events worth timing a trip around

Vincy Mas

The national carnival, and the biggest thing in the Vincentian year. It builds through the second half of June with shows and competitions and finishes with two days of mas at the turn of June into July, in and around Kingstown. The dates move from year to year, so we print none here that the carnival’s own organisers have not published for the year you are asking about. If you want it, book months ahead; if you do not, avoid that fortnight in Kingstown.

Easter

Not a fixed date and not only a church festival here — it is a sailing and regatta weekend in the Grenadines and a family holiday across the country. Rooms go early and prices rise wherever it falls in the calendar.

Independence Day

27 October. Flags, parades, music and a public holiday, in what is otherwise one of the quietest and cheapest months of the year. A good reason to take the risk on late October.

Nine Mornings

The Vincentian Christmas tradition: nine early mornings of music, dancing and sea baths before dawn in the run-up to Christmas Day, in Kingstown and the villages. Unlike almost anything else in the Caribbean calendar it is for the islands themselves rather than for visitors, which is exactly why it is worth seeing.

Prices and what is open

Room rates here follow the dry season rather than the school holidays. The peak runs from about the third week of December to the middle of April, with February and Easter at the top. Rates fall through May, sit low from June to early December, and are at their lowest in September. The difference between a February week and a September week at the same small hotel is the kind of gap that pays for the flights.

What is open changes with the season too, more than it does on the bigger islands, because the businesses are small. Some guest houses, restaurants and dive operations in the Grenadines close or cut back in September and October; ferry and inter-island flight timetables thin out; and a boat trip that runs daily in February may run on demand in August. None of that is a reason to stay away, but it is a reason to confirm with the property or operator directly rather than trusting a timetable you read in advance. In the Southern Grenadines, after Beryl, confirming direct is not optional.

Timing it in your later years

If heat and humidity are the things that spoil a holiday for you, the answer here is clear: come between January and April, and pick the second half of that window for the lower prices. The dry months are not only drier but noticeably less sticky, and the trade wind does the work that air conditioning does elsewhere.

The other reason to prefer the dry season has nothing to do with comfort on land. Getting to the Grenadines means a ferry or a small plane, and the open crossings are calmest from January to May. A rough hour on a crowded boat is unpleasant at any age and genuinely risky if you are unsteady on your feet. Book the crossing for a morning rather than an afternoon, when the sea is usually flatter, ask for a seat low down and in the middle of the boat, and never schedule a crossing for the day you fly home. If you would rather not take a boat at all, Saint Vincent itself has enough in it for a week, and the botanic gardens above Kingstown are the flattest, shadiest outing in the country.

Best time to visit St Vincent & the Grenadines — questions

What is the best month to visit Saint Vincent and the Grenadines?

February and March give you the best odds on weather and the worst on price. For the best balance of the two, take the second half of April into early May, or the first two weeks of December: dry-season sun and sea with high-season rates either not yet started or already finished. If you want a single month and do not want to think about it, March.

What is the cheapest time to go?

September, by a clear margin, followed by August and October. That is also the statistical peak of the Atlantic hurricane season and the time when some small properties and restaurants close, so the saving is real and so is the reason for it. If you go then, book flights you can change and insure for weather disruption.

Is the rainy season bad enough to avoid?

No, and treating it as a write-off costs people a good holiday. From June to November the rain comes as heavy showers that pass rather than as all-day grey, often in the afternoon, and the islands are greener and the sea warmer for it. The genuine reasons to be careful about those months are the storm risk and the thinner timetables, not the rain itself.

When is hurricane season, and how worried should I be?

1 June to 30 November, with the peak from about mid-August to mid-October. This country is inside the belt rather than at its southern edge: Hurricane Beryl crossed the Southern Grenadines on 1 July 2024 and hit Union Island, Canouan, Mayreau and Petit Saint Vincent hardest, and rebuilding on Union Island was still going on more than two years afterwards. The sensible response is not to avoid the season but to travel in it properly: moveable flights, insurance that covers weather disruption, and the forecast checked in the week before you fly rather than the month.

When is Vincy Mas?

It builds through the second half of June and finishes with two days of mas at the turn of June into July, in and around Kingstown. The exact dates move each year and we print none that the carnival’s own organisers have not published, because a wrong date here costs somebody a flight. If carnival is the point of the trip, book months ahead; if it is not, keep that fortnight clear of Kingstown.

What is the best time for the Tobago Cays and the Grenadines?

January to May, and for a specific reason: the crossings. The sea between the islands is calmest in the dry season, the day boats run on a full timetable, and snorkelling visibility inside the reef is at its best. The Cays are a day trip rather than a place to stay, so what you are really timing is the boat ride out and back.

Is the volcano something I should time around?

No. La Soufrière erupted explosively in April 2021 and, when we checked in November 2026, the University of the West Indies Seismic Research Centre listed it at alert level Green, its lowest. Volcanoes do not follow a season, so there is nothing to time; what there is to do is read the Centre’s current status before you book the walk, because it is the body that advises the government and its page is more current than any travel guide.

How far ahead should I book?

Further than you would for a bigger Caribbean island, because the room stock is genuinely small. For January to April, three to six months is sensible and for February or Easter more. For Vincy Mas and for Christmas and New Year, book as early as you can. For September and October you can be casual about it, which is part of the appeal.
