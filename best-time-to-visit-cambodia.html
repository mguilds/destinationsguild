Best Time to Visit Cambodia

The Best Time to Visit Cambodia

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Cambodia has one climate and two seasons, which makes it a far simpler country to time than its neighbours. From November to April it is dry; from May to October it rains, usually in the afternoon and usually not for long. The sweet spot most Guild travellers want is November to February — dry, warm in the day, genuinely cool at dawn, and perfect for walking Angkor before the sun gets up. March to May is the same dry weather with the heat turned up hard. And the green season, which everybody is told to avoid, is quietly the best-value time of year: the moats are full, the rice is brilliant, the crowds thin out and the prices come down.

Two Seasons, One Country

Cambodia is easier to time than its neighbours. Here is what each stretch of the year is actually like.

The cool dry season — November to February

The high season, and it earns it. Days are warm rather than punishing, mornings are cool enough for a jacket at five o’clock, humidity is low and rain is rare. This is when Angkor is most comfortable to walk, when the islands off the south coast are at their calmest, and when hotels are fullest and dearest. Book several months ahead for Christmas and New Year, when Siem Reap sells out.

The hot dry season — March to May

Still dry, and seriously hot: April is the hottest month of the Cambodian year, and the middle of the day at Angkor is not a reasonable place for anyone to be. If you come now, start at first light, plan nothing between eleven and three, choose a hotel with a pool and mean it. In exchange you get thinner crowds than January and lower room rates right up until the New Year holiday.

The green season — June to October

The southwest monsoon, and much misunderstood. It rarely rains all day; the pattern is a bright morning, a heavy downpour in the afternoon and a washed-clean evening. The countryside turns an extraordinary green, the Angkor moats and the Tonlé Sap fill, the light is better for photographs than at any other time, and both crowds and prices fall away. The caveats are real but narrow: remote unsealed roads get difficult, and September and early October are genuinely wet.

The south coast and the islands

Kampot, Kep and the islands follow the same calendar, with the sea at its clearest and calmest from November to April. In the green season the water is choppier and boat crossings to the islands are occasionally cancelled, so leave a spare day either side of a flight if an island is on your itinerary.

Month by Month

What to expect, and what it costs you, in each month of the year.

January

The best month of the year and priced accordingly. Dry, warm by day, cool at dawn, and Angkor at its most comfortable. Expect company at every sunrise and book rooms well ahead.

February

As good as January, with the heat starting to build toward the end of the month. Still dry, still clear, still busy. A fine month for combining the temples with the south coast.

March

Dry and noticeably hotter. The crowds thin after the New Year peak and rates ease. Start early at the temples and take the afternoon off; the sea at Kep and Koh Rong is still calm.

April

The hottest month, often fiercely so. Mid-month brings Khmer New Year, the biggest holiday in the country: families travel home, much of Phnom Penh empties, many shops close for several days and transport is full. Wonderful to witness, awkward to travel through — plan around it rather than into it.

May

The first storms arrive and the country exhales. Still hot, but the dust is gone and the green is coming back. Prices drop sharply from the middle of the month and the temples are quiet.

June

Green season proper. Mornings are usually fine, afternoons bring a downpour that clears. Excellent value, easy temple visits, and the countryside around Battambang at its best.

July

Warm, wet in the afternoons, and one of the loveliest times at Angkor — moss on the stone, water in the moats, few people in the frame. Pack a light rain jacket and sandals you do not mind soaking.

August

Much like July, a little wetter. Fine for the main temple circuits and the towns; less reliable for remote unsealed roads and for island boats on the south coast.

September

The wettest month, and the one to think twice about if your trip is short. It is also when the Tonlé Sap is at its fullest, which is the right time to see the floating villages. Pchum Ben, the fifteen-day festival of the ancestors, usually falls in September or early October.

October

Wet at the start, clearing through the month, and by the end of it one of the best-kept secrets of the Cambodian year: green landscape, full waterways, soft light and low-season prices with high-season weather arriving.

November

The sweet spot. The rains have ended, the country is still green rather than dusty, the air is dry and the heat has gone out of the day. The Water Festival fills Phnom Penh’s riverfront for three days around the November full moon — boat races, enormous crowds and rooms booked months in advance.

December

Dry, bright and the coolest month of the year, with mornings that genuinely need a layer. Peak season: Siem Reap is full, rates are at their highest, and Christmas and New Year need booking far ahead.

The Cheapest Time to Go

Where the money is, and the two fortnights to avoid if you are watching it.

Green season — May to early October

The cheapest months by a clear margin, with room rates in Siem Reap often half what they are in January and the same temples almost to yourself. You are buying an afternoon downpour and the chance of a difficult rural road. For most travellers that is a good trade.

The two expensive fortnights

Christmas through New Year, and the week either side of Chinese New Year, are the two stretches when Siem Reap genuinely runs out of rooms and prices go where they like. If your dates are fixed inside either window, book months rather than weeks ahead.

Heat, Rain & Crowds

The four things that actually shape a Cambodian itinerary.

Heat, and how to beat it

From March to May the middle of the day is the enemy, and it is also the single most common reason visitors cut Angkor short. The answer is the one every Cambodian uses: be out at dawn, be indoors or in the water from late morning to mid-afternoon, and go out again at four. Carry far more water than feels necessary and put salt back in as well.

Rain, and what it actually does

Green-season rain is heavy, warm and brief, and it does not ruin a day — it reshapes it. What it does affect is unsealed roads in remote provinces, boat crossings to the islands, and the dirt tracks to a few outlying temples. Build one spare day into a wet-season itinerary and almost nothing can go wrong.

Crowds, and where they are

Cambodia’s crowds are concentrated: Angkor Wat at sunrise, the Bayon at mid-morning, Ta Prohm all day, and the Old Market quarter of Siem Reap in the evening. Everywhere else in the country is quiet in every month. Running the main circuit in reverse, or starting at the outlying temples, changes the day entirely.

Festivals worth planning around

Khmer New Year in mid-April and the Water Festival in November are both national events on a scale that fills hotels and empties offices, and both are a privilege to see. Pchum Ben in September or early October is quieter and more domestic. Dates follow the lunar calendar and move from year to year, so confirm them before you fix flights.

Best Time by Region

The country is small, but the right month differs from the temples to the coast.

Siem Reap & Angkor

November to February for comfort, July to October for green moats, soft light and far fewer people. Whichever you choose, the temples are a dawn activity in every month of the year.

Phnom Penh

November to February, when the riverfront is pleasant to walk in the evening. The city copes with rain better than the countryside does, so the green season is no real obstacle here — the heat of April is a bigger one.

Battambang & the northwest

November to January for cycling the villages in comfort, or June to August when the rice is at its greenest and the countryside is at its most photogenic. Avoid remote dirt roads in September.

Kampot, Kep & the islands

November to April for calm seas and reliable boat crossings. Kampot itself is agreeable year round; the islands are the part of the country most affected by green-season weather.

Frequently Asked Questions

The timing questions travellers ask most before booking Cambodia.

What is the best time to visit Cambodia?

November to February — the cool dry season. Days are warm without being punishing, mornings are genuinely cool, rain is rare, and Angkor is comfortable to walk from first light. It is also the busiest and most expensive stretch of the year, so book ahead, particularly over Christmas and New Year. If you want the same dry weather with fewer people, take March and plan around the heat.

What is the cheapest time to visit Cambodia?

May to early October, the green season, when room rates in Siem Reap can be close to half their January level and the temples are quiet. You are trading a heavy afternoon downpour, and occasional trouble on remote unsealed roads, for a green landscape, full moats and a far emptier Angkor. For most travellers it is a good bargain.

When is the rainy season in Cambodia?

The southwest monsoon runs roughly from May to October, with September the wettest month. It is not all-day rain: the usual pattern is a clear morning, a heavy downpour in the afternoon and a bright evening. The countryside is at its greenest, the Tonlé Sap is at its fullest, and photographs are better then than at any other time of year.

Is April a bad time to visit Cambodia?

It is the hottest month of the year and the middle of the day is hard going, especially at the temples, so it is the one month the Guild suggests thinking twice about. It also holds Khmer New Year, when much of the country travels home and a good deal of Phnom Penh closes for several days. If April is your only option, start at dawn, book a hotel with a pool, and treat the holiday as something to witness rather than something to travel through.

When is the Water Festival in Cambodia?

Bon Om Touk, the Water Festival, is held over three days around the full moon in November, with the largest celebration on the Phnom Penh riverfront — longboat races, fireworks and crowds in the hundreds of thousands. The exact dates follow the lunar calendar and change each year, so confirm them before booking, and reserve a Phnom Penh room months rather than weeks in advance.
