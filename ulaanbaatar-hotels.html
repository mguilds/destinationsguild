Where to Stay in Ulaanbaatar — the Square, and Terelj

Researched and compiled by the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Ulaanbaatar is the hinge of every Mongolian trip: you land here, you leave from here, and you come back through it between one piece of countryside and the next. Nearly everything a visitor wants is within a few streets of Sukhbaatar Square, and the hotels below are the ones our booking partner actually carries, each checked against Trip.com’s own listing for this city.

Ulaanbaatar Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best place to stay in Ulaanbaatar?

Within walking distance of Sukhbaatar Square, in the Sukhbaatar district. The square is the centre of the city and the museums, the Choijin Lama temple complex, the state department store and most of the restaurants a visitor will want are around it. Traffic in Ulaanbaatar is heavy and unpredictable, so being able to walk is worth more here than a slightly better room further out.

How many nights do you need in Ulaanbaatar?

Two at the start and one at the end is the shape most trips take. Two nights covers the Gandan monastery, the National Museum, the square and a decent dinner without rushing, and leaves you a recovery day after a long flight. The single night at the end is for the drive back from the countryside, a shower and a flight out. Everything that makes Mongolia extraordinary is outside the city.

Can you stay in a ger near Ulaanbaatar?

Yes, out at Gorkhi-Terelj, about an hour east. The lodges there are listed under Ulaanbaatar by our booking partner because the park falls inside the capital's own Nalaikh district, so they appear in an Ulaanbaatar search. It is the easiest way to sleep in real Mongolian landscape if you are short of days; it is not a substitute for the Gobi or the north.

Do hotels in Ulaanbaatar take cards?

In the city, generally yes — hotels, restaurants and supermarkets take cards without fuss. Outside the capital, assume they do not. Draw tugrik in Ulaanbaatar before you leave it and keep small notes for roadside stops, because camps and drivers will want cash.

Is Ulaanbaatar walkable?

The central square area is, and that is the part you will spend your time in. Beyond it the city sprawls a long way up the valley and traffic is slow, so plan on a taxi or a ride app for anything outside the middle. Pavements can be uneven and in winter they are icy; proper boots matter more here than in most capitals.
