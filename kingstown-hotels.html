Where to Stay in Kingstown and on Saint Vincent

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Almost nobody actually sleeps in Kingstown, and almost everybody who comes to Saint Vincent passes through it. The rooms are a few miles south of the capital, strung along the sheltered south-west corner of the island between Indian Bay and Calliaqua, with the ferry jetty for the Grenadines and the dive shops on the same short stretch of coast. This page explains which part is which, how far it really is to the sand, the ferry and the airport, and what to ask a property before you send money. We name no hotels on this page, and the reason is below.

Where to stay on Saint Vincent, area by area

Five places a visitor might reasonably sleep, and what each one is actually like.

Villa and Indian Bay

The usual answer, and a sound one. A sheltered stretch of coast about fifteen minutes south of Kingstown with most of the island’s visitor rooms, several restaurants, the dive operators and the jetty where the Grenadines boats and the Young Island ferry leave. Small beaches rather than great ones — you are here for the base, not the sand.

Calliaqua and the south-east corner

A little further round, quieter, more residential, and close to Argyle airport. Guest houses and apartments more than hotels. A good choice for a short stay built around a flight, or for a longer one if you are happy to drive or take minibuses for an evening out.

Kingstown itself

A working port town of market stalls, arcaded streets, two cathedrals and ships. There are a few rooms in it, and they are for people with business in town rather than for holidays: it is steep, it is noisy in the morning and it empties oddly in the evening. Walk it, eat in it, sleep outside it.

Young Island

A small island a few hundred yards off Villa, reached by a short ferry, run as a single resort property. Mentioned here because people ask about it and because the crossing, short as it is, is a boat — which matters if you have luggage or unsteady legs.

The leeward coast and the north

Barrouallie, Chateaubelair and the villages up the sheltered west coast, where the island looks most like itself: black sand, fishing boats, breadfruit and the volcano at the end of the road. Very little to book and a long way from a restaurant at night. Lovely for a drive, demanding as a base.

The Grenadines are a different page

If what you want is Bequia, Mustique, Canouan or Union Island, this page will not help you much — those are separate islands, reached by ferry or small plane, with their own room stock and their own seasons. Read the islands section of our country guide first.

Three questions settle most of it before you pay. How far is it, in minutes on foot, from the room to swimmable water? How many steps are there between the car and the bed? And is the price you are looking at inclusive of government tax and service charge, which are added here and are not small?

Getting around

Minibuses are the island’s real transport: cheap, frequent, loud, and running from the terminal in Kingstown out along the coast roads until early evening. They are an experience worth having once and a nuisance with luggage. Taxis are everywhere, fares are negotiated rather than metered, and the only rule that matters is to agree the figure before you get in — drivers expect it and nobody is offended.

Hiring a car makes sense for two or three days rather than a fortnight: the leeward coast drive and the north of the island repay it, and the rest of the time the car sits. Driving is on the left, visitors need a temporary local driving permit which the hire company arranges, and the roads are narrow, steep and busier than you expect near Kingstown. The Grenadines are reached from the Villa jetty and from Kingstown by ferry, and from Argyle by small plane; Argyle International Airport is on the windward side of the island, about half an hour from the capital, and opened in 2017.

When to come and what it costs

January to May is the dry season: the best weather, the calmest crossings and the highest rates. June to November is hotter and wetter, carries the Atlantic hurricane season from 1 June to 30 November, and costs considerably less. The two best-value windows are the first half of December and late April into May. Our best time to visit page goes through it month by month.

Two things to budget for that catch visitors out. Government tax and a service charge are added to hotel and restaurant bills here, so a quoted room rate is not the figure you pay — ask what is included before you book. And the Grenadines cost money to reach: a return ferry crossing, a day boat to the Tobago Cays, or a seat on a small plane are all real line items, and a week that includes two island trips is a different budget from a week that does not. Prices are in Eastern Caribbean dollars, pegged at EC$2.70 to the US dollar; US dollars are widely taken and your change may well come back in local money.

Travelling in your later years

Stay at Villa or Indian Bay. That is most of the advice. It is flat, it is close to restaurants and dive shops and the ferry jetty, and it spares you both the hills of Kingstown and the long drive from the far side of the island. Ask about steps before you book: properties on this coast are often built down a slope towards the water, and a lovely room can be twenty steps below the car park.

In Kingstown, the botanic gardens are the outing we would recommend to anyone who does not want a climb — flat, shaded paths, benches, and genuinely cool after the heat of the market streets. The town itself is steep and the pavements are uneven in places, so take a taxi up and walk down rather than the other way round. Medical facilities are concentrated near the capital and thin out quickly beyond it, and they are limited in the Grenadines; if you travel with a condition that needs watching, that is an argument for basing yourself on the main island and taking day trips rather than moving from island to island with your luggage.

Questions about staying in Kingstown

Should I stay in Kingstown itself?

Probably not. Kingstown is a working port and market town rather than a resort, it is steep, and it is busy early and quiet late. The rooms visitors actually use are a few miles south at Villa and Indian Bay, which is flat, has the restaurants and dive shops, and is where the Grenadines ferries leave from. Stay there and walk Kingstown on a market morning, which is the best way to see it anyway.

How far is the airport from the hotels?

Argyle International Airport is on the windward side of the island and the hotel coast is on the sheltered south-west, so reckon on about half an hour by road to Villa or Indian Bay, and less to Calliaqua and the south-east corner. The airport opened in 2017 and replaced the old strip that sat beside Kingstown, which is why older guidebooks give a much shorter transfer than you will actually have.

Why does this page not name any hotels?

Because nobody at the Guild has yet read these properties' own published claims about themselves, and we will not recommend a hotel on the strength of a search result. Our country and city pages name hotels only when we have checked what the property itself states about steps, distances, what is included and what is open. For Saint Vincent that work is still to do, so what you get here is the areas, the questions worth asking, and a pinned hotel list for the right place rather than a name search that lands you somewhere else.

Is there a beach at Villa?

Small ones, and that is the honest answer. Indian Bay and Villa have sheltered strips of sand that are perfectly pleasant for a swim before breakfast, and they are not the long pale crescents that sell Caribbean holidays. Saint Vincent is a steep volcanic island with dark sand on much of its coast. If a great beach is the point of the trip, the Grenadines are where the great beaches are, and you reach them by boat.

How do I get to Bequia and the other Grenadines?

By ferry from Kingstown or the Villa jetty, and by small plane from Argyle. Bequia is the busy route and takes about an hour on the water; boats down the chain to Canouan, Mayreau and Union Island run less often and on a timetable that changes with the season. Confirm the sailing a day or two beforehand rather than trusting a schedule you read months ago, and do not plan a crossing for the day you fly home.

Do I need a hire car?

Not to stay on the hotel coast, where taxis and minibuses cover everything you need. Hire one for two or three days if you want the leeward coast drive, the north of the island and the volcano road, which are the best of Saint Vincent and awkward to reach otherwise. Driving is on the left and visitors need a temporary local permit, which the hire company arranges when you collect the car.

What will be added to my bill?

Government tax and a service charge, on hotel and restaurant bills both, and they are not trivial. Ask whether a quoted room rate includes them before you book, because the difference between the headline figure and the total is the most common unpleasant surprise on this island. Prices are in Eastern Caribbean dollars at EC$2.70 to the US dollar; cards work in hotels and supermarkets, and you want cash for minibuses, markets and small bars.

Is Saint Vincent safe to walk around?

Kingstown in daylight is a normal busy Caribbean market town and walking it is the right way to see it. As anywhere, the sensible precautions apply: keep valuables out of sight, do not walk unlit streets or empty beaches after dark, and take a taxi back in the evening rather than walking the coast road. Your own government’s travel advice is the current word on this and is worth reading before you go, because it is updated and a guide page is not.
