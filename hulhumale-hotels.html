Where to Stay in Hulhumalé

Where to Stay in Hulhumalé — The Airport Island

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Hulhumalé is a made island — pumped out of the lagoon beside the airport and laid out in wide, flat streets with a long beach down one side. It is the practical answer to a great many Maldives problems: ten minutes from the terminal, a real stretch of sand you can swim from, ordinary hotels at ordinary prices, and a bridge into the capital when you want a city. Stay at the beach end for the water, in the central blocks for the cheapest rooms, and anywhere at all if what you need is a bed near a dawn flight.

Choosing Your Area in Hulhumalé

The island is flat, gridded and walkable. What changes is how far you are from the sand.

The beach end

The eastern shore is where the public beach runs: a long strip of real sand with a marked swimming area, palms, and cafes along the road behind it. The guesthouses here are the ones that charge a little more, and the ones worth it, because you can be in the sea in two minutes.

Friday afternoons it fills with Maldivian families, which is the best thing about it rather than a drawback.

The central blocks

A few streets inland the island becomes residential — apartment buildings, small supermarkets, cafes serving short eats, and a great many guesthouses occupying a floor or two. Rooms here are the cheapest on the island and the beach is a five- or ten-minute walk.

There is nothing scenic about it. For a night before a flight, that is not the point.

The airport side

The western end, nearest the causeway and the terminal. Convenient to the point of being the only consideration: if your flight leaves at six in the morning or lands at midnight, a room here is ten minutes from the door.

It is the least interesting corner of the island, so take it for the night you need it and move for the nights you do not.

Know Before You Book

What makes Hulhumalé work, and where it does not.

This is the cheapest way to touch the Maldives

A guesthouse here costs a small fraction of a resort night, and you still get warm sea, pale sand and a reef not far off. For travellers who want a few extra days in the country without a second island’s worth of money, Hulhumalé is the answer, and plenty of people now build a trip around it.

What you do not get is a house reef at the end of your garden, or a sunset with nobody else in it. That is what the outer islands are for.

It is an inhabited island, with inhabited-island rules

Swimwear belongs on the marked part of the public beach; shoulders and knees covered elsewhere. No alcohol is sold on the island. The call to prayer sounds five times a day and shops shut briefly for it.

None of this is onerous and none of it is negotiable. It is the ordinary life of a Maldivian town, which is what you came to see.

Day trips are easy, and the price should be agreed first

Snorkelling trips, sandbank picnics, dolphin cruises and resort day passes are sold all over the island and leave from the harbour. The near atolls are a short speedboat ride, so a full day out is genuinely a day out rather than a journey.

Fix the price, the inclusions and the return time before you board. The good operators volunteer all three.

Hulhumalé Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room beside the airport.

Should you stay in Hulhumal&eacute; or in Mal&eacute;?

Hulhumalé, for almost everybody. It is ten minutes from the airport by road, it has a long public beach you can actually swim from, the streets are wide and quiet, and the rooms are generally newer and better value than in the capital. Malé is the more interesting place to look at and the better place to eat, but it has no beach and a good deal more noise. Many travellers stay in Hulhumalé and go into Malé for an afternoon.

How far is Hulhumal&eacute; from the airport?

About ten minutes by taxi. Velana International Airport sits on the island of Hulhulé, immediately next door, and the two are joined by a causeway, with the Sinamalé Bridge carrying on to Malé beyond. There is also a frequent public bus that costs a fraction of the taxi fare. It is the most convenient place in the country to sleep before an early flight or after a late arrival.

Is there a proper beach on Hulhumal&eacute;?

Yes. The public beach along the eastern side is a long stretch of real sand with a swimming area, and it is free. It is an inhabited island, so swimwear belongs in the marked bikini-beach section and modest dress applies everywhere else. It is a genuine beach rather than a token one, and on a Friday afternoon it is full of Maldivian families, which is part of the pleasure of it.

Can you do day trips from Hulhumal&eacute;?

Easily, and it is one of the better reasons to base yourself there. Snorkelling trips, sandbank picnics, dolphin cruises and day passes to nearby resorts are all sold from the island and run from the harbour, and the near atolls are a short speedboat ride. Agree the price, what is included and what time you will be back before you get on the boat.
