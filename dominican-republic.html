Why visit the Dominican Republic

Few Caribbean countries offer so many different holidays inside one short flight.

The beaches

Thirty-odd miles of continuous white sand at Punta Cana and Bávaro, the reef-sheltered calm of Playa Dominicus, and the empty Atlantic sweeps of Playa Cosón and Playa Rincón on the Samaná peninsula.

Five centuries of history

Santo Domingo’s Zona Colonial, founded in 1498 and UNESCO-listed since 1990, holds the first cathedral in the Americas, the first paved street and the palace of Christopher Columbus’s son.

The whales

From mid-January to the end of March, Samaná Bay is the principal breeding ground for the entire North Atlantic humpback population — properly regulated, and among the finest whale watching on earth.

Value, and ease

The widest choice of all-inclusive resorts anywhere in the Caribbean, short direct flights from most of the United States, and a country that still costs noticeably less than its neighbours.

How the country divides up

The east — Punta Cana

Most first trips land at Punta Cana (PUJ), and with reason: thirty miles of sand, the widest choice of all-inclusive and adults-only resorts in the Caribbean, and short direct flights. Understand the geography before booking — Bávaro for choice, Cap Cana for the calmest water, Uvero Alto for quiet, and Los Corales if you want to walk to dinner.

The south — Santo Domingo

A working capital of three million people, and the reason the country is more than a beach. The Zona Colonial is flat, barely a square kilometre and holds the first cathedral in the Americas, the oldest paved street and the Alcázar de Colón. Two nights here transforms a beach week into a proper trip.

The north — the Amber Coast

Greener, cheaper and more lived-in. Playa Dorada is a gated resort complex with flat private roads — the most genuinely walkable resort base in the country. Sosúa has a beautiful bay, Cabarete is a world kiteboarding capital. Remember that this coast’s wettest months are November to January, the opposite of the east.

The south-east and Samaná

Bayahíbe and Playa Dominicus face south behind reef and have the clearest, most dependably sargassum-free water in the country, with Saona Island offshore. North-east, the Samaná peninsula is jungle-backed and barely developed — and hosts the North Atlantic’s humpback whales each winter.

Top things to do in the Dominican Republic

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Walk the Zona Colonial

Santo Domingo’s UNESCO old town: the Catedral Primada, the Alcázar de Colón, Calle Las Damas and the Fortaleza Ozama, all within a flat square kilometre.

See the humpback whales

Samaná Bay, 15 January to 31 March. The principal breeding ground for the whole North Atlantic humpback population, with sighting rates over ninety-five per cent at peak.

Saona Island & the natural pool

A catamaran day from Bayahíbe into Cotubanamá National Park, with a stop at the waist-deep sandbar famous for its starfish. A long day, and a memorable one.

Altos de Chavón

A stone Mediterranean village above the Chavón River gorge with a five-thousand-seat amphitheatre that Frank Sinatra opened in 1982. Cobbles and steps — go early.

Los Haitises by boat

Mangrove channels, limestone haystack islets and caves painted with Taíno pictographs, reached only by boat — the gentlest great excursion in the country.

Merengue and bachata

Both are on UNESCO’s intangible heritage list, and both are everywhere. The Zona Colonial and Cabarete have the most welcoming live venues.

Golf at Casa de Campo

Three Pete Dye courses including Teeth of the Dog, seven holes of it played along the sea — among the finest golf in the Caribbean, with carts and caddies standard.

The 27 Waterfalls of Damajagua

Canyon pools you slide and jump back down, near Puerto Plata. Wonderful, and genuinely strenuous — the seven-fall option is the realistic one for most visitors.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

December to April is the dry season for Punta Cana, La Romana and the south-east, with March the driest month. This is peak season, so book ahead and expect Christmas and Easter prices.

The north coast and Samaná run the opposite way: the trade winds make November to January their wettest spell, and February to April their best. If you are heading to Puerto Plata in December because it is peak season elsewhere, think again.

April is the value sweet spot countrywide — dry-season weather at post-Easter prices. See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit the Dominican Republic.

Getting there & getting around

Punta Cana (PUJ) is the busiest gateway by a wide margin, with Santo Domingo (SDQ), Puerto Plata (POP), La Romana (LRM), Santiago (STI) and Samaná El Catey (AZS) serving their own regions. Match the airport to the coast; the country is bigger than it looks.

Everyone must file the free government E-Ticket online before flying — use only the official migracion.gob.do site, as look-alike sites charge for what costs nothing. No visa is needed for US, Canadian, UK or EU visitors.

We would not advise renting a car. Official guidance for visitors is to hire a professional driver, and pre-booked transfers plus day tours cost little more. Never drive at night, and avoid the motoconcho motorbike taxis. Tap water is not safe to drink — use bottled, including for brushing your teeth.

Dominican Republic travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit the Dominican Republic?

It depends which coast. For Punta Cana, La Romana and the south-east, the dry season runs December to April, with March the driest month. For the north coast and Samaná, the calendar is reversed — November to January is the wettest stretch there, and February to April is the sweet spot. April is the best value month countrywide: dry-season weather at post-Easter prices.

Do I need a visa to visit the Dominican Republic?

No. Citizens of the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and the EU do not need a visa for tourism and are normally admitted for thirty days, extendable for a fee. Everyone must complete the free government E-Ticket online before flying — it covers arrival and departure and produces a QR code for check-in. File it only at the official migracion.gob.do site; several look-alike sites charge for what is free. Carry a passport valid for at least six months.

What currency is used in the Dominican Republic?

The Dominican peso (DOP, written RD$), running at roughly 58 to 59 to the US dollar and about 41 to 42 to the Canadian dollar in mid-2026. US dollars are widely taken at resorts and tour desks but at a rate of the hotel’s choosing, so pay in pesos wherever a peso price is shown and keep small dollar bills for tips. Always decline a terminal’s offer to charge you in your home currency. Restaurant bills carry 18% tax plus a 10% service charge on top of the menu price.

Is the Dominican Republic safe for travellers?

The US State Department rates it Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution, the same level as France, Italy and the United Kingdom, with no ‘do not travel’ zones anywhere in the country. Resort areas have a good record. The bigger practical risk is the roads, not crime: the Dominican Republic has one of the highest road-fatality rates in the Americas, and official guidance is to hire a driver rather than drive yourself. Keep phones and jewellery out of sight on city streets and take a car rather than walk after dark.

Which coast should I choose for the clearest water?

Bayahíbe and Playa Dominicus, near La Romana. They face south behind sheltering reef and are the most dependably sargassum-free beaches in the country, along with Saona and Catalina islands. The north coast and Samaná are also largely spared. The east-facing beaches of Bávaro and Punta Cana take the heaviest seaweed landings, roughly June to August; within that area Cap Cana and Playa Juanillo stay noticeably cleaner.

How risky is hurricane season?

The Atlantic season runs 1 June to 30 November, concentrated from mid-August to mid-October and peaking around 10 September. Roughly six systems a year come within the country’s sphere and about half never reach land; a genuinely damaging landfall is a once-in-several-years event, not an annual one. You get three to five days of usable forecast, and airlines issue waivers well ahead. Book a flexible rate, buy insurance before a storm is named, and consider late June, early July or the second half of November for lower risk at lower prices.

Should I rent a car in the Dominican Republic?

For most visitors, no. The country has one of the highest road-fatality rates in the Americas, and the official recommendation for visitors is to hire a professional driver familiar with local roads. Pre-booked transfers plus day tours with a driver is the low-stress answer and rarely costs much more. Uber works well in Santo Domingo and Santiago, patchily in Puerto Plata, and cannot collect at Punta Cana airport at all. Avoid the motoconcho motorbike taxis entirely, and never drive at night.

How many days do you need in the Dominican Republic?

Seven to ten days suits most first trips. A classic pairing is two nights in Santo Domingo’s Zona Colonial followed by five or six on the coast — Punta Cana for choice, La Romana for clear water, Puerto Plata for a walkable resort complex. Add two or three days for Samaná if you are coming between mid-January and the end of March for the humpback whales, which is worth reshaping a trip around.
