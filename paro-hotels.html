Where to Stay in Paro — the Valley, the Town, the Airport

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Paro is where you land and, for most people, where the trip ends as well. The valley is broad, flat-bottomed and farmed, with the Rinpung Dzong above the river, the country’s main international airport on the valley floor, and the Taktsang monastery — the Tiger’s Nest — on the cliff at the top end, about two hours’ walk up from the car park. Where you sleep depends on what the night is for: a quiet valley hotel if Paro is the point, something near the town or the runway if it is a 5 a.m. departure. Paro airport is in the middle of the valley, not out on the edge of it, so ‘near the airport’ here means ten or fifteen minutes rather than an hour. These are the practical choices for a dawn departure or a late arrival.

Paro questions, answered

Should you stay in Paro or Thimphu on your first night?

Paro, if your flight lands in the afternoon and you are not in a hurry. You are already in the valley, the drive to Thimphu is about an hour and a half you do not have to do while jet-lagged, and Paro at around 2,200 metres is a gentler place to start than Thimphu a little higher up. Stay in Thimphu first only if your itinerary runs east immediately or you want the capital at the front of the trip. Nearly everyone ends up back in Paro for the last night anyway, because the international flights go early.

How close are the hotels to the Tiger's Nest?

Closer than the walk suggests. The Taktsang car park is at the top end of the valley, twenty minutes or so by road from most Paro hotels and less from the ones up the valley. The climb itself is the long part: roughly two hours up to the monastery on a steep forest path, with a cafeteria about halfway and a flight of steps down and up across the gorge at the end. Start early, because the valley clouds over in the afternoon and the light for photographs is gone.

Is it worth paying for a valley hotel rather than one near the town?

If Paro is a destination for you rather than a transit point, yes. The valley hotels are quieter, they look out over fields and forest instead of a street, and several are built in the Bhutanese manner, which is a large part of what people come for. If the night exists only to get you onto a 6 a.m. Drukair flight, it is money spent on a view you will leave in the dark.

Is Paro airport really as dramatic as people say?

The approach is genuinely tight - the runway sits on the valley floor with high ground on both sides, the aircraft turn between the ridges on the way in, and only a limited number of pilots are certified to fly it. It is a scheduled commercial operation flown every day, not a stunt. Take a window seat and, on a clear morning flying in from the west, look out for the high Himalaya on the northern side.

Do hotels in Paro include the Sustainable Development Fee?

No, and you should not expect them to. The fee is USD 100 per adult per night for most nationalities and it is paid to the Department of Tourism with your visa application, well before you arrive. Nu. 1,200 per person per night applies to Indian nationals. What you pay a hotel is the room, and on top of that sits the fee, your guide and your vehicle. Build the whole number before you compare hotel prices.
