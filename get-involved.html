Cozumel Travel & Adventure Guide

An island on the second-largest reef on earth — drift the walls of Palancar, ride the empty east coast, and find the Maya sanctuary in the middle of the jungle.

Find Cozumel Hotels Search Flights to CZM

Cozumel is the island that diving built. It lies twelve miles off the Riviera Maya, and along its sheltered western shore runs a stretch of the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef — the second-largest reef system in the world — with a steady current that lets divers simply descend and let the coral walls of Palancar and Santa Rosa drift past. Jacques Cousteau filmed here in the 1960s and made the island's name; it has been a pilgrimage for divers ever since. But Cozumel is bigger than its reputation. It is also one of the busiest cruise ports on the planet, a Maya sanctuary to the goddess Ixchel, and — on its wild, unprotected eastern side — a coast of empty windswept beaches where almost nobody builds anything. This guide covers where to stay, what to do, when to go, and how to book it.

Long before the divers and the cruise ships, Cozumel was sacred ground. The Maya knew it as Cuzamil, and it was a pilgrimage site dedicated to Ixchel, goddess of the moon, fertility and childbirth — women crossed from the mainland by canoe to visit her shrine. The ruins of that sanctuary still stand in the middle of the island at San Gervasio, half-swallowed by jungle and far less crowded than anything on the coast.

The island is roughly 30 miles long and 10 wide, and almost everything human sits on the calm western side facing the mainland: San Miguel, the one real town, plus the hotel strips running north and south of it. The eastern side takes the open Caribbean full in the face — the surf is rough, the swimming is often dangerous, and apart from a few beach bars there is essentially nothing there. That emptiness is one of the island's best surprises.

Renting a jeep or a scooter for a day is close to compulsory. A single road loops the island, and driving it — town, then the southern reefs, then the long empty run up the wild east coast — is the best few hours you can spend on Cozumel that don't involve a tank of air.

Why visit Cozumel

Four reasons divers keep coming back — and everyone else should too.

The reef

Palancar, Santa Rosa, Columbia and Punta Sur — walls, swim-throughs and coral gardens on the second-largest barrier reef in the world.

Drift diving

The current does the work. You descend, and the reef moves past you. It's the easiest hard diving in the hemisphere, and the reason Cousteau made the island famous.

An island you can loop

Rent a jeep, drive the ring road, and have the whole wild east coast almost to yourself an hour after leaving the cruise crowds.

The Maya sanctuary

San Gervasio, in the island's jungle interior, was a pilgrimage site for the goddess Ixchel — and it's quiet in a way the mainland ruins never are.

Top things to do in Cozumel

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Dive Palancar

The signature reef — coral towers, caverns and drop-offs on the southwest shore. Book a two-tank morning boat and go.

● Snorkel Chankanaab

A sheltered park lagoon with easy reef access straight off the shore — the best place on the island for first-timers and families.

● San Gervasio ruins

The Maya sanctuary to Ixchel, in the jungle at the island's heart. Small, atmospheric, and blessedly uncrowded.

● Punta Sur Eco Park

The island's southern tip — a lighthouse, a crocodile lagoon, and a long beach with almost nobody on it.

● Drive the east coast

Wild surf, rock arches and open sand. Stop at a beach bar, but heed the flags — the currents on this side are serious.

● Wander San Miguel

The waterfront plaza in the evening, once the ships have sailed, is when the town belongs to the islanders again.

Where to stay in Cozumel

Everything worth booking is on the calm western side. San Miguel, the town, is the budget-and-character choice: small hotels, real restaurants, the ferry pier, and dive shops on every block — you can walk to a boat. The southern hotel zone is where serious divers stay, because it is closest to Palancar and the great southern reefs; the resorts here tend to be dive-focused with their own piers. The northern hotel zone has the calmer, sandier swimming beaches and suits families and non-divers better. The east coast is glorious to visit and almost impossible to stay on — that's the point of it.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

December–April is the dry season and the peak — best weather, most cruise ships, highest prices. May and November are the value windows. June–November is hurricane season. Diving is good all year and the water clarity is famously excellent, though winter northerlies can occasionally close the boats for a day.

Getting there

Cozumel has its own airport, CZM, with direct flights from a number of US cities. The alternative is to fly into Cancún (CUN), drive an hour south to Playa del Carmen, and take the passenger ferry — about 45 minutes, running frequently through the day.

Cozumel travel FAQ

Why is Cozumel famous for diving?

Cozumel sits on the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the second-largest reef system in the world, and a steady current runs along its sheltered western shore. That current allows drift diving — you descend and let the reef move past you — over walls and coral formations like Palancar and Santa Rosa. Jacques Cousteau's filming here in the 1960s put the island on the map, and it has been a diving destination ever since.

How do you get to Cozumel?

Two ways. Cozumel has its own international airport (CZM) with direct flights from several US cities. Otherwise, fly into Cancún (CUN), drive about an hour south to Playa del Carmen, and take the passenger ferry — roughly 45 minutes, running frequently through the day.

Can you visit Cozumel as a day trip?

Yes — the ferry from Playa del Carmen makes it easy, and thousands of cruise passengers do exactly that. But divers, and anyone wanting the wild east coast or the San Gervasio ruins, should stay a night or two. The island is bigger and emptier than a day allows.

When is the best time to visit Cozumel?

December through April brings the driest weather and the best all-round conditions — and the busiest cruise season. May and November are quieter and good value. June to November is hurricane season. Diving is good year-round, with excellent water clarity.
