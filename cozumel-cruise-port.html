🗺️Harbor map — where everything is

Check which pier you are on — it matters. Punta Langosta puts you in town on foot. The International and Puerta Maya piers are a few kilometres south, so budget a short taxi (agree the fare first — taxis here are fixed-rate, not metered).

🚕Getting around — taxis, Uber & the truth

Taxis are the workhorse of Cozumel. They run on fixed government zone rates, not meters — so agree the fare before you get in, and carry small bills. It's a short, cheap ride from the cruise piers into downtown San Miguel or out to the beach clubs.

Uber technically operates on the island, but here's the honest part: ride‑share pickups right at the cruise piers are often blocked by the taxi union, and it can be more hassle than it's worth for a few‑hour visit. Walk out of the port gate first if you want to try it — but don't make it your only plan. For the mainland (Tulum, Playa del Carmen), the passenger ferry from downtown runs about 45 minutes each way — doable, but watch the clock.

⚓Stepping off the ship — the pier & the pop‑up markets

You'll walk off into a lively shopping village built for the ships — duty‑free jewelry and tequila, souvenir stalls, and the pop‑up vendors and market tables that appear whenever a ship is in. Expect friendly, persistent selling. At the fixed shops the price is the price; at the stalls and market tables, haggling is normal and expected — smile, offer about half, meet in the middle. There are ATMs at the pier (they dispense pesos and add fees).

🏖️What to do with 6–8 hours ashore

  • Beach clubs — Mr. Sancho's, Paradise Beach and Nachi Cocom sell day passes with pools, loungers, food and snorkeling gear. The easy crowd‑pleaser.
  • Snorkel or dive — Cozumel's reefs are among the best in the hemisphere; even first‑timers can snorkel straight off many beaches.
  • Chankanaab Park — a natural lagoon with snorkeling, gardens and sea‑life shows, close to the piers.
  • Downtown San Miguel — the waterfront Malecón, the main square, shops and proper tacos.
An easy, foolproof day: taxi to a beach club for the morning, head back to San Miguel for lunch and a wander, and be back aboard by mid‑afternoon. Save Tulum for a full land day another time — it's a long round‑trip for a port stop.
New to cruising? Three quick things for Cozumel: most ships dock (the downtown pier is walkable; the others are a short taxi to San Miguel); a passport is strongly recommended; and note your ship's all‑aboard time — go by ship time, which can differ from the island's.

👕What to wear & the weather

Hot and humid year‑round. The wetter months (roughly June–October) bring short, heavy afternoon downpours, so pack a light rain layer then. Beachwear with a cover‑up for town; the sun is intense even through clouds.

💵Money, ATMs & tipping

US dollars are widely accepted; carry small pesos for market stalls and taxis. ATMs at the piers dispense pesos (with fees). Tip ~10–15% at restaurants, round up taxis, and tip your dive or beach‑club staff.

📶Staying connected

Wifi in the pier shopping areas and most cafés. A Mexico eSIM or a Telcel SIM is cheap if you want all‑day data, but wifi usually covers a port stop just fine.

🛍️What to buy — and what to skip

Worth it: real Mexican vanilla, silver stamped .925, and good tequila or mezcal from a reputable shop. Skip: "black coral," pushy pharmacy pitches, and street silver with no stamp. Haggle at the market stalls — not the fixed stores.

⚠️Scams & hassles to dodge

Agree the taxi fare before you get in (no meters), be firm with timeshare/"free tour" touts at the pier, check silver for the .925 stamp, and book dives and tours through established shops.

🩹Health, water & sun

Drink bottled water, not tap. The big one for Cozumel: the marine parks require reef‑safe (biodegradable) sunscreen — regular sunscreen is banned to protect the reef, so bring the right kind. Mind the heat and hydrate.

♿Accessibility & families

The downtown pier and Malecón are walkable and mostly flat; beach clubs have restrooms and easy‑entry water. Family wins: gentle snorkeling straight off a beach club, Chankanaab's lagoon and sea‑life, or the waterpark at Playa Mia.

⭐Best excursions, ranked

  • Reef snorkel or beginner scuba — Cozumel's world‑class reefs are the whole point.
  • Beach‑club day pass — Mr. Sancho's or Paradise Beach; pools and snorkeling included.
  • Chankanaab Park — snorkeling, gardens and sea‑life close to the piers.

Only have 3 hours? Taxi to Chankanaab or the nearest beach club for a quick snorkel, or stroll San Miguel's waterfront and shops — both are 10–15 minutes from the piers.

🍴Where to eat — worth leaving the pier for

Skip the chains at the terminal. These are the tables Cozumeleños actually love:

  • El Moro — a family‑run local institution off the tourist track since the 1970s; the Yucatecan home cooking islanders swear by. (short taxi)
  • La Choza — downtown San Miguel's go‑to for authentic, generous Mexican — mole, cochinita pibil, tortillas made by hand.
  • Kinta — chef‑driven modern Mexican in a lush garden courtyard, for when you want something special.
  • Guido's — a beloved waterfront institution for wood‑fired fare and sangria, an easy walk from the downtown pier.

🎰Gaming, sports & a flutter

Straight talk: gambling is legal in Mexico, but Cozumel is not a casino island. You'll find only a small local gaming and sports‑book spot in San Miguel — not a resort‑casino scene. If a night at the tables is your thing, this isn't the port for it.

Sports: mainland Mexico is baseball‑ and soccer‑mad, but on Cozumel there's no pro game to catch in a few hours ashore. The island's real sport is what's under the water — the reef is Cozumel's world‑class card, so spend the day there.

🧭Ship excursion or do‑it‑yourself?

Booking through the cruise line costs more, but the ship waits for its own tours if they run late — peace of mind, zero logistics. DIY is cheaper and more flexible, but the risk of the clock is yours, so build in a buffer. Rule of thumb: close‑in beach or snorkel day → DIY is easy; anything far (Tulum) → take the ship's tour.

⏰Don't miss the ship

Go by SHIP time, not island time. Cozumel can sit in a different time zone than your ship — always set your watch and your plans to the ship's clock. Note your all‑aboard time (usually 30 minutes before sailing), set a phone alarm, and give yourself margin. The ship will leave without stragglers, and it's a long, expensive way to catch up.

🆘Emergency numbers & staying safe

In Mexico, dial 911 for police, fire or ambulance — nationwide, with English‑language help.
Nearest care: Costamed and Cozumel International Hospital, both in San Miguel.
The one cruisers forget — your ship's Port Agent: your cruise line lists a local port agent in each day's program/app. Write that number down before you step off. If you're delayed and might miss the ship, call the port agent right away — they coordinate with the ship.
Also save: your cruise line's 24/7 emergency line (in your boarding documents), and keep a phone photo of your passport plus your ship and stateroom details.

🏨Stay the night before — or after — you sail

Cozumel makes a good add-on for divers and for anyone flying into Cancun the day before. San Miguel keeps you walkable to the Punta Langosta pier.

❓Good to know — quick answers

Is Cozumel safe for cruise passengers?
Yes. Cozumel is one of the Caribbean's safest cruise stops. Use normal traveler common sense and you'll be fine.

Do I need pesos in Cozumel, or are US dollars okay?
US dollars are widely accepted. A little cash in pesos helps at market stalls and for small taxis.

How far is the beach from the Cozumel cruise port?
The beach clubs are about a 10 to 15 minute taxi ride from the cruise piers.

Is there Uber in Cozumel?
Technically yes, but pickups at the cruise piers are often restricted. Taxis with fixed zone fares, agreed before you get in, are the easier bet.

Do I need a passport to get off in Cozumel?
On most closed‑loop cruises you can sail with a birth certificate and photo ID, but a passport is strongly recommended. Check your cruise line's rules.
