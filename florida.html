A short history

Florida's peninsula was home to the Calusa, Timucua and other Indigenous nations for thousands of years before Spanish explorer Juan Ponce de León landed in 1513, naming it for the Easter season. St. Augustine, founded in 1565, is the oldest continuously occupied European-established city in the continental United States, and Florida passed between Spanish, British and briefly Confederate control before becoming a U.S. territory in 1821.

Florida achieved statehood in 1845 as the 27th state, but it remained sparsely populated and swampy until railroad and land-boom development in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, led by figures like Henry Flagler, opened the peninsula to settlement and tourism. Cuban immigration reshaped Miami through the 20th century, giving the city its distinctive Latin American character.

Today Florida's economy runs on tourism, citrus, aerospace at Cape Canaveral, and a booming retirement and winter-sun population. Its landmarks span Walt Disney World and Universal Orlando, the Everglades' river of grass, Miami's art-deco South Beach, and the Florida Keys' overseas highway, making it the most visited U.S. state by Canadians seeking winter sun.

When to go

November–April is peak snowbird season; summers are hot, humid and stormy.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

peak snowbird season with warm, dry, comfortable days across the state; book accommodations early.

Spring (Mar–May)

Warm and increasingly humid, with spring break crowds in March; a good shoulder-season value window. March is prime spring-break season, with students flocking to Panama City Beach, Daytona Beach, Fort Lauderdale and Miami's South Beach.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Hot, very humid and prone to daily thunderstorms and hurricane risk; theme parks stay busy despite the heat.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Hurricane season lingers into November, but late autumn brings drier air and returning winter-sun crowds.
