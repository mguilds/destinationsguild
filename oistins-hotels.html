Oistins

Where to Stay in Oistins

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Oistins is a fishing town in the parish of Christ Church, on the breezy south coast, and it is the home of the best evening in Barbados: the Friday night fish fry at the Bay Garden, where half the island eats grilled fish at trestle tables by the water. Most visitors to this coast actually sleep a mile or two either side of the town, along the strip that runs from Hastings through Worthing and Dover to St Lawrence Gap and on past Oistins to Enterprise. It is the liveliest part of the island, the best value, and the closest to the airport — and the trade wind that makes it so pleasant also puts a shore break on the beaches that the west coast does not have.

Where to base yourself in Oistins

One breezy coast, four distinct places on it, and the best value on the island at every one of them.

St Lawrence Gap

The south coast’s restaurant and nightlife strip: a loop of road off the main highway with the sea on one side and most of the island’s bars and eating places along it. Walkable, cheerful and noisy at the right end, quieter at the other. The one part of Barbados where you can leave the hotel after dinner and find something still happening.

Dover and Worthing

Either side of the Gap, with the two best swimming beaches on this coast. Dover Beach has calm water inside a reef and a boardwalk along it; Worthing is shallower and gentler still, and popular with Bajan families at the weekend. Good hotels, sensible prices, and a five-minute walk to dinner.

Oistins and Enterprise

The town itself is a working fishing port rather than a resort: the fish market, the Bay Garden and a bus terminus. Just east of it, Enterprise — often called Miami Beach — is a wide, pale, breezy beach with a cliff of sea grapes behind it and very little development. Stay here for the fish fry on your doorstep and the quietest sand on the coast.

Hastings and Rockley, towards Bridgetown

The western end of the strip, blurring into the capital. A flat paved boardwalk runs along the sea for over a mile, there are shops and restaurants, and the bus into Bridgetown takes ten minutes. The practical choice if you want the town and the coast at once.

What your money is worth in Barbados
US $1 = 2.00 Bds$
Bds$ 1 = US 50¢
The Barbados dollar is pegged to the US dollar at two to one, so halving the local price gives you the dollar price. US dollars are accepted almost everywhere and change comes back in Barbados dollars. Hotel bills normally carry a nightly government room levy and a service charge on top of the room rate, and restaurants usually add service too — ask what is included when you book rather than at check-out. Keep some cash for buses, rum shops and the fish fry.

Oistins in your later years

Dover and Worthing have the gentlest water on this coast

Both sit behind reef and are shallow and calm, unlike the open south-coast beaches where the trade wind puts a shore break on the sand. If you want the south coast’s restaurants and the west coast’s easy swimming, these two beaches are the compromise.

The Hastings boardwalk is the best flat walk in Barbados

Over a mile of paved, level path beside the sea between Hastings and Rockley, with benches and shade in places. It is used by everybody, at every hour, and it is the reason we send unhurried walkers to this end of the coast.

The fish fry is easier than it sounds

Oistins Bay Garden is flat, open ground with trestle tables and plenty of seating, and the food comes to the table. Go at about seven rather than at nine, when it is fullest and loudest, and ask the taxi to collect you from the same spot it dropped you.

Ask which side the road is on

The main south coast highway runs close behind many of these hotels. A room on the sea side is markedly quieter, and in the Gap itself the music carries until late at the western end. Both are worth naming when you book.

Nearby, and the rest of the island

Bridgetown is twenty-five minutes west along the coast road — our Bridgetown and Garrison guide covers staying there — and Holetown and the Platinum Coast are about an hour north. Harrison’s Cave and the east coast at Bathsheba make the best inland day from this side of the island. The island guide is here, the weather month by month here.

Oistins hotels — questions

Should I stay in Oistins or on the west coast?

It depends on what your evenings look like. The south coast around Oistins and St Lawrence Gap has the restaurants, the bars, the fish fry, the best value and the airport fifteen minutes away, and a breeze with some surf in it. The west coast has flat, clear water, grander hotels, and quiet nights at a much higher price. Families and independent travellers tend to prefer the south; honeymooners and swimmers the west.

When is the Oistins fish fry?

There is cooking at the Bay Garden most evenings, but Friday night is the one everybody means: it is the busiest, has the most stalls and the most music, and runs from the early evening until late. Saturday is a good second. Nothing needs booking, you pay at the stall, and the fish is grilled in front of you.

Can you swim on the south coast?

Yes, and it is more lively water than the west coast. Dover and Worthing sit behind reef and are calm and shallow; the open beaches, including Enterprise, have a real shore break that children love and unsteady swimmers should treat with respect. It is nothing like the east coast, where swimming is genuinely dangerous.

How far is the airport?

About ten to fifteen minutes by taxi from most of this coast — Grantley Adams is in the same parish, just inland of Oistins. It is the shortest airport transfer on the island, which is worth something on a late arrival or an early departure. You may hear aircraft on the approach at some hotels nearer the airport.

Is St Lawrence Gap noisy?

At the western end, yes, several nights a week, and that is exactly why people stay there. The eastern end of the Gap, Dover and Enterprise are all much quieter. If you want the restaurants without the music, book towards Dover and walk the five minutes.

Is this a good area without a car?

One of the best in the Caribbean. Buses run along the south coast constantly on a flat fare, the restaurants are within walking distance of most hotels, and taxis are everywhere. Hire a car for a day if you want to see the east coast and the interior, and leave it alone otherwise.
