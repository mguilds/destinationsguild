Orlando Travel & Adventure Guide

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Three hours from Toronto, and the entire argument against February. The most-visited theme-park city on earth — and the one place half of Canada has already been.

Find Orlando Hotels Search Flights to MCO

There is no point being coy about Orlando: it is a city built to separate visitors from their money, and it is extraordinarily good at it. It is also, for a Canadian family, one of the great value propositions in travel — three hours of flying and you have gone from a salted driveway to a warm afternoon, and everybody from the four-year-old to the grandparents has something to do. Walt Disney World is not a park but four of them, plus water parks, spread across an area larger than some cities. Universal Orlando has two parks and a third, Epic Universe, its most ambitious yet. Add SeaWorld, the outlet malls, and the Kennedy Space Center an hour east on the coast, and you have a fortnight without repeating yourself. This guide is written from the Canadian side: what it costs once the exchange rate lands, when to go, where to stay, and the small things that catch us out.

Orlando is inland, which surprises people who assume Florida means beach: the nearest coast is about an hour away in either direction — Cocoa Beach and Cape Canaveral to the east, Clearwater and the Gulf to the west. The city itself sprawls, and the tourist geography is really three clusters: Walt Disney World and Lake Buena Vista to the southwest, Universal and International Drive in the middle, and Kissimmee to the south, which is where the value hotels and the rental villas are.

The great convenience for us is the clock: Orlando is on Eastern time, the same as Toronto, Montréal and Ottawa. No jet lag, no time-change argument with the children, and you can phone home at a civilised hour.

What it really costs a Canadian

Everything you read about Orlando is priced in US dollars — then the exchange rate happens, and then Florida sales tax is added at the register, because American prices on the shelf are pre-tax. Budget in Canadian dollars from the start and you'll avoid a nasty arithmetic moment at the end of the week.

Multi-day park tickets are where the real money goes — far more than the flights. Buy multi-day rather than single-day passes; the per-day cost drops sharply the longer you go. And when a card terminal offers to charge you in Canadian dollars, decline — that convenience is nearly always a worse rate than your own bank's.

Travel medical insurance is not optional. Your provincial plan covers a small fraction of American medical costs, and a Florida hospital visit can cost more than the entire holiday. Buy it for every trip, declare pre-existing conditions, and read the exclusions.

Why visit Orlando

Four reasons it stays at the top of the Canadian list, year after year.

It's the closest warm

Under three hours from Toronto, non-stop from Montréal, Ottawa, Halifax and Winnipeg. No jet lag — same time zone as home.

Nobody is bored

The rare destination that genuinely works for a four-year-old, a fifteen-year-old and a grandparent in the same week.

More than the parks

Kennedy Space Center, two coastlines within an hour, the outlet malls, and some seriously good eating on the edges of town.

Competition keeps flights honest

Air Canada, WestJet, Porter, Flair, Sunwing and the US carriers all fight over this route. Watch for seat sales.

Top things to do in Orlando

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Walt Disney World

Four parks — Magic Kingdom, EPCOT, Hollywood Studios, Animal Kingdom — plus water parks. You cannot do it in a weekend; don't try.

● Universal Orlando

Universal Studios and Islands of Adventure, plus Epic Universe, its newest and most ambitious park. The Harry Potter areas remain the best-built things in Orlando.

● Kennedy Space Center

An hour east on the coast. A Saturn V hanging over your head and a genuine shuttle — the best non-park day in Florida, and worth it even for teenagers.

● SeaWorld & the smaller parks

Fewer crowds and shorter lines than the giants, and often a much cheaper day.

● The outlet malls

Orlando's outlets are a Canadian institution. Just remember the CBSA limits on the way home — under 24 hours, there's no exemption at all.

● Escape to a coast

Cocoa Beach east, Clearwater west — both about an hour. A day of actual ocean is the best possible antidote to a week of queues.

Where to stay in Orlando

Three areas, three different holidays. On Disney property costs the most but buys convenience: the transport, the early-entry perks, and the ability to send an exhausted child back to the room at two in the afternoon — which, with small children, is worth real money. Universal's hotels do the same trick, and the closest ones include skip-the-line privileges that can save you hours. International Drive is the middle ground: hotels of every price, walkable restaurants, and a short drive to everything. Kissimmee is the value play, and it's where Canadian families with more than two children usually end up — rental villas with a kitchen and a pool cost less than two hotel rooms, and a week of breakfasts made at home is a real saving. Wherever you land, check for resort fees and parking charges: US hotels routinely add them on top of the advertised rate.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

January–March is the best weather and, inevitably, the biggest crowds and highest prices — and March break is the single most expensive, most crowded week of the Canadian year. Late April–May and September–early November are far better value with shorter lines, though hurricane season runs roughly June to November. Summer is hot, humid, and interrupted by a thunderstorm most afternoons.

Getting there

Orlando International (MCO) takes non-stops from Toronto, Montréal, Ottawa, Halifax, Winnipeg and more. Several Canadian airports offer US preclearance — you clear American customs before boarding and arrive as a domestic passenger. You will want a rental car; get the toll transponder from the rental desk, because unpaid Florida tolls turn into expensive admin fees.

Orlando travel FAQ

When is the best time for Canadians to visit Orlando?

January to March is the sweet spot on weather — warm, dry and comfortable, which is exactly why it's also the busiest and most expensive stretch. March break is the single most crowded and costly week of the Canadian year. Late April to mid-May and September to early November are far better value, though hurricane season runs roughly June to November. Summer is hot, humid and thunderstorm-prone.

Do you need a rental car in Orlando?

Almost always yes. The city is spread out and the parks, hotels and outlets are far apart. If you're staying on Disney property and never leaving it, you can manage without. Otherwise rent — and take the toll transponder from the rental company, because unpaid tolls become expensive administrative fees.

How much do park tickets really cost a Canadian family?

Budget in Canadian dollars, not US. Multi-day tickets are priced in USD and vary by date and park, and once the exchange rate lands, a family of four can easily spend well over a thousand Canadian dollars on tickets alone — before a single hotel night. Buy multi-day rather than single-day passes; the per-day cost drops sharply.

Do Canadians need travel insurance for Orlando?

Yes — treat it as essential. Your provincial plan covers only a small fraction of American medical costs, and a hospital visit in Florida can cost more than the whole holiday. Buy travel medical insurance, declare any pre-existing conditions, and read the exclusions before you fly.
