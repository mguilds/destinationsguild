Echternach

Where to Stay in Echternach

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Echternach is the oldest town in Luxembourg and the way into its strangest landscape. It grew around the Benedictine abbey that Saint Willibrord founded here at the end of the seventh century, and the basilica, the abbey buildings and the arcaded old town hall still set the shape of the place. Behind it rises the Mullerthal — Little Switzerland — a plateau of sandstone cut into ravines, overhangs and squeezes between rock walls, with waterfalls, moss and staircases bolted to the stone. The Sauer runs along one side of town and Germany begins on the far bank. Most people come to walk, and the town is organised around that.

The abbey square and the old town

Most centralWalk to everythingWhere the hotels are

The centre is a handful of streets between the basilica, the abbey park and the Place du Marché with its arcaded Denzelt, and nearly all of the town’s hotel stock is in or immediately around it. Everything a visitor needs — restaurants, the bus station for the valley routes, the tourist office, the trailheads — is within a few minutes on foot. For a walking base this is the obvious choice and there is little reason to look further.

The lake and the sports quarter

QuieterGood for familiesShort walk in

A little south-west of the centre the town has an artificial lake with a path right round it, a watersports centre and the municipal sports grounds, and a scatter of accommodation around them. It is flat, green and quiet, it suits families and anybody who would rather not be on the square in the evening, and the walk into the old town takes a quarter of an hour on the level. It is also where several of the Mullerthal trail sections conveniently start.

The Sauer riverfront and the German bridge

On the waterOver the border in a minuteThrough traffic

The river side of town, where the bridge crosses into Echternacherbrück in Germany and the riverside promenade runs along the Luxembourg bank. It is pleasant and convenient, with the German shops and the Trier road immediately over the water, and the only drawback is that the through route to the bridge carries the town’s traffic. Remember that the free Luxembourg transport covers this bank rather than the other one.

Out in the ravines: Berdorf, Beaufort and the Mullerthal hamlets

The villages scattered across the plateau behind Echternach — Berdorf among the rock formations, Beaufort below its two castles, the Mullerthal hamlet itself near the Schiessentümpel waterfall — have country hotels, inns and holiday houses, and staying in one puts you on the trail from the front door. The Guild lists no separate booking page for them: our partner’s inventory out here is thin and patchy, and a search would send you somewhere you did not choose. Book these direct with the house, or stay in Echternach and use the free buses, which reach all of them.

Getting around Echternach

On foot

The old town is small and level and you will not need anything else inside it. The walking that brings people here starts at the edge of town: the Mullerthal Trail and its branches are waymarked from the centre, and several good loops begin and end on the square.

Buses

Echternach has no railway. It is served instead by frequent regional buses, chiefly to Luxembourg City and along the valley, and — like everything else inside the country — they are free. The bus station is in the middle of town, and the valley routes are how you reach Berdorf, Beaufort and the trailheads without a car.

Into Germany

The bridge over the Sauer puts you in Germany in a minute on foot, and Trier, with its Roman gates and baths, is well under an hour away by road. This is an internal Schengen border with no checks of any kind. Luxembourg’s free transport applies on the Luxembourg side, so check the fare for the German leg before you set off.

Driving

A car is genuinely useful here, more so than anywhere else in the country: the ravine trailheads are scattered, the buses run to a timetable and a wet afternoon changes the plan. Driving is on the right. Parking in Echternach itself is straightforward outside the summer weekends, when the car parks at the popular trailheads fill by mid-morning.

When to come, and what it costs

Echternach runs on the walking season rather than the business week. From April to October the town is busy and fully open, with July and August the peak and the trail car parks full by ten on a fine weekend. The Guild would come in May, late September or early October, when the beech woods are at their best at one end or the other and the paths are quieter. One date dominates everything: the dancing procession on Whit Tuesday, which moves with Easter and falls in May or early June, fills the town completely and needs booking months ahead. Winter is very quiet, some restaurants take a break and the rock is slick, but the ravines in mist are a particular pleasure if you are properly shod.

Getting in: what changed, and what has not

Europe’s Entry/Exit System is now in force. Since 10 April 2026 it has operated at every Schengen border crossing and passport stamps have been discontinued. On your first crossing you will have your fingerprints taken and your photograph made at the border, which adds a few minutes; after that the check is quicker. There is nothing to apply for and nothing to pay.

ETIAS - the travel authorisation you may have read about, expected to cost around EUR 20 and last three years - is not in force yet. No applications are being taken, and the European Union has not announced the exact start date; it has said it will give several months’ notice before the system goes live. It has been postponed repeatedly and no start date is confirmed, so treat it as coming rather than cancelled, and ignore any website offering to sell you one today - nobody can issue one yet. When it does begin there will be six months in which you can travel without one, then six months in which only first-time arrivals are exempt.

What this means for your trip right now: you do not need ETIAS. You do need a passport issued within the last ten years and valid for at least three months beyond the date you plan to leave.

Luxembourg is in the Schengen area from its beginning, so the land borders with Belgium, Germany and France carry no checks at all and an arrival from outside Schengen is a single check at Findel airport. There is no separate permission of any kind to visit the Grand Duchy.

Echternach — questions

Is Echternach a good base for the Mullerthal?

It is the obvious one. The town sits at the edge of the sandstone country with waymarked trails starting from the centre, it has most of the area’s hotel beds and restaurants, and the free regional buses fan out from here to Berdorf, Beaufort and the valley hamlets. Staying out in a village is lovely if you have a car and a booking made direct; otherwise stay in town.

How hard is the walking?

Easier than the photographs suggest, but not flat. The classic sections of the Mullerthal Trail involve staircases, ladders in one or two places, narrow squeezes between rock walls and a good deal of up and down, all on sandstone that is slippery in the wet and often damp even when it has not rained. Boots with real grip matter more than fitness. There are also easy level riverside and lakeside walks for a gentler day.

Can I get here without a car?

Yes. There is no railway, but frequent buses run from Luxembourg City and they are free like all transport inside the country, so a day trip or a transfer costs nothing but the time. Once you are here, the valley buses reach the main trailheads. A car saves waiting and lets you follow the weather, which on a short visit is worth something.

What is the hopping procession?

A dancing procession held on Whit Tuesday, in which thousands of participants move through the town to the basilica of Saint Willibrord in a stepping rhythm to a single repeated tune. It goes back centuries, it is a religious event rather than a show, and UNESCO lists it as intangible cultural heritage. The date moves with Easter. If you want to see it, book very early; if you do not, avoid that week.

Is Trier worth a day from here?

Very much so, and it is one of the best reasons to stay in Echternach rather than the capital. Trier is the oldest city in Germany, with a Roman gate, baths, an amphitheatre and a cathedral, and it is under an hour away over the bridge. There are no border formalities. Check what your transport ticket needs to cover on the German side, because Luxembourg’s free travel stops at the river.
