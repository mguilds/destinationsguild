Where to Stay in Boston

Where to Stay in Boston — Back Bay, Beacon Hill, the Waterfront & Fenway

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Boston is a walking city with a subway underneath it. The National Park Service’s Freedom Trail runs 2.5 miles through downtown and Charlestown, and most of what a first-time visitor wants — Boston Common, the Public Garden, Faneuil Hall, the North End — sits within a few T stops of each other. Choosing the right neighbourhood matters more than choosing the hotel. Back Bay is elegant and central, Beacon Hill and the Common put the Freedom Trail at your door, the waterfront gives you the harbour, and Fenway is for ballgames, concerts and college visits. This page sorts the areas and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in Boston

Four bases, each suited to a different trip.

Back Bay and Copley Square

Meet Boston, the city’s official visitor bureau, describes Back Bay as centred on Copley Square, home to Trinity Church and the Boston Public Library. Newbury Street’s shops run through it, and the Public Garden sits at its eastern end.

Stay here for a first visit if you like handsome streets, good shopping and an easy walk to almost everything. It suits couples and families alike; expect city-centre prices.

Beacon Hill, Boston Common and the Theater District

Meet Boston calls Beacon Hill a half-square-mile neighbourhood of Federal-era architecture, topped by the gold dome of the State House, and home to the Museum of African American History. Below it lies Boston Common, the start of the Freedom Trail, with the Theater District on its southern side.

Stay here for history on foot and for evening shows. It is the most central base for the Freedom Trail, Faneuil Hall and the North End.

The Waterfront and Seaport

The harbour edge, from Long Wharf and Rowes Wharf round to the Seaport District, which Meet Boston calls the city’s fastest-growing and newest neighbourhood. The National Park Service runs a ferry from Long Wharf, near the New England Aquarium, to the Charlestown Navy Yard, home of USS Constitution.

Stay here for harbour views, seafood and a short ride to the airport. Good for a special-occasion stay.

Fenway and Kenmore

Meet Boston describes Fenway/Kenmore as best known for Fenway Park and as one of the city’s academic and cultural hubs, with Boston University next door. It is a few stops west of Back Bay on the Green Line.

Stay here for a Red Sox game, a concert, or a college visit. Hotels here are often a little more relaxed than in the centre.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Boston details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Boston

The airport is Boston Logan International. The MBTA, Boston’s transit agency, says the Silver Line SL1 bus stops at every Logan terminal and runs to South Station downtown, and that service from the airport is always free. A free Massport shuttle also links every terminal to Airport station on the Blue Line (read 24 November 2026). By rail, the National Park Service notes Boston’s three train stations: South Station, North Station and Back Bay Station.

Getting around

The National Park Service recommends the “T” — the MBTA subway — as the easiest way to reach its downtown sites, and warns there is no parking at or around Faneuil Hall. Many visitors do not need a car at all. If you bring one, ask your hotel about parking before you book.

When to go

Boston works all year. In summer the National Park Service hosts Town Meeting programmes in Faneuil Hall, and it is ballgame season at Fenway. Autumn is a favourite for walking the Freedom Trail. Winter is cold, but the museums, theatres and restaurants carry on. We have not printed monthly temperatures because we did not read an official climate source for this page.

Boston Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in Boston?

Back Bay, around Copley Square and Newbury Street, for a first visit and easy walking. Beacon Hill and the streets around Boston Common for the Freedom Trail and the theatres. The waterfront for harbour views. Fenway and Kenmore for a ballgame, a concert or a college visit.

Do I need a car in Boston?

Usually not. The National Park Service recommends the MBTA subway, called the T, for reaching its downtown sites, and says there is no parking at or around Faneuil Hall. If you bring a car, ask your hotel about parking before you book.

How do I get from Logan Airport to my hotel?

The MBTA says the Silver Line SL1 bus stops at every Logan terminal and runs to South Station downtown, and that service from the airport is free. A free Massport shuttle also runs from every terminal to Airport station on the Blue Line.

Where should I stay for a Red Sox game at Fenway Park?

In Fenway or Kenmore Square. Hotel Commonwealth and The Verb Hotel, both listed above, are close to the ballpark by their own accounts.

How long is the Freedom Trail?

The National Park Service gives it as 2.5 miles, winding through downtown Boston and Charlestown. Hotels around Boston Common and Beacon Hill put its start within a short walk.
