Why go

The most recognized memorial in America, and still the most moving.

The Lincoln Memorial stands at the west end of the National Mall, looking down the length of the Reflecting Pool to the Washington Monument and the Capitol beyond. Inside, the great seated figure of Abraham Lincoln gazes out over the city he held together through the Civil War.

It is free, it never closes, and it is one of the few places in Washington that looks better at night than by day. Few visitors leave without reading the Gettysburg Address carved into the wall, and few read it without feeling it.

Since June 2026 there is more to see: a new museum in the undercroft, the vast space beneath the memorial that had been hidden from the public since 1922.

What to see at the Lincoln Memorial

Six things not to miss.

The heart of the memorial

The statue of Lincoln

Designed by Daniel Chester French and carved in Georgia white marble by the Piccirilli brothers, the seated Lincoln is about 19 feet tall. Notice the hands, one clenched and one open.

South wall

The Gettysburg Address

Lincoln's 272 words, carved in full. Stand and read it slowly. It takes about two minutes and stays with you far longer.

North wall

The Second Inaugural Address

"With malice toward none, with charity for all." Delivered six weeks before his death, with a mural by Jules Guerin above it.

The architecture

The 36 columns

Architect Henry Bacon modeled the memorial on a Greek temple. Its 36 columns stand for the 36 states of the Union when Lincoln died.

On the steps

"I Have a Dream"

An inscription on the steps marks the spot where Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. stood to deliver his speech on August 28, 1963. Look for it as you climb.

The view

The Reflecting Pool

Turn around at the top of the steps for the classic view down the Reflecting Pool to the Washington Monument and the Capitol, best of all at sunrise.

The Lincoln Memorial undercroft museum

New in 2026: a museum beneath the memorial.

For a century, the space beneath the Lincoln Memorial was closed to the public. In June 2026 the National Park Service opened it as a 15,000-square-foot museum. You walk among the forest of concrete columns that hold the memorial up, with interactive exhibits on how it was built and on its place in American history and the civil rights movement. Hands-on exhibits carry braille labels, and the undercroft has accessible restrooms.

Tickets: entry is by free timed ticket, and everyone two years and older needs one.

Advance tickets can be reserved up to 30 days ahead at Recreation.gov or by calling (877) 444-6777. New tickets are released at 11 a.m. daily, a $1.00 service fee applies per reservation, and each reservation covers up to six people.

Same-day tickets are free, first come, first served, from the kiosk at the Korean War Veterans Memorial, starting at 8:45 a.m. daily. One person aged 16 or older may collect up to six.

History on the steps

The memorial was dedicated on May 30, 1922, Memorial Day, with Lincoln's only surviving son, Robert Todd Lincoln, in attendance.

Its steps have since become America's stage. On Easter Sunday 1939, after she was barred from performing at Constitution Hall, the contralto Marian Anderson sang here to a crowd of some 75,000 people. On August 28, 1963, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. delivered his "I Have a Dream" speech from these steps to the March on Washington.

The memorial is also on the back of the U.S. five-dollar bill, and it appeared on the penny for more than four decades.

Best time to visit the Lincoln Memorial

Sunrise

The quietest hour, with the sun rising over the Capitol at the far end of the Reflecting Pool.

After dark

Floodlit and far less crowded. Many visitors think it is at its most beautiful at night.

Spring & autumn

The most comfortable weather. Late March and early April bring cherry blossoms, and crowds.

Summer

Hot, humid and busy at midday. Come early or late, and bring water.

How to get to the Lincoln Memorial

By Metro

The nearest station is Foggy Bottom-GWU (Blue, Orange and Silver lines), about 0.8 miles away on foot. Smithsonian station is about 1.2 miles away, and makes a fine walk along the Mall past the Washington Monument and the World War II Memorial.

By car

Parking is limited. There is metered street parking along Ohio Drive SW, between the Lincoln and Jefferson Memorials, and in lots south of the Jefferson Memorial. Most spaces have a two-hour limit, and ticketing and towing are enforced.

Lincoln Memorial accessibility

Elevator. There are about 87 steps from the Reflecting Pool up to the chamber, but you do not have to climb them. An elevator to the left of the main steps goes up to the chamber, and the undercroft museum is fully accessible.

Parking. Seven accessible street parking spaces are on Daniel Chester French Drive, just south of the memorial, with a two-hour limit.

For visitors who are blind or have low vision. A touchable bronze model with audio description stands at the information panel on the south side of Memorial Circle Plaza, and the park brochure is available in braille.

ASL interpretation is available free on request, with at least three weeks' notice.

Lincoln Memorial FAQ

Is the Lincoln Memorial free?

Yes. The Lincoln Memorial is free to visit. The undercroft museum beneath it is also free, but needs a timed ticket, and a $1.00 service fee applies to each online reservation.

What are the Lincoln Memorial's hours?

The Lincoln Memorial is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Many visitors find it at its best early in the morning or after dark.

Do I need tickets for the Lincoln Memorial undercroft?

Yes. Everyone aged two and older needs a free timed ticket. Reserve up to 30 days ahead at Recreation.gov, or collect same-day tickets from the kiosk at the Korean War Veterans Memorial from 8:45 a.m.

What is the closest Metro station to the Lincoln Memorial?

Foggy Bottom-GWU, on the Blue, Orange and Silver lines, about 0.8 miles away. Smithsonian station is about 1.2 miles away.

Is there an elevator at the Lincoln Memorial?

Yes. An elevator to the left of the main steps takes visitors up to the memorial chamber, and the undercroft museum is fully accessible.

Where did Martin Luther King Jr. give his "I Have a Dream" speech?

On the steps of the Lincoln Memorial, on August 28, 1963. An inscription on the steps marks the spot where he stood.

What is written on the walls of the Lincoln Memorial?

The Gettysburg Address is carved on the south wall and Lincoln's Second Inaugural Address on the north wall.
