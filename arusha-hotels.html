Arusha — Where the Northern Circuit Begins

Where to Stay in Arusha — Area by Area

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Nearly every safari in northern Tanzania starts and ends in Arusha, and nearly every visitor books it as an afterthought — one night before, one night after, wherever the operator put them. It is worth five minutes’ thought, because the town centre and the coffee-farm lodges in the hills are completely different experiences, and the airport you are using decides which one is sensible.

Arusha sits at about 1,400 metres under Mount Meru, which makes the evenings cool and the first morning a little more tiring than you expected. It is a staging post and it is honest about that: the days belong to the parks.

The town centre — for the briefing, the bank and the last shop

Arusha’s centre is small enough to cross on foot and it is where the safari companies keep their offices, so if your trip begins with a morning briefing and a kit check this is the convenient place to be. It is a working East African town rather than a pretty one, with the market, the bus stands and the clock tower within a few streets, and rooms at every price.

Along the Moshi road — the lodge and coffee-farm belt

East of town the road to Moshi runs through coffee estates in the foothills of Mount Meru, and this is where most of the garden lodges and converted farmhouses are. You get cooler nights, birds in the morning and a view of Meru when the cloud lifts, in exchange for a taxi into town. For a first and last night either side of the northern circuit, we would choose this over the centre almost every time.

Usa River — closer to the airport, further from the noise

Twenty-odd kilometres out, Usa River is a cluster of lodges in large grounds roughly halfway between Arusha and Kilimanjaro International Airport. It makes most sense if you are flying in late or out early and the safari vehicle is collecting you from the door anyway. It makes least sense if you wanted to walk to dinner.

Njiro and the southern suburbs — quieter, residential

South of the centre, Njiro and the streets around it are residential, with guesthouses and serviced apartments rather than hotels. This is the part of Arusha for anyone staying a week or more, working here, or simply wanting a quiet street and a kitchen.

Before you book

Government travel insurance is compulsory

Every visitor to mainland Tanzania and to Zanzibar, residents aside, must hold inbound travel insurance provided by the Tanzanian government — and you must hold it even if you already have a policy of your own from home. You buy it online beforehand or at your port of entry, it covers the length of your stay up to 92 days, and you only buy it for your first port of entry. Arriving in Zanzibar first means buying from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation; arriving on the mainland first means the National Insurance Corporation. You may be refused entry without it. Source: UK Foreign Office entry requirements for Tanzania, still current at 22 November 2026.

Get the e-visa before you fly

You need a tourist or business visa, and you apply for an e-visa online before you travel — it is no longer possible to get one from the Tanzanian High Commission in London. A single-entry visa is available on arrival at the main ports of entry if you meet the requirements, and you may be asked to show your return or onward ticket; a multiple-entry visa cannot be had on arrival. Your passport needs an expiry date at least six months after you arrive and at least one blank page.

Health screening at the border

Tanzania’s Ministry of Health announced heightened screening on 17 October for travellers arriving from high-risk regions and affected countries, at every point of entry, and every traveller has to complete a Travellers Health Surveillance Form before departure or on arrival. You also pass through mpox screening — body temperature checks — at all points of entry. Check the current list of affected countries before you fly.

Your phone, and what you photograph

This one catches visitors out. It is illegal in Tanzania to take or share photographs or video of protests and violence, and the security services may ask to inspect your phone and other devices. The police have said it is an offence to hold or share images that “cause panic or degrade a person’s dignity”. Keep away from anything that could be read as political, online as much as in public. Source: UK Foreign Office safety and security advice for Tanzania, updated 30 October 2026.

Demonstrations, and a change in the mood

The general election of 29 October 2025 was followed by protests and a security response that caused deaths and injuries in many parts of mainland Tanzania, and further demonstrations are possible at any time. Official advice also notes an increase in anti-foreigner rhetoric, and that the authorities have limited internet access during and after protests before now. Write down your airline, insurer and embassy numbers rather than keeping them only online, and leave any crowd you come across.

Malaria, and where the hospitals are

Malaria is a risk across Tanzania, and dengue, cholera and mpox are all named in official health advice. Get travel health advice at least eight weeks before you fly and ask about your whole itinerary rather than one city. Medical facilities are limited, particularly outside Dar es Salaam, so insurance that covers medical evacuation is not a luxury here. The emergency number is 112.

Questions readers actually ask

Where should I stay in Arusha before a safari?


If your safari company is collecting you from the door, stay in the lodge belt along the Moshi road east of town: cooler nights, gardens, and a view of Mount Meru. If your trip starts with an office briefing or you still have kit to buy, stay in the centre, where you can walk to the safari offices, the bank and the market. Usa River suits a late arrival or an early departure from Kilimanjaro airport.

Which airport do I use for Arusha?


Kilimanjaro International Airport is the one most long-haul visitors use, about an hour east of Arusha and the airport that also serves Moshi. Arusha has its own small airport much closer to town, which is where the light aircraft to the Serengeti and the other northern parks leave from. Many northern-circuit trips use both: the international flight into Kilimanjaro, and the bush flight out of Arusha.

How long do I need in Arusha itself?


A night at each end is normal and usually enough. Arusha is a staging town rather than a destination, and the days are better spent in the parks. If you want one day in the area, the options are the coffee farms and the Meru foothills, a walk at Arusha National Park under the mountain, or the cultural tourism programmes in the villages nearby.

Is Arusha affected by the Foreign Office advice against travel?


No. The only part of Tanzania that advice against all but essential travel covers is the strip within 20km of the border with the Cabo Delgado province of Mozambique, in the far south-east; there is separate advice about armed robberies in the Kigoma region on the Burundi border in the far west. Arusha is a long way from both. The general cautions about armed crime, demonstrations and photographing protests apply here as they do everywhere in the country.

Do I pay park fees separately?


Usually yes, and they are not small. Entry to the national parks is set by Tanzania National Parks, and the Ngorongoro Conservation Area is charged separately by the Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority. Both are revised from time to time, so we do not print a figure here that would be wrong by the time you read it. Ask your operator in writing whether park and conservation fees are inside the quoted price or on top of it, and confirm the current rate with the authority before you budget.
