When to come to the Emirates

Best Time to Visit the United Arab Emirates

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Few countries the Guild writes about have so stark a difference between their good season and their bad one. For roughly five months of the year the Emirates are close to ideal — dry, warm, every beach and dune and mountain road usable, the evenings made for eating outside. For roughly four they are among the hottest inhabited places on earth, and a holiday becomes a sequence of short walks between air-conditioned rooms. The money follows the thermometer exactly, so the choice is a genuine trade rather than a matter of taste. Here is what each month is actually like, and what Ramadan does to all of it.

The short answer

Our pick: November, or March

The shoulders of the good season, and the best balance of the year. Warm, dry days in the high twenties and low thirties Celsius, evenings you want to be outside in, the sea swimmable, the desert and the mountains comfortable, and rates below the winter peak. March edges it for sea temperature; November edges it for the chance of a quiet week before the Christmas rush.

For the best weather: December to February

This is as pleasant as the Emirates get — mid-twenties by day, genuinely cool after dark, and the only time of year a long walk or a mountain hike is a good idea at any hour. It is also the peak: the highest room rates of the year, booked-out beach resorts and a Christmas-to-New-Year scramble. Bring a jacket for the evenings and for the desert, which surprises people.

For the lowest prices: July and August

Rates fall further here than almost anywhere, and if your holiday is a pool, a mall, an aquarium and a good dinner, the summer is a defensible bargain. Understand what you are buying: forties by day, humidity that makes it feel hotter, a sea like a warm bath, and no realistic outdoor sightseeing.

Avoid, if you can choose: June to September

The four months we would steer a first-time visitor away from, and July and August most of all. Nothing is closed and nothing is dangerous if you are sensible, but you will not see the country — you will see the inside of it. Anyone with a heart or respiratory condition should treat these months as a real health matter rather than a comfort one.

Month by month

What each month is actually like for a visitor, rather than what a climate table says.

January

The coolest month and one of the loveliest. Days in the mid-twenties Celsius, nights that can drop to the low teens and colder in the desert and the mountains. Everything outdoors is at its best: dune camps, Jebel Jais, the corniches, the souks at midday. The sea is brisk at around twenty-two degrees, and many hotels heat their pools. Peak rates continue through the first week, then ease. Pack a jumper for the evenings — visitors routinely do not and regret it.

February

Arguably the best weather of the year, and priced accordingly. Warm, dry, clear and comfortable from morning to night. This is the month for the long desert drive, the mountain walk and the outdoor dinner. It is also busy with European and Gulf visitors, and school half-terms push the beach hotels to capacity. Book early or accept the rate.

March

Our pick, with November. The weather holds and the sea warms to the point where swimming is a pleasure rather than a dare. Late in the month the first properly hot afternoons arrive, which is the signal that the season is closing. Prices begin to slide from the winter peak. If you want one month that does everything the country offers, this is it.

April

The turn. Early April is still comfortable and good value as the winter crowds leave; by the end of it the thirties are routine and the middle of the day is to be planned around rather than used. The desert is still manageable in the early morning. A decent compromise month for a cheaper trip that still gets you outdoors, provided you treat midday as indoor time.

May

Hot, and the humidity begins to build on the coast. Dawn and the hour before sunset remain usable and the beaches are quiet; everything in between is hard work. Rates are low and falling. The best of the cheap months for anyone who still wants to see something, and the last one in which a dune trip is anything other than an endurance exercise.

June

Summer proper. Forties inland, high thirties with heavy humidity on the coast, and the shamal winds can throw dust across the Gulf for days. Outdoor attractions cut their hours or close. The malls, the indoor ski slope, the aquariums and the hotel pools are the holiday now. Prices are low. School holidays begin towards the end of the month, which fills the family resorts despite the weather.

July

The hottest month, with August. Daily highs well into the forties, nights that stay close to thirty, and a sea too warm to cool you down. Dubai Summer Surprises and the shopping promotions are built around exactly this, and the city is busy indoors. The cheapest rooms of the year. Not a month to come for the desert, the mountains or the architecture.

August

As hot as July and more humid on the coast, which makes it the least comfortable month of all. It is also, oddly, a sociable one: Gulf families travel and the indoor attractions are full. Rates stay at their floor. If you are coming anyway, book a hotel whose pool is chilled and whose beach has shade, and do not plan a single outdoor excursion for the middle of the day.

September

The heat begins to break, slowly and only at the end. The first half is still full summer; by the last week the evenings are bearable and the dune trips start running properly again. Humidity is the lingering problem. Prices begin to climb from the summer floor. A gamble — a late-September trip can feel like October or like August depending on the year.

October

Recovery. Daytime temperatures fall through the thirties, the sea is warm and clear, the outdoor restaurants reopen their terraces and the events calendar restarts. Mornings and evenings are genuinely pleasant and only the middle of the day still bites. Rates are reasonable before the winter season sets in. An underrated month, especially the second half.

November

Our pick, with March. The good season arrives in earnest: high twenties by day, warm sea, cool evenings, and everything — desert, mountains, old quarters, beaches — usable at any hour. The Abu Dhabi Grand Prix falls now and pushes capital-city hotel rates sharply for its weekend, so check the date if you are not coming for it. Otherwise the best combination of weather and price in the year.

December

Lovely weather and the most expensive weeks of the year. The first two or three weeks are superb value for how good the conditions are; from roughly the twentieth the beach resorts move to holiday pricing that can be several times the summer rate, and New Year’s Eve, with its fireworks over the Burj Khalifa, is the single busiest night in the country. Evenings are cool and the desert at night is cold.

By emirate

The seasons are broadly national, but the country is not uniform and the differences matter more than their size suggests.

Dubai, Sharjah and the northern coast

The Gulf coast pattern described above, with humidity as the defining summer problem. Dubai’s indoor offer is the deepest in the country, which is why it carries the summer better than anywhere else. Where to stay in Dubai and where to stay in Sharjah set out the districts.

Abu Dhabi and the interior

Marginally hotter than Dubai by day and a little less humid on the island itself. Inland at Al Ain and the Liwa oases the air is drier and the summer heat is fiercer but more bearable in the shade, and winter nights fall close to single figures. See where to stay in Abu Dhabi.

Ras Al Khaimah and the mountains

The one place in the country with real altitude. Jebel Jais is several degrees cooler than the coast year-round and can approach freezing on a winter night, which makes it the local escape in summer and a genuinely brisk outing in January. See where to stay in Ras Al Khaimah.

Fujairah and the east coast

A different sea. The Gulf of Oman is deeper, cooler and clearer than the Arabian Gulf, so the water is more refreshing in summer and the diving is better in every month. The mountains behind can funnel cloud and the occasional heavier shower in winter. See where to stay in Fujairah.

Ramadan and holidays

Ramadan is the one calendar matter that will change your trip more than the weather, and because the Islamic calendar is lunar it moves about eleven days earlier each year — so look up the dates for your year rather than assuming. During the month, eating, drinking and smoking in public during daylight hours is restricted and that applies to visitors as well as residents; hotel restaurants generally serve guests as usual, often discreetly, but many cafés and food courts shut until sunset. Licensed bars curtail service, live music largely stops, and government and many private offices work shorter hours, which shifts attraction opening times too. The compensation is real: after sunset the country comes out to eat, and the iftar and late-evening suhoor spreads that hotels put on are among the best meals of the year.

Eid al-Fitr, at the end of Ramadan, and Eid al-Adha about ten weeks later are the two big public holidays, each of several days. Domestic tourism peaks then, hotels in Fujairah and Ras Al Khaimah fill with residents escaping the cities, and rates rise accordingly. National Day on 2 December, marking the federation of 1971, is a national holiday with fireworks and heavy traffic, and it sits right at the start of the best weather — worth knowing if you are booking that week. Fixed non-moving dates aside, Islamic holidays are confirmed by moon sighting and announced only days in advance, so treat any date you read for them as approximate until the official announcement.

When to visit the UAE — questions

What is the single best month to visit the UAE?

If you can pick one, take March, or November. Both sit at the edges of the good season: warm enough to swim and to sit outside in the evening, cool enough to walk around a souk at midday without suffering, and either side of the December-to-February peak when the beach resorts charge the most and fill up. February is arguably the most pleasant weather of the year, but it is also the busiest and dearest. If the desert or the mountains are the point of the trip rather than the pool, the cooler months of December to February are worth the premium.

How bad is the summer, really?

Worse than most first-time visitors expect. From June to September daytime temperatures sit in the forties Celsius, which is above a hundred Fahrenheit, and on the Gulf coast humidity is high enough that the apparent temperature is higher still and your glasses fog when you step outside. Nights offer little relief. The sea reaches bath temperature and stops being refreshing. The country is built for this — everything is air-conditioned and the malls, aquariums, museums and indoor ski slope all do their best business then — but any plan that involves being outdoors for more than a few minutes at a time does not survive. Room rates are at their lowest, sometimes dramatically so, and for a family who intend to move between a pool and a mall that is a real offer.

When is it cheapest?

July, August and the first half of September, by a wide margin, and that is a straight trade against the heat. There is a second, smaller dip in late April and May, when the weather is hot but still usable early and late in the day, and this is the best value in the year for anyone who wants to actually see the country. The most expensive weeks are Christmas and New Year, when the beach hotels charge several times their summer rate, with a second peak around the Formula 1 weekend in Abu Dhabi and the big Dubai shopping and sporting events.

Should I avoid Ramadan?

Not necessarily, but go knowing what changes. Ramadan moves through the calendar by about eleven days a year, so check the dates for your year. During it, eating, drinking and smoking in public during daylight hours is restricted for everyone including visitors; many restaurants close until sunset or serve behind screens, though hotel restaurants generally continue to feed guests. Live music and bars are curtailed, working hours shorten, and attraction times change. In exchange the evenings are extraordinary — the iftar meal after sunset is the social event of the year and hotels do elaborate versions of it — and the cities are quieter by day. Eid al-Fitr at the end is a public holiday when domestic travel peaks and hotels fill.

Does it ever rain?

Rarely, and when it does it makes the news. Average annual rainfall is low and almost all of it falls between December and March, usually as a handful of short, heavy downpours rather than steady rain. Because the ground and the drainage are not built for volume, a serious storm can flood underpasses and snarl the motorways for a day; the April 2024 storms were the recent example and were exceptional. Pack for sun and bring one light layer for air conditioning and for cool desert evenings, not a raincoat.

When is the sea warm enough to swim?

Almost all year, which is the point of a winter trip here. The Gulf is at its most pleasant from October to May, running from the low twenties Celsius in the depth of winter — brisk but entirely swimmable, and many hotels heat their pools then — up to the high twenties in spring. By July and August it passes thirty-two degrees and feels like a warm bath, so hotels chill their pools instead. The Gulf of Oman side at Fujairah runs a little cooler and clearer, which is why the diving is better there.
