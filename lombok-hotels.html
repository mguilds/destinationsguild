Where to Stay in Lombok

Where to Stay in Lombok — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Lombok is Bali’s quieter neighbour — greener, far less built up, and dominated by the volcanic bulk of Mount Rinjani. It divides into three moods. Senggigi and neighbouring Mangsit, on the west coast, are the island’s old-guard resort strip: calm bays, sunset views back towards Bali, established hotels with gardens and spas, and the easiest choice for travellers who want comfort without effort. Kuta Lombok and the government-developed Mandalika zone in the south are newer and more dramatic, with wide white-sand bays and headland views, though the roads and restaurants are more spread out. Offshore to the north-west lie the three car-free Gili Islands — Trawangan the liveliest, Meno the sleepiest, Air in between — reached by fast boat, where transport is by bicycle or pony cart and the snorkelling begins a few steps from the shore. One practical note: the boat crossings to the Gilis are lovely in calm months and uncomfortable in rough ones, and most boats require a step down into surf. Luxury

Lombok’s luxury is a quiet, low-rise affair — a beach estate on the north-west coast, and the most polished modern resort in the south.

$$$$ LuxuryMedana BayPrivate beachWalled villas

A beachfront estate of pavilions and walled villas on quiet Medana Bay, on the island’s north-west coast about an hour from the airport. Best for couples and unhurried travellers who want genuine five-star polish and do not mind being well away from restaurants and shops.

All-inclusive & Resort

A word of honesty: true all-inclusive resorts effectively do not exist on Lombok. One property below operates on a genuine full-board basis; the other is a full-service resort with optional meal plans, and we would rather say so than blur the two.

Jeeva Beloam Beach Camp

$$$ UpscaleBeloam BayFull boardRemote

Fifteen or so timber beach bungalows inside a fifty-five-hectare forest reserve on the far south-east coast, with two private white-sand beaches and no neighbours for miles. Rates are quoted as a full-board package covering meals and soft drinks, which is as close to all-inclusive as Lombok gets; in exchange, electricity and internet run only at certain hours. For travellers who genuinely want to disconnect and do not mind a long transfer and rustic comforts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of Lombok should I stay in?

Choose Senggigi or Mangsit on the west coast for calm bays, established resorts and the easiest arrival from the airport or the Bali ferry. Choose Kuta Lombok or Mandalika in the south for dramatic white-sand bays and newer resorts, accepting longer drives between things. Choose a Gili island if you want no cars, no traffic and snorkelling from the beach — and are comfortable with the boat crossing.

How do I get to Lombok?

Lombok International Airport (LOP) in the south has direct flights from Jakarta, Surabaya, Bali and Kuala Lumpur; from Bali the hop takes under half an hour. Fast boats also run from Bali’s Padang Bai and Serangan to the Gili Islands and Bangsal, taking around two hours in decent weather. The airport is roughly 20 minutes from Kuta Lombok and about 90 minutes from Senggigi.

Are the Gili Islands suitable for older travellers?

Gili Air and Gili Meno are gentle, flat and very quiet, and can be delightful. The honest cautions are the crossing — fast boats bounce, and boarding often means a step down into shallow surf — and the fact that there are no cars, so luggage travels by pony cart and everything else is on foot or by bicycle. Ask your hotel to arrange the transfer end to end, and choose a calm-season month.

Is Lombok safe after the 2018 earthquake?

Yes. The island was badly shaken in 2018, but rebuilding is long finished and the hotels listed here are all trading and reviewed by recent guests. The everyday cautions are the ordinary ones: strong currents on the south coast, uneven roads, and choosing established boat operators in fair weather.

When is the best time to visit Lombok?

The dry season, roughly May to September, brings the calmest seas and the easiest boat crossings; January and February are the wettest and roughest, when some Gili crossings are cancelled. See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Indonesia.
