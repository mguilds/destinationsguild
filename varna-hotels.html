Varna

Where to Stay in Varna

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Varna is the sea capital of Bulgaria and a real city rather than a resort, which is what makes it a better base than anywhere on the coast built for holidays. It has the largest Roman bath complex in the country sitting in the middle of town, a cathedral with silver domes, a long nineteenth-century grid of handsome streets, and the Archaeological Museum, which holds the Varna necropolis gold — the oldest worked gold yet found anywhere, from graves dug in the fifth millennium BC. Along the front runs the Sea Garden, a kilometre and a half of mature park above the beach, with a terrace walk, an aquarium and an observatory in it. Then the resorts begin: St Constantine and Helena immediately north, and Golden Sands about seventeen kilometres up the coast. The city works all year, which the resorts do not.

The parts of Varna and its coast a visitor chooses between — starting with whether you want a city or a beach holiday, because here you can have either.

The city centre and the Sea Garden

Most centralOpen all yearWalk to the beach

The grid between the cathedral and the sea: the pedestrianised Knyaz Boris I with its shops and cafes, the Roman baths, the Archaeological Museum, the opera house, and the Sea Garden running along the top of the beach at the bottom of it. Most of the city’s year-round hotels are here. It is flat, it is a ten-minute walk from the main square to the sand, and it has the restaurants, museums and transport that the resorts lack. This is the right choice for a first visit, for anything outside July and August, and for anyone who wants a city with a beach attached rather than the other way round.

The Greek quarter and the streets behind the cathedral

QuietCharacterfulShort walk in

The older low-rise streets inland of the cathedral, once the Greek merchants’ district, with small houses, courtyards, local bakeries and a scattering of small hotels and apartments. It is five to ten minutes on foot from the centre and markedly quieter, and prices are lower than on the front. A good choice for a longer stay or for anyone who would rather sleep away from the summer noise of the Sea Garden terraces.

St Constantine and Helena

SpaWoodedQuieter resort

The oldest resort on the Bulgarian coast, immediately north of the city and effectively a wooded suburb of it: mineral springs, a monastery in the middle, large spa hotels among old trees, and small beaches between the rocks. It is calmer and greener than Golden Sands, it is a short bus or taxi ride from central Varna, and it suits a spa week or a family that wants the sea without the volume. Many hotels here are seasonal, so check before booking outside the summer.

Golden Sands

Big beachPackage resortSeasonal

Seventeen kilometres north of Varna: a long, genuinely excellent sand beach backed by a wall of large hotels in a nature park, with a strip of restaurants, bars and water sports and the Aladzha rock monastery in the woods behind. It is a proper package resort — busy and loud in July and August, considerably cheaper and quieter in June and September, and substantially closed from October to May. Buses run into Varna through the day in season. Choose it for the beach; do not choose it expecting a town.

The port and station end

Best valueTransportWorking district

South-west of the centre around the railway station and the commercial port. This is the cheapest part of the city and it is convenient for an early train or the bus station, with the centre a ten to fifteen-minute walk away. It is a working district with the harbour machinery at the end of the street and not much to look at. Sensible for a night around a journey, or on a tight budget in high summer when the front is expensive.

Getting around Varna

On foot and by bus

Central Varna is flat and compact, and the walk from the cathedral through the pedestrian street and down into the Sea Garden is most of what a visitor needs. Beyond that, city buses and trolleybuses run frequently along the coast road to St Constantine and Helena and on to Golden Sands, and inland to the suburbs. Buy a ticket on board or pay contactless where it is fitted.

Taxis

Plentiful and cheap, with the same caution as the rest of Bulgaria: the per-kilometre rate is displayed on the rear side window, so look at it before you get in, or use an app. A taxi from the centre to Golden Sands is a reasonable fare shared between two or three people and much faster than the bus in the evening.

To and from the airport

Varna Airport is about eight kilometres west of the centre and carries heavy charter traffic from across Europe through the summer, with a thinner year-round schedule. A city bus runs into town and taxis wait outside; most package visitors are met by a transfer coach that runs straight to the resorts.

Along the coast

North from Golden Sands the road continues to Albena and Balchik, where the Romanian queen’s palace and its botanical garden sit above the sea, and on to Kaliakra, a red cliff headland with a fortress on it. South of Varna the coast runs down past Byala and Obzor towards Nessebar and Burgas, about two hours away. A car is the comfortable way to do any of this; buses connect the main towns.

Day trips inland

The Madara Rider, the eighth-century horseman carved into a cliff and on the UNESCO list, is about an hour and a half west, usually combined with the medieval capitals of Pliska and Preslav and the rock-cut monastery there. Closer in, the Stone Forest at Pobiti Kamani is a field of natural stone columns twenty minutes from the city, and the Aladzha rock monastery is in the woods above Golden Sands.

When to come

Varna has two calendars. As a beach destination it runs from the middle of June to the end of September, with August the warmest and busiest and the first half of September the best value of the year — the sea stays warm and the crowds have gone. As a city it works all year: the museums, the Roman baths, the opera and the restaurants are open in February, the Sea Garden is a fine winter walk, and prices are at their lowest.

What does not work out of season is the resort strip. A great many hotels at Golden Sands and a good number at St Constantine and Helena close from October to May, along with most of the restaurants and bars along the beach. If you are coming between October and May, stay in the city and treat the coast as somewhere to walk rather than somewhere to stay.

Getting in: what applies, and what does not

Bulgaria is in the European Union and, since 1 January 2025, a full member of the Schengen area — air and sea borders opened in March 2024 and the land borders followed on New Year’s Day 2025. Arriving from another Schengen country is now an internal journey with no routine passport check, which is a real change from older guidebooks. Bulgaria is also in the euro area: it adopted the euro on 1 January 2026 at a rate fixed at 1.95583 lev to the euro, and the lev stopped being legal tender on 1 February 2026, so prices here are in euro and there is no currency to change.

Europe’s Entry/Exit System is now in force. Since 10 April 2026 it has operated at every Schengen external border crossing and passport stamps have been discontinued. On your first crossing you will have your fingerprints taken and your photograph made at the border, which adds a few minutes; after that the check is quicker. There is nothing to apply for and nothing to pay.

ETIAS - the travel authorisation you may have read about, expected to cost around EUR 20 and last three years - is not in force yet. No applications are being taken, and the European Union has not announced the exact start date; it has said it will give several months’ notice before the system goes live. It has been postponed repeatedly and no start date is confirmed, so treat it as coming rather than cancelled, and ignore any website offering to sell you one today - nobody can issue one yet. When it does begin there will be six months in which you can travel without one, then six months in which only first-time arrivals are exempt.

What this means for your trip right now: you do not need ETIAS. You do need a passport issued within the last ten years and valid for at least three months beyond the date you plan to leave. Ninety days in any one hundred and eighty is the visa-free tourist allowance for American, Canadian, British and Australian visitors, and it is counted across the whole Schengen area rather than against Bulgaria alone.

Varna — questions

Should I stay in Varna or at Golden Sands?

Varna if you want a city with a beach: museums, restaurants, the Roman baths, the Sea Garden, transport and everything open all year, with the sand a ten-minute walk from the main square. Golden Sands if the beach is the holiday and you want a big resort with water sports and a strip of bars, which is excellent in July and August and largely shut from October to May. St Constantine and Helena sits between the two: wooded, spa-ish and quieter.

Is Varna worth visiting outside the summer?

Yes, which is what sets it apart from the resorts. It is a working city of three hundred thousand people with the country’s best archaeological museum, the largest Roman baths in Bulgaria, an opera house and a long seafront park, and all of it is open in winter at low-season prices. You will not be swimming, and the resort strip north of town will be closed, but the city itself is unaffected.

What is the Varna gold?

A hoard from the Varna necropolis, excavated in the 1970s, from graves dug in the fifth millennium BC — and it is the oldest worked gold yet found anywhere in the world. It is in the Varna Archaeological Museum, in a couple of rooms of an old-fashioned building, which somehow makes the age of it land harder than a grand display would. It is the single most remarkable thing on the Bulgarian coast and it takes an hour.

How far is Nessebar from Varna?

About an hour and a half to two hours south by road, which makes it a long but perfectly manageable day trip; it is much closer to Burgas, so if the old towns of the southern coast are your main interest, base yourself there instead. Buses run along the coast in season. Nessebar itself is a walled old town on a rock peninsula and is on the UNESCO World Heritage list.

Is the beach in Varna itself any good?

Yes, and it is genuinely convenient — a wide strip of sand running the length of the Sea Garden, with a terrace walk, bars and the aquarium above it. It is a city beach, so it is busy in season and it is not the clean sweep of sand that Golden Sands offers, but you can be swimming fifteen minutes after leaving a city-centre hotel, which no resort can match in the other direction.

Do I need cash in Varna?

Not much. Bulgaria has used the euro since 1 January 2026, cards work almost everywhere a visitor goes, and contactless is normal in the city. Keep some cash for a beach bar, a market stall or a bus ticket. Any price quoted in lev online is from before the changeover.
