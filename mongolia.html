Mongolia Travel Guide

Researched and compiled by the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Gorkhi-Terelj National Park. Photo: Wikimedia Commons.

Mongolia is the emptiest country on earth that still has a capital city in it — a little over three million people spread across a land the size of western Europe, most of them in Ulaanbaatar and most of the rest moving with their animals. It is not a country you see from a hotel. You see it from a van on a dirt track, and then from a felt tent with a stove in the middle of it. This guide covers the capital, where our booking partner actually holds rooms, and is straight with you about the places where it holds none.

The regions of Mongolia

Five pieces of country, and they are a long way apart. Where our partner lists rooms we link them; where it lists none we say so plainly and leave the booking to the operator who drives you there.

Ulaanbaatar & the Tuul valleyPART I

The capital in its ring of hills, the Gandan monastery, and the granite country an hour east at Gorkhi-Terelj. Trip.com files the Terelj lodges under Ulaanbaatar, because Terelj sits inside the capital's own Nalaikh district — so that is where those links land.

The Orkhon valley & KharkhorinPART II

Erdene Zuu, the walled monastery raised beside the ruins of Karakorum, the imperial capital of the Mongol empire, in a grass valley that has been the centre of the country for eight hundred years. About a long day's drive west of Ulaanbaatar. Our booking partner lists no rooms at Kharkhorin, so this one is booked through the operator who drives you there.

The GobiPART III

Not sand for the most part but gravel, grass and red rock — the Flaming Cliffs where the first dinosaur eggs were found, the Yolyn Am gorge that holds ice into the summer, and the dunes at Khongoryn Els. Reached by a short flight to Dalanzadgad or two hard days by road. Our booking partner lists no rooms in the Gobi: the ger camps are sold through tour operators, not through hotel sites.

Khuvsgul & the northPART IV

Larch forest, reindeer herders and Khuvsgul lake up against the Siberian border — the coldest, wettest, greenest part of the country, and the one serious fishermen come for. Our booking partner lists no rooms at Khatgal or on the lake; the camps are booked through operators.

The Altai & the westPART V

Bayan-Olgii, where most people are Kazakh rather than Mongol, the mountains carry glaciers, and a few families still hunt with golden eagles. The furthest and the least visited part of the country, and another where our partner lists nothing — it is operator country.

Before you book

Visas — read this one carefully

The Immigration Agency of Mongolia says the government has exempted nationals of 34 countries from the tourist visa requirement for stays of up to 30 days during 2026. Its published list names the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland, Monaco, Liechtenstein and most of the European Union. It does not name the United States or Canada, and neither appears on the agency's separate 95-country e-visa list. We read both lists on 17 November 2026 and we are not going to invent a figure for North American passports: check immigration.gov.mn or your nearest Mongolian consulate before you book anything. The same agency has published a warning that forged Mongolian visa permits are in circulation and that travellers should use only its official site and its own information line.

Getting there and getting about

The international gateway is Chinggis Khaan International Airport, south of Ulaanbaatar; from North America the usual routes connect through an Asian hub such as Seoul or Tokyo. The Trans-Mongolian railway runs through the capital between Beijing and Russia and is a fine way to arrive if you have the days. Inside the country, the distances are the whole problem: there are domestic flights to the provincial centres, and otherwise you are in a Russian van on tracks rather than roads, at an average speed that will surprise you. Budget days, not hours.

When to go

June to early September, and it is not a close call: that is when the grass is green, the camps are open and the tracks are passable. Ulaanbaatar averages a daily high near 76°F (24°C) in July and near 10°F (−12°C) in January, and the countryside runs colder than the city in every month. The month-by-month figures, the shoulder seasons and what a winter trip actually involves are in the best time to visit Mongolia.

Money

The currency is the tugrik (MNT). Cards work in Ulaanbaatar hotels, restaurants and supermarkets; outside the capital, assume they do not. Draw cash in the city before you leave it, keep small notes for roadside stops, and expect camps and drivers to want cash.

Fishing here

Mongolia holds the taimen, the largest member of the salmon and trout family on earth, in the cold rivers of the north — it is strictly catch and release, and it is the reason a certain kind of angler comes here and nowhere else. We have written the trip up in full in our Mongolia fly fishing guide, and the rest of the shelf is in The Guild Fishing Library.

Mongolia questions, answered

Do you need a visa for Mongolia?

It depends entirely on your passport, and the honest answer in November 2026 is that we will not print a number for North American readers. The Immigration Agency of Mongolia publishes a list of 34 countries whose nationals are exempt from the tourist visa requirement for stays of up to 30 days during 2026. That list names the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland and most of the European Union. It does not name the United States or Canada, and neither country appears on the same agency's 95-country e-visa list either, which means American and Canadian passports are handled under a separate arrangement that the agency's own English pages do not set out. So check immigration.gov.mn or your nearest Mongolian consulate before you book a flight, and do not trust a figure you read on a travel blog. The agency also warns in writing that forged Mongolian visa permits are being sold: use only its own site and its own hotline.

When is the best time to go to Mongolia?

June to early September, and the short answer is that this is not a close call. Ulaanbaatar averages a daily high around 76F in July and around 10F in January, and the countryside is colder and far more exposed than the city. Summer is also when the grass is green, the camps are open, the Naadam festival is held and the roads are passable. May and late September are real shoulder months with thinner crowds and sharper nights. Winter travel is a specialist trip rather than a cheaper version of the summer one, and should be booked with an operator who runs it regularly.

How many days do you need in Mongolia?

Ten days to two weeks, and more of it in the countryside than in the capital. Ulaanbaatar is worth two nights at the start and one at the end; everything that makes the country extraordinary is several hours' drive beyond it. Gorkhi-Terelj can be done as an overnight from the city. The Gobi, the Orkhon valley and Khuvsgul each need their own block of days because the distances are long and most of the driving is not on sealed road. A week spent trying to reach all three is a week spent in a van.

Can you book Mongolian ger camps through Trip.com?

Mostly not, and we would rather say so than send you to an empty page. Our booking partner holds a good spread of hotels in Ulaanbaatar, and a handful of lodges out at Gorkhi-Terelj which it files under Ulaanbaatar because they sit inside the capital's own Nalaikh district. For Kharkhorin, Dalanzadgad and the Gobi, Khatgal and Khuvsgul, and Olgii in the far west, we could find no bookable inventory at all. Those places are normally booked as part of a tour, through the operator who is driving you there, and that is how we have described them on this page rather than pointing a booking button at nothing.

What is a ger, and will you sleep in one?

A ger is the round felt-and-lattice tent that Mongolian herding families live in; yurt is the Russian word for the same thing. Almost every countryside camp houses guests in gers, usually with beds, a stove and a shared shower block rather than the bare floor of a working family's home. It is warmer than it looks and the stove is the whole trick: someone lights it at night and it goes out by the small hours, so take a layer to sleep in even in July.
