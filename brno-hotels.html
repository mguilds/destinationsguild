Where to Stay in Brno

Where to Stay in Brno — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Brno is the Czech Republic’s second city and, for a certain kind of traveller, the more rewarding one. It has a cathedral on a hill, a fortress on another, the second-largest ossuary in Europe under its streets, a Mies van der Rohe villa that changed twentieth-century architecture, and — the real point — a student population that keeps the bars, cafes and wine rooms genuinely alive rather than merely photogenic. Nobody is here to see you see it. The centre is compact and, apart from the two hills, refreshingly flat after Prague and Český Krumlov. Below are the places to stay by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com. Choosing Your Area

Brno’s centre is small enough to cross on foot in twenty minutes. Where you sleep is mostly a question of noise.

The old centre, the station side, or out towards the villas?

OrientationMostly flatTramsNoise

The historic centre — the triangle between náměstí Svobody (Freedom Square), the Cabbage Market and Petrov hill — is where you want to be, and it is largely level; only Petrov and Špilberk climb, and neither is compulsory. It is compact, well served by trams and full of places to eat. The area around the main station is a few minutes’ walk south, cheaper, perfectly convenient and considerably less charming; it is a sensible trade if you are arriving late or leaving early. Verandů, Černá Pole and the streets towards Villa Tugendhat are quiet residential quarters ten minutes out by tram, greener and calmer, and the right choice for a longer stay. One practical note: Brno has an excellent tram network that runs late, so being a few stops out costs you very little.

Luxury

Boutique & Character

Small hotels in old houses — Brno does these quietly and well, and at prices Prague stopped offering years ago.

Adults-only — an honest answer

We found no genuinely adults-only hotel in Brno that we could verify, so we are not going to invent one.

None verified — but here is the useful advice

Honest noteVerifiedNo invented listingsBrowse instead

Brno is a university city with a lively evening, not a resort town, and it has no formal adults-only hotel market that we could confirm on Trip.com. What it does have, in abundance, is quiet grown-up accommodation: the small historic houses such as Royal Ricc above, and the residential quarters towards Černá Pole and Villa Tugendhat, ten minutes out by tram and genuinely peaceful. If it is quiet you are after rather than a policy, book away from Freedom Square and the student streets around Šilingrovo náměstí, which are noisy well past midnight at weekends.

Brno Hotel FAQ

Is Brno worth a night, or just a stop between Prague and Vienna?

Worth two nights. Most people give it an afternoon and miss the point. It has the Villa Tugendhat, the ossuary beneath St James’s church, Špilberk fortress, the cathedral on Petrov, and an evening culture — wine rooms, cafes, small theatres — that belongs to the city rather than to tourism. It is also the natural base for the Moravian wine country and the Lednice-Valtice chateau landscape.

How do I get there from Prague?

By train, in roughly two and a half hours. Both the state railway and the private operators run frequent fast services with comfortable seats and refreshments, and the fares are low by any western standard. Book a few days ahead for the cheapest ticket and a reserved seat. Brno’s main station is a short flat walk from the old centre.

Is Brno flat, or is it another climb?

Mostly flat, which is a relief after Prague. The historic centre between Freedom Square and the Cabbage Market is level and easy. The two exceptions are Petrov hill, where the cathedral stands, and Špilberk, the fortress on its own rise — both are proper climbs, and both are optional. The trams are frequent and cheap.

Do I need to book Villa Tugendhat in advance?

Yes, and well in advance. It is a UNESCO site with strictly limited timed entry and tickets frequently sell out weeks ahead, particularly for the full interior tour. Book directly with the villa as soon as your dates are fixed — this is the one thing in Brno that will not accommodate a change of plan.

When is Brno busiest?

During the big trade fairs at the exhibition grounds, when hotel rates across the city rise sharply and rooms genuinely run out. Check the fair calendar before booking if your dates are flexible. Otherwise Brno is never crowded in the way Prague is, even in high summer.

Should I drink beer or wine here?

Wine, at least one evening. Brno is the capital of Moravia and Moravia is Czech wine country — the whites in particular are very good and almost never exported. The wine rooms in the old centre are the local habit. The beer is excellent too, but you can drink that anywhere in the country.

Is English widely spoken?

Less than in Prague, but generally yes in hotels, and commonly among the large student population. In older neighbourhood restaurants you may need to point at the menu, which is part of the pleasure. A few words of Czech go a long way here.
