Where to Stay in Scottsdale — Old Town, Camelback & the Resorts

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Scottsdale sits on the east side of the Phoenix valley, about ten miles from Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport, and it has built its name on sunshine — the visitors bureau counts more than 330 sunny days a year — and on resorts, golf, spas and a walkable Old Town full of galleries and restaurants. The town is long and spread out, so the right area matters more than the right hotel: stay in Old Town and you can walk to dinner; stay at a resort and the resort is the holiday. This page sorts Scottsdale into its real choices and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in Scottsdale

Scottsdale is big. These are the four choices that matter for a visitor.

Old Town, for walking everywhere

Old Town is the historic centre: western-style shopfronts, the Arts District galleries, and a dense run of restaurants and bars. It is the one part of Scottsdale where you can leave the car parked for a whole stay, and several of the town’s most characterful hotels sit within or just beside it.

It is also the busiest part of town on weekend nights. If you want quiet, choose a room away from the pool scene or the main bar streets.

Camelback Mountain, for a classic resort

At the southern end of Scottsdale, The Phoenician and its sister hotel sit beneath Camelback Mountain on Camelback Road. Here the resort is the destination: golf, several pools, spas and restaurants on the grounds, with Old Town a short drive away.

This is the choice for a celebration or a proper rest. It is less suited to a traveller who wants to explore on foot.

Pima Road and the east side, for families

East of Old Town, along Pima Road and the edge of the Salt River Pima-Maricopa Indian Community, are the big family and casino resorts: an indoor water park, a casino hotel with golf and spa.

Everything here is reached by car, and it suits families who want the children entertained on site.

North Scottsdale, for desert golf and spas

Further north, the town opens out into high Sonoran desert with golf courses and spa resorts. It is beautiful and quiet, and it is a longer drive to Old Town and the airport.

We have not listed a North Scottsdale hotel on this page yet, because none that we tried could be confirmed on its own website today. The full Scottsdale list on Trip.com, linked below, includes them.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Scottsdale details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Scottsdale

Most visitors fly into Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport, which the Scottsdale visitors bureau places about 10 miles west of downtown Scottsdale. The bureau also lists Scottsdale Airport, about nine miles north of downtown.

Scottsdale is spread out, and outside Old Town most visitors get about by car or rideshare. The City of Scottsdale runs a free trolley on weekdays, with trolleys every 20 minutes from 5:45 a.m. to 7:45 p.m. and no service on major holidays, according to the city’s website.

When to go

Winter and spring are the high season. The visitors bureau’s averages run from about 69°F in January and 78°F in March to 86°F in April, and the bureau calls spring the season for people who love being outdoors. Rooms are hardest to find and dearest in these months, so book early.

Summer is very hot — the bureau gives average highs of 103°F to 105°F from June to August — and that is when resort rates drop. Autumn cools down again, with average highs of about 89°F in October and 78°F in November.

In the summer heat

If you visit between June and September, plan outdoor activity for early morning, carry water, and use the resort pool in the afternoon. The desert trails around Scottsdale are not the place for a midday walk in summer.

Scottsdale Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best place to stay in Scottsdale?

Old Town if you want to walk to restaurants, galleries and bars. A resort beneath Camelback Mountain if the resort itself is the holiday. The Pima Road side for families who want a water park or casino resort with everything on site. North Scottsdale for desert golf and quiet spas, with longer drives.

How far is Scottsdale from Phoenix airport?

The Scottsdale visitors bureau places Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport about 10 miles west of downtown Scottsdale. Most visitors drive or take a taxi or rideshare from the airport.

When is the best time to visit Scottsdale?

Winter and spring, when average highs run from the high 60s to the mid 80s Fahrenheit by the visitors bureau’s figures. Summer averages over 100°F from June to August, when resort rates fall. Book winter and spring rooms early.

Is there a free trolley in Scottsdale?

Yes. The City of Scottsdale says its trolley service is free. Trolleys run Monday to Friday from 5:45 a.m. to 7:45 p.m., every 20 minutes, with no service on major holidays. Check the city’s website for current routes.

Is Scottsdale good for families?

Yes, if you choose the right hotel. Great Wolf Lodge Arizona has an indoor water park the lodge keeps at 84 degrees all year, and The Phoenician lists a family-fun programme on its website. Old Town is busy at night and better suited to adults.
