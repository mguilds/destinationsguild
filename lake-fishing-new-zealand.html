Lake Fishing in New Zealand — Taupō, the Rotorua Lakes and the Southern Lakes

Lake Taupō, the country’s biggest lake and its own licensed fishery; the trout lakes around Rotorua; Lake Wakatipu at Queenstown; Lakes Wānaka and Hāwea; and the Mackenzie hydro canals and Lake Benmore around Twizel. What swims in each, the 2026/27 rules from the bodies that set them, the two licences a visitor needs, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against the Department of Conservation and Fish & Game New Zealand, 26 November 2026

This is the lake-fishing companion to our Fly Fishing in New Zealand book. That book walks the clear rivers of the South Island, looking for one big brown trout. This one stays on the lakes, and it is for a wider crowd: families who want a morning on a charter boat, trollers and jiggers, shore anglers at a stream mouth, and anyone who would like a good chance of a trout for dinner.

New Zealand’s trout are not native; they were brought from Britain and California in the 1800s and took to the cold lakes. Today the lakes are some of the most reliable trout water in the world, and most of them can be fished all year. The northern half of the country is the place for numbers: Lake Taupō and the Rotorua lakes are fished from boats and from the shore every month. The South Island lakes are colder and wilder, and around Twizel the hydro canals grow trout that Fish & Game itself calls “extremely large”.

Every rule, limit and price below was read on 26 November 2026 from the body that sets it, and is dated. Three things to know before you plan: Lake Taupō has its own licence, sold by the Department of Conservation, and everywhere else on this page needs a Fish & Game licence — a visitor fishing both needs both; the Taupō district allows no bait at all; and this is the southern hemisphere: the Fish & Game licence year starts on 1 October, and a northern angler who comes in our winter fishes their summer. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

The best months, in plain words

Southern-hemisphere seasons: summer is December to February, winter is June to August. The big lakes on this page do not close in winter.

LakeBest monthsWhy
Lake TaupōAll year; May to September at the stream mouthsThe lake is open the whole licence year, 1 July to 30 June (a rule). Our guidance: in the cooler months the trout gather off the river mouths before they run upstream to spawn, which is the classic time to fish from the shore; in summer, troll or jig deeper water from a boat.

Lake Taupō — the country’s biggest lake, and its own fishery

A volcanic crater lake in the middle of the North Island, between the town of Taupō at the north end and Tūrangi at the south. Fishing rules and licences: the Department of Conservation (DOC), which manages the Taupō fishery in partnership with the Tūwharetoa Māori Trust Board.

Lake Taupō, from Taupō to Tūrangi

What swims there. Wild rainbow and brown trout. DOC calls the lake “the largest lake in New Zealand with a wide variety of trout fishing options all year round”. The fish are wild, not stocked: DOC says the lake’s spawning streams are so productive that harvest is needed to keep the trout in balance with their main food, smelt.

Keeping a fish. For the 2026/27 season (from 1 July 2026) the daily limit is eight trout, of any species — up from six. Every trout you keep must be at least 35 cm, measured from the tip of the snout to the fork of the tail. Stop fishing once you have your eight. Selling trout is illegal.

How people fish it. No bait, anywhere in the district: only flies and artificial lures, on single-point hooks, no more than one hook to a lure and no more than three flies or lures on the line. Trolling, jigging and spinning are allowed on the open lake, but not within 200 metres of the landmark posts (ringed white, black and yellow) at the stream mouths — those are fly fishing only. You may fish between 5 am and midnight, with one rod at a time. Electronic devices that attract fish are banned, and so are felt-soled boots. Our guidance: a charter boat is the easy way to start; on your own, fly-fish the stream mouths from the shore.

Getting to the water. A Taupō licence gives the holder access on foot over the designated lakeshore and riverbank rights of way; it does not give access to private land, including Motutaiko Island. We did not check the public boat ramps for this page — ask in Taupō or Tūrangi before you tow a boat.

Nearest towns: Taupō at the north end, where the Waikato River leaves the lake, and Tūrangi near the south end and the mouth of the Tongariro River. The Tongariro National Trout Centre, run by DOC, is 4 km south of Tūrangi on State Highway 1, with an aquarium, an underwater trout-viewing chamber and kids’ fishing days — a good half-day for a family.

Two neighbours with their own rules. Lake Otamangakau, in the same district, is known for very large trout and has tighter rules: 2 trout a day, no minimum size but a maximum of 55 cm, and a season of 1 October to 31 May with a summer closure if the water gets too warm for the fish. Lake Rotoaira is run by the Lake Rotoaira Trust and needs its own licence, not a Taupō one.

Confirmed with the Department of Conservation, 26 November 2026 — DOC’s Taupō Trout Fishery pages (rules and regulations, licences, where to fish, the Tongariro National Trout Centre), the Taupō Fishing District map and summary of regulations (September 2026), and DOC’s media release of 18 June 2026, “Regulation changes to enhance Taupō fishery”.

The Rotorua lakes — Rotorua, Rotoiti, Tarawera and Ōkataina

A cluster of volcanic lakes within half an hour of Rotorua city, in Fish & Game’s Eastern region. Fishing rules: Fish & Game New Zealand.

Lake Rotorua, Lake Rotoiti, Lake Tarawera and Lake Ōkataina

What swims there. Rainbow and brown trout. Lake Rotorua is the big, shallow lake beside the city; Rotoiti is joined to it by the Ohau Channel; Tarawera and Ōkataina are deep, clear lakes in the bush to the east. Cruise & Fish Rotorua, a local charter, sums the lakes up as Lake Rotorua for “the best catch rate”, Rotoiti for “a surprise sized trout”; Tarawera Lakes Fishing says Tarawera is up to 87 metres deep. Those are the operators’ words, not ours.

Keeping a fish. The minimum size for trout in the Rotorua lakes and the streams that flow into them is 350 mm. The Eastern region’s daily limit is 8 trout, of which no more than 2 may be brown trout.

How people fish it. No fishing for sports fish between midnight and 5 am. A rod set in a holder is allowed only when you are fishing from a boat. Scented soft-bait lures are allowed everywhere except fly-only waters. From 1 July to 1 October nobody may fish from a boat in the marked winter shoreline-fishing areas on Tarawera, Rotoiti and Ōkataina. All the streams flowing into Lake Tarawera are closed to fishing, and no boat may pass through the Tarawera Outlet spawning sanctuary. Many streams around these lakes are closed; read the list before you fish one. Our guidance: a charter boat trolling or jigging in summer; a fly rod at a stream mouth in winter.

Nearest town: Rotorua. Most charters pick up from the city or meet you at the lake; Tarawera Lakes Fishing leaves from the private jetty at Solitaire Lodge on Lake Tarawera.

Checked with Fish & Game New Zealand, 26 November 2026 — the Eastern region regulations page. That page did not let us tie an open season to each lake, so we do not print one: check the Eastern regulations for your lake before you fish. The size limit, bag limit, hours and winter-shoreline rule above are quoted from it.

Lake Wakatipu — Queenstown’s lake

A long, deep, cold lake in the mountains of Otago, with Queenstown halfway along its shore and Glenorchy at its head. Fishing rules: Fish & Game New Zealand, Otago region.

Lake Wakatipu

What swims there. Fish & Game says the large southern lakes — Wānaka, Wakatipu, Hāwea and Dunstan — “hold rainbow and brown trout and Chinook salmon, attracting boat and shore anglers”.

Keeping a fish. Open all year. Fly, spin and bait are all allowed. Daily limit: 2 trout and 4 salmon. In the Otago region there is no minimum length for trout on these lakes.

Where you may not fish. Closed: the water of Queenstown Bay, inside a line from the outermost point of the Queenstown Gardens to where the south bank of One Mile Creek meets the lake; and within 100 metres of the wharf in Black Bay (the Walter Peak jetty).

Boats. You may fish from a boat, canoe or float tube; a boat under motor must stay at least 100 metres from anyone fishing from the shore. Our guidance: a short charter from Queenstown is the simplest way onto the water; the lake is very deep, very cold and can turn rough quickly.

Nearest town: Queenstown, with an international airport. Glenorchy, at the head of the lake, is the start of the rivers in our fly fishing book.

Confirmed with Fish & Game New Zealand, 26 November 2026 — Sports Fishing Regulations 2026/2027, South Island, Otago region (Lake Wakatipu, and the region’s notes 1 and 2).

Lakes Wānaka and Hāwea — side by side above the Clutha

Two big glacial lakes a few kilometres apart in the Upper Clutha, both served by the town of Wānaka. Fishing rules: Fish & Game New Zealand, Otago region.

Lake Wānaka

What swims there. Rainbow and brown trout and Chinook salmon (Fish & Game, as above).

Keeping a fish. Open all year; fly, spin and bait allowed; daily limit 2 trout and 1 salmon. No minimum length for trout.

Where you may not fish. Within 150 metres of the launch wharf in Wānaka township, within 150 metres of the public jetty at the Wānaka Marina, and anywhere within 150 metres of the shore between the wharf and the jetty. That is the town waterfront: walk or drive along the lake before you cast.

Boats. Allowed; boats under motor keep 100 metres from shore anglers.

Lake Hāwea

What swims there. Rainbow and brown trout and Chinook salmon.

Keeping a fish. Open all year; fly, spin and bait allowed; daily limit 2 trout and 4 salmon. No minimum length for trout. Boats allowed, on the same 100-metre rule.

Nearest town: Wānaka for both lakes. The small settlement of Lake Hāwea sits at the south end of its lake; we found holiday homes listed there but could not show hotel rooms, so it has no button of its own — use Wānaka’s.

Confirmed with Fish & Game New Zealand, 26 November 2026 — Sports Fishing Regulations 2026/2027, South Island, Otago region (Lake Wānaka, Lake Hāwea and the region’s notes).

The Mackenzie canals and Lake Benmore — the big-fish water

The hydro canals and lakes of the Mackenzie Basin, around the town of Twizel. Fishing rules: Fish & Game New Zealand, Central South Island region.

The canals — Tekapo, Pūkaki–Ōhau and Ōhau

What swims there. Fish & Game’s own words: the canals “are world-famous for producing extremely large trout”. Rainbow trout “commonly reach 4–10 kg and each season several brown and rainbow trout weighing 12–18 kg are caught”. Some of the big fish are trout or salmon that have escaped from the salmon farms in the canals.

Keeping a fish. 2 sports fish a day in each canal. There is no minimum length for trout in this region. The seasons: the Ōhau canal (from the Lake Ōhau outlet to the Pūkaki–Ōhau A canal), the Pūkaki–Ōhau A canal and the Ōhau B and C canals are open all year; the Tekapo canal is open all year below the State Highway 8 bridge, and above it from 1 October to 31 May and 1 to 30 September only.

How people fish it. Fly, spin and bait are allowed, but no fish-farm feed pellets as bait in the Tekapo, Pūkaki–Ōhau A and Ōhau canals. In this region you must stop fishing for the day after foul-hooking three sports fish, and any fish you do not keep must go straight back. Our guidance: the canal banks are steep and the water is cold and fast — children need a hand and a life jacket.

Lake Benmore and Lake Ruataniwha

Lake Benmore, the largest of the Waitaki hydro lakes: open all year; fly, spin and bait; 4 sports fish a day. Its tributaries (except the Tekapo, Ahuriri and Otamatapaio rivers, which have their own lines) open on the first Saturday in November. Fish & Game says the Tekapo River and Lake Benmore “contain good numbers of trout which are not overly difficult to catch” — a good lake for a beginner.

Lake Ruataniwha, beside Twizel: open all year; 2 sports fish a day.

Nearest town: Twizel, for the canals, Ruataniwha and the upper arms of Benmore. Omarama is closer to the southern arms of Benmore, but we could not show hotel rooms there, so it has no button.

Confirmed with Fish & Game New Zealand, 26 November 2026 — Sports Fishing Regulations 2026/2027, South Island, Central South Island region (the canals, Lake Benmore, Lake Ruataniwha, notes 1 and 8, and the region’s introduction).

The licence and the limits, plainly

Two separate licences. Buy them online before you go, and carry them on the water. Prices are in New Zealand dollars.

Lake Taupō — the Department of Conservation licence

What it covers. The Lake Taupō district only: Lake Taupō and the rivers that flow into it, the Waikato River down to Huka Falls, and Lakes Kuratau, Moawhango and Otamangakau. It does not cover Fish & Game waters, or Lake Rotoaira.

Prices on 26 November 2026 (GST included): 24 hours — adult $23, child $5. One week — adult $49, non-resident $89. Whole season — adult $105, senior $90, non-resident $169, family $159, child $13.50. DOC says the fees are unchanged from previous years.

Who counts as a non-resident. Anyone who is neither a New Zealand citizen nor a permanent resident. A child is under 18 on 1 July; a senior is 65 or older on 1 July.

When and where. The season licence runs from 1 July to 30 June. Licences are sold online around the clock; DOC’s online store takes only Mastercard and Visa.

Confirmed with the Department of Conservation, 26 November 2026 — the Taupō fishery licences page and the 18 June 2026 media release.

Everywhere else on this page — the Fish & Game licence

For a visitor. Fish & Game sells a Non-Resident Licence that “entitles the holder to fish for sports fish in all Fish & Game regions, excluding the Taupo Fishing District”. That covers the Rotorua lakes, Wakatipu, Wānaka, Hāwea and the Mackenzie. On 26 November 2026 Fish & Game’s own website showed two different prices: its overseas-visitor page said $264 for an adult whole season and $37 for one day; its licence-options page said $273 and $39. We cannot tell you which is current — the price at checkout is the one that counts.

For a New Zealand resident. An adult whole-season licence was $158, valid 1 October to 30 September.

The daily limits, side by side. Rotorua lakes 8 trout (no more than 2 brown), minimum 350 mm. Wakatipu 2 trout and 4 salmon. Wānaka 2 trout and 1 salmon. Hāwea 2 trout and 4 salmon. The Mackenzie canals 2 sports fish. Benmore 4 sports fish. Ruataniwha 2 sports fish.

Checked with Fish & Game New Zealand, 26 November 2026 — the Non-Resident Licence page, the standard licence options page, the Eastern region regulations page, and the Sports Fishing Regulations 2026/2027 for the South Island.

Check, Clean, Dry

DOC’s Taupō map carries the national Check, Clean, Dry message for anyone moving between waterways: check your boots, waders, lines and boat for weed and mud, clean them, and dry them before the next lake or river. It matters more in New Zealand than almost anywhere, because the lakes are clear and the pests are few.

Questions

Do I need a separate licence to fish Lake Taupō?

Yes. The Lake Taupō district is managed by the Department of Conservation (DOC), and it sells its own licence. A Fish & Game licence does not cover it, and a Taupō licence does not cover Fish & Game waters such as the Rotorua lakes, Wakatipu or the Mackenzie canals. On DOC's licence page on 26 November 2026 the Taupō fees were: 24-hour adult $23, child $5; one week adult $49, non-resident $89; whole season adult $105, senior $90, non-resident $169, family $159, child $13.50 (New Zealand dollars, GST included). Taupō licences run from 1 July to 30 June and are sold online.

How many trout can I keep at Lake Taupō, and can I use bait?

Eight trout a day, of any species, on Lake Taupō and most of the Taupō district for the 2026/27 season (DOC raised the limit from six on 1 July 2026). Each fish must be at least 35 cm from the tip of the snout to the fork of the tail. Bait is not allowed anywhere in the district: only flies and artificial lures, on single-point hooks. You may fish between 5 am and midnight. Checked with DOC on 26 November 2026.

What licence do I need for the Rotorua lakes, Queenstown and the Mackenzie canals?

A Fish & Game New Zealand licence. A visitor buys a Non-Resident Licence, which covers every Fish & Game region but not the Taupō district. On 26 November 2026 Fish & Game's own pages gave two different prices for it: $264 or $273 for a whole season, and $37 or $39 for one day, so check the price when you buy. A New Zealand resident's adult whole-season licence was $158, valid 1 October to 30 September.

Can you fish the Mackenzie canals all year?

Mostly. In the 2026/27 Central South Island regulations the Ōhau canals and the Pūkaki–Ōhau A canal are open all year, and so is the Tekapo canal downstream of the State Highway 8 bridge. The Tekapo canal upstream of that bridge is open 1 October to 31 May and 1 to 30 September, so it is closed in June, July and August. The canal limit is 2 sports fish a day, and fish-farm feed pellets may not be used as bait.

Which towns should I stay in?

Taupō for the north of Lake Taupō and Tūrangi for the south end and the Tongariro River mouth; Rotorua for the Rotorua lakes; Queenstown for Lake Wakatipu; Wānaka for Lakes Wānaka and Hāwea; and Twizel for the Mackenzie canals and Lake Benmore.
