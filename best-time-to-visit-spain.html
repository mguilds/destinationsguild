When to Go to Spain

The Best Time to Visit Spain

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

Short answer: the shoulder seasons — roughly April–May and September–October — are the sweet spot. You get warm, settled weather and full opening hours without the summer heat, queues, and peak prices. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the crowds, and the best timing for the cities, the coast, and the mountains.

Spain is a year-round destination, but it is not the same destination year-round. The country you meet in a golden October — harvest tables, warm plazas, room to breathe in the Alhambra — is a very different place from mid-August, when Seville pushes past 40°C, the Mediterranean coast is elbow-to-elbow, and much of Spain has locked up and headed to the beach itself. And because the land ranges from rainy green Atlantic north to sun-baked southern Andalusia, the “best” month shifts with where you're going. Choosing your dates well is the single biggest lever you have over the trip. Here is how to pull it.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$ ShoulderMild & bloomingBest for citiesFewer crowds

The countryside greens up, orange blossom scents the south, and city sightseeing is a pleasure at 18–26°C. Late April and May are prime — everything is open, gardens are at their peak, and the summer crowds haven't yet surged. Watch two spikes: Semana Santa (Holy Week, the single biggest domestic peak, especially in Seville and Andalusia) and Seville's Feria de Abril. March can still be cool and showery, especially in the north, but it's cheap.

Summer — June to August

$$$$ PeakHot & busyBeach & coastBook early

High season, and it feels like it. June is the most bearable of the three — long days, warm seas, festivals — before July and August bring serious heat and the biggest crowds and prices of the year. Inland Andalusia and Madrid can be punishing at midday, so this is the time for the coast, the islands, and cooler northern Spain — San Sebastián, Asturias, Galicia — rather than marathon city sightseeing. If summer is your only window, book months ahead and start your days early.

Autumn — September to November

$$ ShoulderWarm & mellowHarvest & foodGreat value

Many travelers' favorite season. September still has warm water and summer light with the peak crowds ebbing; October is mellow, golden, and one of the best-value months of the year, with the grape harvest and the first mushrooms and olives. Rain increases as November arrives — heaviest in the green north — but the cities are quiet and prices soft, ideal for Madrid, Barcelona, and Andalusia.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCool & quietSki & sunCheapest

The bargain season, and a genuinely varied one: crowd-free cities, Christmas lights, mild winter sun in Andalusia and the Canary Islands, and real skiing in the Pyrenees and the Sierra Nevada. Central Spain and the interior are cold and days are short, and most Mediterranean beach resorts largely wind down. Sidestep the two-week Christmas–New Year spike and January and February are the cheapest weeks in Spain.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ CheapestCold interiorSki season

The quietest, cheapest month once the New Year holiday passes. Cold in Madrid and the interior, mild and sunny in the south and the Canaries. Ideal for near-empty museums, winter sales, and prime skiing in the Sierra Nevada and Pyrenees. Book cities and the mountains, not the mainland beaches.

February

$ CheapCoolCarnival

Still low season and low prices, with lively exceptions: Carnival (spectacular in Cádiz and Santa Cruz de Tenerife) and the almond blossom in the south. Good skiing continues. A great month for a quiet, affordable city break in Madrid, Seville, or Granada.

March

$$ Value shoulderCool, variableSpring begins

Shoulder season opens. Weather is variable — mild days, cool evenings, some rain in the north — but crowds are thin and prices low. Valencia erupts with Las Fallas mid-month. If Semana Santa falls in late March, expect a sharp surge around it, especially in Andalusia.

April

$$ ShoulderMild & greenHoly Week

One of the loveliest months: green hills, blossom, comfortable sightseeing weather across most of the country. The catch is Semana Santa and Seville's Feria de Abril, which bring big domestic crowds and higher rates in the south. Outside those dates, superb value and mood.

May

$$$ PrimeWarm & settledEverything open

Arguably the best all-round month. Warm, stable weather, long days, patios and gardens at their peak (Córdoba's famous patios open now), and the sea warming for a first swim — all before the crowds and the fierce summer heat arrive. Popular, so book ahead, but worth it.

June

$$$$ Peak beginsHot, long daysCoast & islands

Summer proper, and the most pleasant of the hot months. The longest days of the year, warm seas, and festival season, with crowds and prices climbing toward the July–August peak. Excellent for the Costa Brava, the Balearics, and the beaches, though inland Andalusia is already heating up hard.

July

$$$$ PeakVery hotBusy

Hot, crowded, and pricey, especially on the coast and in the honeypot cities. Seville and Córdoba can top 40°C. Great for beach holidays, the cool north, and Pamplona's San Fermín; hard work for midday sightseeing in the south. Reserve accommodation and headline sights well ahead.

August

$$$$ PeakHottestSpain on holiday

The hottest, busiest month — and the trickiest. Much of Spain itself is on vacation: the coast and islands are packed and expensive, while inland cities can feel half-shuttered as family-run shops and restaurants close for weeks. Doable, but plan around the closures and the heat — head to the beaches, the islands, or the green north.

September

$$$ PrimeWarm seasHarvest

The other star month. Summer warmth and swimmable seas linger while the crowds thin and prices ease after the first week. The grape harvest begins, food festivals multiply, Barcelona celebrates La Mercè, and cities become comfortable again. A superb time for coast and culture combined.

October

$$ Best valueMild & goldenQuiet

Mellow, golden, and one of the best-value months of the year. Comfortable city weather, autumn color, harvest season, and low crowds. Rain increases late in the month, especially in the north, and coastal resorts start winding down, but the cities are at their calm best.

November

$ Low seasonCool & wetQuiet cities

Low season returns: cool, one of the wettest months in the north and interior, and quiet. But prices are low, museums empty, and Madrid, Barcelona, and Seville rewarding for travelers who don't mind a coat and an umbrella. The Canary Islands stay warm and sunny.

December

$ → $$$ SplitFestiveSki & lights

A month of two halves. Early December is quiet and cheap, with Christmas lights glowing and ski resorts opening. The two weeks around Christmas and New Year are a sharp peak — festive and beautiful, with the Three Kings parades on January 5, but book early and expect higher rates.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

November to February — with January and February the lowest

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSkip Christmas fortnight

Transatlantic airfares and hotel rates bottom out in winter. January and February are the cheapest weeks of the year for both, with November and early December close behind — the one carve-out being the roughly two-week Christmas–New Year holiday, when prices briefly jump. You'll trade warm coastal life for the savings, but for city breaks, mild southern sun, and skiing the value is unbeatable.

Want warm weather and a deal? Target the edges of shoulder season: late March to early April (avoiding Holy Week) and late October. You get mild days and open sights at a fraction of the July–August cost. As a rule, midweek flights and stays beat weekends, and booking two to three months out lands the best fares.

When to Avoid the Crowds

The busy periods in Spain are predictable, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks to sidestep

July & AugustSemana SantaFeria de AbrilChristmas & NY

July and August are the busiest, hottest, and most expensive stretch, with August the peak, when Spaniards take their own holidays and the coast and islands are at their fullest. Semana Santa (Holy Week) and Seville's Feria de Abril are shorter, sharper spikes concentrated in Andalusia, and the Christmas–New Year fortnight fills the cities. In the south, midsummer also brings extreme heat over 40°C.

To have the big sights closer to yourself, come in the shoulder months — late April, May, September, October — or in winter. Whenever you go, the crowd-beating tactics are the same: reserve timed-entry tickets for the Alhambra, the Sagrada Família, and the Prado well in advance, and visit at opening or in the last hours of the day. Best Time by Region

Spain is large and climatically varied — the ideal month depends on where you're headed.

The Cities — Madrid, Barcelona, Seville, Granada

Best: Apr–May, Sep–OctValue: Nov–FebAvoid: Jul–Aug midday

Cities are about walking, queuing, and standing in sun-struck plazas — so heat is the enemy, and it is fiercest inland in Madrid and Andalusia. Spring and autumn are ideal; winter is quiet and cheap if you can take cool days. Skip peak-summer midday sightseeing in the south, when the temperature and the lines peak together. The Coast & Islands — Costa del Sol, Costa Brava, Balearics, Canaries

Best: late May–Jun, SepSeason: May–OctCanaries: year-round

Mainland beach Spain runs on a season — roughly May through October, with many resorts quieter the rest of the year. The sweet spots are late May to June and September: warm water, open everything, and lighter crowds than the July–August crush. The Canary Islands are the exception, with mild sun and swimmable seas all year — the go-to for winter warmth. Plan your base on the Spain guide.

The North & the Mountains — Basque Country, Galicia, Pyrenees, Sierra Nevada

North: Jun–SepSki: Dec–MarHike: Jun–Sep

Green, Atlantic northern Spain — San Sebastián, Bilbao, Asturias, Galicia — is at its best in summer, precisely when the south is too hot; July and August are warmest and driest here, and the coolest escape in the country. The mountains have two seasons: December to March for skiing in the Pyrenees and Sierra Nevada (most reliable snow in January and February), and June to September for hiking and wildflower meadows.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a Spanish trip.

What is the best month to visit Spain?

May and September are the two strongest all-round months. You get warm, settled weather, long daylight, everything open, and noticeably thinner crowds and lower prices than July and August. May is green and fresh before the summer heat; September brings warm seas and the grape harvest. April and October are close behind and even cheaper.

What is the cheapest time to visit Spain?

November through February is the cheapest window, with January and February the lowest of all for both flights and hotels — the two weeks around Christmas and New Year excepted. If you want warm-weather value, the true bargains sit at the edges of shoulder season: late March to early April (avoiding Holy Week) and late October.

When should I avoid Spain because of crowds?

July and August are the most crowded and expensive months, with August the peak, when much of Spain itself is on holiday and the coast is packed. Semana Santa (Holy Week) and Seville's Feria de Abril are short, sharp spikes in the south, and the Christmas–New Year period fills the cities. In Andalusia, midsummer also brings extreme heat over 40°C.

Is Spain worth visiting in winter?

Yes, for the right trip. Winter is the best time for crowd-free cities — Madrid, Barcelona, Seville, and Granada at their quietest and cheapest — plus Christmas lights, mild days in Andalusia and the Canary Islands, and skiing in the Pyrenees and Sierra Nevada from December to March. The trade-offs are short days, cool-to-cold interiors, and Mediterranean beach resorts that largely wind down until spring.

What is shoulder season in Spain and why is it best?

Shoulder season is spring (April–May) and fall (September–October), the stretches on either side of the summer peak. It's widely considered the best time to visit Spain because you get pleasant weather and full opening hours without the peak-season heat, queues, and prices — the sweet spot for combining cities with the coast or the countryside.
