Puerto Varas & the Lake District

Where to Stay in Puerto Varas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Puerto Varas is a small German-founded town on the shore of Lake Llanquihúe, with the perfect cone of the Osorno volcano across the water and the Chilean Lake District all around it. It is the pleasantest base in the south: shingled houses, good restaurants, and a day’s drive to waterfalls, forest and the island of Chiloé. The hotels divide simply between those on the lakefront, where you pay for the view, and those a few streets back, where you do not. On the lakefront

Puerto Varas faces Lake Llanquihúe with two volcanoes, Osorno and Calbuco, standing on the far shore. The lakefront road and the streets immediately behind it hold most of the hotels, the restaurants and the German-built shingled houses the town is known for. Pay for a lake-facing room if you can: on a clear morning the view is the reason to be here, and on a wet one it is still the best thing in the window.

Hotel Cumbres Puerto Varas (Enjoy Puerto Varas)

The large comfortable hotel of the town, on Klenner above the waterfront, with a spa, an indoor pool and wide windows aimed at the lake and Osorno. It is a short, mostly level walk down into the restaurants. Best for a traveller who wants a proper hotel with facilities as a base for the Lake District rather than a small guesthouse.

Using Puerto Varas as a base

Osorno and the Petrohué falls

The classic day out: east along the lake to the Petrohué waterfalls and the green water of Lake Todos los Santos, with the Osorno volcano above you the whole way. In winter there is a small ski operation on Osorno; in summer the road goes to the chairlift and the view. An easy half day by hire car, a full day by tour.

Chiloé

The big island south of here, reached by a short ferry from Pargua, is a world of its own — wooden churches listed by UNESCO, palafito houses on stilts, and weather that comes in off the Pacific without warning. It deserves its own nights rather than a day trip, but many visitors do it from Puerto Varas in a long day.

Puerto Montt is not the same place

Puerto Montt, twenty minutes south, is the regional capital, the airport and the ferry port for Patagonia. It is a working city and most visitors prefer to sleep in Puerto Varas and travel in. If a booking says Puerto Montt, check which town you are actually being put in.

The weather is the Lake District’s weather

This is a green, wooded, rainy part of Chile — that is why it looks the way it does. Rain is possible in any month and common outside the December-to-March summer, and the volcanoes spend a good deal of the year in cloud. Pack for it and treat a clear day as the gift it is.

Fishing here

This is the northern end of Chile’s great trout country. The rivers and spring creeks around the lakes hold brown and rainbow trout, the season runs roughly through the southern summer, and the lodges and guides work out of the small towns rather than the cities. Our full guide is fly fishing in Chile, and the whole shelf is in The Guild Fishing Library.

Where to stay in Puerto Varas — questions

Puerto Varas or Puerto Montt?

Puerto Varas, for almost every visitor. It is the prettier town by a long way, it faces the lake and the volcanoes, and it has the restaurants and the small hotels. Puerto Montt has the airport, the bus terminal and the ferries, and is a working port city rather than a holiday one. The two are about twenty minutes apart, so arriving at Puerto Montt airport and sleeping in Puerto Varas is the normal thing to do.

When should I come to the Lake District?

December to March is the southern summer and the reliable season: long evenings, the best chance of clear volcano views, and everything open. It is also when Chilean families take their holidays, so book ahead for January and February. April and November are quieter and often lovely, with a real chance of rain. In winter the town is subdued, the days are short, and the small ski field on Osorno is the draw.

Do I need a car?

It helps a great deal. The town itself is walkable, but the reason to be here is the country around it — the falls, the volcano road, the lake shore, the back roads to Fruitillar and Frutillar’s lakeside concert hall. Buses and tours cover the main sights; a hire car covers them on your own timetable, and the roads are good.

Is this a good place to break a long trip?

It is one of the best in Chile. Puerto Varas sits at the point where the road south becomes Patagonia and the ferries begin, and it is a comfortable, gentle, well-fed town to spend two or three nights in before or after something harder. Travellers heading on to Chiloé or the Carretera Austral routinely use it as the last soft landing.
