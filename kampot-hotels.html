Where to Stay in Kampot

Where to Stay in Kampot — Riverside, Town & Kep

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Kampot is the place people mean to stop at for one night and leave four days later. A green river running down from the Bokor hills to the sea, a grid of faded colonial shophouses, the best pepper in the world growing half an hour away, and almost nothing you are obliged to do. Stay in the old town if you want to walk to dinner, upriver if you want a hammock over the water, and out toward Kep if what you are really here for is crab and the sea.

Choosing Your Area in Kampot

Where you sleep shapes the whole stay. Here is the honest lay of the land, quarter by quarter.

The old town

A few walkable blocks of shuttered shophouses between the market and the river, now full of small hotels, bakeries and bars. The best base for a first visit: you can eat anywhere, walk to the night market and the river promenade, and find a driver for Bokor or the pepper farms without arranging anything in advance. Rooms above a bar will hear the bar.

Upriver

Follow the Kampot River northwest out of town and the bank fills with bungalows, guesthouses and a few genuinely good small hotels, each with a jetty, a kayak and a view of the Bokor massif turning pink at six o’clock. Five to twenty minutes by tuk-tuk from town. This is the Kampot of the photographs; take it if the point of your stay is to sit still.

Toward Kep and the coast

Kep is a half-hour down the road: a faded 1960s seaside resort of ruined modernist villas, a famous crab market and a small national park behind it. Several hotels along the way and in Kep itself look out over the water toward Rabbit Island. Stay here if your priority is seafood and sea air rather than the river and the town.

Know Before You Book

The handful of things that change how a Kampot trip goes.

Pepper, and how to buy it

Kampot pepper carries a protected geographical indication, and the working farms half an hour from town will show you the vines, explain the difference between the green, black, red and white harvests, and sell you the result. Buy at a farm or from a reputable shop in town rather than from a stall at a bus stop, and keep the packaging — the genuine article is labelled, and it is worth the small premium over what is sold loose.

Bokor and the old hill station

A sealed road climbs the Bokor plateau to what is left of a French hill station — an abandoned casino hotel, a church on a bare ridge, a great deal of cloud and, on a clear afternoon, a view all the way to Vietnam. Go with a driver rather than on a rented scooter; the road is long, the weather at the top is its own thing, and the mist can close in within minutes.

The pace, and who it suits

Kampot runs slowly and takes a dim view of schedules, which is precisely its appeal. There is no beach in town — the river is for swimming and kayaking and the sea is at Kep. Pavements in the old town are uneven and the riverside places are reached by unlit lanes, so a torch and a pre-arranged ride back after dinner are worth having.

Kampot Hotel FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room in Kampot.

Where is the best place to stay in Kampot?

In the old town if you want to walk to restaurants and the night market, and upriver if you want a bungalow on the water with the Bokor hills in front of you. Choose a hotel toward Kep if seafood and the sea matter more than the river. Many visitors do two nights in town and two upriver, which is a good way to get both.

How do you get to Kampot?

By road, about three to four hours from Phnom Penh by bus or private car, and roughly two hours from Sihanoukville. There is also a slow train from Phnom Penh on the southern line, which is cheap, scenic and takes most of the day. Kep is a half-hour further on from Kampot.

How many nights do you need in Kampot?

Three, and plan for four. One day for a pepper farm and the salt fields, one for Bokor, one for Kep and the crab market, and at least one afternoon with nothing in it at all — which is the day most people remember.

Is Kampot good for a quiet, slow trip?

It is the best place in Cambodia for exactly that. It is small, green, inexpensive and entirely unbothered, the river is beautiful at both ends of the day, and nothing is more than half an hour away. If the rest of your trip is temples and cities, put Kampot at the end of it rather than the beginning.
