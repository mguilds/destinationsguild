Historic Hotels of Texas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

12 members of Historic Hotels of America · oldest first

Every hotel in this chapter is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the National Trust for Historic Preservation's program for hotels that have kept their history, their architecture and their sense of place. We list all 12 in Texas, whether or not a booking earns us anything, because a guide that leaves out the best of them is not much of a guide.

Est. 1852

Omni La Mansion del Rio

San Antonio, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2010

In 1852, sixteen years after the Alamo fell, four Society of St. Mary brothers began a limestone school on College Street, and St. Mary's Institute first rang its bells on March 1, 1853. It grew into St. Mary's College and later housed the university's downtown law school until 1966. Former law student Patrick J. Kennedy then turned the buildings into a Spanish Colonial Revival hotel, opening it in April 1968 for HemisFair, San Antonio's world's fair. Omni took it over in 2006.

  • The 1929 Majestic Theatre

Est. 1859

The Menger Hotel

San Antonio, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 1989

William and Mary Menger opened a two-story, 50-room hotel beside their brewery, the first in Texas, in early 1859, about 100 yards from the Alamo. Architect John M. Fries designed it, and a 40-room annex was finished that same year. It became a gathering place for cattlemen, travelers and politicians. Robert E. Lee, Ulysses S. Grant, Sam Houston and Babe Ruth all stayed, and Theodore Roosevelt recruited Rough Riders in the Menger Bar.

Est. 1860

Stagecoach Inn

Salado, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2018

Spanish explorers named Salado for its mineral springs, and the town was organized in 1859 on the Chisholm Trail. This two-story frame building went up soon after as a stop for the stagecoach lines; its Historic Hotels page credits early settler W. B. Armstrong. Sam Houston is said to have spoken against secession from its balcony, and George Custer and Jesse James passed through. Restored in 1943 by Dion and Ruth Van Bibber, it reopened as a hotel.

Est. 1876

P.A. Smith Hotel

Navasota, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2026

Navasota boomed after the railroad came, and in 1876 Louisa, wife of Philip Aurene "P.A." Smith, paid for a three-story limestone hotel near the station and named it for him. Smith, a New York-born newspaperman, also built the Navasota Opera House. After Louisa died in 1890 the family lived on the upper floors. Stephen and Janice Scheve bought the worn building in 2017 and restored it, joining it to the Giesel House next door, Grimes County's oldest building.

Est. 1909

The Crockett Hotel

San Antonio, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2010

Juan Bautista de la Zerda received this ground east of the Alamo in a 1793 grant and it was farmed for decades; in 1836 Crockett's men held the mission's southeast palisade close by. The Odd Fellows laid the cornerstone of their temple here on April 26, 1909: hotel rooms on four floors, their lodge on the top two. A seven-story west wing followed in 1927. John Blocker bought it in 1982, and Jeanne Blocker and her sister, Mary Ann Castleberry, led its restoration.

Close by

  • The Alamo, steps away
  • San Fernando Cathedral, built in 1731
  • Hemisfair Park and the Tower of the Americas

Est. 1909

The Gunter Hotel San Antonio Riverwalk, a Tribute Portfolio Hotel

San Antonio, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2025

Iowa-born developer Lawrence J. Hart built an eight-story hotel of steel, concrete and tile on the site of the old Mahncke Hotel, opening it in 1909 and naming it for rancher Jonathan "Jot" Gunter, a key investor. Cattlemen bound for the Union Stock Yard were early guests, followed by Mae West, Joe DiMaggio, Will Rogers and President Harry Truman. In November 1936 blues singer Robert Johnson recorded "Sweet Home Chicago" and "Cross Road Blues" in Room 414. Hotelier T.B. Baker later added three floors.

  • San Antonio Museum of Art, in an old brewery
  • Briscoe Western Art Museum on the River Walk

Est. 1915

The Ashton Hotel

Fort Worth, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2008

Fort Worth's Commercial Club, founded in 1885 by Khleber Van Zandt, John Peter Smith and B.B. Paddock, grew into the Fort Worth Club, and in 1915 it chose architect John James Pollard of Sanguinet & Staats to design an Italianate clubhouse at Sixth and Main. Oilmen Sid Richardson and Thomas G. Shaw joined. The club moved out in 1926 and the building became offices. Next door, the 1890 Winfree Building once held the White Elephant Saloon, a favorite of Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid.

Est. 1921

Hilton Fort Worth

Fort Worth, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2016

Fort Worth business leaders formed the Citizens' Hotel Company and hired the firms Sanguinet & Staats and Mauran, Russell & Crowell, who designed a 15-story Beaux-Arts hotel of nearly 600 rooms. It opened as the Hotel Texas in 1921. President John F. Kennedy and Jacqueline Kennedy stayed in Room 850 in November 1963, and he spoke in the new Crystal Ballroom that November 22. It was his last address; he was assassinated in Dallas hours later.

Est. 1924

The Emily Morgan San Antonio - a DoubleTree by Hilton Hotel

San Antonio, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2015

Developer Clifton George, who also ran a Ford dealership, and architect Ralph Cameron began a 13-story Gothic Revival tower of doctors' offices on Alamo Plaza in 1924, and for a time it was the tallest building in San Antonio. Cameron lined it with gargoyles, each suffering a different ailment. The doctors moved away by mid-century, and in 1984 hoteliers converted it, naming it for Emily D. West, a young indentured servant of Colonel James Morgan captured by Mexican forces in April 1836.

  • San Antonio River Walk

Est. 1930

Hotel Settles

Big Spring, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2011

Will R. and Lillian Settles struck oil on their ranch in Big Spring's boom of the late 1920s and spent about $500,000 on a 15-story hotel by architect David S. Castle. When it opened in October 1930, no building between El Paso and Fort Worth stood taller; it held 150 rooms, a pharmacy and a dining room. Elvis Presley, Lawrence Welk and President Herbert Hoover stayed. Empty from the early 1980s, it was restored from its original blueprints and reopened in December 2012.

Est. 1956

The Statler Dallas, Curio Collection by Hilton

Dallas, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2018

The Statler Hilton opened in Dallas in January 1956 with a four-day celebration, the Statler chain's final hotel and the city's first major new hotel in 30 years. A cantilevered slab let it wear a curtain wall of glass and teal porcelain; it was the first building with elevator music and had a rooftop heliport. Liberace, Tony Bennett and the Jackson 5 played its ballroom. It closed in 2001, was nearly torn down, and was saved by a $175 million restoration.

  • Deep Ellum

Est. 1970

Le Méridien Fort Worth Downtown

Fort Worth, Texas · Historic Hotels member since 2024

By the mid-1960s downtown Fort Worth was in decline, and the Chamber of Commerce made a new convention center and a renewed Hotel Texas the heart of its revival plan. Sid W. Richardson and Amon G. Carter gave the old hotel to the city through their foundations, and Fort Worth architect Preston Geren Jr. designed a Mid-Century Modern annex of 230 rooms across the street, joined to it by a pedestrian bridge. Hilton later separated the two buildings, and the annex is now this hotel.

Close by

  • Kimbell Art Museum
  • Fort Worth Zoo
  • Sundance Square
