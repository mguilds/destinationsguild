Why visit Singapore

A tiny island that packs in four cultures, world-class food and a skyline from the future — all spotless, safe and gloriously easy to get around.

The Marina Bay skyline

Marina Bay Sands, the ArtScience Museum and the Merlion frame a waterfront that lights up nightly, with the Supertrees of Gardens by the Bay glowing beside it.

Hawker food

Chicken rice, laksa, char kway teow, chilli crab and kaya toast — some of the best-value great food on earth, in hawker centres awarded Michelin stars.

Gardens in a city

The UNESCO-listed Botanic Gardens, the cloud forest domes of Gardens by the Bay, and one of the world's best zoos and night safaris — Singapore is a city in a garden.

Four cultures, one island

Chinatown temples, the mosques and shophouses of Kampong Glam, the colour of Little India, and grand colonial civic buildings — all within a few MRT stops.

How the island divides up

Marina Bay & the Civic District

The showpiece of modern Singapore. Marina Bay Sands and its rooftop infinity pool crown the waterfront, Gardens by the Bay puts its Supertrees and cooled conservatory domes right beside it, and the Merlion, the ArtScience Museum and the Esplanade complete the view. Just inland, the colonial Civic District around the Padang holds the National Gallery, museums and grand old civic buildings.

Chinatown & the Quays

Chinatown layers temples, medicine halls and hawker centres among restored shophouses, and after dark the action shifts to the river — Clarke Quay, Boat Quay and Robertson Quay for dining and drinks along the water. It's a lively, central base within easy reach of the bay.

Orchard Road & the Heritage Quarters

Orchard Road is Southeast Asia's most famous shopping street, a mile of malls and flagship stores, with the Singapore Botanic Gardens a green escape at its western end. Nearby, Little India dazzles with garland stalls and temples, and Kampong Glam wraps the golden-domed Sultan Mosque in hip cafés and Arab Street's textile shops.

Sentosa & the Southern Islands

Sentosa is the island playground: Siloso, Palawan and Tanjong beaches, Universal Studios and Resorts World, aquariums, luge rides and a string of resorts and spas. Reached by cable car, monorail or causeway, it's an easy switch from city sightseeing to sand, pools and family attractions.

Top things to do in Singapore

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Gardens by the Bay

The Supertree Grove and its nightly light show, plus the Cloud Forest and Flower Dome conservatories — futuristic gardens right on Marina Bay.

Marina Bay Sands SkyPark

Ride to the top of the ship-shaped roof for the city's most famous view; the infinity pool is for hotel guests, but the observation deck is open to all.

Feast at a hawker centre

Maxwell, Lau Pa Sat, Old Airport Road or Chinatown Complex — chicken rice, laksa, satay and chilli crab for a handful of dollars.

Chinatown, Little India & Kampong Glam

Temple-hop and shop across three historic quarters, from Buddhist and Hindu temples to the Sultan Mosque and Arab Street.

Singapore Zoo & Night Safari

Consistently rated among the world's finest, with open enclosures by day and the pioneering Night Safari after dark in the northern rainforest.

Sentosa & Universal Studios

Beaches, the S.E.A. Aquarium, the luge and Universal Studios theme park — the island reached by cable car with skyline views.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Singapore is hot and humid all year, sitting just north of the Equator, so there's no cool season — days hover around 31°C and afternoon showers are common.

February to April is the driest, most comfortable stretch, while the November–January monsoon brings the heaviest, though usually short, downpours.

See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Singapore for weather, crowds, events and prices through the year.

Getting there

Singapore Changi Airport (SIN) is one of the world's great hubs and a destination in itself, with the Jewel complex and its indoor waterfall — direct flights arrive from across Asia, Europe, the Middle East, Australia and North America.

From Changi, the city centre is 20–30 minutes by taxi or the MRT metro. Singapore's public transport is clean, cheap and superb; a contactless card or phone taps you through trains and buses.

The currency is the Singapore dollar. Singapore is highly cashless, though a little cash is handy for hawker stalls and markets. Most visitors are visa-free but must submit the free SG Arrival Card online before arrival — confirm the current rules with Singapore's official ICA before booking.

Singapore travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Singapore?

Singapore is hot and humid all year, sitting just north of the Equator, so there is no cool season — but there are drier and busier windows. February to April is the driest and one of the most comfortable stretches, while the year-end monsoon from November to January brings the heaviest, though usually short, afternoon downpours. Many travellers simply pick their dates around events and airfares, carry an umbrella, and duck into air-conditioned malls and museums when the midday heat peaks.

What currency is used in Singapore?

The Singapore dollar (SGD, symbol S$). Singapore is highly cashless — cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay and QR payments are accepted almost everywhere, including many hawker stalls — but it is worth carrying a little cash for small stalls, wet markets and hawker centres. ATMs are everywhere, and when a card machine offers to charge you in your home currency, decline and pay in Singapore dollars to avoid a poor built-in rate.

Do I need a visa to visit Singapore?

Many nationalities, including US, UK, Canadian, Australian and EU citizens, can visit Singapore visa-free for short stays as tourists. All arriving travellers must submit a free SG Arrival Card online within three days before arrival, and passports should be valid for at least six months. Rules change, so always confirm the current entry requirements with Singapore's official Immigration and Checkpoints Authority (ICA) before you travel.

How many days do you need in Singapore?

Three to four days is enough to see the highlights: a day around Marina Bay and Gardens by the Bay, a day exploring Chinatown, Little India and Kampong Glam with hawker meals, and a day on Sentosa or at the Singapore Zoo and Botanic Gardens. Add a day or two if you want to slow down, shop Orchard Road, take a day trip, or use the city as a stopover on the way to the rest of Southeast Asia.

Is Singapore safe for travellers?

Singapore is one of the safest destinations in the world, with very low crime, excellent public transport and high standards of hygiene and healthcare. The main things to watch are the heat and humidity, the strict local laws — littering, jaywalking, smoking outside designated areas and drug offences carry heavy penalties — and staying hydrated. As always, check your government's current travel advice before you go.
