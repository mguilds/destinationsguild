Why visit Singapore

Few cities pack so much into so little space — a future-facing skyline, four cultures at the table, and greenery around every corner.

The Marina Bay skyline

Marina Bay Sands and its rooftop infinity pool, the ArtScience Museum, the nightly light-and-water show, and a waterfront promenade made for an evening stroll.

A garden in the city

Gardens by the Bay and its towering Supertrees, the cloud-forest domes, the UNESCO-listed Botanic Gardens, and street trees that make the whole island feel green.

Hawker food heaven

Some of the best-value great eating on earth — Hainanese chicken rice, laksa, satay and chilli crab, served in bustling open-air hawker centres.

Four cultures, one island

Temples, mosques and churches within a few streets — the shophouses of Chinatown, the colour of Little India and the cafés of Kampong Glam.

How the city divides up

Marina Bay & Orchard Road

Marina Bay is the Singapore of the postcards — Marina Bay Sands with its ship-topped towers and infinity pool, the glowing Supertrees of Gardens by the Bay, and a waterfront promenade that comes alive at night. A short ride inland, Orchard Road runs a tree-lined mile of malls and hotels, the easy, central base for first-timers who want everything on the MRT.

Chinatown, the CBD & Clarke Quay

Chinatown's restored shophouses hide temples, tea houses and hawker gems beneath the towers of the Central Business District. Follow the river to Clarke Quay, where old godowns have become the city's buzziest cluster of riverside restaurants and bars — day-time history, night-time energy, all within a walk.

Sentosa & the south

Sentosa is the island at play — beaches, Universal Studios, the S.E.A. Aquarium and a string of big resorts, reached by cable car, monorail or a short bridge. It's the pick for families and anyone who wants a poolside holiday feel a few minutes from the skyline.

Little India, Kampong Glam & the green edges

Beyond the centre, Little India blazes with colour and spice, and Kampong Glam wraps the golden-domed Sultan Mosque in indie cafés and Haji Lane boutiques. Further out lie the Botanic Gardens, the zoo and Night Safari, and the rustic escape of Pulau Ubin.

Top things to do in Singapore

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Gardens by the Bay

The Supertree Grove and its nightly light show, plus the Cloud Forest and Flower Dome — futuristic gardens that have become the city's signature sight.

Marina Bay Sands SkyPark

Ride to the top for the city's best skyline view, and time your evening around Spectra, the free light-and-water show over the bay.

Feast at a hawker centre

Maxwell, Lau Pa Sat or Old Airport Road — chicken rice, laksa, satay and chilli crab for a few dollars a plate, the heart of Singapore's food culture.

Chinatown, Little India & Kampong Glam

Wander three cultural quarters in a day — temples and tea, marigolds and spice, and the golden dome of the Sultan Mosque above Haji Lane.

Sentosa Island

Beaches, Universal Studios, the S.E.A. Aquarium and a cable car with harbour views — the family-friendly playground just offshore.

Zoo & Night Safari

One of the world's finest open-enclosure zoos by day, and the pioneering Night Safari after dark — a tram ride past nocturnal animals in the rainforest.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

February–April is usually the driest, sunniest stretch, and the most comfortable for sightseeing — though Singapore is warm and humid all year, just north of the equator.

November–January brings the wetter northeast monsoon, but showers are typically short, heavy afternoon bursts rather than washouts, and the year-end festive lights are spectacular.

See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Singapore for weather, festivals and prices through the year.

Getting there

Singapore Changi (SIN) is one of the world's great airports and a major hub, with direct flights from across Asia, Europe, the Middle East, Australia and North America.

From Changi, the city centre is about 20 minutes by taxi or a cheap ride on the MRT metro. Singapore is compact, spotless and superbly connected, so most visitors get around entirely on foot and by train.

The currency is the Singapore dollar, and the city is almost entirely cashless. Many nationalities visit visa-free, but all travellers must file an SG Arrival Card online before arrival — confirm the current rules with Singapore's Immigration and Checkpoints Authority before booking.

Singapore travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Singapore?

Singapore is hot and humid all year, sitting just north of the equator, so there is no true off-season. February to April tends to be the driest and sunniest stretch, ideal for sightseeing. The wettest months are November to January, during the northeast monsoon, though showers are usually short, heavy afternoon downpours rather than all-day rain. Time your trip around big events if you can — Chinese New Year, the Formula 1 night race in September, and the year-end festive lights are all memorable.

What currency is used in Singapore?

The Singapore dollar (SGD, symbol S$). Singapore is highly cashless — cards and contactless phone payments are accepted almost everywhere, from taxis to hawker stalls. Still, carry a little cash for the smallest food stalls and wet markets. ATMs are everywhere, and when a card machine offers to charge you in your home currency, decline and pay in Singapore dollars to avoid a poor built-in rate.

Do I need a visa to visit Singapore?

Many nationalities, including US, UK, Canadian, Australian and EU citizens, can visit Singapore visa-free for short tourist stays. All arriving travellers must submit an electronic SG Arrival Card online within three days before arrival, and passports should be valid for at least six months. Requirements change, so always confirm the current rules with Singapore's Immigration and Checkpoints Authority before you travel.

How many days do you need in Singapore?

Three to four days is enough to see the highlights: Marina Bay and Gardens by the Bay, a hawker-centre feast, Chinatown, Little India and Kampong Glam, plus a day on Sentosa. Five or six days lets you slow down, add the zoo and Night Safari, the Botanic Gardens and Pulau Ubin, and treat Singapore as a relaxed stop rather than a rush. It also works beautifully as a two- or three-day stopover on the way to the wider region.

Is Singapore safe for travellers?

Singapore is one of the safest destinations in the world, with very low street crime, spotless and reliable public transport, and clean tap water. The main thing to know is that laws are strict and firmly enforced — penalties for drugs are severe, and there are fines for things like littering, jaywalking and eating or drinking on the MRT. Take normal precautions with your belongings and you will find it an easy, welcoming city to explore.
