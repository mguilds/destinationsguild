Where to Stay in San José

Where to Stay in San José — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Most Costa Rica trips begin and end in San José, and the city rewards a night at each end rather than a dash straight to the coast. The historic centre holds the gilded National Theatre, the Gold Museum and the Central Market, and is the one part of town you can genuinely walk by day. West of it, Escazú and Santa Ana are prosperous hillside suburbs full of restaurants; Belén and Cariari sit close to the airport in gardens, which is a kindness after a long flight. Below are the city’s most dependable hotels by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Luxury

San José’s grandest addresses sit outside the old centre — in Escazú, or on a coffee estate near the airport.

InterContinental Costa Rica at Multiplaza Mall

$$$$ LuxuryEscazúThree poolsSpa & saunaMall next door

For four decades this has been where San José puts its visiting dignitaries, and the bones still show it: three pools, a proper spa with sauna and steam, and a lobby where the coffee arrives before you have finished asking. It sits in Escazú, the prosperous hillside suburb west of the city, joined by footbridge to a large mall, so dinner, a pharmacy and a bank are a three-minute walk from your room. Be honest with yourself about the geography — downtown and the National Theatre are a 20-to-40-minute taxi ride depending on traffic, and older guidebooks and taxi drivers still call this the Real InterContinental.

Costa Rica Marriott Hotel Hacienda Belen

$$$$ LuxurySan Antonio de BelénCoffee estateTwo poolsAirport shuttle

Built as a Spanish colonial hacienda on a working coffee farm, this is the rare airport-area hotel that feels like a destination rather than a waiting room — tiled courtyards, arcaded walkways and thirty acres of green to walk off the flight. There are two pools with a jacuzzi, a full spa, tennis and a driving range, plus a complimentary airport shuttle. The trade-off is real: you are two miles from the runway and about twenty miles from downtown, so plan on a taxi and a good half-hour each way if you want the museums.

Boutique

Two very different small hotels — a Victorian mansion in the city, and an art-filled house in the western suburbs.

Hotel Grano de Oro

$$$ UpscalePaseo ColónVictorian mansionFamous restaurant39 rooms

A tropical-Victorian mansion turned thirty-nine-room hotel, and for thirty years the sentimental favourite of travellers who want San José to feel like somewhere rather than a layover — dark wood, wrought iron, orchids in the courtyard, and a rooftop terrace with soaking tubs and a view to the volcanoes on a clear morning. The restaurant is genuinely one of the best in the country and worth booking even if you sleep elsewhere. It is walkable by day to La Sabana park and the Paseo Colón museums; at night take a taxi the short distance back, and note there is no swimming pool.

Budget & Value

Two well-run, long-established hotels that cost a fraction of the resort rates — one in a garden, one in the middle of everything.

Hotel Aranjuez

$ BudgetBarrio AranjuezGarden breakfast1930s housesGreat value

Five 1930s wooden houses knitted together into a rambling, plant-filled warren, run with real warmth and priced at a fraction of what it should cost. The breakfast buffet — served in a garden where squirrels and songbirds get on with their morning — is famous among repeat visitors and alone justifies the rate. Rooms are simple and vary a good deal, so ask for one away from the street; Barrio Escalante’s restaurant row is a flat ten-minute walk by day and a very cheap taxi at night.

Hotel Presidente

$$ Mid-rangeDowntownWalk to the Teatro NacionalRooftop terracePets welcome

The best-run hotel actually inside the historic core, on the pedestrianised stretch of Avenida Central within two minutes of the National Theatre, the Gold Museum and the Plaza de la Cultura. Eighty rooms, two restaurants and a rooftop terrace bar, all kept tidier and friendlier than the price suggests. It is downtown, so expect street noise and the ordinary big-city caution after dark — but you can leave the taxi money in your pocket all day, which no suburban hotel allows.

Family-friendly

Space, lawns and swimming pools — both of these sit outside the centre, which is exactly the point.

DoubleTree by Hilton Hotel Cariari San Jose

$$ Mid-rangeCiudad CariariBig poolFree airport shuttleGolf nearby

A low-slung, green-lawned resort of a hotel in the Cariari country-club district, with a large pool complete with rock waterfall and jacuzzi that children will not want to leave. There is a free airport shuttle — the runway is barely five minutes away — a fitness centre, a restaurant and the warm cookie at check-in that reliably wins over the youngest travellers. It is not walkable to anything of interest and downtown is twenty-five to thirty-five minutes by taxi, so treat it as a comfortable base for arrival and departure days.

Ten acres of tropical gardens with labelled plantings, resident toucans and motmots, walking paths, a pool and a tennis court — the sort of place where grandparents can sit with binoculars while grandchildren run themselves tired. Adults-only

An honest word first. San José is not an adults-only town. Several city hotels carry the label but are casino or ‘gentlemen’s’ hotels and are not what most couples have in mind. Only two are places a couple would actually enjoy — and if adults-only peace is the point of your trip, spend one night in the city and take Guanacaste, Manuel Antonio or Arenal for the rest.

Casa de las Tias

$$ Mid-rangeSan Rafael de EscazúFive roomsChildren not acceptedGarden breakfast

A five-room cedar house behind a garden wall in Escazú, run by hosts who greet returning guests by name and serve breakfast under the trees; it is formally adults-only and children are not accepted. Each room holds original Latin American art, the coffee is local, and the whole thing runs on hospitality rather than amenities — do not expect a pool or a gym. It sits in a quiet residential pocket about three miles from the centre, so restaurants are a short taxi ride and downtown twenty to thirty minutes.

There is a courtyard pool, a small bar and restaurant, and massage on request; it is friendly and unpretentious rather than polished. Rohrmoser is a safe residential district near La Sabana park but not a walking base for sightseeing.

Pet-friendly

Two modern hotels in the western suburbs with written pet policies. Always confirm your dog’s weight and the current fee with the hotel before you book.

The newest of the Escazú hotels and the most explicit about dogs — up to two per room, with a bed, blanket and bowl waiting when you arrive, for a one-off fee. Spa & Pool

When the point of the night is a swim and a massage rather than a museum.

Hotel Alta Las Palomas

$$$ UpscaleOld road to Santa AnaValley viewsPool & sauna23 rooms

A whitewashed, Mediterranean-looking hotel stepped down a hillside on the old road between Escazú and Santa Ana, with twenty-three rooms, every one with a balcony over the valley and the city lights beyond. There is an outdoor pool, a sauna and couples’ massage, plus a restaurant good enough that locals drive up for dinner. It is emphatically not walkable to anything, and recent reviews are affectionate but do flag the occasional maintenance lapse — ask for a recently refreshed room.

Walking, taxis and honest advice

Before you book

By daylight San José rewards walking in a few specific pockets: the historic core along Avenida Central between the National Theatre and the Plaza de la Cultura, the coffee-baron mansions of Barrio Amón, the restaurant row of Barrio Escalante, and the leafy edges of La Sabana park. Keep a hand on your bag near the Central Market and give the Coca-Cola bus terminal a wide berth at any hour. After dark the calculus changes — downtown empties out, streetlights are poor, and the sensible move is a ride-hail car or an official red taxi with a yellow triangle and a running meter, which rarely costs more than a few dollars. If you stay in Escazú, Santa Ana, Belén or Cariari, understand that these are car suburbs with no realistic walk into the city at all: safe and pleasant, but you will be ordering rides for everything.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travellers ask most about staying in San José.

Where is the best area to stay in San José?

For sightseeing on foot, stay in the historic centre near the Plaza de la Cultura — the National Theatre, the Gold Museum and the Central Market are all within a few blocks. For restaurants and comfort, Escazú and Santa Ana are the prosperous western suburbs, though you will need a taxi for everything. For an early flight or a late arrival, the garden hotels of Belén and Cariari near the airport are the kindest choice.

Is San José, Costa Rica safe for older travellers?

It is a normal Latin American capital and the great majority of visits pass without incident, but petty theft is common. Walk the centre by day, not after dark; leave nothing visible in a parked car; take nothing to a crowded market you would mind losing; and use a ride-hail car or an official red taxi at night rather than walking. Hotels with a staffed front desk are a safer choice here than a short-term rental.

How many nights do you need in San José?

One or two is plenty for most itineraries — a night on arrival to recover from the flight and see the National Theatre and the Gold Museum, and a night before your flight home so that a landslide or a slow shuttle cannot cost you the plane. Costa Rica’s pleasures are mostly outside the city, so build the trip around the coast, the volcanoes and the cloud forest and let San José be the bookends.

Do I need a car in San José?

No, and most travellers are happier without one. City traffic is heavy, parking is scarce and theft from parked cars is the single most common problem visitors report. Use taxis and ride-hail cars inside the metro area, and pick the car or the private shuttle up on the day you leave for the coast.
