Where to Stay at the Grand Canyon — Grand Canyon Village & Tusayan

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

There are really only two places to sleep near the South Rim. One is Grand Canyon Village, inside the national park, where a handful of historic lodges stand within a short walk of the rim. The other is Tusayan, a small gateway town just outside the park, which the National Park Service puts 7 miles south of Grand Canyon Village on Arizona Highway 64. Inside the park you wake up with the canyon at your door; in Tusayan you get pools and more rooms to choose from, with a short drive in. This page sorts the two and names the places we could confirm today.

Important, checked 20 November 2026: the lodges inside the park were closed to overnight guests from 31 August 2026, after a flash flood damaged the waterline that supplies the South Rim. On 18 November the National Park Service announced that El Tovar, Bright Angel, Kachina, Thunderbird, Maswik and Yavapai lodges reopen to overnight guests on 20 November, that food service will resume gradually, and that guests are still asked to save water. Phantom Ranch, at the bottom of the canyon, and the North Kaibab Trail remain closed with no reopening date. Tusayan’s hotels were not affected. Check nps.gov/grca before you travel.

Choosing Your Area at the Grand Canyon

Two places to stay at the South Rim, each suited to a different trip.

Inside the park, Grand Canyon Village

The historic lodges sit in Grand Canyon Village, and the Park Service says most of them are within walking distance of the rim. Free shuttle buses run all year around the village and out to the viewpoints, so you can park once and leave the car.

This is the choice for sunrise and sunset at the rim without a drive, and for anyone who wants to stay in the buildings that made the Grand Canyon famous. Rooms book up well ahead, especially for spring break, summer and fall weekends.

Just outside, the town of Tusayan

Tusayan lines Highway 64 a short way south of the park’s South Entrance. It has the larger modern hotels, swimming pools, restaurants and the National Geographic IMAX theatre.

You drive or ride into the park each day. In summer the Park Service runs a free Tusayan (Purple) route shuttle from town to the South Rim Visitor Center; riders need a valid park entrance pass.

El Tovar Hotel

Inside the parkOn the rimHistoric

The grand old hotel of the South Rim, built right on the canyon edge. Its own website says it opened in 1905, was designed for the Santa Fe Railway as a cross between a Swiss chalet and a Norwegian villa, and was named a National Historic Landmark in 1987. It has 78 rooms, many of them suites, with a dining room and lounge. One thing families should know: the hotel says its rooms are reached by stairs only and there are no elevators (checked 20 November 2026). Trip.com lists it as El Tovar Grand Canyon - Inside the Park.

Bright Angel Lodge & Cabins

Inside the parkTrailheadHistoric

A rustic lodge designed in 1935 by the architect Mary Colter, at the top of the Bright Angel Trail. Its own website describes everything from simple historic rooms, some with a shared bath and no television, to historic cabins, and a family-friendly café. It says plainly that rooms with canyon views are very limited and cannot be guaranteed. The pick for walkers and for anyone who wants history at a gentler price than El Tovar.

Maswik Lodge

Inside the parkFamilyKitchenettes

A larger, more modern lodge in the pine forest, which its own website places a quarter-mile walk or bus ride from the canyon’s edge, with a food court and pizza pub in the main building. Its Maswik South buildings include kitchenette rooms with a microwave and a two-burner cooktop, and an elevator to the second floor; Maswik North is stairs only (checked 20 November 2026). A practical inside-the-park base for a family.

Yavapai Lodge

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Grand Canyon details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to the South Rim

Most visitors drive. The Park Service says the South Entrance is 6 miles north of Tusayan, about 60 minutes from Interstate 40 at Williams and 90 minutes from Flagstaff, and roughly four hours from Phoenix, the nearest large city with a major airport. Tusayan has its own airport, and the Grand Canyon Railway brings trains to the South Rim’s historic depot.

Beat the entrance line

The Park Service warns that during spring break, summer and fall weekends there can be waits of up to two hours at the South Entrance between 10 am and 4 pm. Staying inside the park, or arriving early from Tusayan, gets you past it.

South Rim or North Rim?

They are not the same trip. The Park Service says the South Rim is open all year and gets about nine in ten visitors, while the North Rim is open only in season and is about 212 miles and four and a half hours away by road. Everything on this page is on the South Rim side.

When to go

The South Rim is open all year. Spring, summer and fall are the busy seasons, and the Park Service says lodging books up well in advance then. Winter brings snow and winter driving conditions, and roads can close for a while during storms; the park’s road line is 928-638-7496.

Grand Canyon Village Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best place to stay at the Grand Canyon?

Inside the park at Grand Canyon Village if you want the rim on your doorstep for sunrise and sunset; most of the lodges there are within walking distance of the rim. In Tusayan, just outside the South Entrance, if you want a pool or more rooms to choose from.

Are the hotels inside Grand Canyon National Park open?

The National Park Service closed overnight lodging inside the park from 31 August 2026 after flood damage to the South Rim waterline, and announced that the South Rim lodges reopen to overnight guests on 20 November 2026. Phantom Ranch remains closed. Check nps.gov/grca for the latest before you travel.

How far is Tusayan from the Grand Canyon?

The Park Service puts the South Entrance 6 miles north of Tusayan and Grand Canyon Village 7 miles north of town on Highway 64.

How far is the Grand Canyon from Phoenix?

The Park Service says the South Rim is roughly a four-hour drive from Phoenix, about 90 minutes from Flagstaff and about 60 minutes from Williams.

Does El Tovar have an elevator?

No. El Tovar says its rooms are reached by stairs only and there are no elevators. Maswik South, inside the park, has an elevator to its second floor.
