Where to Stay in Freeport & Lucaya — What Is Actually Open

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Written in August 2026. Every hotel named here was checked against the operator’s own information this month — and the closed ones are named, because the booking sites are still selling them.

This is the most cautionary page in our Bahamas book, and we would rather you read it before you book than after you arrive. Grand Bahama has not had an easy decade. Hurricane Matthew took one resort in 2016, Hurricane Dorian flattened the airport and much else in September 2019, and in October 2025 the Grand Lucayan — the island’s flagship, and its last luxury hotel — closed for good. Demolition of the site began in April 2026.

What remains is a small, friendly, inexpensive island with about eight genuinely trading hotels, no luxury tier, no adults-only property, no pet-friendly hotel, and exactly one all-inclusive. That is not a reason to write it off — Port Lucaya Marketplace is lively and walkable, the diving is superb, the beaches are long and empty, and a room here costs a third of what Nassau charges. It is a reason to choose deliberately.

The one warning we would underline: the booking websites for this island are unusually unreliable. Lighthouse Pointe and Memories Grand Bahama are both closed and both still bookable online. Before you pay for any hotel in Freeport, telephone the property and hear someone confirm your dates.

Hurricane season, honestly — and it matters more here. The Atlantic season runs 1 June to 30 November, with the genuine peak mid-August through October. Grand Bahama has more reason than most islands to take that seriously: Hurricane Dorian sat over this island as a Category 5 on 1–3 September 2019 and the recovery is still visibly unfinished seven years later. The 2026 season has been very quiet and NOAA has held to a below-normal forecast all year, but a quiet season still needs only one storm. Luxury

We have to be straight with you: there is no luxury hotel in Freeport or Lucaya in 2026. The one property that qualified has closed. We are not going to dress up a three-star resort as something it is not.

What You Need to Know Before You Book

The honest verdict: should you come at all?

We would rather answer this plainly than sell you a week. Grand Bahama in 2026 is principally a cruise stop with a small residual holiday market. There is no luxury hotel, no adults-only hotel, no pet-friendly hotel and one all-inclusive. Roughly eight hotels are genuinely trading; only two of them rate above four out of five. The old Lucaya resort core is an active demolition site, and the airport is still working out of temporary facilities.

And yet. Port Lucaya Marketplace is open, lively and safe, with real restaurants, shops and live music on flat ground you can walk. The diving and snorkelling are excellent — Deadman’s Reef and the reefs off Paradise Cove — the Lucayan National Park is beautiful, the food is Bahamian rather than resort-manufactured, and rooms cost a third of what Nassau charges. If you want a quiet, inexpensive, unpolished week with good water and good local food, book Pelican Bay and walk to Port Lucaya every evening. If you want an all-inclusive on a beach, book Viva Fortuna. If you want luxury, a spa, an adults-only resort or to bring a dog, go to Nassau, Paradise Island, Exuma or Harbour Island, and look at Grand Bahama again in 2028.

Do not trust the booking sites here

We say this on no other page in the Guild library. On Grand Bahama, the major travel websites are still selling Lighthouse Pointe and Memories Grand Bahama, both of which are closed — Memories since 2016. Before you pay for any Freeport hotel, telephone the property and hear a human being confirm your dates. It takes four minutes and it is the single most valuable thing on this page.

What is being built, and when

Three large projects are under way and none of them is a hotel you can book this year. Carnival’s Celebration Key opened in July 2025 at Sharp Rocks, twenty miles east of Port Lucaya — a six-hundred-million-dollar private beach destination for Carnival ships, with a pier expansion finishing in July 2026. It brings day visitors, not overnight guests, and there is no accommodation there. Royal Caribbean bought the shuttered Xanadu Beach Resort in May 2026 and is building a private beach club on the site; Xanadu will not reopen as a hotel, and completion is unlikely before 2028. And the Grand Lucayan site is being cleared for a redevelopment that is meant to include a five-hundred-and-fifty-room Hilton, an MSC beach club, two casinos and a marina, targeted at early 2028. That is where new hotel rooms will come from. Treat every date with healthy scepticism.

Getting there

Grand Bahama International Airport (FPO) was very badly damaged by Hurricane Dorian in September 2019 and has been operating out of temporary facilities ever since; reconstruction finally mobilised in January 2026, so expect a building site and an unglamorous arrival for the next year or two. Flights are thin but useful: Miami with American, Fort Lauderdale and Orlando with Bahamasair and others, seasonal Charlotte, and frequent thirty-minute hops from Nassau. There is also the Baleària fast ferry from Fort Lauderdale, about three hours, fares from around $235 — a pleasant alternative, and the one option that carries pets. There is no passenger ferry from Nassau.

Getting around

Port Lucaya and its immediate hotels are walkable; nothing else is. Taxis are the sensible way to move between Freeport town, Lucaya and the beaches, and fares are set by zone rather than metered — agree the price before you get in. A rental car makes more sense here than in Nassau if you intend to see the Lucayan National Park or drive out to West End, but remember the Bahamas drives on the left while most rental cars are left-hand-drive American models. Roads outside town are unlit at night.

Money and safety

The Bahamian dollar is pegged one-for-one to the US dollar, both circulate, and there is nothing to change. VAT is ten per cent. The US State Department advisory of 28 July 2026 places the Bahamas at Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution, and names Freeport alongside Nassau as where crime concentrates — in practice that means the ordinary sense you would use in any unfamiliar town: stay in the tourist districts, do not walk deserted beaches after dark, and use licensed taxis. Firearms and ammunition are absolutely prohibited, including a stray round in a bag.

Entry paperwork

Americans, Canadians and Britons need no visa; a valid passport is the practical requirement for arriving by air. A Digital Arrival Card entered a soft launch on 5 May 2026 on selected flights and runs alongside the paper form for now. Freeport, like Nassau, offers US preclearance when it is staffed, so allow extra time for your flight home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Grand Lucayan open?

No. The Grand Lucayan — including Lighthouse Pointe, its last operating wing — stopped taking guests on 20 October 2025. Staff were let go over the following winter, the electricity was disconnected in March 2026, and demolition of the site began on 14 April 2026. A large redevelopment including a 550-room Hilton and an MSC beach club is planned, with completion talked about for early 2028; that project has slipped repeatedly and we would not book around the date. Several major travel websites still sell Lighthouse Pointe rooms. Those listings are dead.

How many hotels are actually open in Freeport?

About eight that we would call genuinely trading: Pelican Bay, Bell Channel Inn, Castaways, Viva Fortuna Beach, The Marlin at Taino Beach, Flamingo Bay, Ocean Reef and Island Seas. Only two of them — Pelican Bay and Bell Channel Inn — rate above four out of five. The rest cluster around three, with recurring complaints this year about damp, maintenance and amenities that no longer work. That is the honest state of the island's hotel stock.

Is there an all-inclusive resort on Grand Bahama?

One: Viva Fortuna Beach by Wyndham, on twelve hundred feet of private beach at Fortuna Beach, with five dining venues, drinks, entertainment, non-motorised watersports and a children's club included, typically $80–$125 per person per night. There is no second one. Lighthouse Pointe closed in October 2025 and Memories Grand Bahama has been shut since Hurricane Matthew in 2016 — both are still, wrongly, bookable online.

Is Freeport worth a holiday, or is it just a cruise stop?

Both answers are true and it depends what you want. If you want luxury, a spa, an adults-only resort, a wide choice of restaurants inside your hotel, or to bring a dog, Freeport cannot give you those in 2026 and Nassau, Paradise Island, Exuma or Harbour Island can. If you want a quiet, genuinely inexpensive week with superb diving and snorkelling, empty beaches, the Lucayan National Park, real Bahamian food and a walkable evening at Port Lucaya Marketplace, it is very good value indeed and much less crowded than Nassau.

What is Celebration Key and does it affect my stay?

Celebration Key is Carnival's six-hundred-million-dollar private beach destination at Sharp Rocks, on the eastern end of Grand Bahama, which opened in July 2025 and is expanding to four berths in July 2026. It is twenty miles from Port Lucaya, it is exclusive to Carnival passengers, and it has no hotels — guests sleep on the ship. So it will not affect a stay in Freeport or Lucaya directly. What it does mean is a great many more day visitors on the island as a whole, and rather more construction traffic.

Can I bring my dog to Freeport?

Not practically. No hotel in Freeport or Lucaya accepts pets, and Castaways states that it takes neither pets nor service animals. The only pet-friendly accommodation on the whole of Grand Bahama is at West End, twenty-five miles away, at a remote East End fishing lodge, or in two very small guesthouses. Nassau has at least eight pet-friendly hotels and is the sensible alternative. Whichever island you choose, every animal entering the Bahamas needs an import permit from the Department of Agriculture, obtained in advance, plus a veterinary health certificate issued within ten days of travel.

How do I get to Freeport?

By air, mainly from Miami with American, from Fort Lauderdale and Orlando with Bahamasair and smaller carriers, seasonally from Charlotte, and by frequent thirty-minute hop from Nassau. Grand Bahama International Airport is still working from temporary facilities after Hurricane Dorian in 2019 — reconstruction only mobilised in January 2026 — so arrivals are functional rather than comfortable. There is also the Baleària fast ferry from Fort Lauderdale, roughly three hours, from about $235, which is the only option that carries pets. There is no passenger ferry from Nassau.
