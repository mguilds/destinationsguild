Melbourne’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Melbourne — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Melbourne rewards walking more than almost any Australian city, so the address matters. The CBD grid holds the laneways, the arcades, the galleries and the free tram zone, and it is where most visitors should sleep. Southbank, one bridge over the Yarra, adds river views and the arts precinct; South Yarra and Prahran are leafier, calmer and full of good restaurants; and Carlton and Fitzroy put you among the terraces and the coffee. We’ve grouped Melbourne’s best-run hotels by the kind of stay you’re after — each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com.

Luxury

Melbourne’s luxury hotels have gone vertical. The best rooms in the city now sit sixty to eighty floors up, trading harbour glitter for an enormous, quietly beautiful view across the bay, the gardens and the sporting grounds.

The Ritz-Carlton, Melbourne

$$$$ LuxuryCBD westLevel 80 sky lobbyInfinity pool

The highest hotel in Australia occupies the upper floors of a tower on Spencer Street, with a level 80 sky lobby, an indoor infinity pool that appears to spill toward Port Phillip Bay, and 257 rooms that all start above the 200-metre mark. Service is polished and unhurried, the club lounge is genuinely worth the upgrade, and Southern Cross station and the Docklands are at the foot of the building. If you want one unforgettable view in Melbourne, this is it — ask for a bay-facing room.

Crown Towers Melbourne

$$$$ LuxurySouthbankCrown SpaRiverside dining

The grande dame of Southbank, sitting on the Yarra’s south bank with generously proportioned rooms, marble bathrooms with deep tubs, an indoor pool and the large Crown Spa, plus a whole promenade of restaurants and shops downstairs. It is a self-contained world, which suits travelers who want everything under one roof and a level, sheltered walk to dinner. The city centre is a five-minute stroll over the Queensbridge or Princes Bridge footbridges.

Boutique

Melbourne does small hotels two ways: the grand Victorian original, and the tightly edited modern house with a dozen or so rooms. Both are more interesting than a chain, and both put you closer to the city’s real character.

The Hotel Windsor

$$$ UpscaleSpring StreetOpened 1883Afternoon tea

Australia’s great surviving grand Victorian hotel, opposite Parliament House on Spring Street since 1883 — all high ceilings, stained glass, a sweeping staircase and a famous afternoon tea in the Grand Ballroom. Rooms are traditional rather than minimal, which many older travelers much prefer, and the location is superb: the Parliament tram and station, the Princess Theatre, the Treasury Gardens and the top of Bourke Street are all within a block or two.

United Places Botanic Gardens

$$$$ LuxurySouth Yarra12 suitesGardens opposite

Twelve one- and two-bedroom suites facing the Royal Botanic Gardens on Domain Road, each with its own kitchen, a marble bathroom and a calm, understated Australian design sensibility. There is no lobby bustle and no tour groups — just a small team, an excellent restaurant downstairs and one of the loveliest morning walks in the city directly across the road. Perfect for a quiet, grown-up few days with the Shrine, the gallery and the tram into town all close by.

Budget & Value

Melbourne is kinder to a budget than Sydney, and the free tram zone means a central room genuinely saves you money as well as walking. These two are the reliable choices inside the grid.

Family-friendly

Families and multi-generation groups want the same three things in Melbourne: a pool, a room that is not a shoebox, and somewhere to eat without a booking. Southbank and the CBD’s larger hotels deliver all three.

Crown Promenade Melbourne

$$$ UpscaleSouthbankRooftop poolSkybridge to Crown

The friendlier, better-value sister to Crown Towers, with generously sized rooms, a heated rooftop pool and gym, and an enclosed skybridge that carries you into the Crown complex’s cinemas, food courts and restaurants without stepping outside — a real blessing on a wet Melbourne afternoon. The Yarra promenade, the aquarium and the arts precinct are all a walk away, and the Clarendon Street trams run past the door.

Adults-only

As in Sydney, Melbourne has no significant hotel that enforces a strict adults-only age policy, and we will not invent one to fill a heading. The grown-up stay here is a matter of choosing well: a twelve-suite boutique house, a club-level floor high in one of the towers, a room away from the pool deck and the function rooms. Say what you want when you book — Melbourne front desks are unusually obliging about quiet floors.

Pet-friendly

Melbourne is one of the most dog-friendly cities in the country, and its hotels have followed. Both of these run proper programmes rather than merely tolerating a dog — but both cap the size and charge a fee, so confirm as you book.

Ovolo’s South Yarra house runs the group’s v.i.pooch programme — a plush dog bed, a mat with bowls, toys and treats, and staff who are visibly pleased to see your dog. The hotel itself is bold and playful, with a complimentary minibar, free breakfast and a rooftop bar, and it sits on Chapel Street with the Royal Botanic Gardens, the Como House parkland and plenty of dog-friendly cafes within an easy walk.

Spa & pool

Melbourne’s weather can turn in an afternoon, which is precisely why a serious pool and spa earn their keep here more than in most Australian cities. These two are the best indoor sanctuaries in town.

Park Hyatt Melbourne

$$$$ LuxuryParliament / East MelbourneRoman-style poolVery large rooms

Tucked behind St Patrick’s Cathedral on the quiet eastern edge of the grid, the Park Hyatt has the largest standard rooms in Melbourne, deep marble bathrooms, and a handsome Roman-inspired indoor pool with a spa, steam room and a proper day spa attached. It is a low-key, adult sort of hotel, and the Fitzroy and Treasury Gardens on the doorstep make it a lovely base for slow mornings and long walks.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask us most when choosing where to stay in Melbourne.

What is the best area to stay in Melbourne for first-time visitors?

The CBD is the clear first choice, because Melbourne is a walking and tram city and the grid puts the laneways, the arcades, Federation Square, the National Gallery and Queen Victoria Market within a few blocks of one another. Southbank, just across the Yarra, gives you river views, the arts precinct and the Crown complex a short bridge walk from the centre. South Yarra and Fitzroy suit repeat visitors who want leafy streets, good restaurants and a quieter night.

How much do Melbourne hotels cost?

Melbourne is generally a little cheaper than Sydney. Expect roughly 130 to 220 Australian dollars a night for clean budget and value rooms, about 220 to 380 for comfortable four-star and apartment stays, and from around 500 upward for the top suites and sky-high luxury. Rates spike around the Australian Open in January, the Melbourne Cup carnival in early November and the Grand Prix, and they are softest in the cold, quiet weeks of June and July.

Is the free tram zone useful for hotel guests?

Very. Melbourne’s Free Tram Zone covers the whole central grid, Docklands, the Queen Victoria Market and Federation Square, and you pay nothing at all when you board and alight inside it, with no ticket or card needed. If your hotel is in the CBD or Docklands you can reach most of the sightseeing without spending a cent on transport. Step outside the zone, to South Yarra or St Kilda for example, and you will need a myki card, so buy one at a station or 7-Eleven on arrival.

Which Melbourne hotels take dogs?

Ovolo South Yarra runs the v.i.pooch programme with a dog bed, bowls, treats and toys included, and The Langham, Melbourne on Southbank accepts both cats and dogs for a pet fee with its Pampered Pets package, including a bed, bowls and a room-service menu for pets. QT Melbourne and Hyatt Centric Melbourne also take smaller dogs. Every one of them sets a weight limit and a fee and requires the specific pet-friendly room type, so confirm before you pay.

Are there adults-only hotels in Melbourne?

Melbourne has no significant hotel with a strict adults-only age policy, and we would rather tell you that than name one that does not exist. For a grown-up trip, choose a small boutique house such as United Places or a high, quiet club-level floor in one of the luxury towers, and lean on the city’s spas, wine bars and restaurants. Ask for a room away from lifts, function rooms and pool decks when you book.

Do I need a car in Melbourne?

No. Trams, trains and buses cover the city and the inner suburbs on the tap-and-go myki card, the central grid is flat and easy on foot, and CBD parking is expensive. Hire a car only for the Great Ocean Road, the Yarra Valley, the Mornington Peninsula or Phillip Island, and pick it up on the morning you leave town. Note that some central intersections require the unusual hook turn, which is another good reason to let the trams do the work.
