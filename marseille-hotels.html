Where to Stay in Marseille — Best Hotels & Areas

Where you stay is a choice of mood: the buzz of the old port, the arty cobbled lanes of Le Panier on the hill, or the sea air of the Corniche. The areas, and a word about the hills and the sea

Marseille is bigger and hillier than it first looks, and it rewards choosing your base with care. The Vieux-Port and its flat quays are the walkable heart and where most hotels are; Le Panier, the old town on the hill just north, is enchanting but steep and cobbled; La Canebière and the market streets are central and lively; and the Corniche runs south along the sea to the beaches, calmer and scenic but a tram or bus from the centre. We say plainly under each hotel how central it is, whether there are hills, and whether a lift is confirmed.

The Vieux-Port (Old Port)

The beating heart of Marseille — the rectangular old harbour ringed by cafes, the morning fish market, and ferries to the islands. Flat along the quays, central and walkable, with most of the city's hotels: our first suggestion for older travellers.

Le Panier

The ancient old town on the hill just north of the port — narrow cobbled lanes, artists' studios, small squares and the grand Vieille Charité. Wonderfully atmospheric, but steep and uneven underfoot, so weigh it if hills are a concern.

La Canebière & Noailles

The grand boulevard sweeping down to the port and the vivid North-African market streets of Noailles beside it — central, lively and full of life, if busier and grittier than the quays. Handy for the metro and everything on foot.

The Corniche & the beaches

The sea road south of the port — the Corniche Kennedy, the little Prado and Pointe Rouge beaches, and views to the Frioul islands. Calmer, scenic and by the water, but a tram or bus ride from the centre.

Marseille — common questions

Which area of Marseille should an older traveller stay in?

Around the Vieux-Port is our first suggestion: central, flat along the quays, walkable, and where most of the good hotels, the ferries and the restaurants are. Le Panier, the old town on the hill just north, is enchanting but steep and cobbled, so choose it only if hills are no trouble. For sea air and quiet, the Corniche to the south is scenic and calmer, but you will rely on a tram, bus or taxi to reach the centre.

Is Marseille a walkable city?

The Vieux-Port and its quays are flat and very walkable, and a good metro, tram and bus network fills in the rest. But Marseille is hilly away from the water: Le Panier and the climb to Notre-Dame de la Garde are steep, and a little tourist train and the buses exist precisely to save your legs. The Corniche and the beaches are a bus or tram ride from the centre rather than a walk.

How do I get to Marseille, and in from the airport?

By train, the TGV from Paris reaches Marseille Saint-Charles station in about three hours and ten minutes, and from the station it is a short metro ride or taxi down to the Vieux-Port. From Marseille Provence airport, the airport shuttle bus (the navette) runs to Saint-Charles station in around twenty-five to thirty minutes, where you pick up the metro or a taxi; a taxi the whole way takes roughly thirty to forty minutes.

What will I pay in Marseille, and are cards accepted?

France uses the euro, and cards — including contactless and phone payments — are taken almost everywhere, so you need little cash beyond a few euros for a market, a ferry or a tip. A small nightly taxe de séjour (tourist tax) is usually added at check-out rather than shown in the booked rate. When a terminal offers to charge you in dollars, decline and pay in euros to avoid a poor built-in exchange rate.

Is Marseille safe?

Marseille has a big-city reputation, but the areas visitors use — the Vieux-Port, Le Panier, the Corniche and the main museums — are perfectly fine to walk with ordinary city sense, day or night. The sensible cautions are the usual ones for a large port: keep valuables in a zipped inside pocket, be alert to pickpockets around Saint-Charles station and in the crowded Noailles market streets, and take a taxi rather than wandering unfamiliar districts late at night. Treated with normal care, it is a warm, rewarding city.
