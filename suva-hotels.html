Where to Stay in Suva

Where to Stay in Suva — Victoria Parade & the Seawall

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Hardly any Fiji holiday includes Suva, and that is a pity and also fair enough. The capital sits on a peninsula on the wet southeast corner of Viti Levu, four or five hours by road from Nadi, and it is a working city of ministries, container wharves, a university and a great deal of rain rather than a resort town. There is no beach. What there is instead is the best museum in the country, a colonial seafront, the liveliest market in Fiji, rainforest twenty minutes up the hill, and the strong sense — rare on this side of a holiday — of being in a real place rather than a prepared one.

Choosing Your Area in Suva

The city is small and the choice is simple: the seafront, the ridge above it, or out of town.

Victoria Parade and the seafront

The parade runs along the harbour past the Government Buildings, Albert Park and Thurston Gardens, and it is where Suva keeps its colonial architecture, its offices and most of its hotels. Staying here puts the museum, the market, the restaurants and the seawall walk within a few minutes on foot, which is the whole argument for being in Suva at all.

Grand Pacific Hotel Holiday Inn Suva

Up the hill, around Gordon and Malcolm Streets

A block or two inland the ground rises, and the hotels up here trade the harbour view for a quieter night and a lower rate. Everything on the parade is still a short downhill walk, and the walk back up is the price.

Tanoa Plaza Hotel

Lami Bay, west of the city

Lami is a separate town on the bay about fifteen minutes west of central Suva, and the hotels there are resort-shaped in a way that nothing in the city centre is — grounds, a pool, a view over the water to the hills. The trade is that you will drive or take a taxi for dinner and for everything on the parade.

Novotel Suva Lami Bay — in Lami, not central Suva.

Everything else in the city

Suva has a good deal of plain, well-run business accommodation that never appears in a holiday brochure and is often the sensible answer for a single night between a flight and a drive.

Know Before You Book

What a first-time visitor to Suva is usually surprised by.

Suva is the wet side of the island, and it is not close

The southeast of Viti Levu sits on the windward side of the central range and catches the trade winds, so Suva is markedly wetter than Nadi and rain is possible on any day of any month. Pack for it and plan indoor things; the city is built for weather and carries on regardless.

It is also four to five hours from Nadi by road, on either the Queens Road along the Coral Coast or the Kings Road around the north. Most people who visit Suva arrive on the way to or from somewhere else, or fly into Nausori airport northeast of the city.

What to actually do here

The Fiji Museum in Thurston Gardens is the single best reason to come: pottery that pushes Fijian settlement back three thousand years, an ocean-going double-hulled canoe under its own roof, and a clear-eyed telling of the colonial century. The municipal market is the other, especially on a Saturday morning, and the kava stalls at the back of it are an education.

Up the hill, Colo-i-Suva Forest Park is about twenty minutes north of the centre: rainforest, marked trails and natural swimming pools, and the quickest way in Fiji to go from a city street to a forest.

It is a city, and behaves like one

Suva is one of the larger cities in the Pacific islands and has a city’s ordinary rules: the central streets are busy and perfectly straightforward by day, and it is sensible to take a taxi after dark rather than walk unfamiliar streets, as you would anywhere. Taxis are metered, plentiful and cheap.

Beqa Lagoon is an hour away

Pacific Harbour, about an hour west along the Queens Road, is the base for diving Beqa Lagoon — including the well-known baited shark dive — and for rafting the Navua river. If you are coming to Suva for more than the museum, it is the obvious day out.

Suva Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Is Suva worth visiting?

For a certain kind of traveller, very much. Suva has the Fiji Museum, a colonial seafront, the country's best market, rainforest with swimming holes twenty minutes from the centre, and the feel of a real working capital rather than a resort strip. It has no beach and a great deal of rain. If your week in Fiji is about water and sand, skip it; if you want to understand the country you are in, give it two nights.

How far is Suva from Nadi?

Four to five hours by road, either south along the Queens Road through the Coral Coast or north around the Kings Road. The Queens Road is the usual choice and the more scenic, and it lets you break the drive on the Coral Coast. There are also short domestic flights between Nadi and Nausori airport outside Suva.

Where is the best place to stay in Suva?

On or just off Victoria Parade. The seafront puts the museum, Albert Park, the Government Buildings, the market and the seawall walk within a few minutes on foot, and walking is how Suva is best seen. The hotels a block or two up the hill are quieter and cheaper for the same access. Lami Bay, fifteen minutes west, is the choice if you want grounds and a pool rather than a city centre.

Does it really rain that much in Suva?

Yes. Suva sits on the windward southeast corner of Viti Levu and is substantially wetter than Nadi and the west coast, with rain possible in any month and heaviest between November and April. It rarely rains all day, and the city is entirely used to it, but do not plan a Suva trip around sunshine.

Is Suva safe for visitors?

Suva is an ordinary city and ordinary city sense applies. The central streets are busy and straightforward during the day, and petty theft is the usual risk rather than anything worse. After dark, take a taxi rather than walking unfamiliar streets — taxis are metered, everywhere and inexpensive. Check your own government's current travel advice before you go, as you would anywhere.
