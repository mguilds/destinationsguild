Where to Stay in Jakarta

Where to Stay in Jakarta — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Jakarta has no single centre, so the district you pick shapes the whole visit. Thamrin and Menteng form the old diplomatic heart — leafy streets, the National Monument, the good museums and the grand old hotels — and it is the easiest base for a first trip and the most walkable on foot. Sudirman and SCBD, a few minutes south, are the modern business spine: glass towers, the best malls and restaurants and the MRT line, but little to see at street level. Kuningan is much the same, with the embassies. Kota Tua, the Dutch old town, deserves a half-day for its colonial squares but has almost no good hotels. And Soekarno-Hatta airport sits a full 45 to 90 minutes west of all of it, which is why airport hotels matter more here than in most cities. Luxury

The city’s best hotels cluster around the Bundaran HI roundabout, where Thamrin meets Menteng — central, and mercifully close to the MRT.

Mandarin Oriental, Jakarta

$$$$ LuxuryThamrinClassicCentral

A grande dame of some 270 rooms on the Bundaran HI roundabout, at the point where Thamrin becomes Menteng and the National Monument is a short ride away. It is the city’s most quietly assured luxury hotel: unhurried service, a well-regarded spa, and a location that lets you reach the museums, the Grand Indonesia mall and the MRT without a long crawl through traffic. Best for travellers who want familiar international standards and a genuinely central address rather than a tower in the business district.

Boutique

Menteng’s old villas and embassies make it the one part of Jakarta where a small hotel with character makes sense.

The Hermitage, a Tribute Portfolio Hotel, Jakarta

$$$ UpscaleMenteng1920s buildingRooftop pool

A restored 1920s Dutch colonial building on a quiet Menteng street, converted into a ninety-room hotel under Marriott’s Tribute Portfolio. The original architecture has been kept, period lift included, alongside a rooftop pool and bar, and the surrounding streets of embassies and old villas are among the few in Jakarta that are pleasant to walk. Ideal for travellers who want character and Marriott reliability in the same building, and who value quiet over being next to a mall.

Budget & Value

Adults-only

We will be straight with you: Jakarta has essentially no adults-only hotels. It is a business and family capital rather than a resort destination, and no established property in the city operates a no-children policy. Booking sites appear to offer them, but those tags are automatic filters applied to ordinary hotels that openly accept children, and we will not repeat them as fact. For a genuinely calm, grown-up stay, a club-floor room at The Dharmawangsa or Park Hyatt Jakarta is the honest answer; true adults-only hotels in Indonesia are found in Bali and Lombok. Best for a Layover

Soekarno-Hatta is a long way from the city and the traffic is unpredictable. For an early flight or a tight connection, stay out here rather than downtown.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which area of Jakarta should I stay in?

For a first visit, stay in Thamrin or Menteng. You are near the National Monument, the museums, the MRT and the best of the older hotels, and it is the one central district where walking is pleasant. Choose Sudirman, SCBD or Kuningan if your business is there. Stay at the airport only for a very early flight.

How long does it take to get from the airport into Jakarta?

Allow 45 minutes at quiet times and up to 90 minutes or more in heavy traffic — Jakarta traffic is genuinely unpredictable. The airport rail link to Sudirman and Manggarai is faster and far more reliable than a car at rush hour. For an early morning departure, an airport hotel the night before is money well spent.

Is Jakarta worth spending time in, or should I go straight to Bali?

Two nights is enough for most visitors: the National Museum, the Monas monument, Kota Tua’s colonial squares and a good dinner. It is a working megacity rather than a sightseeing one, and many travellers use it simply as a connection to Yogyakarta or Bali. If your time is short, spend it in Yogyakarta or Bali instead.

How do I get around Jakarta?

Use the MRT where it goes — it runs beneath Thamrin and Sudirman and is clean, cheap and air-conditioned. Otherwise use the Grab or Gojek apps rather than hailing a car, so the fare is fixed and the route recorded. Do not ride a motorcycle taxi, and do not plan to walk long distances: pavements are uneven, often absent, and the heat is considerable.

When is the best time to visit Jakarta?

The drier months, roughly May to September, are the most comfortable; January and February bring heavy rain and localised flooding. The city is hot and humid all year. See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Indonesia.
