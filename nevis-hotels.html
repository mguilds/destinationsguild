Where to Stay on Nevis

By the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Nevis is a single volcano in the sea with a road around the bottom of it, a small capital at Charlestown facing St Kitts, and no traffic light anywhere. The hotels are mostly old sugar estates turned into small inns up the slopes, plus a handful of beach properties on the western shore. This is one page rather than three because the island is thirty-six square miles and you can drive round it in under an hour.

Where to stay in Nevis — the areas

Plantation inns under the trees, beach resorts on the western shore, and a capital you could walk across twice before lunch.

Charlestown and Pinney’s Beach

The capital is a small, pretty, low-rise town on the west coast where the ferry from St Kitts comes in — a few streets of stone and gingerbread, a market, the museum at Alexander Hamilton’s birthplace. Pinney’s Beach runs north from the edge of town: long, calm, palm-backed, facing the sunset and St Kitts across the channel. This is where the island’s largest resort is, and it is the most convenient base if you want to be able to walk to a town.

The slopes — the plantation inns

Scattered around the mountain at a few hundred feet are the old estate houses: stone sugar mills, long verandahs, gardens that have had two centuries to fill in. They are small, several of them well under twenty rooms, and they are cooler than the coast. None of them is on a beach, though most run a shuttle or keep a beach club down on the water. Staying up here is a deliberate choice for quiet and for the gardens, not a compromise on price.

Oualie Bay and the north-west

Towards the northern end of the west coast, Oualie Bay is a sheltered cove with the island’s diving operation on it and the water taxis across to St Kitts. Quieter than Pinney’s, closer to the airport, and the obvious base if you have come to dive.

The windward side

The eastern and northern coasts face the Atlantic and have the island’s wilder beaches and a scattering of old estates. There is very little accommodation over here, which is rather the point. It is fifteen minutes from anywhere else on the island.

What a room costs here

Nevis runs expensive for the Caribbean and it does so deliberately. There are very few rooms on the whole island, the plantation inns are small, and nothing here is built at scale. That makes the seasons bite harder than they do on St Kitts: in the dry months from mid-December to mid-April the good inns genuinely sell out months ahead, and there is no overflow to fall back on.

July to early November is low season and the difference is substantial. Some of the smaller inns close for part of September. Late April, May and the second half of November are the Guild’s value windows here as elsewhere in the country.

The one local distortion: Culturama, which the Culturama Secretariat’s own site gives as 22 July to 3 August 2027, fills Charlestown and lifts rates across the island for a fortnight in the middle of low season. If the festival is why you are coming, book early. If it is not, that is a fortnight to be on St Kitts instead.

The island’s largest hotel and the one that publishes the most about itself. Four Seasons’ own fact sheet gives the address as Pinney’s Beach, Charlestown, and the accommodation as 189 rooms and suites — 165 rooms and 24 suites — with villa and residence rentals listed separately. It is not all-inclusive.

Travelling in your later years

Nevis needs a frank warning on this point, because its charm and its difficulty are the same thing. The plantation inns are two-hundred-year-old buildings on sloping ground, with rooms often in separate cottages reached up a garden path, steps at the verandah, and no lift anywhere on the property. They are beautiful and they are not step-free. Every one of them will tell you straight how far the room is from the car and how many steps are involved — ring and ask, because the photographs will not show you.

The flat options are on the coast. The large resort on Pinney’s Beach has level ground between the rooms, the pools and the sand, and the beach properties on the west coast generally put you within a short walk of the water. Charlestown itself is small and reasonably level, though the pavements are narrow and uneven in places as they are in most old Caribbean towns.

The crossing from St Kitts is a step down into a boat from the pier rather than a gangway, and the car ferries are easier on this count than the passenger ferry. Health care on Nevis is limited — more so than on St Kitts, and serious cases are flown off the island entirely — so insurance that will move you is not a formality here. Bring prescription medicines in their boxes with a copy of the prescription.

Questions travellers ask about staying in Nevis

Plantation inn or beach hotel?

They are different holidays and the island does both well. The plantation inns are small, cool, quiet and set in extraordinary gardens up the slopes, and none of them is on a beach — most keep a beach club or run a shuttle down to the water. The beach properties on the west coast put the sand outside your door and give up the gardens and the altitude. If you have a week, several people we trust split it: a few nights up the hill and a few down on Pinney’s.

How do I get to Nevis?

By ferry from Basseterre on St Kitts, which the Nevis Tourism Authority gives as twenty-five to forty-five minutes to Charlestown Pier, or by air into Vance W. Amory International, which has a single 1,220-metre runway and regional flights from Antigua, St Maarten and Puerto Rico. There are also two car ferries, Sea Bridge and iConnect, and private water taxis from Reggae Beach or the Park Hyatt across to Oualie Bay. No ferry timetable is published online, so confirm sailings locally rather than planning a tight connection.

Is Nevis expensive?

Yes, relative to most of the Caribbean, and the reason is supply rather than extravagance. There are very few rooms on the island and most of the properties are small, so in the dry season from mid-December to mid-April the good inns sell out and there is nothing to fall back on. The low season from July to early November is a different island on price. Late April, May and the second half of November are where the value is.

Do I need a car on Nevis?

Not necessarily, but it helps. The island is thirty-six square miles with a single ring road and you can drive round it in under an hour. If you are staying on Pinney’s Beach or in Charlestown you can manage on taxis; if you are up at one of the plantation inns, a car turns the holiday from a series of arrangements into a free hand. You drive on the left, and you need a temporary local licence, which the hire firm arranges — the UK Foreign Office gives the cost as EC$62.50 for three months or EC$125 for a year.

What is there to do on Nevis?

Less than on St Kitts, which is why people come. There is diving at Oualie with an operation that has been running since 1985, eighteen holes of Robert Trent Jones II golf at the Four Seasons, a walk up the volcano for the fit, the botanical gardens, the Hamilton museum in Charlestown, and Pinney’s Beach. Most visitors find that the island’s real activity is the absence of them, and adjust by about day three.

When is Culturama?

The Culturama Secretariat’s own site gives Culturama 53 as 22 July to 3 August 2027, thirteen days in Charlestown, with registration closing 30 November 2026. It records Culturama 52 as 23 July to 4 August 2026. One caveat: the site’s footer still reads 2026 beside the 2027 dates, so confirm with the Secretariat before you book flights. It is the biggest event in the Nevisian year and the island fills for it.
