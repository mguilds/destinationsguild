When to Go to China

The Best Time to Visit China

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: the shoulder seasons — roughly April to May and September to October — are the sweet spots. You get mild, dry days and comfortable evenings for the Great Wall, the cities and the Li River, without the heat and rain of summer or the cold of the northern winter. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the big holiday crowds, and the best timing by region.

China is a continent-sized country, and timing matters as much as anywhere on earth — but the pattern is friendlier than it first looks. The China you meet on a golden October morning — crisp air on the Great Wall, autumn colour in the Fragrant Hills, comfortable days in Xi'an and along the Li River — is a world away from the humid, rainy south in July or a grey Beijing January. Choose your dates well, and sidestep the two great holiday crushes, and everything is easier. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$$ PrimeMild & dryBlossomWatch Labour Day

One of the two best times to travel. Warmth returns from south to north, skies clear and the parks bloom — ideal for the Great Wall, the cities and the countryside. April and May are prime. The one thing to plan around is the Labour Day holiday in early May, a short but intense domestic travel spike. Late March is a lovely, quieter value window as the peak begins.

Summer — June to August

$$ MixedHot & humidRainy southDomestic peak

Hot and humid across most of the country, with the rainy season and occasional typhoons in the south and along the coast, and the domestic school-holiday peak filling major sights. The north is hot but drier; the high mountains and grasslands are a cool escape. Foreign-tourist numbers are lower and airfares in the hottest weeks can be keen — if you can take the heat and the odd downpour.

Autumn — September to November

$$$$ PeakMild & clearBest all-roundAvoid Golden Week

The finest season for most trips: the summer heat and rain fade into clear, mild, comfortable weather, and autumn colour lights up the north. September and October are superb across the whole country. The catch is the National Day Golden Week (the first week of October), the year's biggest travel crunch — travel just before or after it, and November brings crisp days and better value.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold northCheapestHarbin ice

The bargain season, if you can take the chill. The north (Beijing, Xi'an) is cold and dry, the south milder; sights and trains are quiet and prices at their lowest — except for the Chinese New Year fortnight, when the whole country travels. Highlights include a snow-dusted Great Wall almost to yourself and Harbin's spectacular ice-and-snow festival. Pack serious layers for the north.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$ Low seasonColdest northHarbin ice

The coldest, quietest month in the north, dry and often sunny, with the Great Wall and Forbidden City blissfully uncrowded and prices low. The south is chilly but milder. Harbin's ice festival is in full swing. Watch the calendar, as Chinese New Year sometimes falls in late January and changes everything.

February

$ → $$$ SplitColdChinese New Year

Still cold, and dominated by Chinese New Year in most years — festive and atmospheric, but with sold-out transport, many closures and higher prices. Outside the holiday fortnight it is quiet and cheap. A rewarding month for the celebrations if you book far ahead, or for empty sights if you dodge the dates.

March

$$ ValueSpring beginsFewer crowds

Spring stirs from the south northward: mild, increasingly pleasant, and excellent value before the peak. The south and Sichuan green up first, while Beijing and Xi'an are still cool. One of the best-value windows of the year for comfortable weather and thin crowds.

April

$$$ PrimeWarm & brightBlossom

A gorgeous month almost everywhere — warm, dry and bright, with blossom in the parks and comfortable sightseeing on the Wall, in the cities and along the Li River. Crowds build but remain manageable. Among the very best months to travel the classic Beijing–Xi'an–Shanghai circuit.

May

$$$ PrimeWarmLabour Day spike

Warm, green and lovely, with the south starting to feel humid. The catch is the Labour Day holiday in the first few days of May, a sharp domestic travel spike at big sights. Around it, May is a superb month for the whole country and prime for the northern grasslands.

June

$$ ValueHotRains begin south

Early summer: hot in the north and increasingly humid in the south, where the plum rains arrive. Foreign-tourist numbers dip and value improves before the school holidays. Good for the north and the cooler mountains; pack for heat and the occasional soaking downpour in the south.

July

$$ Domestic peakHot & humidMonsoon south

Hot and sticky nationwide, with monsoon rains in the south and the domestic summer-holiday peak at big attractions. The upside is lush green landscapes, a full Li River after the rains, and cool relief in the high mountains and Tibetan plateau. Start early and plan around afternoon storms.

August

$$ Domestic peakPeak heatTyphoon risk

The other peak-heat, high-humidity month, busy with domestic travellers and with a typhoon risk on the east coast around Shanghai. The mountains stay blissfully cool. Best suited to heat-tolerant travellers, mountain trips, or those simply working around a summer school schedule.

September

$$$ Prime valueHeat easingExcellent

The heat and rain loosen their grip and the weather turns superb across most of the country — warm, drier and comfortable, with crowds easing after the summer holidays. Prices are still reasonable before the October peak. A genuine sweet spot for weather and value combined.

October

$$$$ PeakGolden autumnGolden Week crush

Arguably the best all-round weather of the year — clear, mild and glorious, with autumn colour in the north. But the first week is National Day Golden Week, when hundreds of millions travel and sights are overwhelmed. Come mid-to-late October instead and it is close to perfect.

November

$$$ ValueCrisp & clearPeak fading

Crisp, clear and comfortable as the autumn peak fades, with the north cooling toward winter and the south still mild. Crowds and prices ease, making it one of the best value-plus-weather months. Lovely for the Wall, the cities and the last of the autumn colour before the cold sets in.

December

$ Low seasonCold northQuiet & cheap

Winter proper in the north — cold and dry, with quiet sights and low prices — while the south stays milder. It is a fine time for uncrowded sightseeing and a dusting of snow on the Great Wall, and Harbin's ice sculptures begin to appear. Bundle up for Beijing and Xi'an, and enjoy the calm before the New Year rush.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Winter — November to March, outside Chinese New Year

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSkip the CNY fortnight

Long-haul airfares and city hotels bottom out in the cold months, when the northern chill thins the crowds. The cheapest weeks are winter outside the Chinese New Year fortnight, when everything resets to peak prices. You trade warmth for value — but a snow-dusted Great Wall and empty Forbidden City are their own reward, and the south stays mild.

Want reasonable weather and a deal? Target the shoulder edges: late March and November, just before and after the spring and autumn peaks. You get comfortable sightseeing at well below peak-season prices. As a rule, midweek flights beat weekends, and booking high-speed train tickets as soon as they release (usually a couple of weeks out) locks in seats on popular routes.

Beating the Heat & the Crowds

China's busy periods are predictable to the day, which makes them easy to plan around.

The holidays and weather to sidestep

Golden Week (Oct 1–7)Chinese New YearLabour DaySummer heat & rain

The two great crushes are the National Day Golden Week (the first week of October) and Chinese New Year (late January or February), when hundreds of millions of people travel and every headline sight, train and flight is packed and pricey. The early-May Labour Day holiday is a smaller spike. On weather, high summer (July–August) is hot, humid and wet in the south. None of these need ruin a trip — they just reward planning.

To have the great sights closer to yourself, travel in the shoulder months — April to May and September to November, avoiding the holiday weeks — and start early wherever you are: the Great Wall, the Forbidden City and the Terracotta Army are coolest and quietest soon after opening. Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travellers ask most when timing a trip to China.

What is the best month to visit China?

April, May, September and October are the strongest all-round months. Spring and autumn bring mild, dry, comfortable weather across most of the country, ideal for the Great Wall, the cities and the Li River, with the autumn colours a bonus. The one thing to avoid is the first week of October, when the National Day Golden Week holiday packs every major sight and train.

What is the cheapest time to visit China?

Winter, roughly November to March but outside the Chinese New Year fortnight, is the cheapest time for flights and hotels, as the north turns cold and the crowds thin. The hottest, most humid weeks of deep summer can also bring deals. For mild weather and a bargain, aim for the shoulder edges of late March and November, just before and after the peak seasons.

When should I avoid China because of crowds?

Steer clear of the two big domestic travel surges: the National Day Golden Week, the first week of October, and Chinese New Year in late January or February, when hundreds of millions of people are on the move and trains, flights and sights are jammed. The early-May Labour Day holiday is a smaller spike. Outside these, spring and autumn weekdays are wonderfully manageable.

Is the weather the same across China?

Not at all — China spans several climate zones. The north, including Beijing and Xi'an, has hot summers and cold, dry winters; the Yangtze cities such as Shanghai are humid, with hot summers and chilly, damp winters; the south and Sichuan are subtropical, mild and often cloudy, with wet summers. Pack for where you are actually going, and for big swings between regions on a multi-city trip.

What about visiting China during Chinese New Year?

Chinese New Year, or Spring Festival, is a fascinating but tricky time to visit. The country is festive and beautifully decorated, but it is also the world's largest annual human migration: transport sells out weeks ahead, many small shops and family restaurants close, and prices climb. Major hotels, sights and tours keep running, so come for the atmosphere if you plan carefully and book far ahead.
