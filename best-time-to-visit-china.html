When to Go to China

The Best Time to Visit China

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: the two shoulder seasons — spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) — are the sweet spots. You get mild, mostly dry days and clear skies for the Great Wall, Beijing, Xi'an and the Yangtze, without the summer heat and downpours or the deep winter cold of the north. Below is the full picture: season by season, month by month, the cheapest time to go, how to dodge the great domestic crowds, and the best timing for Beijing, the Yangtze, Guilin and Tibet.

China is nearly the size of a continent, so “the weather” means many climates at once. A single week in October can bring crisp, golden mornings on the Great Wall near Beijing, warm subtropical air in Guilin, and the first cold snap on the Tibetan plateau. That range is the whole trick of timing a China trip: pick the season that suits your route, and steer clear of the two enormous national holidays when the entire country seems to be on the move. Here is how.

The Seasons at a Glance

The quick read on what each season feels like across most of the country — weather, crowds, price, and who it suits.

Spring — March to May

$$$ ShoulderMild & blossomingGreat all-roundBest late spring

One of the two prime seasons. The country warms and dries out, gardens and blossoms come alive, and late April and May bring comfortable sightseeing across Beijing, Xi'an, the Yangtze and the south. Early spring can still be chilly in the north, and Beijing occasionally sees dusty, windy days, but overall this is a lovely, lower-crowd window — with one caveat, the May Day holiday around the first of the month.

Summer — June to August

$$$$ Peak (domestic)Hot & humidRainy seasonHigh country

Hot, humid and wet across most of the east and south, when the monsoon rains arrive and cities like Beijing and Shanghai turn muggy. It is also the domestic peak, as school holidays fill the Great Wall, Guilin and the Chengdu pandas. The upside: this is the best time for the high country — Tibet, the grasslands, Jiuzhaigou and the far northwest — where summer is the mildest, greenest season. Watch for typhoons on the southeast coast.

Autumn — September to November

$$$ ShoulderClear & crispBest for the northGolden foliage

For many travelers the finest season of all. The heat and rain fade, skies clear, and Beijing, the Great Wall and Xi'an are at their crisp, golden best through late September and October. The Yangtze and the south stay warm and pleasant. The one hurdle is the National Day “Golden Week” around October 1–7, a huge domestic travel surge — aim for the weeks on either side and this is close to perfect.

Winter — December to February

$ Low seasonCold north, mild southCheapestAvoid Spring Festival

The bargain season, with the fewest foreign visitors and the lowest fares — if you can take the cold. The north, including Beijing and Xi'an, is dry and often below freezing, while Harbin turns its winter into a famous ice-and-snow festival. The subtropical south stays mild. The great exception is the Spring Festival (Chinese New Year), in late January or February, when domestic travel spikes and many small businesses close.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best across the main routes.

January

$ Low seasonColdestHarbin ice

The coldest, quietest month for foreign travelers. The north is frozen and dry, Harbin's ice festival is in full swing, and the south is cool but manageable. Prices are low and major sights are calm — but check the dates of Spring Festival, which sometimes falls in late January and changes everything.

February

$ → $$$ SplitColdSpring Festival

Still winter and still cheap outside the holiday, but this is the usual month for the Spring Festival, the year's biggest domestic travel crunch. Around those dates transport sells out and many shops and restaurants close; the rest of February is quiet, cold and good value in both north and south.

March

$$ ValueWarming upSouthern blossoms

Winter loosens its grip. The south warms first, with early blossoms, while Beijing and the north are still cool and can be breezy. Crowds are thin and prices soft — a smart-value month for the Yangtze and southern cities before the spring peak arrives.

April

$$$ ShoulderMild & brightPrime spring

One of the loveliest months: mild, mostly dry and colorful, with comfortable weather from Beijing to Guilin. Excellent for the Great Wall, Xi'an and a Yangtze cruise. The north can still throw the odd dusty, windy day, but on balance April is hard to beat for weather and reasonable crowds.

May

$$$ ShoulderWarm & greenMay Day spike

Warm, green and beautiful nearly everywhere, and superb for the whole classic circuit. The catch is the May Day holiday around the first of the month, a short domestic surge. Book around it and late May is one of the best sightseeing windows of the year before the summer humidity builds.

June

$$$ RisingHumidPlum rains

Summer sets in. The Yangtze basin and Shanghai enter the “plum rains” of early summer, and the east grows hot and sticky. It is a fine time, though, for the high country — Tibet, Yunnan's uplands and the grasslands — which are green and comfortable while the lowlands swelter.

July

$$$$ Peak (domestic)Hot & wetTyphoon season

Hot, humid and the wettest stretch in much of the country, with school holidays packing the headline sights. Beijing and Shanghai are muggy; the southeast coast enters typhoon season. Best suited to the mountains and plateau, or to travelers who don't mind heat and can start early and rest at midday.

August

$$$$ Peak (domestic)Peak heatBusy sights

More of July: hot, humid and busy with domestic holidaymakers, and still typhoon season on the coast. The high grasslands, Tibet and the far northwest remain the sweet spots for comfort. Elsewhere, expect crowds at the Great Wall, Guilin and the pandas, and pack for sudden downpours.

September

$$$ Prime valueCooling & clearingSweet spot

A superb month as the heat eases and the skies clear. Beijing and the north turn crisp and comfortable, the summer crowds thin after schools return, and prices settle before the October holiday. Arguably the best single month for the classic north-and-Yangtze route in comfort.

October

$$$ SplitClear & goldenGolden Week

Glorious autumn weather — clear, mild and golden, ideal for the Great Wall, Xi'an and Jiuzhaigou's colors. The one hazard is the National Day Golden Week of roughly October 1–7, when domestic travel peaks and sights are jammed. Aim for mid-to-late October and this is close to perfect.

November

$$ ValueCool & clearQuiet

An underrated month: cool, dry and clear in the north, still mild in the south, with the autumn crowds gone and prices dropping toward the winter low. Great for Beijing sightseeing in comfort and for the subtropical south before winter, with the year's late color lingering.

December

$ Low seasonCold northMild south

Winter proper in the north — cold, dry and quiet — while Hong Kong and the south stay pleasantly mild. Foreign visitor numbers are low and prices among the year's best, apart from a small bump over Christmas and New Year. A fine time for the south, or for a bracing, crowd-free Great Wall.

The Cheapest Time to Go

If price is your deciding factor, the pattern is clear and consistent.

Winter — November to March, outside the Spring Festival

$ Rock bottomFlights & hotelsSkip the holiday spike

International airfares and hotel rates are lowest in the winter low season, when the cold thins the foreign crowds and the headline sights are calm. The cheapest weeks are generally December through early February, with November a strong-value warm-up. The one exception is the Spring Festival (Chinese New Year), when domestic prices jump and transport fills — check its dates and travel just before or after.

Want reasonable weather and a deal? Target the shoulder edges: March and November. You get mild sightseeing and thin crowds well below spring-and-autumn peak prices. As a rule, booking flights a couple of months out and traveling midweek helps, and avoiding the two national Golden Weeks (early October and Spring Festival) saves the most of all.

Beating the Crowds & the Big Holidays

China's busiest periods are fixed to the calendar, which makes them easy to plan around.

The peaks to sidestep

Golden Week (Oct 1–7)Spring FestivalJul–Aug school breakMay Day

The two crunches to plan around are the National Day Golden Week (roughly October 1–7) and the Spring Festival (Chinese New Year, late January or February) — when hundreds of millions of people travel, trains and flights sell out, sights overflow, and many small businesses close for the New Year. The July–August school holidays and the short May Day break are smaller domestic surges. None need ruin a trip — they just reward planning.

To have the great sights closer to yourself, come in the shoulder months — April to May and September to early October or late October — and start early wherever you are: the Great Wall, the Forbidden City and the Terracotta Army are quietest at opening. Best Time by Region

China is vast and varied — the ideal month depends heavily on where you're headed.

Beijing & the North — the Great Wall, Xi'an

Best: Sep–Oct, Apr–MayAvoid: deep winter, Jul–AugWatch: Golden Week

The classic first-timer's circuit is all about clear, comfortable air, so spring and autumn win outright. Late September and October are crisp and golden on the Great Wall; late April and May are a close second. Winters are cold and dry, often below freezing, and July–August is hot, humid and wet. Just steer around the October Golden Week rush.

Typhoon season runs roughly July to September on the southeast coast, bringing heavy rain; winters in Hong Kong and the far south stay pleasantly mild.

Tibet, the Silk Road & the High Country

Best: May–OctSummer is primeAvoid: winter cold

The high plateau and the far west flip the usual rules: summer is the mildest, greenest and most accessible season. Tibet, the Silk Road oases of Xinjiang and the alpine lakes of Jiuzhaigou are at their best from May to October, with autumn adding spectacular color. Winter brings deep cold, closed passes and thin services, so aim for the warm half of the year.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask most when timing a trip to China.

What is the best month to visit China?

September, October, April and May are the strongest all-round months. Days are mild and mostly dry, skies are clearer, and sightseeing along the Great Wall, through Beijing and Xi'an, and on the Yangtze is comfortable. The one thing to plan around is the National Day “Golden Week” holiday of roughly October 1 to 7, when domestic crowds and prices surge, so aim for the weeks on either side.

What is the cheapest time to visit China?

Winter, roughly November to March, is the low season and the cheapest for international flights and hotels, because the north is cold and the peak-season crowds have gone. The big exception is the Spring Festival, or Chinese New Year, in late January or February, when domestic travel spikes and prices jump. Outside that holiday, winter delivers the lowest fares and the quietest major sights.

When should I avoid China because of crowds?

Avoid the two big domestic travel crunches: the National Day “Golden Week” of roughly October 1 to 7, and the Spring Festival, or Chinese New Year, in late January or February, when hundreds of millions of people travel, transport sells out, and many small businesses close. Summer school holidays in July and August also pack popular spots like the Great Wall, Guilin, and the pandas in Chengdu.

What is the best time to visit the Great Wall and Beijing?

Autumn, especially late September and October, is the classic window for Beijing and the Great Wall: clear, crisp, comfortable days and golden foliage. Late April and May are a close second. Beijing winters are cold and dry, often below freezing, while July and August are hot, humid, and the wettest months, so spring and autumn win clearly. Just plan around the October Golden Week rush.

What is the weather like in southern China, and when is typhoon season?

The subtropical south, including Guilin, Yunnan, Guangzhou, and Hong Kong, is milder in winter and hot and humid in summer. Typhoon season runs roughly from July to September along the southeast coast, and can bring heavy rain and disruption. Spring and autumn are the most reliable times to go, with lush green scenery around Guilin and Yangshuo and comfortable temperatures inland.
