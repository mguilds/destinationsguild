Where to Stay in Monteverde

Where to Stay in Monteverde — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Monteverde sits at about 4,600 feet, and the first thing to say is the thing the brochures leave out: it is cool, damp and often windy. Daytime highs sit in the sixties and low seventies, nights drop to the fifties, and mist can arrive in any month. Bring a fleece, a real waterproof shell and shoes with grip; you will use all three. The second thing is that Santa Elena town, the Cerro Plano ridge and the road out to the Reserve form a chain of steep hills, so almost nothing here is a pleasant stroll. Below are the lodges worth knowing, with an honest word about which are level enough for a guest who is unsteady on rough ground, each with a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Luxury

The two finest addresses in the cloud forest — one with the best long view, one with the gentlest ground.

Hotel Belmar

$$$$ LuxuryCerro PlanoBest long viewFarm-to-table kitchenSteep hillside

Belmar has been the anchor of upscale Monteverde for four decades, and its alpine-chalet buildings still hold the best long view in the region — on a clear afternoon you can see all the way down to the Gulf of Nicoya. The kitchen works largely from the hotel’s own farm, there is a small spa, a heated soaking pool and daily yoga, and the staff are unusually good at pacing a day for guests who do not want to be marched anywhere. Be warned that the property climbs a genuinely steep hillside with outdoor stairs between levels; ask specifically for a lower room if stairs are a problem.

Senda Monteverde Hotel

$$$$ LuxuryCerro Plano24 roomsLevel garden pathsNaturalist guides on staff

Senda is the most polished small hotel in Monteverde and, importantly for older travellers, the most gently laid out — twenty-four rooms in low clusters along broad, mostly level garden paths, with sloths and coatis routinely visible from the balconies. Breakfast and dinner are genuinely good, the naturalist guides are on staff rather than subcontracted, and complimentary trails into the adjoining private reserve let you see cloud forest without a drive. There is no pool and no spa, and Santa Elena’s restaurants are a few minutes away by car, so budget for taxis in the evening.

Boutique

Warm, well-insulated rooms — which matters more here than almost anywhere else in Costa Rica.

Koora Hotel Monteverde

$$$ UpscaleSanta Elena townModern bungalowsWalk into townProperly warm rooms

Koora is the rare Monteverde property that combines contemporary, well-insulated rooms with an address you can actually walk from — the restaurants and tour desks of Santa Elena are a few near-flat blocks away, which matters more here than anywhere else in the country. The cloud-forest bungalows are large, quiet and properly glazed against the damp, and the small restaurant and coffee station mean you need not go out on a wet night. There is no pool and no spa, and despite what some booking sites imply it is not adults-only — families do stay.

Monteverde Lodge & Gardens

$$$ UpscaleEdge of Santa ElenaHeated poolFireplace loungeLevel, well-surfaced grounds

This is the grown-up, slightly old-school choice: thirty-one large rooms in landscaped grounds with a rare heated pool, a fireplace in the lounge for the genuinely chilly evenings, and one of the better naturalist-guide operations in the country behind it. The gardens are level and well surfaced, which makes it one of the easier properties in Monteverde for guests unsteady on rough ground. It is close enough to Santa Elena to walk in daylight, though the final approach is a gravel lane and the uphill walk back after dinner is not one most people enjoy twice.

Budget & Value

Both of these are a short, flat walk from the middle of Santa Elena — which is worth a great deal on a wet night.

Hotel Arco Iris Lodge

$ BudgetSanta Elena townStone cabinsGroomed gardensTwo minutes from the centre

Arco Iris is the value benchmark in Monteverde and has been for thirty years: twenty stone-and-hardwood cabins scattered across a couple of hectares of groomed garden backing onto protected forest, all of it two minutes from the middle of Santa Elena. The paths through the grounds are stone-laid and well maintained, so it is one of the few genuinely cheap options where you are not picking your way over mud and roots after dark. Rooms are plain and some of the cheaper cabins have thin walls and no heating, so bring the fleece and ask for a larger unit if you feel the cold.

Hotel Claro de Luna

$ BudgetSanta Elena townFamily-runSome rooms with jacuzzisFlat walk to dinner

A Costa Rican family hotel built on an old farm right at the edge of town, Claro de Luna delivers the two things that matter most on a budget here — a short flat walk to dinner and a room that stays dry. A handful of the deluxe rooms have private jacuzzis, a small luxury worth the modest upgrade after a cold, wet day on the hanging bridges. Do not expect a resort: the building is dated, the views are partial, and the service is warm rather than slick.

Family-friendly

The two properties here with real facilities — pools, spas and space for children to be children.

El Establo Mountain Hotel

$$$ UpscaleCerro PlanoThree poolsOn-site spaFree shuttle between buildings

El Establo is by far the largest resort in Monteverde and the only one with the infrastructure families actually use: a heated sheltered outdoor pool, two indoor pools, a spa, tennis and football courts and its own trails. Crucially for anyone who cannot manage hills, the hotel runs a free shuttle continuously between its buildings, which are stacked up a very steep slope — without it the upper blocks would be unreachable on foot. Ask about the shuttle at check-in, request a room in a lower building if you would rather not depend on it, and be aware that the size makes it feel more resort than cloud-forest lodge.

Fondavela Mountain Hotel

$$$ UpscaleCloud forest roadClosest to the ReserveIndoor heated poolSpa

Fondavela sits nearer the Reserve gate than almost anything else, spread over nine low buildings on a wooded hillside, which means early-morning birding without a drive and plenty of room for children. The indoor heated pool and jacuzzis are a real asset where outdoor swimming is a chilly proposition most of the year, and there is a small spa, a proper restaurant and a sports bar. The trade-off is isolation — Santa Elena’s shops and restaurants are a ten-minute drive down a winding road you should not attempt after dark. It trades today as Fondavela, having long been known as Hotel Fonda Vela.

Adults-only

An honest finding: Monteverde has exactly one adults-only hotel. Booking-site filters will show you three, but the other two are hostels rather than hotels, and we are not going to pad the list. If adults-only is essential to your trip, this is the one — and it is not for anybody unsteady on stairs.

Hidden Canopy Treehouses Boutique Hotel

$$$$ LuxuryCloud forest roadSix treehousesGuests 15 and overSteep outdoor stairs

Six timber treehouses set in private forest, this is the most romantic address in Monteverde and it enforces a serene adult environment — guests must be fifteen or older. The afternoon tea on the main-house deck at sunset, with the Gulf of Nicoya far below, is the single best hour many guests report from their whole Costa Rica trip. Two important cautions: the treehouses are reached by steep outdoor stairs and elevated walkways and are genuinely unsuitable for anyone with mobility or balance limitations, and the hotel takes reservations by correspondence rather than through a live booking engine, so allow time.

Pet-friendly

Also thin, honestly. Most of Monteverde’s pet-friendly stock is guesthouses, cabinas and holiday rentals. Two established hotels do accept dogs, both with real limits — confirm your dog’s weight in writing before booking.

Fondavela Mountain Hotel

$$$ UpscaleCloud forest roadSmall dogs onlyIndoor poolWooded grounds

Fondavela is the only genuinely upscale Monteverde hotel that will take your dog, and its nine low buildings on wooded grounds give a small dog plenty of room without ever leaving the property. The policy is restrictive, though — small dogs only, two at most, with a modest fee — so confirm your dog’s weight in writing before you book. The indoor heated pool and spa mean the humans are looked after too, which is not always the case at pet-accepting properties.

Hotel Montana Monteverde

$ BudgetAbove Santa ElenaNo pet feePrivate forest and trailsDated rooms

One of the oldest hotels in the district, set on fifteen hectares of private forest with its own trails and pond — excellent for a dog, and there is no pet fee for a medium-sized animal. We should be candid: the rooms are tired and the property has been asking for a renovation for some years, so it earns its place here on the pet policy and the land, not the décor. The town centre is about twenty minutes’ walk downhill and the return climb is steep, so this suits drivers rather than walkers.

Spa & Pool

At 4,600 feet a heated pool is not a frill — the unheated ones in this region go largely unused.

Poco a Poco Hotel & Spa

$$$ UpscaleSanta ElenaHeated pool with swim-up barSpaFire pit

Poco a Poco is the most straightforwardly comfortable spa hotel in Monteverde: a heated semi-outdoor pool with a swim-up bar, a jacuzzi, a proper spa and a good restaurant, all in landscaped gardens with a fire pit for the cold evenings. A heated pool genuinely matters up here, where the unheated ones sit empty. The setting is pleasant rather than dramatic, and you will want a taxi into Santa Elena for dinner variety.

Roads, weather and the walk between things

Before you book

Getting here. The southern approach from the Pan-American Highway is fully asphalted to Santa Elena now — about three and a half hours from San José, and a standard car will manage it in dry weather. The northern route from Las Juntas still alternates concrete and gravel and is narrow and winding; take a high-clearance vehicle. In the green season, take one on either route. The roads within Monteverde, out to the hanging-bridge parks and up many lodge driveways, remain rough gravel year-round. Never drive it after dark — curving mountain road, no lighting, no guard rails, and full darkness by about six every evening of the year. Many visitors sensibly arrive by shared shuttle, or by the jeep-boat-jeep across Lake Arenal from La Fortuna, which is both quicker and far more pleasant than the road around. The weather. Expect sixties and low seventies by day, high fifties at night, humidity above ninety per cent and wind on the ridge in the December-to-April dry season that makes 68°F feel a good deal colder. February and March are driest; September and October are the wettest months of the year. The walking. Genuinely easy for less-mobile guests: Senda Monteverde, Monteverde Lodge, Arco Iris Lodge and Koora. Steep: Hotel Belmar, Hotel Montaña Monteverde, and Hidden Canopy, which is unsuitable for anyone unsteady. El Establo is steep but solved by its free shuttle.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travellers ask most about staying in Monteverde.

Is it cold in Monteverde?

Cooler than most visitors expect. At around 4,600 feet daytime highs sit in the sixties and low seventies Fahrenheit, nights drop to the high fifties, humidity is usually above ninety per cent, and the dry-season wind on the ridge makes it feel colder still. Pack a fleece, a proper waterproof shell and shoes with grip — this is the one part of Costa Rica where you will genuinely want them.

Should I stay in Santa Elena town or out near the Reserve?

Santa Elena if you want to walk to dinner and the tour desks — Koora, Arco Iris and Claro de Luna are all a short flat walk from the centre. Out on the Reserve road if early-morning birding without a drive matters more, which is the case for Fondavela, Senda and Hidden Canopy. Either way, do not plan to walk between the two after dark.

How do you get to Monteverde from Arenal?

The jeep-boat-jeep across Lake Arenal is the pleasant way: a minivan to the lakeshore, a boat across, and a second vehicle up to Santa Elena, taking about three hours in total. Driving around the lake takes rather longer on winding road and is not something to attempt in the dark. From San José, the southern route is fully paved and takes about three and a half hours.

How many nights do you need in Monteverde?

Two nights is the honest minimum — one full day lets you do the Reserve in the morning and hanging bridges or a night walk after, and you will want the second morning in case the first is socked in with mist. Three nights suits birders and anyone who would rather not rush. Monteverde pairs naturally with Arenal on one side and the Guanacaste beaches on the other.
