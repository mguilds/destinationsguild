Why visit South Korea

Few countries pack so much variety into so small a space — five-hundred-year-old palaces and glass towers, beaches and volcanoes, temple food and midnight barbecue, all linked by trains that never run late.

Seoul, ancient & instant

Joseon-dynasty palaces and hanok lanes one block, K-pop, coffee culture and neon towers the next — a capital of ten million that switches centuries at every corner.

Mountains & islands

Two-thirds of the country is mountain. Hike the granite ridges of Seoraksan, climb Jeju's volcanic Hallasan, and ride ferries out to a thousand green islands in the southern sea.

The food

One of the world's great cuisines — sizzling galbi barbecue, bibimbap, cold noodles, tofu stews and endless free side dishes, plus fish markets, night markets and temple food.

History & the DMZ

The royal tombs and temples of Gyeongju, Confucian villages and fortress walls, and the tense, fascinating frontier of the DMZ, visited on organised tours from Seoul.

How the country divides up

Seoul & the Northwest

Almost every trip begins in Seoul, and it should. Five grand Joseon palaces, led by Gyeongbokgung, sit among glass towers; the hanok lanes of Bukchon, the food stalls of Gwangjang market and the shopping of Myeongdong and Gangnam fill the days, and the tense frontier of the DMZ is a half-day tour north. Your gateway is Incheon, itself worth a stop for its Chinatown and the fortress walls of nearby Suwon.

Gyeongsang & the Southeast

Busan, the second city, is Korea at its most relaxed — the beaches of Haeundae and Gwangalli, the vast Jagalchi fish market and a hillside of painted houses at Gamcheon. Inland lies Gyeongju, the "museum without walls," capital of the ancient Silla kingdom, with grassy royal tombs and the serene Bulguksa temple and Seokguram grotto.

Jeju & the South Coast

Jeju, a volcanic island an hour's flight south, is Korea's honeymoon and holiday isle — the crater of Hallasan, lava-tube caves, waterfalls and black-sand beaches, and the famous haenyeo women divers. Back on the mainland, the port of Yeosu strings its harbour with bridges and night lights along the pretty southern coast.

The Centre, Jeolla & Gangwon

Jeonju is the spiritual home of Korean food, with a beautifully preserved hanok village; Andong keeps the old Confucian ways in its riverside folk village and mask dances. East, in mountainous Gangwon, the granite spires of Seoraksan rise above the beach town of Sokcho — glorious in autumn, and near the ski slopes of Pyeongchang in winter.

Top things to do in South Korea

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Seoul's palaces & hanok lanes

Watch the changing of the guard at Gyeongbokgung, wander Changdeokgung's Secret Garden, and get lost in the tiled lanes of Bukchon between the two.

Korean barbecue & markets

Grill galbi and samgyeopsal at your own table, then graze the stalls of Gwangjang and Myeongdong — bindaetteok, tteokbokki, hotteok and more.

Ride the KTX to Busan

Two and a half hours from Seoul to the sea — Haeundae beach, the Jagalchi fish market and the sunset colours of the Gamcheon culture village.

Ancient Gyeongju

Cycle between the grassy royal tombs, the observatory of Cheomseongdae, and the UNESCO-listed Bulguksa temple and Seokguram grotto above the town.

Jeju's volcano & coast

Climb or ride up Hallasan, catch sunrise at Seongsan Ilchulbong crater, walk the olle coastal trails and watch the haenyeo divers surface.

The DMZ & Seoraksan

Peer into North Korea on a DMZ day tour from Seoul, or head east in October to hike the flaming autumn ridges of Seoraksan National Park.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Spring (April–May) and autumn (late September–early November) are the sweet spots: mild, dry days, the cherry blossoms in April and the blaze of maples in October. These are the loveliest and busiest seasons, so book ahead.

Summer (late June–August) is hot and humid with the jangma monsoon rains; winter is cold, dry and clear, good for skiing, with Seoul often below freezing.

See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit South Korea for weather, crowds and prices through the year.

Getting there

Incheon International (ICN), west of Seoul, is the main long-haul gateway and one of the world's best airports, with direct flights from across Asia, Europe, North America and Oceania.

Gimhae (PUS) at Busan and Jeju (CJU) handle regional and some international flights, and the superb KTX high-speed rail and domestic flights make it easy to move between regions.

The currency is the Korean won and the country is largely cashless — cards and a T-money transit card cover almost everything. Most visitors travel visa-free but usually need a K-ETA arranged online; confirm the current rules with the official Korean authority before booking.

South Korea travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit South Korea?

Spring (April to May) and autumn (late September to early November) are the sweet spots — mild, dry days, the cherry blossoms in April and the blaze of autumn foliage in October. These are the loveliest and busiest seasons, so book ahead. Summer (late June to August) is hot and humid with a monsoon rainy season called jangma, while winter is cold and dry, clear and good for skiing near Pyeongchang, with Seoul often below freezing.

What currency is used in South Korea?

The South Korean won (KRW, symbol ₩). Korea is largely cashless — cards are accepted almost everywhere, from department stores to street stalls and taxis, and a rechargeable T-money card covers subways, buses and many convenience stores. Carry a little cash for traditional markets and small rural shops. ATMs marked "Global" accept foreign cards; when a machine or terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, decline and pay in won.

Do I need a visa to visit South Korea?

Many nationalities, including US, UK, Canadian, Australian and EU citizens, can visit visa-free for short tourist stays, but visa-exempt travellers normally need a K-ETA (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization) applied for online before departure. Rules and temporary K-ETA exemptions change from year to year, so always confirm the current requirements with the official Korean immigration authority before you travel.

How many days do you need in South Korea?

For a first trip, seven to ten days is ideal: three or four days in Seoul for the palaces, markets and day trips to the DMZ, then the fast KTX train south to pair Busan's beaches and seafood with the ancient tombs and temples of Gyeongju. Add two or three days on Jeju island if you want volcanic scenery and a slower pace. With only a week, base yourself in Seoul and take one longer trip south.

Is South Korea safe for travellers?

South Korea is one of the safest countries in the world for visitors, with very low violent crime, excellent public transport and a strong culture of honesty — lost phones are routinely returned. Take normal city precautions against petty pickpocketing in crowded nightlife districts. The heavily guarded DMZ border with North Korea is visited only on organised tours. Check your government's current travel advisory before you go.
