Why visit South Korea

Few countries pack so much variety into so small and well-connected a space — palaces and pop culture, mountains and islands, all a short train ride apart.

Palaces & history

The grand Joseon palaces of Seoul, the royal tombs and stone Buddhas of Gyeongju, and hanok villages of tiled wooden houses that have stood for centuries.

Food & night markets

Sizzling Korean barbecue, bubbling stews, fresh seafood and endless side dishes — eaten in humming night markets, backstreet grills and neon-lit alleys.

Coast & islands

The beaches and bridges of Busan, the ferries of the southern archipelago, and Jeju, a volcanic island of craters, waterfalls and lava-tube caves.

Modern & K-culture

The home of K-pop, K-drama and cutting-edge design — glassy skylines, tech-filled subways and a creative energy you feel on every street.

How the country divides up

Seoul & the Capital Region

Almost every trip begins in Seoul, and it should. Half the nation lives in and around the capital, where the Joseon palaces of Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung sit beneath granite peaks, hanok lanes climb Bukchon, and the shopping and street food of Myeongdong, Hongdae and Gangnam run late into the night. On the coast, Incheon is the country's front door — its great airport, the futuristic towers of Songdo and the old Chinatown by the port.

Busan & the South Coast

Busan is Korea by the sea — the beaches of Haeundae and Gwangalli under a lit-up bridge, the vast Jagalchi fish market, cliffside temples and hillside mural villages. West along the coast lie the islands and harbours of Tongyeong and the pottery towns and national parks of the south.

Gyeongju & the Historic Southeast

Gyeongju was the capital of the Silla kingdom for a thousand years, and it wears its history openly — grassy royal tombs rising from the town, the observatory of Cheomseongdae, and the UNESCO-listed temple of Bulguksa with the Seokguram Grotto above it. Inland, Andong guards Confucian academies and the folk village of Hahoe.

Jeju & the Islands

Jeju, a short flight south, is a volcanic island built around Hallasan, Korea's highest peak. It is a land of lava-tube caves, waterfalls, the sunrise crater of Seongsan Ilchulbong and the free-diving haenyeo women — ringed by a resort coast at Jungmun and Seogwipo. The smaller islands of the South Sea reward a slower, ferry-hopping trip.

Top things to do in South Korea

The experiences worth building a trip around.

Seoul's grand palaces

Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung with its Secret Garden, the changing of the guard, and the hanok lanes of Bukchon between them — best in a rented hanbok.

Korean barbecue & markets

Grill your own pork belly and beef at a backstreet table, then graze the night markets of Gwangjang and Myeongdong for tteokbokki, mandu and hotteok.

Busan's beaches & Jagalchi

Haeundae and Gwangalli sands, the neon Gwangan Bridge at night, and Korea's biggest fish market where you pick your catch and eat it upstairs.

Ancient Gyeongju

The royal tomb mounds, the stone Buddha of Seokguram and the temple of Bulguksa — a whole open-air museum of the Silla kingdom.

Jeju's craters & coast

Climb the sunrise crater of Seongsan Ilchulbong, hike volcanic Hallasan, and follow the coastal Olle trails past waterfalls and black-rock beaches.

Ride the KTX & a temple stay

Cross the country by high-speed train in a couple of hours, and swap a hotel for a night in a working mountain temple, waking for dawn meditation.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

Spring (April–May) and autumn (late September–November) are the sweet spots: mild, dry and lovely, with cherry blossom in spring and fiery foliage in autumn.

Summer (June–August) is hot, humid and brings the monsoon rains, but it is peak beach season on Jeju and in Busan; winter is cold and dry, good for skiing and quiet sightseeing.

See our month-by-month guide to the best time to visit South Korea for weather, crowds and prices through the year.

Getting there

Incheon International (ICN), just west of Seoul, is the main long-haul gateway and one of the world's best airports, with direct flights from across North America, Europe and Asia.

Inside the country, the KTX high-speed train links Seoul, Busan and Gyeongju in a few hours, and cheap domestic flights and ferries reach Jeju. A single T-money card covers trains, subways, buses and taxis.

The currency is the South Korean won. Cards and mobile payments work almost everywhere; carry a little cash for markets and small eateries. Most visitors travel visa-free but should confirm the current K-ETA rules with the official Korean authority before booking.

South Korea travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit South Korea?

Spring (April to May) and autumn (late September to November) are the sweet spots — mild, dry and beautiful, with cherry blossoms in spring and blazing foliage in autumn, ideal for Seoul, Gyeongju and the temples. Summer (June to August) is hot, humid and brings the monsoon rains of late June and July, though it is peak beach season on Jeju and in Busan. Winter (December to February) is cold and dry, excellent for skiing and quiet sightseeing if you pack warm layers.

What currency is used in South Korea?

The South Korean won (KRW, symbol ₩). Prices run into the thousands and tens of thousands, so amounts look large — a simple coffee is a few thousand won. South Korea is highly card-friendly: cards and mobile payments are accepted almost everywhere, even at small shops and taxis, though it is worth carrying some cash for traditional markets, small eateries and temple areas. ATMs marked "Global" accept foreign cards.

Do I need a visa to visit South Korea?

Many visitors, including US, Canadian, UK and EU citizens, can enter South Korea visa-free for short tourist stays, but most visa-free travellers must obtain an approved K-ETA (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization) online before flying, unless they fall under a current temporary exemption. Rules change, so always confirm the latest K-ETA and entry requirements with the official Korean authority before you travel.

How many days do you need in South Korea?

For a first trip, seven to ten days works well: three or four days in Seoul for the palaces, markets and day trips, a couple of days in Busan by the sea, and either the temple city of Gyeongju or the island of Jeju, all linked by the fast and punctual KTX train and cheap domestic flights. With two weeks you can comfortably combine all of them at a relaxed pace.

Is South Korea easy to get around?

Very. The KTX high-speed train connects Seoul, Busan and other cities in a few hours, city subways are clean, cheap and signposted in English, and a single rechargeable T-money card works on trains, buses and taxis nationwide. Jeju is reached by short, frequent domestic flights or ferries. English is common on transport and at major sights, and translation apps smooth the rest.
