National park fees & passes in 2026

Three changes for 2026 are worth knowing before you go.

The America the Beautiful pass covers entrance fees at every national park for a year. It costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for visitors from outside the U.S. If you will visit three or more fee-charging parks in a year, it usually pays for itself.

A $100 fee for nonresidents at 11 parks. Since January 1, 2026, visitors who are not U.S. residents and are 16 or older pay an extra $100 per person at Acadia, Bryce Canyon, Everglades, Glacier, Grand Canyon, Grand Teton, Rocky Mountain, Sequoia and Kings Canyon, Yellowstone, Yosemite and Zion. An America the Beautiful pass covers it.

Fee-free days are now for U.S. residents. The Park Service still names several free-entrance days each year, but from 2026 the free entry applies to U.S. residents only.

Passes that cost little or nothing. The Senior Pass is $80 for life or $20 a year for U.S. citizens and permanent residents aged 62 and over. The Access Pass is free for life for U.S. citizens and permanent residents with a permanent disability. Veterans and Gold Star families get a free Military Lifetime Pass, and current service members a free annual pass.

Fees and rules change, so check the park's page on nps.gov before you travel. Our park books were checked against nps.gov in November 2026.

National parks FAQ

How many national parks are there in the United States?

There are 63 national parks, in 30 states and two U.S. territories. California has the most, with nine, followed by Alaska with eight. They are part of a larger National Park System that also includes monuments, historic sites, seashores and other areas.

Which national parks are the most visited?

According to the National Park Service, the ten most-visited national parks in 2025 were Great Smoky Mountains, Zion, Yellowstone, Grand Canyon, Yosemite, Rocky Mountain, Acadia, Grand Teton, Olympic and Glacier. Each one has its own book on our shelf.

Is there a pass that covers every national park?

Yes. The America the Beautiful pass covers entrance fees at every national park for a year. In 2026 it costs $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for visitors from outside the U.S. Seniors, veterans, current military and people with a permanent disability can get discounted or free passes.

Do visitors from other countries pay more in 2026?

At 11 of the busiest parks, yes. Since January 1, 2026, non-U.S. residents aged 16 and older pay an extra $100 per person at Acadia, Bryce Canyon, Everglades, Glacier, Grand Canyon, Grand Teton, Rocky Mountain, Sequoia and Kings Canyon, Yellowstone, Yosemite and Zion. An America the Beautiful pass covers that extra fee.

Are there free entrance days?

Yes, the National Park Service names several fee-free days each year. From 2026 those free days apply to U.S. residents only. Check the NPS website for this year's dates.

Do I need a reservation to enter a national park?

Only at some parks, and the rules change every year. In 2026, Rocky Mountain used timed entry in summer and Acadia required a reservation for Cadillac Summit Road. Each of our park books explains that park's current rules.

Can I share my own national park story?

Yes, please do. Use the form on this page or on any park's book. The Guild reads every story before it is published, and it appears under your own name.

Where should I stay when visiting a national park?

Most visitors stay in a gateway town just outside the park, where hotels and vacation homes are easier to find. Lodges inside the parks are wonderful but book up many months ahead. Each park book lists the best gateway towns with hotel and cabin links.
