When to go

Best Time to Visit Croatia

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Croatia is one of those countries where the month you choose matters more than almost anything else you decide. The same hotel room in Dubrovnik can cost three times as much in the first week of August as in the first week of October, and the same alley can hold four thousand people or forty. This page goes through the year honestly — the air, the sea, the crowds, the ferry timetable and the price — so that you can pick your fortnight on purpose rather than by accident.

The short answer

If you read nothing else on this page, read this.

Go in September

The best single month in the Croatian year for most travellers. The sea is warmer than it was in June, the heat has broken, the ferries are still on the summer timetable for most of the month, the coach parties thin after the first week, and the rooms cost roughly a third less than they did in August. Istria’s truffle season starts. There is no catch.

Or go in May, if you are not set on swimming

Green, flowering, cool enough to walk all day, everything open, and cheaper still. The sea is only just tolerable by the last week of the month. For sightseeing, Plitvice, Istria and the old towns, May is superb.

Avoid the first fortnight of August

Half of Europe is on holiday at once. It is the hottest, fullest and most expensive fortnight of the Croatian year, and it is the one time we would tell a first-time visitor to change their dates if they possibly can.

Winter belongs to Zagreb

From late November the coast shutters — hotels close, ferries thin out, restaurants take their holiday. Zagreb does the opposite: its Advent market has been voted Europe’s best more than once, and December there is a genuine pleasure.

Croatia month by month

Average daytime high and average sea temperature, taken at Dubrovnik; the northern coast and Istria run two or three degrees cooler, and Zagreb is colder in winter and hotter in July than the coast.

January

55°F air · 57°F sea

The quietest month on the coast and the cheapest. Dubrovnik and Split are open and almost entirely local; many island ferries run a winter-only skeleton timetable and a good number of seaside hotels are shut. Zagreb, on the other hand, is lively and cold. Come only if emptiness is what you are after.

February

55°F air · 57°F sea

Much like January, with one splendid exception: Rijeka’s carnival, one of the largest in Europe, fills the northern coast in the run-up to Lent. Otherwise still low season, still cheap, still half-shut on the islands.

March

59°F air · 57°F sea

The country starts to wake. Almonds and then wisteria come out, the light turns, and the first hotels reopen for Easter. Too cold to swim, and the sea is at its coldest of the year, but excellent for walking Dubrovnik’s walls without queueing or perspiring.

April

65°F air · 59°F sea

A lovely, underrated month. Wild asparagus in Istria, green hills, everything open by Easter, and prices still low. Rain is possible and the sea is bracing, but for sightseeing rather than swimming this is one of the two or three best months in the year.

May

73°F air · 65°F sea

The month we would choose for a first trip. Warm enough for long days out, cool enough to walk at midday, everything open, ferries on the summer timetable by the end of the month, and the sea just about swimmable for the hardy by the last week. Prices rise through the month but have not yet reached the summer level.

June

80°F air · 71°F sea

Properly warm, the sea now comfortable for everyone, the longest days of the year, and the crowds still gathering rather than arrived. The first half of June is one of the sweet spots of the Croatian calendar. By the last week the coast is filling and the prices are climbing hard.

July

86°F air · 76°F sea

High summer: hot, dry (it is the driest month of the year in Dubrovnik), busy and expensive. Every ferry runs, every hotel is open, and every one of them is full. The Dubrovnik Summer Festival traditionally begins on 10 July. Book everything months ahead, and plan the day around the shade.

August

86°F air · 77°F sea

The warmest sea of the year and the fullest country. The first fortnight, when Italy and much of central Europe is on holiday at once, is the busiest and dearest fortnight in Croatia. It is still a wonderful place to be if you have booked well; it is a poor month to improvise in.

September

79°F air · 74°F sea

Our favourite month, and it is not close. The sea is warmer than in June, the air has come off the boil, the ferries are still running the summer timetable through most of the month, the coach parties thin out after the first week and the prices fall with them. Istria’s truffle season begins. If you can travel in September, do.

October

71°F air · 69°F sea

The shoulder proper. Swimming is still perfectly pleasant in the first half, the light is beautiful, the old towns are quiet, and rooms are a bargain. Ferries begin trimming back mid-month and rain becomes likelier. An excellent month for Istria, the national parks and the interior.

November

64°F air · 64°F sea

The wettest month in Dubrovnik and the point at which the coast closes for the winter: hotels shut, ferries cut back, restaurants take their holiday. Inland it is truffle season and the Zagreb restaurants are at their best. Not a beach month by any reading.

December

57°F air · 60°F sea

The one month when the interior clearly beats the coast. Zagreb’s Advent market has been voted Europe’s best more than once, and the city is genuinely festive for the whole of December. Dubrovnik is quiet, mild and half-asleep, which some travellers love and others find forlorn.

The sea, and why September beats June

This is the single most useful thing an American visitor can know about the Adriatic, and it catches people out every year. The sea here warms slowly through the summer and gives its heat back slowly through the autumn, so the water lags the air by about six weeks. At Dubrovnik the sea averages about 65°F in May, 71°F in June, 76°F in July, 77°F in August, 74°F in September and still about 69°F in October.

Read those numbers again and the planning follows by itself. September swimming is better than June swimming. October swimming is better than May swimming. And the two months either side of high summer — June and September — give you nearly the same sea as July at a fraction of the crowd.

One caution that has nothing to do with temperature: the Croatian shore is stone, pebble and shingle, almost never sand, and the rocks are slick underfoot and sometimes sharp. Rubber swimming shoes cost a few euros in any supermarket and are the single best purchase you will make on this trip.

What is actually open, and when

The ferries

The full network — car ferries plus the fast catamarans that make island-hopping practical — runs roughly from late May to early October. Outside that, the main car-ferry links to the larger islands keep going on a reduced winter timetable, but many catamaran routes stop altogether. If a particular island crossing is load-bearing for your trip, check the operator’s own timetable before booking the room at the far end.

Coastal hotels

A great many seaside hotels, especially the resort ones outside the old towns, close from about November to Easter. The town-centre hotels in Dubrovnik, Split, Zadar and Rovinj generally stay open all year, but with fewer restaurants and no pool.

The national parks

Plitvice and Krka are open all year and are genuinely beautiful under snow, though some trails and the boat links close in winter. Entry to Plitvice is by timed ticket bought in advance in the busy months, and the tickets for the best morning slots go early.

Zagreb and the interior

Open all year and largely unaffected by the season, which is exactly why the capital is the right answer for a winter trip to Croatia.

Events worth timing a trip around

Zagreb Advent · late November to early January

Repeatedly voted the best Christmas market in Europe, and spread across the squares and parks of the lower town rather than penned into one. Mulled wine, skating, and a city at its most likeable.

Rijeka Carnival · the weeks before Lent

One of the largest carnivals in Europe, in a working port city that does not otherwise trouble the guidebooks. Late winter, cold, and tremendous fun.

Dubrovnik Summer Festival · traditionally 10 July to 25 August

Six weeks of theatre, opera and concerts staged in the courtyards, forts and squares of the old city. It is the one thing that makes high-summer Dubrovnik worth the crowds — but book both tickets and rooms a long way ahead.

Istrian truffle season · September to November

The white truffle comes in around September and runs to late autumn, and Istria’s hill towns hold fairs through October. Pair it with a coast that has emptied out and hotel prices that have halved.

Timing it in your later years

If you are travelling in your seventies or eighties, the calendar matters more, not less.

Avoid July and August outright if you can

Not for the crowds — for the heat. Dalmatia in high summer sits in the mid-eighties and often higher, with fierce glare off white stone, very little shade on the Dubrovnik walls and inside Diocletian’s Palace, and long queues in the sun. May, June, September and October are simply kinder.

Late September is the gentlest fortnight of the year

Warm sea, mild air, thin crowds, everything still running, and a hotel room at a sensible price. If somebody asked us to pick two weeks for a couple in their eighties, it would be the second half of September without hesitating.

Winter is quiet but slippery

The coast in winter is mild, empty and rather lovely — and its polished limestone is treacherous in the rain. If you go, go for the towns rather than the walking, and take shoes with real grip.

Book the ferry and the timed tickets early

Plitvice’s morning slots, the Dubrovnik walls at opening time and the catamaran seats all sell out in the busy months. Being able to go at the cool end of the day is worth far more than saving a few euros by leaving it to chance.

Best time to visit Croatia — questions

What is the single best month to visit Croatia?

September, if you want to swim, and May if you do not. September gives you a sea that is warmer than it was in June, air in the high seventies rather than the high eighties, a nearly complete ferry timetable and about a third off the August room rate. May gives you green hills, wild flowers, open hotels, cool walking weather and the lowest prices of the open season, at the cost of a sea that only the brave will enjoy.

How warm is the sea, and when can you swim?

Warmer and for longer than most people expect. At Dubrovnik the sea averages around 65°F in May, 71°F in June, 76°F in July, 77°F in August, 74°F in September and still about 69°F in October. Most visitors find it comfortable from mid-June to early October, and hardy swimmers go from mid-May. The Adriatic warms slowly and cools slowly, which is why September beats June for swimming and April is colder than November.

Is Croatia too crowded in July and August?

In particular places at particular hours, yes. Dubrovnik's old town, Hvar town and Plitvice in the middle of a July day are genuinely uncomfortable. But Croatia is a long country and the crowding is very unevenly spread: Zadar, Šibenik, the interior, the smaller islands and Istria's hill villages are busy rather than overwhelmed even in August. If you must travel in high summer, choose the quieter towns as your base and visit the famous ones early in the morning.

Do the ferries run all year?

The main car-ferry links to the larger islands run all year, but on a much reduced winter timetable, and the fast catamarans that make island-hopping practical are largely a summer service. The full timetable is roughly late May to early October. If your trip depends on a particular island crossing outside those months, check the operator's own timetable before you book the hotel at the other end.

When is it cheapest?

November to March on the coast, when rooms can be a third of their August price — with the caveat that much of the coast is closed. Within the open season, late April, May, late September and October are the value months. The difference between the first week of September and the first week of August, for the same room, is often forty per cent.

What about the winter, and Zagreb at Christmas?

Winter is the one time we would send you inland rather than to the sea. Zagreb from late November to early January is charming: an Advent market that has repeatedly been voted the best in Europe, skating in the park, and a city that is warm indoors and thoroughly used to being cold outdoors. The coast in winter is mild but shuttered, and a good many restaurants and hotels simply close.
