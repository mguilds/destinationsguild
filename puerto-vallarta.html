Puerto Vallarta Travel & Adventure Guide

Cobbled streets running down a mountainside into the Pacific — a sculpture-lined Malecón, beaches you can only reach by boat, and humpbacks in the bay all winter.

Puerto Vallarta has something most Mexican beach resorts do not: a town that existed before the tourists. It was a quiet fishing and mining port on Banderas Bay until 1963, when John Huston filmed The Night of the Iguana down the coast and Richard Burton and Elizabeth Taylor conducted their affair in full view of the world's press — and Vallarta was made. What survived that fame is the good part: cobbled streets, whitewashed walls with red-tiled roofs, a crowned church on the skyline, and a seafront Malecón studded with bronze sculptures. Behind it all the Sierra Madre rises straight out of the sea, which is why the southern arm of the bay has no coast road at all: to reach Yelapa or Las Ánimas you take a boat. Add humpback whales from December, and this becomes one of the most complete beach towns in the country.

Banderas Bay is one of the largest bays in the Americas, and its shape explains the town. The north shore is flat and open — that's where the airport, the marina, and the big resort strips of the Hotel Zone and Nuevo Vallarta are. The centre is the old town, climbing the hillside in cobbles. And the south shore is mountain: the Sierra Madre drops directly into the water, the road simply ends at Boca de Tomatlán, and the beaches beyond it — Colomitos, Las Ánimas, Quimixto, Yelapa — are reachable only by water taxi. That geography is Vallarta's best asset.

The heart of town is the Zona Romántica, south of the Río Cuale: cobbled, walkable, dense with restaurants and bars, fronted by Playa Los Muertos and its pier. It is also the centre of one of the most established LGBTQ+ scenes in Latin America, and the town as a whole is notably relaxed and welcoming. North of the river runs the Malecón, the seafront promenade, lined with the bronze sculptures — Sergio Bustamante's ladder-climbing figures, the seahorse — that have become the city's signature.

Vallarta is a walking town, and a hilly one — the cobbles are charming and murder on wheeled suitcases and bad knees. Buses along the bay are cheap and frequent, water taxis handle the south shore, and the whole place is compact enough that you rarely need a car.

Why visit Puerto Vallarta

Four reasons Vallarta outlasts the resorts that copied it.

It's a real town

Cobbles, a crowned church, a plaza, a river running through it. You are staying somewhere that existed before tourism, and it shows.

Beaches by boat only

The Sierra Madre kills the road south, so Yelapa, Las Ánimas and Colomitos can only be reached by water. Few resorts can offer that.

Whales in the bay

Humpbacks arrive around December to breed and calve in Banderas Bay's deep, sheltered water, and stay until roughly March.

The Malecón

A mile of seafront promenade lined with bronze sculptures, buskers and sand artists — and the best sunset walk in Mexico.

Top things to do in Puerto Vallarta

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Walk the Malecón at sunset

Start at the Hotel Rosita and work south past the sculptures to Los Arcos amphitheatre. Do it every evening; nobody minds.

● Water taxi to Yelapa

A village with no road in — a waterfall behind it, a long beach in front, and pie ladies working the sand. Stay the night if you can.

● Whale watching

December to March. The bay is deep and the humpbacks come right in; breaching whales are a routine sight in season.

● Zona Romántica & Los Muertos

The Old Town's cobbles, the pier at Playa Los Muertos, and the best concentration of restaurants in the city.

● Marietas Islands

A protected archipelago off the bay's mouth, famous for its hidden beach inside a collapsed crater. Visitor numbers are capped — book ahead.

● Day trip to Sayulita

An hour north in Nayarit: a surf-and-bohemia town of coloured flags, board rentals and very good tacos.

Where to stay in Puerto Vallarta

The Zona Romántica (Old Town) is the best base for most travellers: cobbled, walkable, packed with restaurants and bars, and fronted by Playa Los Muertos and its pier — you can live here without a taxi, and it's the departure point for the south-shore water taxis. The Centro, just north across the river, puts you on the Malecón itself. The Hotel Zone and Marina Vallarta, further north, hold the larger resorts, the calmer swimming beaches and the boat docks — more convenient, less charming. Nuevo Vallarta and the Riviera Nayarit, over the state line, are where the big all-inclusives sit on long flat sand; excellent for families, but a real drive from the old town. And Conchas Chinas, on the hillside just south of the centre, is the quiet upscale option with the best views over the bay.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

November–April is the dry season and the peak: warm, sunny, reliably pleasant, with whale season from December to March. June–October is the rainy season — hot, humid and green, with heavy afternoon downpours and a tropical-storm risk, but much cheaper and far quieter. May and early November are the shoulder sweet spots.

Getting there

Puerto Vallarta International (PVR) sits just north of the city, roughly 15–30 minutes from the Zona Romántica, and is very well served with direct flights from across the US and Canada — especially in winter. Take an authorised airport taxi or a pre-booked transfer; as in Cabo, the arrivals hall has persistent timeshare touts.

Puerto Vallarta travel FAQ

Where should you stay in Puerto Vallarta?

The Zona Romántica — the Old Town south of the river — is the best base for most visitors: cobbled streets, Playa Los Muertos, the best restaurants, everything walkable. The Hotel Zone and Marina Vallarta, north of the centre, have the large resorts and calmer beaches. Nuevo Vallarta, further north in Nayarit, is where the big all-inclusives are, but it's a drive from town.

When can you see whales in Puerto Vallarta?

Humpback whales come into Banderas Bay to breed and calve roughly from December to March. The bay is deep and sheltered, and sightings in season are common — boat tours run daily from the marina, and whales are often visible from shore.

How do you get to Yelapa and the jungle beaches?

By boat. The beaches on the bay's southern shore — Yelapa, Las Ánimas, Quimixto and Colomitos — have no road access. Water taxis leave from Los Muertos pier in the Zona Romántica and from Boca de Tomatlán, a short bus ride south, which is the cheaper and quicker departure point.

When is the best time to visit Puerto Vallarta?

November through April is the dry season — warm, sunny and reliably pleasant, with whale season from December. The rainy season runs roughly June to October: hot, humid, green, with heavy afternoon downpours and the possibility of tropical storms — but far cheaper and much quieter.
