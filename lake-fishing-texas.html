Lake Fishing in Texas — Six Big Lakes, from Lake Fork Bass to Texoma Stripers

Sam Rayburn, Toledo Bend, Lake Fork, Falcon, Lake Texoma and Choke Canyon: what swims in each, how people fish them, the rules Texas Parks and Wildlife set for each lake, how full the water is this autumn, and the towns to sleep in.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against Texas Parks and Wildlife and the Texas Water Development Board, 10 November 2026

Texas has very few natural lakes. Almost every big lake in the state is a reservoir, built on a river within the last hundred years, and that is exactly why the fishing is so good: flooded timber, creek channels and miles of weed give bass, crappie and catfish somewhere to live and grow. The six lakes on this page are among the best known in the country. Lake Fork, Sam Rayburn and Toledo Bend are the big-bass lakes of East Texas; Lake Texoma on the Oklahoma line is famous for striped bass that breed on their own; Falcon on the Rio Grande and Choke Canyon south of San Antonio are the South Texas lakes, where the water rises and falls with the droughts.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers, crappie and catfish fishermen, and anyone who wants a lake, a cabin and perhaps a fish fry. Texas sets special rules lake by lake, and two of these lakes cross a state or national border, so we have quoted Texas Parks and Wildlife (TPWD) wherever a number is involved. Two of the six — Falcon and Choke Canyon — were only about a fifth full on 9 November 2026. We say exactly what that means below.

Where these facts come from. Every rule, limit and price on this page was read on 10 November 2026 from the bodies that set them: Texas Parks and Wildlife Department’s lake pages, its lake-by-lake fishing regulations, its licence pages, its fish consumption advisories and its state park pages for Eisenhower, Falcon and Choke Canyon; and the Texas Water Development Board’s Water Data for Texas for how full each lake is. The best months, in plain words

Texas lakes fish all year. Summer is hot, so the bite moves to early morning, evening and night.

Sam Rayburn Reservoir — 114,500 acres of Piney Woods bass water

114,500 acres on the Angelina River in the Piney Woods. The dam is about 15 miles north of Jasper.

Sam Rayburn, from Jasper, Lufkin and Brookeland

What swims there. Largemouth bass first — TPWD calls it an excellent fishery all year — with excellent crappie and catfish, white bass in spring, and plenty of bluegill and redear sunfish, which TPWD singles out as good fishing for children and beginners.

How people fish it. Mostly from boats, along the edges of the hydrilla beds, on flats and humps, and on creek channels in the clearer lower lake; around standing timber and brush in the upper third. In autumn, winter and spring, crankbaits and spinnerbaits in shallow water; in summer, topwater at dawn and dusk, then worms, jigs and Carolina rigs on the deep ledges. Crappie come on jigs and minnows around brush piles. TPWD and its partners have sunk fish attractors, and its Habitat Structure Viewer gives their GPS points.

Keeping a fish. Bass at least 14 inches, five a day. Crappie at least 10 inches, 25 a day. Blue and channel catfish have no minimum but a generous 50-a-day limit, with no more than five of 30 inches or over. There is a fish consumption advisory on this lake — see the box below before you plan a fish fry.

Boat ramps. TPWD counts 22 maintained access areas around the lake, each with a ramp; thirteen are Corps of Engineers parks. With the lake at 60% this autumn, some may be closed: TPWD advises checking “current water levels and ramp conditions” first.

Confirmed with Texas Parks and Wildlife, 10 November 2026 — Sam Rayburn lake page and regulations: “For largemouth and smallmouth bass, minimum length limit = 14 inches”; “Daily bag limit for all black bass species is 5”; “For blue and channel catfish, no minimum length limit; daily bag and possession limit = 50 in any combination, of which no more than 5 may be 30 inches or greater in length.” Crappie: 10 inches, 25 a day. White bass: 10 inches, 25. Striped and hybrid bass: 18 inches, 5. Access: “Twenty-two maintained access areas (including parks and private concessions) are located around the reservoir.” Consumption advisory (dioxins and mercury), for adults: “Smallmouth buffalo: Do not eat. Flathead catfish and gar: no more than one 8-ounce serving per month. Blue catfish over 30 inches long, largemouth bass over 16 inches and spotted bass over 16 inches: no more than two 8-ounce servings per month.” Women of childbearing age and children under 12 “should not consume the indicated fishes”.

Nearest towns: Jasper to the south, Lufkin to the west, and little Brookeland near the dam.

Toledo Bend — the Texas side of a two-state lake

181,600 acres on the Sabine River, straddling the Texas–Louisiana line. The dam is about 24 miles north-east of Jasper.

Toledo Bend, from Hemphill

What swims there. Largemouth bass, again excellent all year, with good crappie and catfish, white bass, and striped bass, which TPWD keeps going by stocking them every year because they cannot usually spawn here. Bluegill and redear are plentiful — TPWD calls the bream fishing “excellent” for young or new anglers.

How people fish it. Like Sam Rayburn: hydrilla edges, flats, humps and creek channels in the clear lower and middle lake, timber and brush in the upper lake, and flooded bushes reservoir-wide when the water is high. Toledo Bend moves much less than Rayburn — TPWD gives 1 to 5 feet.

One licence for both sides. A properly licensed Texas or Louisiana resident, or anyone with a non-resident licence from either state, may fish the whole lake. Bass, crappie, catfish and white bass limits are the same on both sides. Other species, including alligator gar, can differ: for Louisiana’s rules, ask the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries.

Keeping a fish. Here the bass limit is eight a day, not five, each at least 14 inches, with ten in possession. Crappie have no minimum length (25 a day). Striped bass: five a day, only two of them 30 inches or more. There is a mercury advisory for largemouth bass and freshwater drum.

Confirmed with Texas Parks and Wildlife, 10 November 2026 — Toledo Bend regulations: “For largemouth and smallmouth bass, minimum length limit = 14 inches”; “Daily bag limit for all black bass species is 8 in any combination. Possession limit = 10.” “For black and white crappie there is no MLL and daily bag = 25”; blue and channel catfish 50 a day, no more than 5 over 30 inches; flathead 18 inches, 10; “For striped bass, no MLL, daily bag = 5, and only 2 striped bass, 30 inches or greater, may be retained each day.” Lake page: “Limits for catfish, crappie, white bass and black basses apply on both the Texas and Louisiana portions of the lake.” Advisory (mercury): “For largemouth bass and freshwater drum, adults should limit consumption to no more than two, 8-ounce meals per month, and children should limit consumption to no more than two, 4-ounce meals per month.”

Nearest town: Hemphill, the Sabine County seat on the Texas shore. Jasper is the bigger town to the south-west, between Toledo Bend and Sam Rayburn.

Lake Fork — the trophy bass lake

27,264 acres on a fork of the Sabine River, about five miles north-west of Quitman and under two hours from Dallas.

Lake Fork, from Quitman, Emory, Alba and Mineola

What swims there. Big largemouth bass. TPWD says more than 65% of the Texas Top 50 largest bass, including the current state record, and more than half of all the bass entered in its Toyota ShareLunker programme, came from Lake Fork. Also good crappie, channel catfish (with flatheads and the odd big blue), white bass, bluegill and redear, and in winter yellow bass — “barfish” — small but, TPWD says, “as tasty as crappie”.

How people fish it. By boat, along buoyed boat lanes: submerged timber covers much of the lake and TPWD warns it is “a substantial hazard”. Bass anglers work hydrilla, docks, boathouses, points and brush piles. In the spring peak (mid-February to April) it is spinnerbaits, soft plastics, jigs and lipless crankbaits on the bank; in summer, night fishing; in winter, spoons, jigs and crankbaits. Crappie anglers sit under the bridges — Highway 154, Highway 515, CR 2946 and CR 514 — in late spring and early autumn, and deep near the dam in winter.

Keeping a fish — the slot. Lake Fork’s bass rule is what keeps it great. Every largemouth between 16 and 24 inches goes back. You may keep bass of 16 inches or less, or 24 inches or more — but only one of 24 inches or more a day — and five black bass in all. Crappie have a winter rule of their own: from 1 December to the end of February there is no minimum length and every crappie you catch must be kept, up to 25.

Boat ramps. The Sabine River Authority runs four public ramps and a free day-use area, and many private marinas and resorts have their own (see Marinas & lodges).

Confirmed with Texas Parks and Wildlife, 10 November 2026 — Lake Fork regulations: “For largemouth bass, length limit is a 16–24” slot. Bass 16 inches and less or 24 inches or greater in length may be retained. Only one largemouth bass 24 inches or greater may be retained each day.” “Daily bag limit for all black bass species is 5 in any combination.” “For black and white crappie caught from December 1 through the last day of February, there is no minimum length limit and all crappie caught must be retained. From March through November, minimum length is 10 inches. Year-round daily bag limit is 25 crappie.” Channel and blue catfish: 25, only 10 of 20 inches or more; flathead: 18 inches, 5. Lake page: “More than 65% of the Texas Top 50 largest bass (including the current state record) and more than half of those entered in the Toyota ShareLunker Program, were caught from Lake Fork.” Access: “The Sabine River Authority (903) 878-2262 operates four public boat ramps and a free day use area.”

Nearest towns: Quitman (south-east), Emory (west), Alba (on the west side, with many of the lodges), and Mineola, a little further south.

Falcon International Reservoir — big bass on the Rio Grande

83,654 acres when full, on the Rio Grande about 40 miles east of Laredo in Zapata and Starr counties. The far shore is Mexico.

Falcon, from Zapata and Falcon State Park

What swims there. TPWD says Falcon “has long been regarded as one of the best largemouth bass lakes in the state” — winning a tournament here can take a five- to six-pound average per fish. Channel catfish too, with the occasional blue or flathead. Recent droughts have hurt the white bass and crappie.

This year. The lake was 21% full on 9 November. Falcon has come back from low water before, and big bass can fish well in a low lake, but ramps change with the level. Ask Falcon State Park or Zapata County before you tow a boat.

Stay on the Texas side. The international line runs down the lake. On the Texas side, statewide rules apply except for alligator gar (five a day here, and no harvest report needed). In Mexican waters, every person in the boat needs a Mexican fishing licence. For a family trip our plain advice is to stay in Texas waters, and to read the U.S. State Department’s current travel advisory for the Mexican state of Tamaulipas before you go.

Boat ramps. TPWD lists two: the free Zapata County ramp at Arroyo Veleno, off US 83 about a mile south of the Arroyo Veleno bridge, and the ramp in Falcon State Park at the lower end (park entry fee), with a fish-cleaning station and campsites, shelters and cabins.

Confirmed with Texas Parks and Wildlife, 10 November 2026 — Falcon lake page: “On the Texas side of the lake, the daily bag limit for alligator gar is 5 fish, with no minimum length limit… All other fishes are managed under statewide regulations.” “For recreational anglers fishing Mexican waters, a Mexico fishing license is required for everyone in the boat. Mexico boat permits are no longer required.” TPWD names two Zapata shops that sell Mexican licences: Robert’s Fish N’ Tackle (2425 S. Highway 83) and Falcon Lake Tackle (2195 S. Highway 83). Fluctuation: “Severe, 40 to 50 feet or more.” Falcon State Park notice of 4 November 2026: the park “is within an infested zone for the new world screwworm” — check pets daily for cuts and wounds.

Nearest towns: Zapata, on US 83 beside the lake; Rio Grande City, further down the Rio Grande, for more hotels.

Lake Texoma — striped bass that breed on their own

74,686 acres on the Red River, on the Texas–Oklahoma line north-west of Sherman and Denison. About two-thirds of it is in Oklahoma.

Lake Texoma, from Denison, Pottsboro and Gordonville

What swims there. Striped bass are the draw. TPWD says Texoma is one of the few freshwater lakes in the United States with a self-sustaining population of landlocked stripers: the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation stocked them in 1965, and they were found breeding by 1974. There are also largemouth, smallmouth and spotted bass, white bass, crappie, and big catfish — the Texas state record blue catfish, 121.5 pounds, came from Texoma in 2004.

How people fish it. Striper guides run trips all year and, TPWD says, can take families and big groups. Bank anglers fish for catfish at Eisenhower State Park and Hagerman National Wildlife Refuge. Eisenhower State Park has fishing piers (one lighted for night fishing), a fish-cleaning station, a boat ramp with a courtesy dock, and rods and tackle you can borrow.

Keeping a fish. Stripers and hybrids: ten a day (20 in possession), but only two of 20 inches or more, and culling is not allowed. Crappie: 37 a day at 10 inches. Blue and channel catfish: 15 together, only one blue of 30 inches or more. Walleye: five, only two under 16 inches. No alligator gar may be taken in May. Zebra mussels are in the lake: drain your boat, livewell and bait bucket before you leave — it is the law.

Which licence. A Texas licence covers the Texas waters. A $12 Lake Texoma licence lets you fish the whole lake (see Licences).

Confirmed with Texas Parks and Wildlife, 10 November 2026 — Texoma regulations: “For striped bass and hybrid striped bass, no MLL, daily bag = 10 and possession limit = 20, and only 2 striped or hybrid striped bass, 20 inches or greater, may be retained each day. Culling of striped bass and hybrid striped bass is prohibited.” “For black and white crappie, minimum length limit = 10 inches and daily bag limit = 37 in any combination. Possession limit = 50 crappie.” “For blue and channel catfish, no minimum length; combined daily bag limit = 15 fish. Only 1 blue catfish 30 inches or greater in length may be retained each day.” Walleye and saugeye: “only two of the 5-fish daily bag can be under 16 inches.” Gar: “During May, no person shall take alligator gar from, or possess alligator gar while on, the Red River (including Lake Texoma)”. “Zebra mussels have been found in this reservoir.” Eisenhower State Park notices: Fossil Ridge fishing pier closed (7 May 2026); because of road construction, take Exit 72 off Highway 75.

Nearest towns: Denison, by the dam; Pottsboro, on the western side of Denison near Eisenhower State Park and the marinas; and Gordonville, at the far west end by Cedar Mills.

Choke Canyon Reservoir — South Texas bass, catfish and alligator gar

25,670 acres when full, on the Frio River four miles west of Three Rivers, between San Antonio and Corpus Christi.

Choke Canyon, from Three Rivers and George West

What swims there. Largemouth bass and catfish are the favourites. TPWD reports “frequent reports of double-digit fish” — bass of ten pounds and more. Blue catfish are the most common catfish, with good flatheads and channels. White bass are excellent in the cooler months. And alligator gar: TPWD says catches of trophy fish over six feet long are frequent.

How people fish it. From a boat or from the shore of Choke Canyon State Park. The park has two parts: Calliham, with campsites and lodging, and South Shore, for day use only (6 a.m. to 10 p.m.). You do not need a licence to fish from shore inside the state park; park entry fees still apply. Alligators live here too — the park posts alligator safety tips.

This year. The lake was 21% full on 9 November. TPWD’s park notice lists four ramps open — Calliham, South Shore, Mason Point and Highway 99 — and asks for “extreme caution” at the Highway 99 and Mason Point ramps because of Frio River conditions. The park closes for permitted hunts: South Shore from 5 p.m. on 30 November to 10 p.m. on 3 December, and Calliham from 5 p.m. on 4 January to 10 p.m. on 8 January.

Keeping a fish. Blue and channel catfish must be at least 14 inches, 15 a day together; bass at least 14 inches, five a day; one alligator gar a day, which must be reported to TPWD within 24 hours.

Confirmed with Texas Parks and Wildlife, 10 November 2026 — Choke Canyon regulations: “For blue and channel catfish, minimum length = 14 inches and combined daily bag limit = 15 fish. For flathead catfish, minimum length = 18 inches and daily bag = 5.” “For alligator gar, daily bag limit is 1 fish of any size.” “All alligator gar harvested from the public waters of the state other than Falcon International Reservoir must be reported within 24 hours.” Lake page: “Bass are abundant with frequent reports of double-digit fish being boated.” “Catches of trophy fish (> 6-ft) by anglers are frequent.” Park notice (26 July 2026): “You may launch below the concrete ramp when conditions are safe. Please watch for debris in the water and launch at your own risk.”

Nearest towns: Three Rivers, at the east end of the lake; George West, a little further east.

The licence and the limits, plainly

One Texas licence covers every lake on this page. Children under 17 fish free.

Who needs one, what it costs, how long it lasts

From 17. Everyone 17 and over needs a fishing licence with a freshwater endorsement, resident or visitor. The easy way is a freshwater package, which includes both. Texans born before 1 January 1931 are exempt.

The licence year ends on 31 August. A package bought in November 2026 is good until 31 August 2027.

Just a day or two? A one-day all-water licence needs no endorsement, and you can buy consecutive days at once.

Free fishing. No licence is needed to fish from shore, or from a pier, inside a Texas state park (entry fees still apply) — that includes Eisenhower, Falcon and Choke Canyon. And on Free Fishing Day, the first Saturday in June (5 June 2027), nobody needs one.

Border lakes. Toledo Bend: a Texas or Louisiana licence works on the whole lake. Lake Texoma: a Texas licence for Texas waters, or the Lake Texoma licence for the whole lake. Falcon: Texas waters only, unless everyone aboard has a Mexican licence.

Confirmed with Texas Parks and Wildlife, 10 November 2026 — licence pages: Resident Freshwater Package (item 231) $30; Senior Freshwater Package (Texas residents 65 and over, item 234) $12; Non-resident Freshwater Package (item 250) $58, “valid from the date of sale to Aug. 31”; One-Day All-Water License $11 resident (item 213), $16 non-resident (item 214), “Endorsements are not required for this license”; Freshwater Fishing Endorsement on its own $5; Lake Texoma License (item 208) $12, “valid until December 31 following the date of issuance”, “valid only on Lake Texoma”. Not required: “a person under 17 years of age”; “a person born before January 1, 1931”; also exempt in Texas: Louisiana residents 65 or older with a valid Louisiana licence, and Oklahoma residents 65 or older. “Free Fishing Day: Saturday, June 5, 2027.” Prices change. Buy from Texas Parks and Wildlife or a licensed retailer.

Questions

Do I need a fishing licence to fish a lake in Texas, and what does it cost?

Yes, from age 17, resident or visitor, unless you are fishing from shore inside a Texas state park. Texas Parks and Wildlife lists a resident freshwater package at $30, a senior resident (65 and over) package at $12 and a non-resident freshwater package at $58, each valid to 31 August. A one-day all-water licence is $11 for residents and $16 for non-residents. Under-17s fish free. Check the current price with TPWD when you buy.

What is the bass slot limit on Lake Fork?

Largemouth bass between 16 and 24 inches must go back. Bass of 16 inches or less, or 24 inches or more, may be kept, but only one of 24 inches or more a day, and the daily bag for all black bass together is five. That is the TPWD regulation for Lake Fork, read on 10 November 2026.

Which licence do I need for Lake Texoma?

A Texas fishing licence covers the Texas waters of Lake Texoma. About two-thirds of the lake is in Oklahoma. To fish the whole lake, TPWD sells a Lake Texoma licence for $12, valid until 31 December following the date it is issued; it is valid only on Lake Texoma. Ask TPWD or the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation which licence suits you before you cross the line.

Can I fish the Louisiana side of Toledo Bend with a Texas licence?

Yes. TPWD says residents of Texas or Louisiana licensed in their own state, or exempt by age, and anyone holding a non-resident licence from either state, may fish any part of the border lakes, including Toledo Bend. The limits for catfish, crappie, white bass and black bass are the same on both sides; some other species, such as alligator gar, differ. Fish landed in Texas must meet Texas limits.

Can I fish the Mexican side of Falcon Lake?

Only with a Mexican fishing licence for everyone in the boat, TPWD says; Mexican boat permits are no longer required. The licence is issued under Mexico’s fisheries agency, CONAPESCA. Our honest advice for a family trip is to stay in Texas waters, and to read the U.S. State Department travel advisory for the Mexican state of Tamaulipas before you go.

What we could not check today, and will not pretend to have

The part of a fishing page most sites leave out.

Five honest gaps

Ramp-by-ramp status. We read TPWD’s park notices for Choke Canyon, Falcon and Eisenhower, but not the Corps of Engineers or Sabine River Authority ramp lists for Sam Rayburn, Toledo Bend and Lake Fork. With Rayburn at 60% and Falcon and Choke Canyon near 21%, call before you tow a boat.

Park entry fees. Texas state parks charge a daily entry fee, and some Corps parks charge a day-use fee; we did not confirm today’s figures. Check with each park.

Louisiana, Oklahoma and Mexico. We checked the Texas rules only. For the Louisiana side of Toledo Bend ask the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries; for the Oklahoma side of Texoma, the Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation; for Mexican waters on Falcon, CONAPESCA. The U.S. State Department’s website would not open for us today, so we have not quoted its Mexico advisory — read it yourself before going near the border.

Fish consumption advice changes. We have quoted the Sam Rayburn and Toledo Bend advisories as TPWD printed them today. The Texas Department of State Health Services issues them; check the current wording before you eat your catch, especially if you are pregnant or feeding children.

Guides on Falcon, Texoma and Choke Canyon. We have listed the businesses whose own websites we opened. We have not yet checked the many striper and bass guides on Texoma and Falcon one by one. They will be added at the same standard.
