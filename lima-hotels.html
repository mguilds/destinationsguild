Where to Stay in Lima

Where to Stay in Lima — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Lima is enormous, and the part of it you want is small. Miraflores is the answer for nearly every visitor: a clifftop district above the Pacific with wide level pavements, a mile of public park along the edge, and the restaurants, pharmacies and taxis all within a few flat blocks. Barranco, next door, is prettier and quieter — painted houses, galleries and the old wooden bridge — but it has cobbles and steps. San Isidro is the green, calm, moneyed business district, lovely by day and rather silent in the evening. The colonial centre holds the Plaza Mayor and the catacombs, and is worth a morning with a driver rather than a night. Below are the hotels we would send our own readers to, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

A word for travellers taking it gently. Lima is at sea level, so nothing here is an altitude problem — enjoy that while it lasts, because Cusco is not. The clifftop malecón in Miraflores is the best walking in the city: paved, level, railed and continuous for several miles, with benches and cafes the whole way. Lima’s drivers are fast and its crossings are enthusiastic rather than orderly, so cross at lights. From June to October the city sits under a grey sea fog and rooms can feel damp; check that yours has heating if you feel the cold.

Lima Hotels — Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of Lima should I stay in?

Miraflores for almost everyone. It sits on the cliffs above the Pacific, the pavements are wide and level, the clifftop park runs for miles, and the restaurants, pharmacies and taxis are all at hand. Barranco next door is quieter, prettier and more bohemian, with cobbles and some steps. San Isidro is the leafy business district — calm and green, shorter on evening life. The colonial centre is worth a morning with a driver but is not where you want your bed.

Should I stay near Lima airport?

Only if your onward flight leaves very early or lands very late. The airport is at Callao, a district apart from the tourist city, and the drive to Miraflores takes forty minutes in light traffic and well over an hour in the rush. There is a large hotel connected to the terminal, and for a six o’clock departure to Cusco it is worth every penny. For any other night, stay by the sea.

Is it safe to walk around Miraflores and Barranco?

Both are among the safest districts in Lima and are comfortable to walk by day and in the early evening on the main streets. The usual city sense applies: keep your phone out of sight, use a hotel taxi or a ride-hailing app rather than flagging a cab in the street, and take a taxi rather than walk between districts after dark. Lima’s drivers are quick, so cross at lights.

Do Lima hotels have air conditioning and heating?

Lima almost never freezes and almost never bakes, so a good many older and smaller hotels have neither. In the grey winter months, June to October, rooms can feel damp and chilly. If that matters to you, check the room description on the booking page before you pay rather than assuming, and ask for a room away from the street if you are a light sleeper.

Do I need a car in Lima?

No. Traffic is heavy and fast and parking is difficult. Each district is walkable within itself, and taxis and ride-hailing apps are inexpensive for moving between them. Book a car through your hotel for the airport run, and allow far more time than the map suggests.
