Chiang Mai Travel Guide

The old Lanna capital in the northern hills — a moated Old City thick with temples, mountains on the doorstep, and the best food and markets in Thailand.

Find Chiang Mai Hotels Search Flights to CNX

Chiang Mai is Thailand turned down a few degrees — cooler, greener and slower than the coasts and the capital, wrapped in the hills of the far north. Founded in 1296 as the capital of the Lanna kingdom, its Old City still sits inside a square moat and the crumbling remains of a brick wall, and within that grid you'll find dozens of temples, teak monasteries and glinting golden chedis. But the city is only half of it. The Lanna highlands rise almost from the edge of town: mist-wrapped national parks, waterfalls, hill-tribe villages, and the sanctuaries where rescued elephants roam. Add markets that go on for hours, food that many travellers rate the best in the country, and prices that make it easy to linger — and you understand why so many people who come for a few days end up staying a season.

Orientation is easy, because the city was built around a square. The Old City is the moated grid — roughly a mile on each side — and it holds the greatest concentration of temples: Wat Chedi Luang with its vast ruined stupa, Wat Phra Singh at the western gate, and a hundred smaller wats down the sois. Just west, across the moat, Nimmanhaemin — everyone calls it “Nimman” — is the modern quarter of cafés, design shops and coworking spaces near the university. To the east, the Ping River runs past quieter, more upscale hotels. And rising over the whole city to the west is Doi Suthep, the mountain whose golden temple you can see glinting on a clear day.

One thing to weigh before you book your dates, because it genuinely affects the trip: the March–April burning season. Farmers across the north clear their fields by fire in the dry months, and the smoke settles in Chiang Mai's mountain-ringed valley, pushing air quality to some of the worst readings on earth for weeks at a stretch. It is not a rumour or an exaggeration — the mountains disappear into haze and the sunsets turn an eerie brown. If you have any choice at all, come in the cool-dry window of November to February instead.

Chiang Mai is famously good value — a bowl of khao soi for a couple of dollars, guesthouses for the price of a coffee back home, and even the smart boutique hotels a fraction of what the beaches charge. Thailand keeps Indochina Time (UTC+7) year-round with no daylight saving, so plan your calls home accordingly. Cash is still king at the markets, though cards and mobile payment are increasingly accepted in Nimman and the malls.

Why visit Chiang Mai

Four reasons the old Lanna capital is worth the trip north.

Temples in the Old City

Inside the moat you can walk between centuries-old wats all day — teak halls, ruined stupas and monks in saffron, all within the same square mile.

Mountains on the doorstep

National parks, waterfalls and cool cloud-forest ridges begin at the city limits. Doi Inthanon, Thailand's highest peak, is a day trip away.

Ethical elephant sanctuaries

The hills around Chiang Mai are home to no-riding sanctuaries where you can watch and feed rescued elephants living out their lives in peace.

Food, markets & prices

Northern kitchens, night bazaars and weekend walking streets — some of Thailand's best eating, at prices that make it easy to stay a while.

Top things to do in Chiang Mai

The experiences worth building the trip around.

● Old City temples

Start with Wat Chedi Luang and its enormous ruined stupa, then Wat Phra Singh's gilded Lanna halls — the two great temples inside the moat.

● Wat Phra That Doi Suthep

Climb the 300-step naga staircase to the mountain temple above the city; the golden chedi and the valley view are the classic Chiang Mai image.

● An ethical elephant sanctuary

Spend a half or full day at a no-riding, observation-only sanctuary in the hills, feeding and watching rescued elephants roam and forage.

● Sunday Walking Street

Ratchadamnoen Road closes to traffic and fills with handicraft stalls, street food and buskers — the best of the city's weekend markets.

● The Night Bazaar

The nightly market east of the moat trades in silverwork, textiles and lanterns, with food courts humming until late.

● A Thai cooking class

Learn khao soi and northern curries from a market tour to the wok — or spend the day instead on Doi Inthanon, Thailand's highest peak.

Where to stay in Chiang Mai

Choose by the trip you want. The Old City, inside the moat, is for travellers who want to walk to the temples and wake up in the heart of old Lanna — guesthouses and boutique hotels down quiet sois, everything on foot. Nimmanhaemin — “Nimman” — is the modern quarter: cafés, design shops and coworking spaces near the university and the airport, and the choice for a younger, café-hopping stay. The Riverside, along the Ping, is quieter and more upscale, with leafy grounds and some of the city's smartest hotels. And the Night Bazaar area, between the moat and the river, is central and convenient — handy for markets and transport, if a little busier. First-timers who want temples on the doorstep should look hard at the Old City; anyone after cafés and design gravitates to Nimman.

Best time to visit & getting there

When to go

November–February is the cool-dry season and by far the best time: clear skies, comfortable days, and the Loy Krathong and Yi Peng lantern festivals in November. Avoid March–April, the “burning season,” when smoke from farm-field fires chokes the valley and air quality plummets. June–October is green, lush and rainy, with lower prices and afternoon downpours.

Getting there

Chiang Mai International (CNX) sits just southwest of the centre, about 15 minutes from the Old City, with one-hour flights from Bangkok and direct services from other Asian hubs. You can also arrive by overnight train or bus from Bangkok. Around town, songthaews — the red shared pickup trucks — and Grab get you where you're going cheaply.

Chiang Mai travel FAQ

When is the best time to visit Chiang Mai?

The cool-dry season from November to February is by far the best — clear skies, comfortable days and the Loy Krathong and Yi Peng lantern festivals in November. Avoid March and April, the farm-field burning season, when smoke settles in the valley and air quality can turn genuinely unhealthy. June to October is green, lush and rainy, with cheaper rooms and afternoon downpours.

How many days do you need in Chiang Mai?

Three to four days covers the essentials: a day for the Old City temples, a morning up at Wat Phra That Doi Suthep, an ethical elephant sanctuary and an evening market or two. Add a couple more days for a cooking class, a trip to Doi Inthanon, or a side journey to Chiang Rai or Pai.

How do I choose an ethical elephant sanctuary?

Choose an observation-only or feed-and-walk sanctuary that offers no elephant riding and no performances. Riding harms the animals' spines and the training behind it is cruel. Look for a genuine no-riding, no-show policy, small group sizes, and a focus on letting the elephants roam and forage rather than perform.

Which airport do you fly into for Chiang Mai?

Chiang Mai International Airport (CNX), just southwest of the city and about 15 minutes from the Old City. It has frequent one-hour flights from Bangkok and direct services from other Asian hubs; you can also arrive by overnight train or bus from Bangkok.
