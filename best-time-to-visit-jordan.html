Jordan Month by Month — When to Walk Petra and When to Swim

Best Time to Visit Jordan — Month by Month

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Jordan has a short, excellent spring, a short, excellent autumn, a severe summer in the south and a genuinely cold, wet winter in the north. Almost every decision about when to go follows from those four facts, and from one more: Aqaba runs on its own calendar and is at its best when everywhere else is at its worst.

The short answer

Go in March to May, or September to November. Those are the months when Amman, Petra, Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea are all comfortable at the same time, which is the only thing that matters on a trip that visits all four. April and October are the two best months and are priced accordingly. Go in December to February only if Aqaba is the point, or if you will accept cold rain in Amman and at Petra in exchange for low prices and empty sites. Avoid July and August in the south unless you are willing to rearrange the day around the heat.

Month by month

January and February $

Coldest and wettest. Amman is genuinely cold, grey and occasionally snowy; Petra sits high enough that snow has closed it for a day or two in past winters. The compensation is real: the sites are nearly empty and rates are at their lowest. Aqaba, meanwhile, is mild and dry and is the one place in the country having a good time.

March $$

Spring arrives, unevenly. Early March can still be wet; by the end of the month the hills are green, the wildflowers are out around Dana, and days are warm with cool nights. One of the best-value months in the year if you accept some weather risk.

April $$$

Prime, and widely agreed to be the best single month alongside October. Warm, dry, everything open and comfortable, the landscape still green. Easter brings crowds to the Christian sites and pushes Amman and Madaba rates up; book well ahead.

May $$$

Still excellent in the north and at Petra, but the south starts to turn by the second half of the month. Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea begin to feel like summer. The sea at Aqaba is warm and swimmable without being unpleasant.

June $$

Hot and emptying out. Wadi Rum, the Dead Sea and Aqaba are severe by midday; Amman, high and dry, remains workable. Prices soften as demand falls. Petra at opening time is still a fine experience; Petra at two in the afternoon is not.

July and August $$

The hardest months. Daytime temperatures in the Jordan Valley, Wadi Rum and at Aqaba regularly pass 40C, and the Dead Sea is the most punishing of all because of how far below sea level it sits. August also brings a wave of regional summer visitors to Amman, which lifts city hotel rates even as the south empties. If this is your window, start at dawn and stop at noon.

September $$

The first half is still hot in the south; the second half is where the good season begins again. By late September Petra is comfortable, the nights at Wadi Rum are pleasant, and the sea at Aqaba is at its warmest of the whole year.

October $$$

Prime, and our pick for the single best month. Warm days, cool nights, dry, with the sea still holding its summer heat. It is also busy and priced for it, so book early.

November $$

Excellent in the first half and cooling fast in the second. By late November Amman is reaching for a coat and the first rains arrive, while Aqaba steps into its own best season. Very good value in the last ten days.

December $

Cool and wet in the north, mild at Aqaba. Christmas brings pilgrims to Bethany Beyond the Jordan and Madaba, and a short price spike around the holiday itself. Outside those dates it is a quiet, cheap, cold month with the sites to yourself.

By region

Amman and the north

High, dry and four-seasoned. Cold and rainy from December to February, beautiful in spring and autumn, hot but bearable in summer because the air is dry and the nights cool off. Jerash is best in spring, when the ground around the ruins is green rather than dust.

Petra and Wadi Musa

The season that matters most, because Petra is a full day outdoors. March to May and September to November are right. Winter is cold, can be wet underfoot in the Siq, and very occasionally snowed out. Midsummer means starting at the gate when it opens and being finished by lunchtime.

Wadi Rum

A desert, so the gap between day and night is wide all year. Spring and autumn are ideal. Winter nights in a camp are properly cold and the better camps heat their tents — ask. Summer days are severe, though the nights are the best part of a summer trip.

The Dead Sea

The hottest place in the country in summer, by a margin, because of the altitude. Wonderful from October to April. In July and August treat it as an early-morning or evening activity and stay in the water or the shade.

Aqaba and the Red Sea

The exception to every rule above. Mild, dry and pleasant from November through April, which is precisely when Amman and Petra are cold. The water is warmest in September and October. Midsummer is very hot, though the sea takes the edge off it.

Dates that fill the country

Easter brings pilgrims and tour groups to Madaba, Mount Nebo and Bethany Beyond the Jordan, and fills Amman. Christmas and New Year do the same on a smaller scale and give Aqaba a short peak. Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are domestic and regional holidays when Jordanians travel: Aqaba and the Dead Sea fill with local families, roads are busy and some businesses close for several days. Ramadan itself shifts the rhythm of the day rather than closing the country. All of these move against the Western calendar from year to year — the Islamic dates by roughly eleven days earlier each year — so check them against your own dates rather than against any printed list, including this one.

Jordan seasons — questions

What is the single best month to visit Jordan?

October, narrowly, with April just behind it. Both give warm days and cool nights in Amman, a Petra you can walk all day, and a Wadi Rum night that is pleasant rather than freezing or baking. October has the edge because the water at Aqaba is still warm from the summer.

Is Jordan too hot in summer?

In the south, yes, for most people. Wadi Rum, the Dead Sea and Aqaba regularly pass 40C in July and August, and the Dead Sea is the worst of the three because it sits so far below sea level. Amman is high and dry and copes much better. If summer is your only option, do Petra at opening time, rest in the middle of the day and treat the Dead Sea as an evening swim.

Can Petra really close for snow?

Occasionally, yes. Wadi Musa sits at altitude and gets cold, wet winters, and heavy snow has closed the site for a day or two in the past. It is uncommon rather than rare. If you are travelling in January or February, keep a spare day in the plan and do not book Petra for the last morning before a flight.

When is Aqaba best?

November to April, which is exactly when the rest of the country is at its coolest and wettest. Aqaba is Jordan's winter-sun exception: mild, dry and comfortable for diving when Amman is grey. In midsummer it is very hot indeed.

How does Ramadan affect a trip?

Opening hours shift, daytime eating and drinking in public is restricted, and the evenings become lively after the fast breaks. Sites stay open and tourists are not expected to fast, but plan lunches around it and expect a slower morning. The dates move about eleven days earlier each year, so check them against your own travel dates rather than against a guide.
