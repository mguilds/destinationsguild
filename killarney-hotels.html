Where to Stay in Killarney

Where to Stay in Killarney — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Killarney is the gentlest introduction to Kerry there is: a small, flat, thoroughly walkable town with a national park at the end of the street and the Ring of Kerry starting from its doorstep. The one real decision is whether to sleep in the town, where restaurants, the railway station, the chemist and the jaunting-car rank are all on foot, or out by the lakes, where the views and the quiet are, and where you will need a car or a taxi for every dinner. Both are good. Below are Killarney’s most dependable hotels by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

The town is flat, small and genuinely easy

This is Killarney’s quiet advantage over prettier but steeper Irish towns. High Street, Main Street, New Street and College Street form a compact, level grid you can cross in about ten minutes, and the railway and bus station sits at the edge of it. Killarney National Park’s gate and Ross Castle are a comfortable fifteen to twenty minutes on foot for anyone steady on their feet, with jaunting cars and taxis on hand for anyone who would rather not. Pavements in the centre are good, though older streets narrow and the kerbs are uneven in places — worth knowing if you use a stick or a walker.

Town centre or lakeside resort — the honest trade

Sleeping in town means everything is on foot: restaurants, the tour desks, the chemist, the station. For an older traveller without a hire car that removes the daily friction of taxis and parking, and puts shops and help within easy reach. What you pay for it is atmosphere — town streets carry pub noise and coach traffic in high season, and the view from the window is a street. A lakeside or parkland hotel buys scenery, grounds to walk in and real quiet, at the cost of depending on a car or taxi for everything. A great many travellers split the difference: two or three nights in town, then two or three out by the water.

The Ring of Kerry: which way round, and whether to drive it at all

The coach tours run the Ring almost universally anticlockwise, which is exactly why a self-driver should go clockwise. The road narrows sharply in places — around Waterville and over the Coomakista Pass in particular — and meeting a full-size coach head-on in a hire car is genuinely unpleasant. Going clockwise keeps you moving with the flow rather than against it, and puts your passenger on the sea side for most of the day. If narrow coastal roads are not something you want to take on, a coach tour removes the problem entirely and lets you look at the scenery instead of the road.

The Gap of Dunloe — take the jaunting car, not the walk

Be clear-eyed about this one. Walking the full Gap is about ten kilometres over an unsealed, hilly mountain road and takes four to five hours; it is a proper hike, not a stroll. Almost nobody needs to do it. The jaunting cars run the length of the Gap, and the classic combination trip — coach out to Kate Kearney’s Cottage, jaunting car through the Gap, then a boat back across the lakes — gives you the whole of the scenery with very little effort. It is one of the best days out in Ireland. Book it a day or two ahead in season.

Luxury

One grand hotel above the lakes, one grand hotel in the middle of the town — and the choice between them is really the choice between view and convenience.

Aghadoe Heights Hotel & Spa

$$$$ LuxuryAghadoeLake viewsSpa5 min drive out

Set on the hill at Aghadoe with a sweep of Lough Leane and MacGillycuddy’s Reeks below it, this is the grande dame of Killarney luxury: hushed, formal, and built for settling into rather than dashing out from. The spa is well regarded and the dining room is among the best in Kerry. The honest caveat is that you will need a car or a taxi for everything, dinner in town included, so it does not suit anyone who wants to walk out of an evening into the pubs.

Adults-only

There is no genuinely adults-only hotel in or around Killarney, and we would rather say so than dress up a family hotel as one.

Killarney’s hotel trade is built around families and coach groups, and every property we looked at — five-stars included — welcomes children as standard. The nearest thing to a policy is at the Killarney Park Hotel, whose swimming pool becomes adults-only after seven in the evening. If what you actually want is a grown-up atmosphere rather than a written rule, Aghadoe Heights and Cahernane House both skew strongly towards couples, sit outside town and have few children’s facilities — but families may still book them.

Killarney Hotels — common questions

Is Killarney a good base if we would rather not drive much?

It is one of the best in Ireland. The town is small and flat, with the railway and bus station, the restaurants, the chemist and the entrance to the National Park all within an easy stroll of a central hotel. Jaunting cars stand in for walking inside the park itself, and organised coach tours cover both the Ring of Kerry and the Gap of Dunloe, so nobody need drive a narrow mountain road who does not want to. The one caveat is that Killarney’s charm is partly its bustle — the centre gets lively with tour groups and pub music in high season — so anyone who prizes quiet may prefer a lakeside hotel and a taxi in for dinner.

Should we drive the Ring of Kerry ourselves or take a coach tour?

Both are genuinely good, and the choice comes down to how you feel about narrow, winding roads. Driving yourself gives complete freedom over the pace and the photograph stops, but the road narrows considerably in places and it is worth going clockwise, against the flow of the coaches, so you are not meeting a bus head-on on a tight bend. A coach tour removes the driving entirely, adds a guide who knows the place, and lets you actually look at the scenery — at the cost of a fixed itinerary and shared stops. A good many older travellers simply prefer the coach, and are right to.

How strenuous is the Gap of Dunloe? Can we skip the hard parts?

The full walk through the Gap is a serious undertaking — roughly ten kilometres over an unsealed, hilly mountain road, four to five hours of it — and it is not for anyone with joint, breathing or balance concerns. The good news is that almost nobody needs to walk it. Jaunting cars run the length of the Gap, and the popular combination trip takes you by coach to Kate Kearney’s Cottage, through the Gap by jaunting car, and back across the lakes by boat, seeing all of the scenery with very little effort. It is one of the finest days out from Killarney; book it a day or two ahead in season.

Is Muckross House worth visiting if stairs are difficult?

The gardens and grounds absolutely are — they are level, free to enter, and many visitors are quite as happy strolling the lawns and lakeshore paths as touring the house. The house itself, though, is a genuine nineteenth-century mansion and its own visitor information warns of many challenging steps and stairs, with only a courtesy wheelchair for the ground floor. If you cannot manage stairs, plan on the gardens and skip the upper rooms without regret. Jaunting cars run directly to Muckross from the town and from within the park, which is a pleasant and low-effort way to arrive without a car.

Can we bring our dog to a hotel in Killarney?

Yes, though the choice is smaller than in many places and the policies are specific. Randles Hotel and the Dromhall Hotel, both on the Muckross road close to the centre, are established dog-friendly four-stars, each charging a flat fee of around €20 for the stay rather than a nightly charge, and each with sensible house rules — typically one dog per room, on a lead in the public areas, and never left alone in the bedroom. Book directly by telephone rather than through a third-party site to be certain of a pet-friendly room, and confirm any size or breed restriction before you travel.

We want somewhere quiet without children about. Does Killarney have that?

Not as a formal adults-only hotel — there genuinely is not one in or immediately around the town, because Killarney’s hotel trade is built on families and coach groups. The nearest thing to a rule is the Killarney Park Hotel, whose pool becomes adults-only after seven in the evening, though the hotel as a whole welcomes families. For a quieter atmosphere by nature rather than by policy, a resort such as Aghadoe Heights or a manor house such as Cahernane tends to draw couples and older guests, simply because they sit outside town, cost more and have little for children — but families may still book them.
