Where to Stay in Kuala Belait — the Coast and the Oil Town

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Kuala Belait is Brunei’s second town and the centre of the district that made the country rich: oil and gas country, an hour and a half west of the capital, with Seria and its Billionth Barrel Monument between the two and the Sarawak border a few miles further on. Nobody comes to Brunei for Kuala Belait, and we are not going to pretend otherwise — but people do come through it, on the road to or from Miri, and people work here. The hotels below are the ones that exist, in the two places they cluster: the town centre, and the coast road out towards Mumong. Kuala Belait questions, answered

Is Kuala Belait worth a stop?

As a destination, not really — and a guide that told you otherwise would be wasting your time. As a night on the way between Brunei and Sarawak, or as a base if you are working in the oil district, it is perfectly comfortable: a quiet coastal town with an old market, a long beach, and a handful of straightforward hotels. The Billionth Barrel Monument at Seria, a few miles east, marks the oilfield that paid for modern Brunei and is a stranger and better monument than it sounds.

Where is Seria, and can you stay there?

Seria is about ten miles east of Kuala Belait, between it and Tutong, and it is the other half of the oil district. Our booking partner does not file it as a town of its own — ask it for Seria and it offers you the capital — so there is no Seria list for us to point you at and no Seria page on this site. Stay in Kuala Belait, which is twenty minutes away, or at the Garden Sentral out at Mumong, which is closer still.

How do you get to Kuala Belait?

By road. It is about an hour and a half west of Bandar Seri Begawan on the coastal highway, and there is no railway anywhere in Brunei. Hire a car or arrange a driver. The Sungai Tujoh crossing into Sarawak is a short distance beyond the town, which is why this is the natural overnight stop for anyone driving on to Miri.

Can you buy alcohol in Brunei?

No. Brunei does not sell alcohol — not in hotels, not in restaurants, not in shops. Non-Muslim visitors have long been allowed to bring in a personal quantity for their own consumption and must declare it on arrival; the allowance is set by the Royal Customs and Excise Department, and we are not printing a figure here because we could not read the current one off the department’s own site today. Check it before you fly. What is certain is that nothing is for sale once you are there, so a hotel bar is not a thing you will find in this country.

Is there anything to do with children here?

The beach, mostly, and the Silver Jubilee Park in town. Kuala Belait is an unhurried place and the sea front is its best feature. For a day that children will remember, drive back east: the Jerudong Park Playground is about two hours away on the way to the capital.
