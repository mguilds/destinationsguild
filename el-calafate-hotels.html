Where to Stay in El Calafate — Best Hotels for the Perito Moreno Glacier

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Almost nobody comes to El Calafate for El Calafate. They come for the Perito Moreno Glacier, about eighty kilometres away — and that distance is the same from every hotel in this guide, so choose your hotel for the town, not the glacier. The town is built on a slope running down to Lago Argentino. Avenida del Libertador is the main street and holds nearly all the restaurants; a hotel on or just off it means you can walk to dinner. Everything further out — and some very good hotels are further out — means a shuttle, a taxi or a car every evening. Most of the out-of-town hotels run a free shuttle, and we have said so where they do. Below are our picks by style, each with a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

The honest shape of an El Calafate trip. Two nights is usually enough: one full day at the glacier, one for the boat trips or the Glaciarium museum. Book the glacier excursion before you arrive in high season. And be aware that El Calafate is strongly seasonal — from June to September days are very short, some hotels and restaurants close, and excursions thin out. The glacier itself is open all year.

El Calafate Hotels — Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of El Calafate should I stay in?

On or just off Avenida del Libertador, if you would like to walk to dinner. El Calafate is built on a slope down to Lago Argentino, so “near the centre” often means a climb on the way home. Genuinely walkable from this page: Posada Los Alamos, Mirador del Lago, Calafate Parque and Kapenke. Everything else — Xelena, Imago, Alto Calafate, Design Suites, Kau Yatún — needs a shuttle, a taxi or a car for every meal, though most run a free shuttle.

Which El Calafate hotels have a lift?

Trip.com confirms lifts at Xelena, Imago, Design Suites Calafate, Esplendor, Posada Los Alamos, Alto Calafate and Calafate Parque. It lists no lift for Kau Yatún, Mirador del Lago or Kapenke — all three are otherwise excellent, so if stairs are difficult, ask for a ground floor room when you book.

Are there adults-only hotels in El Calafate?

Not that we can verify. Every property we checked on Trip.com either welcomes children of all ages or accepts them in some rooms, so we have not listed an adults-only section for El Calafate — we would rather tell you the category is empty than fill it with something that does not fit.

Can I bring a dog to El Calafate?

Not to a hotel, as far as we can establish. Every property we checked states plainly on Trip.com that pets are not allowed. If you are travelling with an animal, look at self-catering rentals instead and confirm the policy directly with the owner before you book.

How far is the Perito Moreno Glacier from El Calafate?

About eighty kilometres, or an hour and a quarter each way, and that is the same from every hotel in town — so do not choose a hotel hoping to be closer to the ice. You will need an organised excursion, a transfer or a hire car. The national park’s viewing balconies are a well-built system of graded walkways and railed platforms, so you can see the glacier properly at whatever pace suits you.

How many nights do I need in El Calafate?

Two is usually right: one full day for the glacier, and one for the boat trips on Lago Argentino or the Glaciarium ice museum. Three if you also want the long day trip to El Chaltén and Mount Fitz Roy.
