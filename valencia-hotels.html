Valencia’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Valencia — Best Neighborhoods & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Valencia spreads from a walkable medieval core to a long Mediterranean beach and Calatrava’s gleaming City of Arts, so where you unpack shapes your trip more than in most Spanish cities — old-town lanes, elegant shopping avenues, or sand and sea air. The centre is flat and easy on foot or by bike along the old riverbed park, and where you stay decides which Valencia you wake up in. We’ve grouped the city’s most characterful and well-loved hotels by neighborhood, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can simply choose your dates.

Ciutat Vella — The Old Town

Valencia’s medieval core is a maze of squares, the great Cathedral with its Holy Grail chapel, the silk-era Lonja and the buzzing Mercat Central. Staying here means everything on foot and the city’s best tapas at your door — lively by day and evening, quieter than you’d fear at night. The classic base for a first visit.

A striking design hotel set in a restored aristocratic mansion in the old town, the Caro reveals layers of the city’s history — a stretch of Arab wall, Roman mosaics and Gothic columns — beneath sleek contemporary rooms. A prized restaurant and a small rooftop round it out. For design lovers who want luxury steeped in Valencia’s past.

Plaza del Ayuntamiento & the Centre

The grand oval of the Plaza del Ayuntamiento is the city’s pivot, ringed by belle-époque facades and a short walk from the market, the station and the old town. Central, well connected and lively — a practical, easy place to base yourself, especially if you are arriving by train.

L’Eixample & Carrer de Colón

East of the old town, the elegant grid of L’Eixample carries Valencia’s smartest shopping along Carrer de Colón, handsome Modernista buildings, and the green ribbon of the old Turia riverbed leading to the City of Arts. Refined, leafy and calm at night — a grown-up base a short walk from the sights.

La Malvarrosa & the Beach

A short tram or taxi from the centre, the Malvarrosa and Cabanyal seafront gives Valencia its long golden beach, a palm-lined promenade and the paella houses where the dish was born. Trade the old-town lanes for sea air and sunsets — wonderful in warm months, a little removed from the sights in winter.

The City of Arts & the Modern South

At the southern end of the Turia park, Calatrava’s white, sail-like City of Arts and Sciences and the Oceanogràfic aquarium anchor Valencia’s modern quarter, close to the port. Newer, roomier hotels and easier parking make this handy for families and drivers — a short ride from the historic centre.
