Lake Fishing in the Midwest — Indiana, Illinois and Iowa

Lake Michigan from the Indiana and Illinois harbours, Patoka Lake in the southern Indiana hills, Rend Lake and Lake Shelbyville in Illinois, and Iowa’s natural lakes at Okoboji, Spirit Lake and Clear Lake: what swims in each, the rules from the agencies that set them, the licence a visitor needs, where the boats go in, and where to sleep.

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Checked against Indiana DNR, Illinois DNR and Iowa DNR, 24 November 2026

Three farm states, three very different kinds of fishing. Indiana and Illinois share the bottom of Lake Michigan, where charter boats chase coho in spring and king salmon in late summer, and anglers line the piers for yellow perch. Inland, both states fish big flood-control reservoirs built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers: Patoka Lake in Indiana’s wooded south, and Rend Lake and Lake Shelbyville in Illinois, famous for crappie and, at Shelbyville, muskie. Iowa is different again — its best lakes are natural, left by the glaciers: West Okoboji, East Okoboji and Spirit Lake in the north-west corner, and Clear Lake in the north, home of the yellow bass.

This guide belongs to The Guild Fishing Library. It is written for families with a spinning rod, boat anglers towing a trailer, and anyone who wants a charter or guide for a day. Every rule, limit and price below was read on 24 November 2026 from the body that sets it, and is dated. Three things to know before you plan: Lake Michigan salmon and trout need a stamp on top of an annual licence in both Indiana and Illinois; Illinois closes yellow perch on Lake Michigan from 1 May to 15 June; and the Iowa Great Lakes and Clear Lake each have their own walleye size rule. Where we could not confirm something, we say so.

Lake Michigan, Indiana shore — coho, kings and perch

Indiana’s short stretch of the lake, between Illinois and Michigan, from Hammond and Whiting to Michigan City. Fishing rules: the Indiana Department of Natural Resources (DNR).

Lake Michigan, from Hammond to Michigan City

What swims there. Indiana DNR describes a year that moves with the fish: coho in spring, when they make up about 90 per cent of the boat catch from March to mid-May and shore anglers catch them from March (sometimes mid-February) into April; lake trout, coho, chinook and steelhead in summer, out in 70 to 200 feet of water; yellow perch from May to September in water under 40 feet; and king and coho salmon runs from late August to early November, with steelhead in the autumn. In winter, warm-water discharges hold brown trout, steelhead, coho and smallmouth bass.

Keeping a fish. Yellow perch: 15 a day on Lake Michigan, no size limit — and that limit applies in Indiana waters even if you hold another state’s licence. Black bass on the lake: 3 a day, 14 inches or longer. Trout and salmon: Indiana’s 2026 fishing guide lists 5 trout and salmon in total a day, no more than 3 of them lake trout, 14 inches or longer — we read this in the online guide’s Lake Michigan table and ask you to confirm it in the printed guide before you keep a limit. Every salmon or trout needs a trout/salmon stamp on top of your licence.

How people fish it. Our guidance: in spring and summer most visitors take a charter from Michigan City, because the fish are far out and the lake can turn rough. Shore anglers fish the piers and harbour mouths for coho in early spring and for perch in summer. In the tributaries, trout and salmon must be hooked in the mouth, and nobody may fish within 100 feet of the sea lamprey barrier on Trail Creek.

Boat ramps. Michigan City’s Port Authority runs Washington Park Marina and the Trail Creek marina, which has a launch; we could not confirm this year’s launch fees, because the marina’s rate page shows no year. Hammond Marina publishes a boating season of 1 May to 15 October. At Portage, the Indiana Dunes National Park’s Portage Lakefront and Riverwalk has an accessible fishing pier, open sunrise to dusk. Indiana DNR says launching at Burns Waterway is through private marinas.

Nearest towns: Michigan City, the charter harbour, and Chesterton, by the Indiana Dunes.

Confirmed with the Indiana Department of Natural Resources, 24 November 2026 — DNR’s Lake Michigan fishing page (updated 19 May 2026) and the 2026 Indiana fishing guide’s Lake Michigan and bass pages, which DNR publishes through eRegulations.

Lake Michigan, Illinois shore — Waukegan and the Chicago harbours

From the Wisconsin line at Winthrop Harbor down through Waukegan to the Chicago lakefront. Fishing rules: the Illinois Department of Natural Resources (IDNR).

Lake Michigan, from North Point to Chicago

What swims there. IDNR’s report on Illinois charter boats in 2024 (published February 2025) gives a clear picture: of 41,574 fish kept, 73 per cent were coho, 16 per cent lake trout, 7 per cent chinook and 4 per cent rainbow trout and steelhead. Coho peaked in May; chinook and lake trout in August, with July close behind; rainbow trout in July. Yellow perch are the shore angler’s fish.

Keeping a fish. From IDNR’s 2026 Illinois Fishing Information booklet: trout and salmon 5 a day in total, 10 inches or longer, of which no more than 2 may be lake trout; yellow perch 15 a day, and no perch may be taken from 1 May to 15 June — the 15 covers Lake Michigan, the Calumet River and the Chicago River system together; black bass 1 a day, 21 inches or longer; whitefish 12 a day. Every line must be attended. To keep salmon or trout from Lake Michigan you need the $6.50 Lake Michigan Salmon Stamp as well as your licence.

How people fish it. Our guidance: a half-day charter out of Waukegan in May, when the coho are near, is a good first outing. Perch anglers fish from the harbour walls in summer, after the closure ends.

Boat ramps and harbours. The Chicago Park District runs ten harbours (31st Street, 59th Street, Belmont, Burnham, Diversey, DuSable, Jackson Park Inner and Outer, Monroe and Montrose), with a season of 1 May to 31 October. North Point Marina at Winthrop Harbor, an IDNR site, has 1,500 slips. The Waukegan Port District runs Waukegan Harbor. We could not confirm which of these launch ramps are open to the public or what they cost — ask the harbour before you tow.

Nearest towns: Waukegan, for the charters, and Chicago, for the city harbours. Our Chicago hotel guide has the neighbourhoods.

Confirmed with the Illinois Department of Natural Resources, 24 November 2026 — the 2026 Illinois Fishing Information booklet (effective 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027), IDNR’s 2024 charter harvest report and its North Point Marina page; Chicago Park District harbours page.

Patoka Lake — Indiana’s big southern reservoir

An 8,800-acre lake in the hills of Orange, Crawford and Dubois counties. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers runs the dam; Indiana DNR runs the recreation and sets the fishing rules.

Patoka Lake, from the dam to the upper arms

What swims there. Indiana DNR names largemouth bass and channel catfish as the best fishing. DNR stocks hybrid striped bass every year and surveys the lake’s walleye, and it has studied Patoka’s crappie.

Keeping a fish. Black bass: Patoka is on Indiana’s list of lakes with a 15-inch minimum, with the statewide limit of 5 a day. Walleye: Indiana’s rule south of State Road 26 is 6 a day, 14 inches or longer. Crappie: 25 a day. Bluegill: no limit. White bass and hybrid striped bass: Indiana’s 2026 guide lists 12 a day in total, no more than 2 over 17 inches — check the printed guide, as that table also covers the Ohio River.

How people fish it. Our guidance: bass anglers work the wooded coves and points; families fish from the bank and docks for catfish and panfish. A pontoon from the marina is the easy way to see the lake.

Boat ramps and fees. DNR lists 10 launch ramps and three marinas — Hoosier Hills, Patoka Lake Marina and Gilligan’s Boats — plus 455 electric and 45 primitive campsites. Entry costs $7 a day for a car with Indiana plates and $15 for out-of-state plates (the same fee as every Indiana state park and state-managed lake). There is also a launch fee, which we could not confirm. DNR’s Patoka page carried a blue-green algae notice when we checked; read it before you swim or let a dog in the water.

Nearest towns: French Lick, the old spa resort town to the north, and Jasper, the nearest city, to the west.

Confirmed with the Indiana Department of Natural Resources and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 24 November 2026 — DNR’s Patoka Lake page (updated 19 March 2026), its entrance-fee page (9 March 2026) and hybrid striped bass page (19 June 2026), the 2026 Indiana fishing guide, and the Corps’ Louisville District Patoka page.

Rend Lake — southern Illinois crappie water

A Corps of Engineers lake of about 18,900 acres between Benton and Mount Vernon. Fishing rules: Illinois DNR. Ramps and campgrounds: the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and IDNR.

Rend Lake, from the dam to the north end

What swims there. IDNR names white and black crappie — best in early spring — largemouth bass, channel catfish, bluegill and a stocked white bass fishery. Illinois’s black crappie record, 4 pounds 8 ounces, came from Rend Lake in 1976.

Keeping a fish. Rend Lake’s own rules in IDNR’s 2026 booklet: black bass 14 inches or longer; crappie 25 a day, no more than 10 of them 10 inches or longer; striped, white, yellow and hybrid bass 20 a day in total, no more than 3 of them 17 inches or longer. Jugs must be attended.

How people fish it. Our guidance: crappie anglers fish the flooded timber and brush in spring; the lake is wide and shallow, so it gets rough in wind — pick your day.

Boat ramps and camping. The Corps lists permit ramps at Ina, Jackie Branch and Turnip Patch, and campgrounds including North and South Sandusky, South Marcum and Gun Creek. On the west shore, IDNR’s Wayne Fitzgerrell State Recreation Area has 243 electric campsites at $20 a night.

Nearest towns: Benton, to the south-east, and Mount Vernon, where Interstates 57 and 64 meet.

Confirmed with the Illinois Department of Natural Resources and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 24 November 2026 — the 2026 Illinois Fishing Information booklet, IDNR’s Wayne Fitzgerrell page and the Corps’ Rend Lake page on Recreation.gov.

Lake Shelbyville — muskie, walleye and crappie in central Illinois

An 11,000-acre Corps of Engineers lake with about 250 miles of shoreline and water up to 67 feet deep. Fishing rules: Illinois DNR.

Lake Shelbyville, from the dam to Sullivan

What swims there. IDNR’s pages for Eagle Creek and Wolf Creek state parks name crappie, largemouth bass, walleye, channel and flathead catfish, bluegill and muskie.

Keeping a fish. Lake Shelbyville’s own rules in IDNR’s 2026 booklet: muskie 48 inches or longer (also on the Kaskaskia below the dam down to the Route 128 bridge); black bass 14 inches or longer; striped bass 2 a day, 32 inches or longer; crappie 15 a day, split by size (no more than 10 of 10 inches or longer). Walleye follow Illinois’s statewide rule: 6 a day, 14 inches or longer. Live bait over 8 inches must be fished on a quick-set rig.

How people fish it. Our guidance: muskie anglers troll and cast the points and creek mouths in spring and autumn; walleye are fished on the flats and points; crappie gather around brush and docks. Hire a guide if muskie are the goal.

Boat ramps and camping. The Corps sells ramp permits at Coon Creek, Bo Wood, Lithia Springs, Lone Point, Opossum Creek, Whitley Creek and Wilborn Creek. Eagle Creek State Park (west side) and Wolf Creek State Park each have a four-lane ramp, a 175-car lot and a high-water ramp; Eagle Creek has 75 Class A campsites, with showers closed from 1 November to 1 May. Marinas: Findlay, Lithia Springs and Sullivan.

Nearest towns: Shelbyville, beside the dam; Sullivan, at the north end; and Mattoon, the bigger town to the east.

Confirmed with the Illinois Department of Natural Resources and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 24 November 2026 — the 2026 Illinois Fishing Information booklet, IDNR’s Lake Shelbyville, Eagle Creek and Wolf Creek pages, and the Corps’ Lake Shelbyville page on Recreation.gov.

The Iowa Great Lakes — West Okoboji, East Okoboji and Spirit Lake

Natural glacial lakes in Dickinson County, in Iowa’s north-west corner. Fishing rules: the Iowa Department of Natural Resources (DNR).

Spirit Lake and the Okobojis

The lakes. Spirit Lake, at 5,684 acres, is Iowa’s largest natural lake and only about 22 feet deep. West Okoboji is 3,847 acres and very deep for Iowa — nearly 139 feet. East Okoboji, 1,835 acres and 21 feet deep, joins it at Arnolds Park.

What swims there. Iowa DNR lists walleye, yellow perch, smallmouth and largemouth bass, northern pike, muskie, bluegill and crappie. On West Okoboji DNR describes bluegill of 8 to 9 inches, plentiful perch and strong year classes of both basses; East Okoboji has an improving largemouth fishery and yellow bass. On 24 November 2026 DNR’s Spirit Lake report called perch and crappie fishing good.

Walleye — the rule to know. On Spirit Lake, East and West Okoboji, Upper and Lower Gar and Minnewashta, every walleye from 19 to 25 inches must be released, and only one over 25 inches may be kept a day; 3 a day, 6 in possession. The 2026 season on Spirit and the Okobojis opened on 2 May and runs to 14 February; it is closed from 15 February to the first Saturday in May.

Other limits. Muskie 40 inches or longer, 1 a day, season 21 May to 30 November. Bass 15 inches or longer, 3 a day. Northern pike 3 a day. Perch, bluegill and crappie 25 a day each.

Boat ramps and parks. Emerson Bay on West Okoboji has a ramp and a fish-cleaning station; Templar Park on Spirit Lake and Upper Gar have ramps with courtesy docks; Marble Beach on Spirit Lake has a fish-cleaning station. Gull Point State Park on West Okoboji has electric campsites and showers, and every site must be reserved in advance.

Nearest towns: Okoboji, between the two Okobojis, and Spirit Lake, at the north end. Arnolds Park and Milford are close by.

Confirmed with the Iowa Department of Natural Resources, 24 November 2026 — DNR’s news release of 21 April 2026 on the Great Lakes walleye season, Iowa Administrative Code 571-81.2 (as of 13 May 2026), DNR’s lake pages for West Okoboji, East Okoboji and Spirit Lake, and its Gull Point State Park page (1 May 2026). DNR’s lake pages still show an older walleye season; we used the 2026 release.

Clear Lake — yellow bass and walleye in north Iowa

A shallow natural lake of about 3,680 acres in Cerro Gordo County, west of Mason City. Fishing rules: Iowa DNR.

Clear Lake, from the town to Ventura

What swims there. Iowa DNR rates the lake’s yellow bass as excellent and its muskie as good, and points families to bluegill and channel catfish. Walleye fishing was rated fair and crappie slow in DNR’s most recent report (October 2026).

Walleye — Clear Lake’s own rule. Every walleye from 17 to 22 inches must be released, and only one over 22 inches may be kept a day; 3 a day, 6 in possession. Muskie: 40 inches or longer, 1 a day. Largemouth bass: 15 inches or longer.

How people fish it. Our guidance: yellow bass school up and bite fast, which makes Clear Lake a good lake for children; drift or anchor with small jigs and keep moving until you find them. Iowa DNR says ice fishing is popular here in winter.

Boat ramps and parks. DNR lists a hard-surface ramp, a fishing jetty, an accessible pier and a fish-cleaning station. Clear Lake State Park, on the south shore, has electric, full-hook-up and non-electric sites from April to October (closed November to March), and allows bank fishing after hours in marked areas. McIntosh Woods State Park, on the north shore at Ventura, has a busy ramp with a fish-cleaning station, campsites and two yurts; the Ventura Access nearby has a two-lane ramp.

Nearest towns: Clear Lake, on the east shore, and Mason City, the nearest city.

Confirmed with the Iowa Department of Natural Resources, 24 November 2026 — DNR’s Clear Lake lake page (report of 21 October 2026), Iowa Administrative Code 571-81.2, and the Clear Lake and McIntosh Woods state park pages (10 December 2025).

The licence and the limits, plainly

Each state sells its own licence. Buy online before you go, and carry it on the water.

Indiana

For a visitor. Nonresident annual licence $60; seven days $35; one day $15 (the one-day licence includes trout and salmon).

For an Indiana resident. Annual licence $23; one day $10.

Lake Michigan salmon and trout. A trout/salmon stamp, $11, on top of an annual or seven-day licence.

Children. Anglers under 18, resident or not, need no licence or stamp.

When it runs. 1 April to 31 March. Sold online at GoOutdoorsIN.com and by hundreds of shops.

Confirmed with the Indiana Department of Natural Resources, 24 November 2026 — DNR’s licence-fee page and fishing FAQ, and the 2026–27 Indiana fishing guide’s licence page.

Illinois

For a visitor. Nonresident annual licence $31.50; three days $15.50; 24 hours $10.50.

For an Illinois resident. Annual licence $15; 24 hours $5.50.

Lake Michigan salmon and trout. The Lake Michigan Salmon Stamp, $6.50, for residents and visitors alike.

Children. Nobody under 16 needs a licence, resident or not.

When it runs. Annual licences expire on 31 March; a licence bought from 1 March 2026 runs to 31 March 2027. IDNR notes that a transaction fee may be added.

Confirmed with the Illinois Department of Natural Resources, 24 November 2026 — the 2026 Illinois Fishing Information booklet and IDNR’s licence press release of 3 March 2026.

Iowa

For a visitor. Nonresident annual licence $48; seven days $37.50; three days $20.50; one day $12.

For an Iowa resident. Annual licence $22; seven days $15.50; one day $10.50.

Children. Nobody under 16 needs a fishing licence, resident or not (a trout fee may still apply on trout water).

Where to buy. Go Outdoors Iowa online, or a local shop or bait store.

Confirmed with the Iowa Department of Natural Resources, 24 November 2026 — DNR’s fishing licence page (updated 26 October 2026). Iowa’s administrative code lists slightly lower base fees; the prices above are those on DNR’s own page, which we take to be what you pay.

Questions

What does an Indiana fishing licence cost a visitor?

On Indiana DNR's licence-fee page on 24 November 2026: a nonresident annual licence $60, a seven-day licence $35 and a one-day licence $15, which includes trout and salmon. To keep Lake Michigan salmon or trout on an annual or seven-day licence you also need an $11 trout/salmon stamp. Anglers under 18 need no licence. Indiana licences run from 1 April to 31 March.

Do I need a stamp to fish for salmon on Lake Michigan in Illinois?

Yes. To take salmon or trout from Lake Michigan, residents and visitors need the Lake Michigan Salmon Stamp, $6.50, as well as a fishing licence. A nonresident annual licence is $31.50, three days $15.50 and 24 hours $10.50; nobody under 16 needs a licence. Checked in the Illinois Department of Natural Resources' 2026 Illinois Fishing Information booklet on 24 November 2026.

When can I keep yellow perch on Lake Michigan in Illinois?

Every day except 1 May to 15 June, when perch may not be taken. The limit is 15 a day, and it covers Lake Michigan, the Calumet River and the Chicago River system together. Checked in the 2026 Illinois Fishing Information booklet on 24 November 2026.

What is the walleye rule on Spirit Lake and the Okobojis?

Every walleye from 19 to 25 inches must be released, and only one over 25 inches may be kept a day; the limit is 3 a day and 6 in possession. The 2026 season on Spirit Lake, East Okoboji and West Okoboji opened on 2 May and runs to 14 February. Checked with the Iowa Department of Natural Resources on 24 November 2026.

Can I keep a walleye on Clear Lake, Iowa?

Yes, if it is under 17 inches or over 22 inches: every walleye from 17 to 22 inches must be released, and only one over 22 inches may be kept a day, with a limit of 3 a day and 6 in possession. Muskie must be 40 inches or longer. Checked with the Iowa Department of Natural Resources on 24 November 2026.

What we could not check today, and will not pretend to have

The part of a fishing page most sites leave out.

Seven honest gaps

Lake Michigan launch ramps. We could not confirm this year’s launch fees at Michigan City, or which Chicago and Waukegan ramps are open to the public and at what cost. Ask the harbour before you tow.

Indiana’s Lake Michigan trout and salmon limit. The online 2026 guide’s table reads as 5 trout and salmon a day, no more than 3 lake trout, 14 inches or longer; the layout left room for doubt, so check the printed guide or ask Indiana DNR.

Patoka’s launch fee and whether DNR’s algae notice is still posted. Check DNR’s Patoka page on the day.

Iowa’s licence year and ice. DNR’s licence page does not say when an annual licence expires, and we found no DNR page on ice conditions on the Iowa Great Lakes. Ask a local bait shop before you walk out, and never trust ice you have not checked.

Best months. Only Indiana’s Lake Michigan page and Illinois’s charter report give a calendar. Where the months table says “our guidance”, it is ours.

Businesses we could not list. We found guides and marinas at Rend Lake, Patoka and Okoboji with websites showing nothing dated 2026, and several Iowa guides with only a social-media page, so we list none of them; we found no Patoka guide with a site of its own. That is not a judgement on them.

Small towns and hotel lists. Sullivan, Okoboji and French Lick are small, so we added bigger neighbours. Our hotel buttons are pinned to town numbers checked two ways with our own resolver; we do not open Trip.com’s hotel-list pages to count the hotels, so small towns may show few.
