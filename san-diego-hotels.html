Where to Stay in San Diego — Downtown, Old Town, the Bays, Coronado & La Jolla

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

San Diego is not one place to stay but several, strung along the bay and the ocean. A downtown hotel puts you a walk from the Gaslamp Quarter and the cruise piers; a Mission Bay resort is a beach holiday with the city ten minutes away; Coronado and La Jolla are their own small worlds. Decide what kind of trip you want first, then pick the area — the hotel comes last. This page sorts the six bases most visitors choose and names eight hotels we checked on their own websites today.

Choosing Your Area in San Diego

Six bases, each suited to a different trip.

Downtown and the Gaslamp Quarter

The city centre: the Gaslamp Quarter’s restaurants and bars, Petco Park, the Convention Center, Little Italy to the north and the Embarcadero waterfront where the cruise ships tie up. San Diego International Airport sits just beyond, on the bay.

Stay here for a short city break, a ballgame, a convention or a cruise. Balboa Park — which the City of San Diego describes as more than 1,000 acres with 15 museums, gardens and the San Diego Zoo — is a short ride east.

Old Town

California State Parks runs Old Town San Diego State Historic Park, where historic buildings and living-history programmes recall Mexican California’s rancho period and life on the American frontier. San Diego’s COASTER and Amtrak trains stop in Old Town on their way downtown.

Stay here for history, Mexican food and good-value rooms, and for train connections without a car.

Mission Bay and the beaches

A large saltwater bay of beaches, lawns and islands just north of the city, with Pacific Beach and Mission Beach on its ocean side. Resorts here have their own sand and calm water for paddling.

Stay here for a family beach holiday that still keeps the city close.

Point Loma and Shelter Island

The peninsula that shelters the bay from the ocean. At its tip, the National Park Service’s Cabrillo National Monument marks where Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo came ashore in 1542, the first European on what is now the West Coast of the United States, and also holds the Old Point Loma Lighthouse and tidepools. Shelter Island and the marinas sit on the bay side.

Stay here for a quieter, boating-town feel with sweeping bay views.

Coronado

Across the bay from downtown, reached by the bridge or the ferry. A small, walkable resort town with a broad sandy beach, dominated by the Hotel del Coronado.

Stay here for a classic beach-resort holiday, especially with children.

La Jolla

A seaside village at the north end of the city, with the long beach of La Jolla Shores and the Torrey Pines golf course and cliffs beyond.

Stay here for a scenic, slower stay by the ocean. It is a drive from downtown, so plan to have a car or budget for rides.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of San Diego details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to San Diego

The airport is San Diego International, on the bay beside downtown. The airport’s own website says MTS Route 992 stops at both Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 and runs every day from 4:15 a.m. to midnight, every 15 minutes, connecting with the Trolley, COASTER and Amtrak stations (read 25 November 2026). Amtrak and COASTER trains also arrive downtown at Santa Fe Depot, with a stop in Old Town. Cruise passengers should see our San Diego Cruise Port guide.

Getting around

Downtown, the Gaslamp and Little Italy sit close together and are easy on foot, and the COASTER and Amtrak trains link downtown’s Santa Fe Depot with Old Town. Mission Bay, Point Loma, Coronado and La Jolla are more spread out, and most visitors who stay there use a car or rides. If you bring a car, ask your hotel about parking before you book.

When to go

San Diego works all year. The National Park Service at Cabrillo National Monument describes a coastal “May Gray” and “June Gloom”, when a cool marine layer often covers the beaches; July and August in the 80s; an autumn when rain is rare and skies are clear, though hot, dry Santa Ana winds can blow; and a rainy season from November through February, with temperatures in the 50s and 60s and the clearest views of the year once storms pass (read 25 November 2026). Dress in layers on the coast at any season.

San Diego Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best area to stay in San Diego?

Downtown and the Gaslamp Quarter for a short city break, a ballgame or a cruise. Old Town for history and value. Mission Bay or Coronado for a family beach holiday. Point Loma and Shelter Island for bay views and quiet. La Jolla for a scenic stay by the ocean.

Which part of San Diego is best for families?

Mission Bay and Coronado. Paradise Point Resort & Spa on Mission Bay and the Hotel del Coronado, both listed above, describe beaches, pools and activities for children on their own websites.

How do I get from San Diego airport to downtown?

The airport says MTS Route 992 stops at Terminals 1 and 2 every 15 minutes from 4:15 a.m. to midnight and connects with the Trolley, COASTER and Amtrak stations.

Do I need a car in San Diego?

Not if you stay downtown or in Old Town and keep to the centre, Balboa Park and Old Town. For Mission Bay, Point Loma, Coronado and La Jolla most visitors use a car or rides. Ask your hotel about parking before you book.

When is the best time to visit San Diego?

Any time. The National Park Service notes a cool, cloudy marine layer on the coast in late spring and early summer, warm summers, clear and dry autumns, and a rainy season from November through February. Autumn is a favourite for clear, mild days.
