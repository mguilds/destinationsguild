St Julian’s

Where to Stay in St Julian’s

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

St Julian’s is a fishing village that resort building grew over, and it is now the place in Malta with the most hotel beds, the most restaurants and by some distance the most noise. It is really four or five separate bays strung along two miles of coast, and which one you pick matters far more here than in any other Maltese town, because one of them - Paceville - is a nightlife district that runs until four in the morning and the next one along is a quiet bay of old boats and seafood restaurants. Booking the wrong bay is the single commonest Malta mistake we see. Read the five below before you choose, and if you are travelling with children or want to sleep before midnight, note where Paceville is and book away from it.

Where to stay in St Julian’s, area by area

The parts of St Julian’s a visitor actually chooses between, and who each one suits.

The Guild does not name a hotel until an Explorer has confirmed it on the hotel’s own website, so this guide sets out the areas and sends you to the full, current list for each one rather than printing a shortlist we cannot stand behind yet. Named picks will be added to this page as they are checked.

Spinola Bay

Best for eatingPrettiest cornerWalk to everything

Spinola is the old fishing inlet at the heart of St Julian’s, still lined with brightly painted luzzu boats and now ringed by the best concentration of restaurants in Malta, from fish places on the water to the fashionable end of the Maltese dining scene. It is five minutes’ walk from Paceville, which is both its advantage and its risk: close enough to walk to the evening, far enough that the noise mostly stays there. For most visitors this is the right address in St Julian’s, and it is the one that books up earliest.

Paceville

Cheapest bedsNightlife districtLoud until 4am

A few blocks of bars, clubs, fast food and casinos directly behind St George’s Bay, and the reason St Julian’s has a reputation. It is genuinely loud - music until three or four in the morning, and crowds on the street at that hour - and the rooms are priced accordingly, which is why it catches out people booking on price alone. Stay here if you are twenty-two and that is the point of the trip. Otherwise book anywhere else; it is small enough to walk to from every other bay in minutes.

St George’s Bay

Big resort hotelsSandy beachPaceville next door

The bay just beyond Paceville holds most of the large international resorts, with pools, several restaurants each and the only sandy beach in St Julian’s - small, imported and crowded, but sand. The hotels are set up for exactly this kind of holiday and they do it well. The honest caveat is the geography: Paceville sits between this bay and Spinola, so the walk into town after dinner goes through it, and on a summer weekend some of that noise carries. Ask for a room on the sea side.

Balluta Bay and the Sliema edge

QuieterPromenade walkingGood value

At the southern end, where St Julian’s runs into Sliema, Balluta Bay has a small sandy-ish beach, a handsome Carmelite church and a row of cafes under the trees. It is the calmest part of St Julian’s and the easiest for walking: the promenade to Sliema and its Valletta ferry starts here and takes about fifteen minutes. You are a fifteen-minute walk from Spinola and its restaurants and a good deal further from the noise. For a family or a quiet week this is the sensible choice.

Ta’ Xbiex and Gzira, towards Valletta

Marina viewsCheapest of allFurthest out

South again, along the creek, Ta’ Xbiex and Gzira face the Valletta fortifications across a yacht marina, with Manoel Island and its fort in the middle of the view. This is residential Malta with aparthotels rather than resorts, the lowest prices in the area, and a flat waterside walk of twenty to thirty minutes into Sliema. You are well away from everything St Julian’s is known for, good and bad. For a longer stay, or for anyone who intends to be in Valletta most days, it is quietly good value.

Getting around

St Julian’s is strung along the coast rather than gathered around a centre, so look at where your hotel actually sits before assuming it is central. The promenade is the spine: Balluta to Spinola is about ten minutes on foot, Spinola to St George’s Bay another ten, and Sliema and its Valletta ferry about fifteen beyond Balluta, all of it flat along the water with some steps and a short climb behind Spinola. Buses run frequently along the main road towards Valletta and north towards Bugibba and Mellieha, and the Sliema ferry is the quickest way into the capital. Taxis and ride apps are everywhere, which matters late at night when the buses thin out. A car is more trouble than help here - parking is very tight and the one-way system around Paceville is bewildering. The airport is about half an hour away.

When to come, and what it costs

The season runs May to October and peaks hard in July and August, when St George’s Bay fills, Paceville is at its loudest and the heat sits in the mid thirties with high humidity. For the same sea and half the crowd, come in late May, early June, late September or October. Winter here is very quiet - a good many of the bars and some of the beach-facing restaurants close or cut their hours from November, the big hotels discount heavily, and the town feels like what it is underneath, a working Maltese suburb. If the evening scene is the reason you chose St Julian’s, do not come between November and March; if it is the reason you were worried about it, that is exactly when to come.

Getting in: what changed, and what has not

Europe’s Entry/Exit System is now in force. Since 10 April 2026 it has operated at every Schengen border crossing and passport stamps have been discontinued. On your first crossing you will have your fingerprints taken and your photograph made at the border, which adds a few minutes; after that the check is quicker. There is nothing to apply for and nothing to pay.

ETIAS - the travel authorisation you may have read about, expected to cost around EUR 20 and last three years - is not in force yet. No applications are being taken, and the European Union has not announced the exact start date; it has said it will give several months’ notice before the system goes live. It has been postponed repeatedly and no start date is confirmed, so treat it as coming rather than cancelled, and ignore any website offering to sell you one today - nobody can issue one yet. When it does begin there will be six months in which you can travel without one, then six months in which only first-time arrivals are exempt.

What this means for your trip right now: you do not need ETIAS. You do need a passport issued within the last ten years and valid for at least three months beyond the date you plan to leave.

Malta is as straightforward as Europe gets for an American or Canadian visitor. It has been in the European Union since 2004, in the Schengen area since 2007 and on the euro since 2008, so an arrival from another Schengen country has no passport control at all, and an arrival from outside it is a single check at Malta International Airport. There is no land border to cross. The ferry between Malta and Sicily is an internal Schengen crossing; the one to Tunisia is not, and is checked.

Staying in St Julian’s — questions

How close is Paceville, and will I hear it?

Paceville is a compact few blocks behind St George’s Bay, and everything in St Julian’s is within a ten or fifteen minute walk of it. Whether you hear it depends almost entirely on which bay you book. In Paceville itself the noise is constant until three or four in the morning at weekends and most summer nights. On St George’s Bay it carries on a Friday and Saturday. At Spinola it is usually a distant hum. At Balluta, Ta’ Xbiex or Gzira you will not hear it at all. If quiet matters, say so when you book and name the bay rather than the town.

Is St Julian’s suitable for a family?

Yes, in the right part of it. St George’s Bay has the sand and the resort hotels with pools and kids’ clubs, and Balluta is calm and well placed for walking. Avoid Paceville entirely. The other thing to know is that Malta’s coast is mostly rock rather than sand, so if beach time is central to the trip, plan a day or two north at Mellieha Bay or Golden Bay, both reachable by bus.

Can I walk to Sliema and take the ferry to Valletta?

Yes, and a great many people base themselves here and do exactly that. The promenade from Balluta to Sliema Ferries is a flat fifteen to twenty minutes along the sea, and the ferry across to Valletta takes about five. Together they are usually quicker than the bus and a good deal more pleasant. Check the last crossing before you plan a late dinner in the capital, especially outside summer.

Which bay has the best restaurants?

Spinola, without much argument. The inlet is ringed with places from simple fish restaurants to the serious end of Maltese cooking, and the quality across the bay is the highest in the country outside Valletta. Balluta has good cafes and a handful of restaurants under the trees. St George’s Bay is mostly hotel dining. Paceville is fast food and bar food. Book ahead at Spinola in summer, particularly for a table on the water.

Is it worth staying here rather than Valletta?

It depends on what the trip is for. St Julian’s gives you a sea-front room with a balcony and a pool, the best restaurant choice in the country and a lively evening, at the cost of a bus or a walk-and-ferry to reach the historic sights. Valletta puts you inside the old city with the art, the fortifications and quiet nights, mostly in small townhouse hotels without pools. Many people split the week between the two, which is easy to do: they are twenty-five minutes apart.
