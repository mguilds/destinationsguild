Where to Stay in Scarborough

By the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Scarborough is Tobago’s capital, its harbour, its ferry berth and the place cruise ships tie up, and it is where the island actually does its business. It is not where most visitors sleep. The town climbs a steep hill behind the port, with Fort King George at the top and the island’s best market at the bottom, and it has very few hotel rooms of its own. What it has instead is Bacolet, a headland of small bays immediately east, where the rooms are. Stay here if you want a real town on your doorstep, a short hop to the ferry, and a quieter side of Tobago than the airport end.

The areas

The town and the harbour

Shops, banks, the bus and maxi-taxi terminus, the ferry to Trinidad and the cruise berth, all at the bottom of the hill. Busy on weekday mornings and on Friday for the market, and largely shut by early evening.

Fort King George and the upper town

The old British fort stands above Scarborough, restored, free to walk and with the best view on the island, with the museum and the botanic gardens on the slopes below it. The climb is real — take a taxi up and walk down.

Bacolet

A headland of small coves a few minutes east of the town, greener and much quieter, where the old plantation-era hotels sit. This is where a Scarborough stay usually means.

Lowlands and the road west

Back towards Crown Point the coast flattens into mangrove and golf, with the island’s largest resort out here. Fifteen to twenty minutes from the town by road.

The windward road east

Out of Scarborough the Atlantic coast road runs through Mount St George and Roxborough towards Speyside and Charlotteville. Big water, few hotels, and the turning inland to Argyle Falls and the Main Ridge Forest Reserve.

What your money is worth in Scarborough
US $1 = about 6.80 TT$
TT$ 100 = about US $14.70
The TT dollar has sat near TT$6.80 to the US dollar for years, which is a guide rather than a fixed rate. Scarborough is the cheapest place on Tobago to buy anything, because it is where the island shops rather than where it holidays: the Friday market, the supermarkets and the cook-shops are priced for residents. Bring local cash for all of that; the banks and ATMs are in the town centre, which is convenient if you are staying nearby. Hotels and car hire will take US dollars. Expect Value Added Tax and often a service charge on hotel and restaurant bills. Taxi fares run by route — agree the figure before you set off, and remember that the hill between the harbour and the upper town is steeper than it looks on a map.

Travelling in your later years

Scarborough is built on a hill and there is no getting around it. The harbour, the market and the banks are at the bottom; the fort, the museum and the botanic gardens are at the top, up a genuinely steep climb. Take a taxi up and walk down, and do it in the morning.

Bacolet, where the rooms are, is quiet and green but the coves are reached down slopes and steps rather than along a flat promenade. Ask about ground-floor rooms and about how many steps there are between your door and the sand before you book.

The island’s hospital is here in Scarborough, which is a genuine argument for this end of Tobago if health is a consideration. Serious cases are flown to Trinidad. Bring prescription medicines in their original boxes with a copy of the prescription.

If you are arriving by ferry from Trinidad, the crossing takes a few hours and the sea can be lively on the windward side. The twenty-minute flight into Crown Point is the gentler option and costs little more, though it fills up around holidays.

Nearby

Fort King George is a ten-minute drive or a hard twenty-minute walk above the town: free, restored, with cannon, the old officers’ quarters, a small museum and the view over the harbour and the Atlantic.

Argyle Falls is about forty minutes east along the windward road — three tiers, a guided walk in and pools you can swim in — and the Main Ridge Forest Reserve, protected since 1776, is on the same trip.

Crown Point, Store Bay and Pigeon Point are about twenty minutes west. Our Crown Point guide has the beach end of the island.

The ferry and the air bridge both go to Trinidad for Port of Spain, Carnival and the Caroni ibis — see Where to Stay in Port of Spain and our Trinidad and Tobago guide.

Questions travellers ask

Should I stay in Scarborough or at Crown Point?

Crown Point, for a first visit or a beach holiday: it has most of the island’s rooms, two of its best beaches, the dive shops and the airport. Scarborough, if you want a working town rather than a resort strip, the hospital nearby, the ferry on your doorstep and the quieter coves at Bacolet — and if you are content with a very short list of places to stay.

Why are there so few hotels in Scarborough?

Because Tobago’s tourism grew at the other end of the island, around the airport and the beaches, and Scarborough stayed what it always was — the port and the capital. The rooms that exist are mostly at Bacolet, just east of the town, with a scattering of guest houses in the town itself.

Where does the cruise ship dock, and can I walk into town?

Ships berth at the edge of Scarborough itself rather than at an industrial terminal out of town, so the market, the shops and the waterfront are a short walk. Fort King George above the town is a stiff climb on foot; most ship passengers take a taxi up and walk back down. A day is enough for the fort and the market, or for a glass-bottom boat over Buccoo Reef at the other end of the island.

Is the Friday market worth timing a visit around?

If you like markets, yes. It is for the town rather than for visitors — ground provisions, fish, peppers, fruit and the occasional stall of craft — and it is the cheapest and most interesting shopping on Tobago. Go early in the morning.

How do I get to Trinidad from here?

The inter-island ferry leaves from Scarborough’s harbour and takes a few hours to Port of Spain; book ahead, particularly around holidays and Carnival. The alternative is the twenty-minute flight from Crown Point, twenty minutes away at the other end of the island.

Is Scarborough safe?

Tobago is markedly quieter than Trinidad and Scarborough is a small capital where life runs in daylight hours. Take ordinary town sense: the centre is busy and fine by day and largely shuts in the evening, so use a booked taxi after dark rather than walking the hill, and do not leave things visible in a hire car.
