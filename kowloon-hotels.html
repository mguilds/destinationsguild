Kowloon’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Kowloon — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Kowloon is the louder, closer, more street-level half of Hong Kong — and the half that owns the view. Tsim Sha Tsui sits right on the harbour with the Star Ferry pier, the museums, the Avenue of Stars and the postcard skyline directly across the water; Jordan, one stop north, is a quieter and better-value shopping-street district with the Temple Street night market; and Mong Kok, further up the line, packs some of the densest market streets on earth. Best of all, Kowloon is flat. We’ve grouped the peninsula’s best-run hotels by the kind of stay you’re after — each with an honest note, the walk to the nearest MTR station, and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com.

The Three Areas, and How They Walk

Everything here strings along Nathan Road and the Tsuen Wan MTR line, which makes choosing simple: the closer to the harbour, the grander and pricier; the further north, the cheaper and noisier. All of it is flat.

Tsim Sha Tsui — the harbourfront, and the view

Tsuen Wan & Tuen Ma linesStar Ferry pierMuseums & promenadeFlat

The southern tip of the peninsula, and the single best-connected sightseeing base in Hong Kong. The Star Ferry to Central and Wan Chai leaves from the pier at the bottom of Salisbury Road — a ten-minute crossing that is still one of the great cheap rides in world travel — and the waterfront promenade beside it gives you the finest free view in the territory, including the eight o’clock Symphony of Lights. Tsim Sha Tsui MTR sits on the Tsuen Wan line, with East Tsim Sha Tsui on the Tuen Ma line joined to it by a long underground walkway; the two together put almost the whole city within easy reach. For the Airport Express, Kowloon Station is about ten minutes away by taxi or free hotel shuttle, not on foot. The ground is flat throughout, though the pavements are crowded and the underground crossings involve stairs and escalators.

Jordan — value, one stop from the water

Tsuen Wan LineTemple Street night marketGood valueVery flat

One MTR stop north of Tsim Sha Tsui — or a fifteen-minute level walk up Nathan Road — Jordan is where the prices drop without the convenience going with them. This is old working Kowloon: jade sellers, herbal-tea shops, roast-goose restaurants and the Temple Street night market, which spills out after dark a few minutes from Jordan station. The Kowloon Park green space is on the doorstep, the Airport Express station at Kowloon is a short taxi ride west, and the harbour promenade is a twenty-minute stroll or one stop on the train. Pavements are flat and wide. Our pick for travelers who want the buzz and the food but not the harbourfront premium.

Mong Kok — the markets, at full volume

Tsuen Wan & Kwun Tong linesLadies’ MarketCheapest roomsLoud

Three stops north of Tsim Sha Tsui, Mong Kok is one of the most densely populated places on the planet and behaves like it: the Ladies’ Market, the flower market, the bird garden and the sneaker streets run more or less continuously, and the neon does not switch off early. Rooms here are the best value in Kowloon, and the transport is excellent — Mong Kok station is an interchange, with Mong Kok East on the East Rail line nearby for trains toward the border. Be honest with yourself about noise: ask for a high floor away from the street, and expect crowds on the pavement at any hour. Flat, but rarely peaceful.

Luxury

Kowloon’s great hotels line the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront, and they have an advantage the island cannot match: they look at the skyline rather than standing in it. Book here when the view from the bath is the point.

The Peninsula Hong Kong

$$$$ LuxuryTsim Sha TsuiSince 1928Afternoon tea

The oldest and most storied hotel in the territory, opened in 1928 and still run with a gloriously old-fashioned sense of occasion — the colonnaded lobby, the string quartet at afternoon tea, the fleet of green Rolls-Royces at the door. Behind the historic front block a 1990s tower adds sweeping harbour views, a Roman-style indoor pool, a serious spa and a rooftop helipad. It stands on Salisbury Road two minutes from the Star Ferry pier, the promenade and Tsim Sha Tsui MTR, all on the flat. The choice for a special occasion, and for travelers who want Hong Kong’s history as well as its skyline.

Rosewood Hong Kong

$$$$ LuxuryVictoria DocksideAsaya wellnessArt-filled

The modern counterweight to the Peninsula, standing at Victoria Dockside on the eastern edge of Tsim Sha Tsui, where a residential, art-filled interior and a striking contemporary tower give almost every room a wide harbour outlook. The Asaya wellness floor is among the best in Asia, the outdoor pool overlooks the water, and the restaurants and bars are destinations in their own right. East Tsim Sha Tsui MTR is a covered five-minute walk, and the promenade runs from the door to the Star Ferry. Best for travelers who want contemporary luxury, serious wellness and the view all in one.

Boutique

Kowloon’s design hotels are a little larger and a good deal cheaper than the island’s, and they sit in the middle of the street life rather than above it.

The Mira Hong Kong

$$$ UpscaleTsim Sha TsuiIndoor pool & spaBeside Kowloon Park

A bold, contemporary design hotel on Nathan Road facing Kowloon Park, with sculptural interiors, an indoor pool, a well-used spa and a strong line-up of restaurants and a rooftop bar. It sits directly above a shopping arcade and about three minutes on the flat from Tsim Sha Tsui MTR, with the harbour promenade a ten-minute walk south. Rooms are noticeably larger than the district average and quieter than the address suggests, thanks to a set-back position off the main road. A stylish, well-run choice that costs a good deal less than the waterfront grandees.

Budget & Value

Kowloon is where Hong Kong becomes affordable. Rooms are small — that is simply the city — but these two are properly run hotels a few flat minutes from an MTR entrance, not guesthouse rooms in a subdivided tower.

Silka Seaview Hotel

$ BudgetYau Ma Tei / JordanHarbour glimpsesNight market nearby

A long-running, sensibly priced hotel on Shanghai Street between Jordan and Yau Ma Tei, where the upper west-facing rooms catch a genuine slice of the harbour despite the modest rate. Rooms are compact and simply furnished but clean and well maintained, and both Jordan and Yau Ma Tei MTR stations are within about five flat minutes, with the Temple Street night market and the Jade Market on the way. This is old Kowloon at street level, with breakfast noodle shops and fruit stalls all around. A dependable budget base for travelers who value location and quiet efficiency over space.

Family-friendly

For families, Kowloon offers something the island rarely does: big rooms, big pools and a mall or a market downstairs, at prices that leave something over for dinner.

Cordis, Hong Kong

$$$ UpscaleMong KokRooftop poolMall downstairs

Sitting on top of the Langham Place mall in Mong Kok, the Cordis is the family fixer of Kowloon: a heated rooftop outdoor pool with sun loungers, roomy accommodation by local standards, a well-regarded dim sum restaurant with a Michelin star, and several floors of shops, a cinema and a food court reachable without going outside. Mong Kok MTR is connected below, putting the whole network a lift ride away, and the markets are a two-minute walk. It is a noisy district, but the hotel itself is a calm island above it. A practical, generous base for families and multi-generation groups.

Adults-only

As on the island, we could not verify a single reputable adults-only hotel in Kowloon, and we will not invent one. Hong Kong is a city of business and leisure towers rather than a resort coast, so the grown-up experience comes from the quieter luxury houses and their spas, lounges and rooftop bars — the Rosewood, the Peninsula, the Mira — rather than from an age policy at the door. Pet-friendly

Very few Kowloon hotels take dogs, but two of the best do — one at the top of the market and one in the middle. Confirm the weight limit, the nightly fee and the designated rooms with the hotel before you commit, and check Hong Kong’s import rules well in advance.

Rosewood Hong Kong

$$$$ LuxuryVictoria DocksideDogs to about 15 kgHarbour promenade

The most comfortable place in Kowloon to arrive with a dog: the Rosewood welcomes pets up to roughly fifteen kilograms at no additional charge, and the waterfront promenade running west from the door gives you a proper harbour-side walk rather than a lap of a hotel block. Inside, the rooms are large and residential, the Asaya wellness floor is exceptional and the harbour views are among the best in the city. East Tsim Sha Tsui MTR is five flat minutes away under cover. A rare combination of true luxury and a genuine welcome for four-legged travelers.

Spa & pool

Kowloon’s pools are its quiet advantage — several are on rooftops with the island skyline as the backdrop, which is a considerable thing to swim toward at the end of a hot day.

A long-established grande dame on Mody Road in East Tsim Sha Tsui, with a heated indoor pool under a glass roof, a full spa and health club, and some of the largest standard rooms in the district — a considerable comfort in a city of small ones. Harbour view

This is the reason to choose Kowloon. From the island you look at Kowloon; from Kowloon you look at the skyline that appears on every postcard, with the ferries crossing in front of it and the Symphony of Lights at eight each evening. Book the words “harbour view, high floor” explicitly — a city-view room in the same building can be a fraction of the price and show you a car park.

Regent Hong Kong

$$$$ LuxuryTsim Sha TsuiFloor-to-ceiling harbour glassBeside the Star Ferry

Rebuilt and restored to its original Regent name in 2024, this is the most famous harbour-view address in Hong Kong — built out on the water at the tip of Tsim Sha Tsui, with a wall of floor-to-ceiling glass in the lobby and a majority of rooms looking straight across at the island. Many bathrooms face the water too, which is exactly as good as it sounds. There are terraced outdoor pools, a spa, and the Star Ferry pier, the Cultural Centre and Tsim Sha Tsui MTR all within a flat five minutes. The definitive choice when the view is the whole reason you came.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions travelers ask us most about choosing a base in Kowloon.

Which is the best area to stay in Kowloon?

Tsim Sha Tsui for a first visit, without much argument: it has the Star Ferry pier, the harbour promenade and the skyline view, the museums, two MTR lines and the widest choice of hotels at every price. Jordan, one stop north, gives you nearly the same convenience for less money, with the Temple Street night market on the doorstep. Mong Kok is cheapest and liveliest, best for market-lovers and light sleepers who do not mind noise. All three are flat and sit on the same MTR line.

Is Kowloon flat and easy to walk around?

Yes — and this is Kowloon’s real advantage over Hong Kong Island. The peninsula is essentially level, with wide pavements along Nathan Road and a paved harbour promenade running for well over a kilometre with benches and railings. The catch is not gradient but crowds: pavements in Mong Kok and around Tsim Sha Tsui station are genuinely packed, and many road crossings are made through underground subways with stairs, though lifts and escalators are usually available. If steep streets are a problem for you, Kowloon is the easier side of the harbour.

How do I get from the airport to a hotel in Kowloon?

Take the Airport Express to Kowloon Station, roughly twenty-one minutes from the terminal, then a free shuttle bus to most major Tsim Sha Tsui, Jordan and Mong Kok hotels; in-town check-in for many airlines is available at that station. Kowloon Station itself is in West Kowloon at the Elements mall, about ten minutes by taxi from central Tsim Sha Tsui — it is not walkable with luggage. A taxi door to door takes about thirty-five to forty-five minutes depending on traffic.

How do I use the Star Ferry from Kowloon?

The Star Ferry pier sits at the southern tip of Tsim Sha Tsui, a few minutes on the flat from most harbourfront hotels, and boats cross to Central and to Wan Chai every few minutes through the day and evening. The crossing takes about ten minutes, the fare is a matter of small change, and you can pay with an Octopus stored-value card or coins at the turnstile. Sit on the open lower deck if you want the breeze, and go once after dark as well as by day — it is the best-value sightseeing in Hong Kong.

Is Kowloon cheaper than Hong Kong Island?

Generally yes, and often by a wide margin for the same standard of hotel, particularly once you move north of Tsim Sha Tsui into Jordan and Mong Kok. Restaurants, markets and street food are cheaper too. The exception is the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront itself, where the harbour-view flagships command island prices or more — you are paying for the view, which from this side is the better one. Prices everywhere climb around Chinese New Year, the big trade fairs and the clear autumn weeks.

Is Mong Kok too noisy to sleep in?

It can be, and we would not send a light sleeper there without a warning. The markets and restaurants run late, the pavements stay busy well past midnight, and street-facing lower floors hear all of it. The fix is straightforward: choose a well-built modern hotel, ask for a high floor facing away from the main road, and pack earplugs. If quiet matters more than price, move one or two MTR stops south to Jordan or Tsim Sha Tsui, where the evenings settle down considerably.
