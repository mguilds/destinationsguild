A short history

For thousands of years the Great Plains of what is now South Dakota were home to the Lakota, Dakota and Nakota Sioux nations, along with the Arikara, whose cultures were built around the vast buffalo herds that once roamed the grasslands. The 1868 Fort Laramie Treaty recognized Lakota claim to the Black Hills, but the 1874 discovery of gold there, in violation of that treaty, triggered a rush of settlers and conflict, including the 1890 massacre at Wounded Knee.

South Dakota became the 40th state in 1889, its economy built on farming, ranching and mining. Between 1927 and 1941, sculptor Gutzon Borglum and hundreds of workers carved the faces of Washington, Jefferson, Roosevelt and Lincoln into Mount Rushmore, a monument that remains both a national icon and, for many Lakota, a painful reminder of land taken without consent, a tension embodied nearby by the unfinished Crazy Horse Memorial.

Today the state's economy blends agriculture, tourism and a light-regulation financial sector centered in Sioux Falls. The Black Hills, Badlands National Park and Custer State Park's free-roaming bison herd draw travellers from across the continent, while roadside institutions like Wall Drug capture the enduring, slightly eccentric spirit of the northern Great Plains.

Bodhi

Mount Rushmore's sculptor, Gutzon Borglum, chose that granite face specifically because it caught the morning sun, which is why the presidents still look their best at sunrise. The Badlands got their name from Lakota and later French trappers who called the eroded terrain 'bad lands to travel across,' a name that's stuck for over two hundred years.

Nolie

Go to Rushmore for the lighting ceremony at dusk, it's free and it's the one time the crowds actually go quiet. And for the real Black Hills secret, drive the Needles Highway at golden hour, when the granite spires glow orange and you'll swear you have the whole road to yourself.

When to go

Late spring through early autumn.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Cold with possible snow; many Black Hills attractions have reduced hours or close.

Spring (Mar–May)

Mild and green, with fewer crowds before the summer road-trip season begins.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Peak season with warm days, long daylight and busy roads around Rushmore and Sturgis.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Crisp, golden and quieter, a favourite time to see bison and the Badlands without summer crowds.
