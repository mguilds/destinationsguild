Seville’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Seville — Best Neighborhoods & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Seville rewards travelers who stay in the thick of it. The historic center is compact and made for walking, its lanes too narrow for much traffic, so where you unpack decides how your days unfold — which orange-tree patio you step into each morning and how far you stroll home after dinner. We’ve grouped Seville’s most characterful and well-loved hotels by neighborhood, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can simply choose your dates.

Barrio de Santa Cruz — The Old Jewish Quarter

The tangle of whitewashed lanes and orange-tree courtyards between the Cathedral and the Murillo Gardens is Seville at its most romantic — and the classic choice for a first visit. You are steps from the Alcázar and the Cathedral, though the narrow streets mean a taxi drops you at the edge and you walk the last little stretch.

Hotel Las Casas de la Judería

$$$ UpscaleSanta CruzConnected palacesPatios & fountains

A warren of interconnected old Sevillian mansions wrapped around a series of quiet, plant-filled patios, this beloved hotel feels like a small hidden village inside the Jewish quarter. Antiques, tiled courtyards and a little rooftop pool give it real Andalusian character. Ideal for travelers who want atmosphere and history a two-minute walk from the Alcázar.

Hotel Casa 1800 Sevilla

$$$ BoutiqueSanta CruzRestored mansionRooftop plunge pool

A lovingly restored 19th-century house in the very heart of Santa Cruz, Casa 1800 is prized for its warm service, a complimentary afternoon tea, and a small rooftop plunge pool with Giralda views. Rooms blend period detail with modern comfort. A romantic, intimate pick for couples who want to be steps from the Cathedral.

Hotel Amadeus Sevilla

$$ Mid-rangeSanta CruzMusic-themedRooftop terrace

Run by a musical family, this charming small hotel scatters pianos and instruments through its salons and even lends them to guests. Set in a converted 18th-century house in Santa Cruz, it offers a rooftop terrace with cathedral views and genuine warmth. Great value for music lovers and anyone who prizes personality over polish.

The Cathedral, Giralda & El Arenal

Just west of Santa Cruz, the streets around the Cathedral and down toward the river in El Arenal put the Giralda, the bullring and the tapas bars of calle Arfe on your doorstep. It is a touch more open and easier for taxis than the old quarter, with grand hotels facing the great monuments.

EME Catedral Mercer Hotel

$$$$ LuxuryOpposite the CathedralRooftop barSpa

A cluster of restored houses fused into a sleek design hotel directly across from the Cathedral, EME is famous for a rooftop terrace that stares straight at the Giralda. Several restaurants, a small spa and a buzzy bar scene make it a destination in itself. For travelers who want a front-row seat to Seville’s greatest monument.

Spacious classic rooms and a spot near the bullring and Cathedral define it. The choice for travelers who love a landmark address.

A long-standing family-run favorite between the river and the shopping streets, the Bécquer pairs comfortable, updated rooms with a small rooftop pool and an easy walk to almost everything. Puerta de Jerez & the Grand Riverfront

Where Santa Cruz meets the river, the elegant avenues around the Puerta de Jerez and the Guadalquivir carry Seville’s most storied hotels, with the Torre del Oro and the university close by. It is open and green, an easy walk to the Cathedral yet a touch removed from the crowds.

Hotel Alfonso XIII, a Luxury Collection Hotel

$$$$ LuxuryPuerta de Jerez1928 landmarkMudéjar palace

Commissioned by King Alfonso XIII for the 1929 exposition, this Mudéjar-Revival palace of tiled courtyards, carved arches and orange trees is the grandest stay in the city, now part of Marriott’s Luxury Collection. Impeccable service and a beautiful pool patio set it apart. The occasion-defining Seville splurge.

Alfalfa, San Bartolomé & the Old Centre

North and east of the Cathedral, the lanes of Alfalfa and San Bartolomé are lived-in, local and full of tapas bars, with the shopping of calle Sierpes close by. This is where Seville’s finest small palace-hotels hide — a little quieter than Santa Cruz, but every bit as central.

Contemporary design sits easily inside the historic bones. For design-minded travelers who want a palace stay away from the busiest lanes.

Nervión & the Modern City

East of the old town, Nervión and the areas around Santa Justa station and the Torre Sevilla are the city’s modern, business-leaning districts — less picturesque, but well connected, easier for parking, and often better value. Handy if you are arriving by train or want a larger, newer room.

Eurostars Torre Sevilla

$$$ UpscaleTorre SevillaLandmark towerPool & spa

Occupying the lower floors of Seville’s tallest building beside the river, this sleek hotel offers big contemporary rooms, a pool, a spa and skyline views across to the old town. A shopping center and the Cartuja gardens sit at its foot. Best for travelers who want modern space and comfort over historic character.
