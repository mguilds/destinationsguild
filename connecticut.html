A short history

Before European settlement, Connecticut's river valleys and coastline were home to the Pequot, Mohegan and other Algonquian-speaking nations. English Puritans founded Hartford, Windsor and Wethersfield in the 1630s, and the Fundamental Orders of Connecticut, adopted in 1639, is often cited as an early framework for constitutional government, giving the state its enduring nickname.

Connecticut was one of the original thirteen colonies and ratified the U.S. Constitution in 1788, becoming the fifth state. Its coastal towns built fortunes in whaling and shipbuilding, most famously at Mystic, while inland cities like Hartford grew into centres of insurance and manufacturing, an industry legacy still visible in the state's grand 19th-century architecture.

Today Connecticut pairs a wealthy, historic character with strong academic and cultural institutions, led by Yale University in New Haven, home of the original apizza style. Its compact geography puts Long Island Sound beaches, the rolling Litchfield Hills, and easy rail access to both New York City and Boston within reach of nearly any base in the state.

Bodhi

Connecticut's nickname comes from the Fundamental Orders of 1639, an early framework of government some historians consider the first written constitution in the Western world, and Yale in New Haven has been shaping American thought since 1701.

When to go

September–October for foliage; summer for the shoreline.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Cold and often snowy; a quiet season best for museums and cosy inns rather than the shoreline.

Spring (Mar–May)

Mild and blossoming, with fewer crowds at Mystic and the coast before summer arrives.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Warm and lively along Long Island Sound, with sailing, beaches and seafood at their best.

Autumn (Sep–Nov)

Peak season for fall foliage in the Litchfield Hills, plus comfortable touring weather statewide.
