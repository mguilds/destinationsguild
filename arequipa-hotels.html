Where to Stay in Arequipa

Where to Stay in Arequipa — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Arequipa is the handsomest city in Peru and, for our readers, the most comfortable: a compact grid of white volcanic stone under three volcanoes, almost always sunny, and — the part that matters — only about 7,660 feet up, some 3,500 feet lower than Cusco. It is the gentlest place in southern Peru to let your lungs catch up. Stay in the historic centre, within a few blocks of the Plaza de Armas and the Santa Catalina convent; nearly everything is inside that square kilometre and the streets are flat by Andean standards. Yanahuara and Selva Alegre, across the little river, are greener and quieter but put you a taxi ride from the sights. Below are the hotels we would send our own readers to, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

A word for travellers taking it gently. Arequipa is the kindest of Peru’s highland cities, but 7,660 feet is still altitude: take the first day slowly and drink more water than feels reasonable. The centre is level and walkable, though the pavements are narrow and the traffic on the streets around the Plaza is brisk. The sun here is fierce even when the air is cool — a hat is not optional. And please read what we say about the Colca day trip below before you book one at your hotel desk.

Before the Colca — please read this

The most common mistake visitors make in Arequipa, and it is a tiring one.

Do not do the Colca canyon in a day

Two-day trip15,000 ft pass3 a.m. start

Every hotel desk in Arequipa will sell you a one-day Colca tour. It leaves at about three in the morning, crosses a pass higher than 15,000 feet, gives you perhaps an hour at the Cruz del Cóndor and gets you back after dark — sixteen hours in a bus, most of it at an altitude your body has not yet met. Take the two-day version instead, with a night at Chivay or down in the canyon. It costs a little more, the condors are the same birds, and you arrive back as a person rather than a casualty. Book it through your hotel once you are in Arequipa, not months ahead.

Arequipa Hotels — Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of Arequipa should I stay in?

The historic centre, within a few blocks of the Plaza de Armas and the Santa Catalina convent. Almost everything a visitor wants is inside that square kilometre of white volcanic stone, the streets are flat by Andean standards, and taxis are everywhere. Yanahuara and Selva Alegre, across the river, are quieter and greener but put you a short taxi ride from the sights. There is no reason to stay out by the airport.

Is Arequipa a good place to get used to the altitude?

Yes, and it is the best in southern Peru. Arequipa sits at about 7,660 feet — roughly 3,500 feet lower than Cusco and nearly 5,000 lower than Puno. Two nights here before going up to Cusco or Lake Titicaca is genuinely gentler on an older traveller than flying straight into either, and the city is flat, warm and sunny while you do it.

Can I do the Colca Canyon as a day trip from Arequipa?

You can, and we would gently suggest you do not. The one-day tours leave around three in the morning, cross a pass above 15,000 feet, give you perhaps an hour at the Cruz del Cóndor and return after dark — close to sixteen hours in a bus for a first-time visitor at altitude. A two-day trip with a night at Chivay or in the canyon is far kinder and costs little more.

How many days do you need in Arequipa?

Two nights to see the city properly: the Santa Catalina convent, the Plaza de Armas and its cathedral, the Museo Santuarios Andinos and one long lunch. Add two more if you are going to the Colca canyon. Arequipa also works well as the acclimatisation stop at the start of a southern Peru trip rather than a detour at the end of one.

Do I need a car in Arequipa?

No. The historic centre is compact and walkable, taxis within the city are inexpensive, and the roads out to the Colca are mountain roads best left to a driver who knows them. Book transport through your hotel rather than on the street, and for the Colca take an organised trip rather than driving yourself.
