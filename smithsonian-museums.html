Before you go

The world's largest museum complex, and almost all of it is free.

The Smithsonian Institution runs more than a dozen museums in Washington, most of them along the National Mall between the Capitol and the Washington Monument, plus the National Zoo. Admission to every one of them in Washington is free.

That is the good news. The challenge is choosing. You could spend a week in the Smithsonian and not see it all, so the best visits are planned around two or three museums that suit your interests, with time left to sit down and rest your feet.

Do I need a pass?

Most Smithsonian museums need no ticket at all: you simply walk in through security. Two of the most popular are different.

National Air and Space Museum. Free timed-entry passes are required for every visitor, whatever their age. They are reserved online through the museum's website, released on the first of each month at noon Eastern Time for two months ahead, and one person can reserve up to nine. Once inside, you may stay as long as you like.

National Museum of African American History and Culture. This museum is often fully booked and uses free timed-entry passes. Same-day passes are released online each morning, and advance passes go quickly. Check nmaahc.si.edu for the current arrangements before you go.

National Zoo. Check nationalzoo.si.edu for current entry pass arrangements before you visit.

Which museums to see first

The most visited

National Museum of Natural History

The Hope Diamond on the second floor, the T. rex in the Hall of Fossils, and megalodon jaws in the Ocean Hall. Open daily, no pass needed. Use the Constitution Avenue entrance.

Free pass required

National Air and Space Museum

The 1903 Wright Flyer, the Apollo 11 command module Columbia, John Glenn's Friendship 7 and Amelia Earhart's Lockheed Vega. Its ten-year renovation is now complete.

The American story

National Museum of American History

The Star-Spangled Banner, the flag that inspired the national anthem, plus three floors on American life, from transportation to entertainment.

Plan ahead

National Museum of African American History and Culture

One of the most moving museums in Washington. Start at the bottom, in the history galleries, and work your way up. Allow at least half a day.

Faces of a nation

National Portrait Gallery

Home of "America's Presidents," the only complete collection of presidential portraits outside the White House, sharing a building with the Smithsonian American Art Museum.

Big aircraft

Udvar-Hazy Center

Air and Space's second home, in Chantilly, Virginia, near Dulles Airport, with huge hangars of aircraft and the Space Shuttle Discovery. Worth the trip for aviation fans.

More Smithsonian museums

National Museum of the American Indian

On the Mall beside Air and Space, with a well-known café serving Native foods from across the Americas.

Hirshhorn Museum

Modern and contemporary art in a round building, with a sunken sculpture garden on the Mall.

National Museum of Asian Art

Art from across Asia, including the famous Peacock Room in the Freer Gallery.

National Postal Museum

Beside Union Station, and a hidden favorite for families and stamp collectors alike.

Tips from the Guild

Two museums a day

Pick no more than two or three museums a day. Museum fatigue is real, and the floors are hard on the feet.

Book Air and Space early

Reserve your passes as soon as they are released, especially for spring break and summer.

Go at opening time

The first hour after 10 a.m. is the quietest, before the school groups and tour buses arrive.

Use a hop-on bus

The museums stretch along the Mall. A hop-on hop-off bus saves a lot of walking between them.

Accessibility

The Smithsonian museums are accessible, with step-free entrances, elevators and accessible restrooms. Many museums can lend a wheelchair on a first-come, first-served basis, so ask at the information desk when you arrive. The Mall itself is long and mostly gravel, so plan your route between museums, or use a bus tour or a taxi to get from one to the next.

Smithsonian museums FAQ

Are the Smithsonian museums free?

Yes. Admission to all Smithsonian museums in Washington, D.C., and to the National Zoo is free.

Do I need tickets for the Smithsonian?

Most Smithsonian museums need no ticket. The National Air and Space Museum requires free timed-entry passes, and the National Museum of African American History and Culture uses free timed passes at busy times. Check each museum's website before you go.

What are the Smithsonian museum hours?

The major museums on the National Mall, such as Natural History and Air and Space, are open 10 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. and closed on December 25. Days and hours vary at some museums, so check before you go.

Where is the Hope Diamond?

The Hope Diamond is on the second floor of the National Museum of Natural History, in the Hall of Geology, Gems, and Minerals.

How do I get Air and Space Museum passes?

Reserve free timed-entry passes online through the museum's website. They are released on the first of each month at noon Eastern Time for two months ahead, up to nine per person.

Which Metro stop is closest to the Smithsonian?

Smithsonian station, on the Blue, Orange and Silver lines, opens directly onto the National Mall among the museums.

Is the Smithsonian Castle open?

The Smithsonian Castle closed in 2023 for a major renovation. Check si.edu for current access.
