Where to Stay in Port Antonio — Best Hotels & Areas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Written in August 2026, after Hurricane Melissa (28 October 2025) — every hotel named here was checked against the property’s own information this month.

Port Antonio is the Jamaica that development passed by, and it is the better for it. Portland parish quietly refused the all-inclusive model that reshaped Ocho Rios and Negril from the 1980s onward, and what remains is a coast of small, individual, sometimes gloriously eccentric hotels set against the best scenery on the island: the Blue Lagoon, Frenchman’s Cove where a cold river meets the warm sea, Reach Falls, and a bamboo raft down the Rio Grande that Errol Flynn invented in the 1940s and which is still the loveliest slow afternoon in the Caribbean.

It is also the part of the island that Hurricane Melissa did not touch. The storm landed at the far south-western end in October 2025; Portland saw only tropical-storm conditions. Every property on this page is confirmed open and trading, with no closures, no rebrands and no repairs to work around — an unusually clean picture by 2026 Jamaican standards.

Two practical things will shape your trip more than the hotel you choose. First, fly into Kingston, not Montego Bay: it is two to two and a half hours from KIN and at least five hours from MBJ, and most American itineraries default to the wrong one. Second, understand that Portland has no all-inclusive resorts and no formally adults-only hotels — we have left both categories below deliberately empty and explained why. And you will need transport every day: nearly every hotel here is strung along ten miles of hilly coast road, well outside the walkable town centre.

Every hotel below is a real, currently trading property that we checked against the operator’s own website or current guest reviews, and each links straight to Trip.com for live prices. Where we could not honestly verify two hotels in a category — or where the category does not exist here at all — we say so plainly and leave the browse link instead of filling the space.

Adults-only

No hotel in Port Antonio is formally adults-only, and we are leaving this empty rather than pretending otherwise. Pet-friendly

Empty, for the same reasons as everywhere else in Jamaica. Nothing in the main Port Antonio hotel set accepts pets; the pet-friendly directories list only a couple of small private guesthouses that we cannot responsibly recommend unseen. And the import barrier is worse here than anywhere: Jamaica requires a Ministry of Agriculture permit, an ISO microchip, a rabies titre test taken three to twelve months beforehand and an international health certificate, with up to fourteen days of possible quarantine — and pets may only enter the country at Kingston, which then leaves you a two-and-a-half-hour drive out to Portland with an unhappy animal. Board your pet at home.

All-inclusive

Port Antonio has no true all-inclusive resort, and that is the point of the parish rather than a gap in it. Portland deliberately resisted the all-inclusive model that reshaped Ocho Rios and Negril from the 1980s onward, which is exactly why it is still green, still quiet and still full of small individual hotels. Several properties — Great Huts, Frenchman's Cove and Hotel Mocking Bird Hill among them — offer meal plans or half-board, and that is the closest thing available. Book a meal plan, and do not let a booking site sell you an 'all-inclusive' here, because there isn't one. If an all-inclusive week is what you want, see Ocho Rios or Negril — or do both, with a few nights in Portland at the end.

What You Need to Know Before You Book

Getting here

From Kingston (KIN): roughly two to two and a half hours for about sixty miles, and about a hundred and thirty dollars each way by private taxi. From Montego Bay (MBJ): at least five hours over a hundred and thirty-four miles via the A1, A3 and A4. Fly into Kingston. The Kingston road climbs through the Blue Mountain foothills and then runs along a dramatic, largely undeveloped coast — genuinely one of the better drives in the Caribbean — but expect traffic through the towns, some badly broken surfaces, and bicycles, goats and pedestrians in every village. It winds throughout, so anyone prone to travel sickness should sit in front. Do not drive it after dark.

Getting about once you are here

You will need transport every day. JUTA taxis run about twenty to twenty-five dollars to San San Beach or the Blue Lagoon; minibuses are a couple of dollars but keep unpredictable hours, and the scheduled bus service is not a realistic option. A hire car is more defensible in Port Antonio than in Kingston — the roads are quiet and there are no neighbourhoods to blunder into — but for a visitor over sixty we would still take a regular local driver: the roads are narrow, unlit and potholed, and a good driver doubles as your guide and gets you to the jerk pits and the raft landing without a wrong turn. Arrange one for the week through your hotel.

What is walkable, and what is not

The town centre is walkable in daylight — the core around West Street, the Errol Flynn Marina and Musgrave Market is compact and flat, the marina is pleasant and safe to sit at, and there is far less hustle here than in Ocho Rios or Negril. Your hotel is almost certainly not walkable to anything: Drapers, San San, Fairy Hill and Boston Bay are spread over ten miles or more of hilly coast road with no pavements.

Meal plans, not all-inclusive

There is no all-inclusive resort in Portland. Great Huts, Frenchman’s Cove and Hotel Mocking Bird Hill offer meal plans or half-board, and given how spread out the hotels are and how much of a production dinner becomes without a car, a meal plan is often the sensible booking here even if you would not normally take one. Ask what is included before you arrive.

What to do, and how demanding each is

Blue Lagoon — best by boat, usually with Monkey Island; far easier on the body than clambering at the edge. Frenchman’s Cove — the gentlest and probably the loveliest, with shade, lawns, calm water and level access. Rio Grande rafting — two to three hours, passive and gentle once aboard, but getting on and off needs a step down and a hand up, and you are in the sun on a raised bench, so take a hat. Boston Bay jerk — twenty minutes east, informal, smoky, low effort and high reward; go for lunch and carry cash. Reach Falls — beautiful and far quieter than Dunn’s River, but this one is physical: wet rocks, uneven footing and guided climbing through the river. There is a viewing area if you would rather watch.

Money and health

Carry Jamaican dollars — Portland is much less dollarised than the resort coast, and the jerk pits, market stalls and route taxis are cash only. Tap water is safe. Medical facilities out here are limited and the good hospitals are back in Kingston, so travel insurance including medical evacuation matters more in Portland than it does at a north-coast resort.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which airport should I fly into for Port Antonio?

Kingston (KIN), and this matters more than almost anything else on the page. From Norman Manley International it is roughly two to two and a half hours, about sixty miles. From Montego Bay (MBJ) it is at least five hours — a hundred and thirty-four miles on the A1, A3 and A4. Most American itineraries default to Montego Bay because that is where the direct flights are, and for Port Antonio that default costs you roughly three hours of hard driving in each direction. If you must arrive at MBJ, break the journey with a night in Ocho Rios rather than attempting it in one push. And do not drive it after dark in either direction.

Is the drive from Kingston scenic or punishing?

Both, and you should know which parts are which. It climbs through the Blue Mountain foothills and then runs along a dramatic, largely undeveloped coast — genuinely one of the better drives in the Caribbean. But it is not a smooth one. Expect traffic-clogged stretches through the towns, some seriously broken road surfaces, and bicycles, goats and pedestrians in every village. It winds throughout, which is a real consideration if anyone in the party is prone to travel sickness — sit in the front. Allow two and a half hours with a stop rather than the two hours the guidebooks quote.

Are there all-inclusive or adults-only hotels in Port Antonio?

No, to both — and that is the deliberate character of the parish rather than an oversight. Portland resisted the all-inclusive model that reshaped Ocho Rios and Negril, which is precisely why it stayed beautiful. Several properties, including Great Huts, Frenchman's Cove and Hotel Mocking Bird Hill, offer meal plans or half-board, and that is the closest you will get; book a meal plan, not an 'all-inclusive'. No hotel here is formally adults-only either. Trident and Geejam are heavily adult in atmosphere and are the practical answer if that is the feel you want, but neither carries an adults-only policy and we will not list them as something they are not.

Is Port Antonio walkable?

The town is; your hotel almost certainly is not. The core around West Street, the Errol Flynn Marina and Musgrave Market is compact, flat and easily covered on foot in daylight, and the marina is a pleasant, safe place to sit. Port Antonio is markedly more relaxed than Kingston and has far less hustle than Ocho Rios or Negril. But nearly every hotel worth booking is outside town along the coast — at Drapers, San San, Fairy Hill or Boston Bay — spread over ten miles or more of hilly coast road with no pavements. You will need transport every single day. Budget for it, or arrange a regular driver for the week.

Was Port Antonio affected by Hurricane Melissa?

No. Melissa came ashore in Westmoreland at the far south-western end of Jamaica on 28 October 2025 and did catastrophic damage there and in St Elizabeth. Portland and the whole eastern end of the island saw only tropical-storm conditions and were reported entirely unaffected. Every one of the eight properties we checked here is confirmed open and trading with no closures, no rebrands and no storm repairs to work around — an unusually clean picture by 2026 Jamaican standards, and a good reason to consider the east this year.

What is genuinely worth doing around Port Antonio?

Five things, and they are the reason to come. The Blue Lagoon — a hundred-and-eighty-foot-deep spring-fed lagoon that shifts from blue to green through the day — is best seen by boat, usually combined with Monkey Island, and the boat is far easier on the body than clambering about at the edge. Frenchman's Cove, where a cold freshwater river meets the warm sea within a few yards, is probably the single most sixty-plus-friendly attraction in Portland: shade, lawns, calm water and easy access. Rio Grande rafting is two to three hours lying back on a bamboo raft while a captain poles you down the valley — an Errol Flynn invention from the 1940s and the signature experience here; getting on and off needs a hand up, but the ride asks nothing of you but a hat. Boston Bay, twenty minutes east, is the birthplace of jerk cooking and still working roadside pits. Reach Falls is beautiful and far less crowded than Dunn's River, but be realistic: it involves wet rocks, uneven footing and guided climbing through the river, though there is a viewing area if you would rather not.
