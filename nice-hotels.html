Where to Stay on the French Riviera

Where to Stay in Nice & the French Riviera — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated July 2026

On the Côte d’Azur, the address you choose sets the tempo of the whole trip. Nice makes an unbeatable base — a real city with an airport, a train line, and a beach — and from it the great Riviera names are all a short hop away: Cannes, Monaco, Antibes, the hill villages. We’ve grouped the coast’s most enduring, well-loved hotels by area and character, each with an honest note and a Book Me link that opens that exact property on Trip.com so you can just choose your dates.

Promenade des Anglais & the Seafront

The great curving seafront of Nice is the classic first-visit base: wake to the blue of the Baie des Anges, walk the palm-lined promenade, and have the beach clubs, gardens, and Place Masséna all at your feet. Rooms with a sea view command a premium here, but the setting is the whole reason to come.

Le Negresco

$$$$ LuxurySeafrontBelle Époque iconArt collection

Crowned by its unmistakable pink dome, the family-owned Negresco has presided over the Promenade des Anglais since 1912 and remains the emblem of Riviera glamour. Its corridors double as a private museum of French art, and the Chantecler dining room is a landmark in its own right. The definitive splurge for travelers who want history, theatre, and a front-row seat on the sea.

Larger, well-equipped rooms and a seafront position make it an easy, polished choice. Best for travelers who want a landmark address with contemporary comforts and space to spread out.

Overlooking the Albert I gardens between the old town and the promenade, this grand hotel is best known for a rooftop terrace with sweeping views over the sea and the city. A recent luxury revival brought a fresh, refined feel to a historic building. A smart pick for those who want to be steps from both the beach and Place Masséna’s buzz.

Vieux Nice & the Port

The old town is Nice at its most vivid — ochre lanes, the Cours Saleya flower market, baroque churches, and socca stands — tucked beneath the green Colline du Château. Stay here for atmosphere and late dinners within stumbling distance; just expect cobbles, some noise, and few cars.

Hôtel La Pérouse

$$$ UpscaleBelow Castle HillPool & gardenSea views

Carved into the rock of the castle hill at the quiet eastern edge of the promenade, La Pérouse is a hidden gem with a lemon-tree garden, a pool, and rooms that look straight out over the Baie des Anges. It feels a world away from the crowds yet sits right beside the old town. Ideal for couples who want a serene, view-rich retreat within walking reach of everything.

Rooms are relatively compact, but the panorama and the old-town-doorstep location carry the day. A characterful mid-luxury choice for travelers who put the view first.

Carré d’Or & City Centre

Between Place Masséna and the Musicians’ Quarter lies the Carré d’Or, Nice’s smart shopping and gallery district, threaded by the tram and the green ribbon of the Promenade du Paillon. It’s central, elegant, and a short walk from both the beach and the old town — a versatile base without the seafront premium.

Hôtel Windsor Nice

$$ Mid-rangeCarré d’OrArtist-designed roomsTropical garden

A beloved eccentric of central Nice, the Windsor commissions artists to design individual rooms and hides a lush tropical garden with a small pool behind its facade. The effect is part boutique hotel, part living art project. Best for creative travelers who want personality and a green oasis a few blocks back from the sea.

Cannes & the Croisette

Half an hour west by train, Cannes trades Nice’s lived-in bustle for pure glamour: the palm-lined Boulevard de la Croisette, private beaches, designer boutiques, and the palace hotels that host the film festival each spring. Base here when the polish and the red-carpet atmosphere are the point of the trip.

InterContinental Carlton Cannes

$$$$ LuxuryOn the CroisetteBelle Époque palacePrivate beach

The white Belle Époque Carlton, with its twin cupolas, is the most storied address on the Croisette and a fixture of festival lore. A major restoration added gardens, a pool, and fresh suites while keeping the grand-hotel soul intact. The archetypal Cannes stay for travelers who want landmark glamour and a private beach across the boulevard.

Its private beach and sea-view suites draw a stylish, returning crowd. Best for design-minded travelers who want Jazz Age elegance with a serious dining scene attached.

Monaco & Monte-Carlo

The tiny principality, twenty minutes east of Nice, is the Riviera’s stage for high glamour — the casino square, the harbour full of yachts, and the Grand Prix streets. Its palace hotels cluster around Place du Casino, and a stay here means casino, spa, and sea all within a few marble steps.

Hôtel de Paris Monte-Carlo

$$$$ LuxuryPlace du CasinoLegendary wine cellarDucasse dining

Facing the casino since 1864, the Hôtel de Paris is the beating heart of Monte-Carlo glamour, home to Alain Ducasse’s Le Louis XV and one of the world’s legendary wine cellars beneath it. A full renovation refreshed the rooms while preserving the Belle Époque grandeur. The definitive Monaco splurge for travelers who want the casino, the spa, and the whole legend on their doorstep.

Hôtel Hermitage Monte-Carlo

$$$$ LuxuryBelle ÉpoqueEiffel glass domeSea views

A rose-hued Belle Époque palace just above the casino, the Hermitage is famous for its winter garden crowned by a stained-glass dome engineered in the workshop of Gustave Eiffel. It shares the vast seawater spa with its grand neighbour but keeps a slightly softer, more romantic mood. Perfect for couples who want landmark luxury with a touch more intimacy.

Fairmont Monte Carlo

Straddling the coast road on its own terraces above the Mediterranean, the Fairmont is best known for a rooftop pool with panoramic sea views — and for the famous Grand Prix hairpin that curls right around its base. Big, buzzy, and full-service, it’s livelier than the palace hotels nearby. A great fit for families and travelers who want space, a pool, and a central Monaco location.

The Cap & the Hills — Antibes, Juan-les-Pins & Saint-Paul-de-Vence

Beyond the cities lie the Riviera’s most romantic retreats: the pine-shaded Cap d’Antibes, the Jazz Age seafront of Juan-les-Pins, and the perched art village of Saint-Paul-de-Vence. Stay out here for privacy, pools, and legend — you trade nightlife and easy transit for gardens, sea, and quiet.

Hôtel du Cap-Eden-Roc

$$$$ LuxuryCap d’AntibesClifftop poolLegendary

Set in twenty-two acres of pines at the tip of the Cap d’Antibes, this 1870 legend has hosted writers, film stars, and royalty for generations, with a celebrated seawater pool carved into the cliff at Eden-Roc. It is discreet, rarefied, and utterly of the Riviera myth. The ultimate special-occasion escape for travelers who want privacy and a place in the pantheon.

Hôtel Belles Rives

$$$$ LuxuryJuan-les-PinsArt DecoWaterfront

A jewel-box Art Deco hotel on the Juan-les-Pins waterfront, Belles Rives occupies the very villa where F. Scott Fitzgerald once summered, and it wears that Jazz Age heritage beautifully. A private jetty, beach, and waterfront restaurant give it an intimate, glamorous feel. Best for couples drawn to period romance and a direct-on-the-water setting.

La Colombe d’Or

$$$$ LuxurySaint-Paul-de-VenceMuseum-grade artHilltop village

At the gate of the perched village of Saint-Paul-de-Vence, this famous auberge holds a museum-worthy collection of Picasso, Matisse, and Léger — works the artists once traded for meals and lodging. Terrace dining under fig trees and a sculpture-dotted pool complete the spell. An unrepeatable choice for art lovers who want the hills rather than the beach.
