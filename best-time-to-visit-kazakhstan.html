Best Time to Visit Kazakhstan

Researched and compiled by the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Big Almaty Lake, above Almaty, in late August. Photo: Wikimedia Commons.

Kazakhstan has one of the most extreme continental climates on earth, and the short answer is May, or September. Both months give you warm days and cool nights in Almaty, green or golden steppe around Astana, and the mountain roads and high lakes open. The country is too big for one answer, though, and the two cities most visitors use are nothing like each other: Almaty sits at about 800 m against the Tian Shan and has a wet spring; Astana stands on open steppe eight hundred miles north and spends four months of the year below freezing. The tables below are for each city in turn. April is the wettest month in Almaty and also, if you do not mind rain, the prettiest. Deep winter is a real season here rather than a figure of speech, and it is the only time the skiing above Almaty is the reason to come.

Season by season

Spring — April to early JuneOUR PICK

The steppe is green for a few weeks and then it is not, which is the single best argument for coming in May. In Almaty the orchards in the foothills blossom through April, the canyons are comfortable to walk before the heat, and the high lakes become reachable as the snow goes off the roads. April is the wettest month Almaty has, so pack for it; May is drier, warmer and the sweet spot. Astana is a month behind — April is mud season there and May is when the city becomes pleasant. Nauryz, the new year, falls on 21–22 March and is worth planning around if you want to see the country at its most festive, but it is still cold.

Summer — late June to August

High season, and the only time the whole country is easy to move around. Almaty gets genuinely hot in July — this is the month to be at altitude by lunchtime, up at Big Almaty Lake or Shymbulak, rather than in the city grid. August is nearly as warm and noticeably drier, and it is the best month for Kolsai, Kaindy and Charyn. Astana is warm and bright with big short thunderstorms; July is its wettest month. If you are going west to Mangystau or south to Turkestan, be aware that the desert and the Silk Road south are brutally hot in high summer and the mornings are the only sensible working hours.

Autumn — September to OctoberOUR PICK

September is the driest month in Almaty and the best single month in the country if you want one answer. Warm days, cool nights, clear air over the mountains for the first time since spring, the apple harvest in, and the crowds gone from the lakes. Astana in September is clear and dry with the steppe turning colour. October is still good in the cities and in the south, and over by the end of the month in the mountains — the high lakes get cold and the passes start to close. Prices come off through October, which is the quiet reward for travelling late.

Winter — November to March

Come in winter for one of two reasons: the skiing above Almaty, or the architecture of Astana under snow. Otherwise it is a hard season. Astana spends January and February with average highs around −10 °C and average lows near −19 °C, and the wind off open grassland makes the real figure worse; this is clothing you have to own, not improvise. Almaty is milder but sits in a bowl that traps winter smog, and November is the raw grey month with nothing to recommend it but the price. Shymbulak and Medeu above the city are the exception and they are good — a genuine ski week an hour from an international airport.

A word on the two cities’ distance from each other

Almaty and Astana are about 1,200 km apart and the weather does not travel between them. It is perfectly normal for Almaty to be in blossom while Astana is still frozen, and a trip that takes in both in April or October should expect to pack for two seasons. If you only have one week and want the warm half, go south.

Kazakhstan weather questions, answered

What is the single best month to visit Kazakhstan?

September, narrowly, over May. September is the driest month of the year in Almaty, the air over the Tian Shan is clear, the high lakes are still warm enough to be worth the drive, and Astana is dry and colourful on the steppe. May is the other answer, and it wins if you want green rather than gold — blossom in the Almaty foothills and steppe that has not yet burnt off. Avoid April if rain would spoil the trip, and avoid July if heat would.

How cold does Astana really get?

Cold enough to be the thing you remember. On the 1991–2020 normals, January averages a daily high of −10.3 °C and a daily low of −18.7 °C, and February is barely milder; individual days go a long way below that, and the wind across open steppe is what actually hurts. Astana is routinely described as one of the coldest capital cities in the world, and the figures in the table above are why. If you are going in winter, that means a proper insulated parka, a hat that covers your ears and boots rated for it — a city coat will not do.

When is the ski season above Almaty?

Broadly December to March, with February usually the most reliable month for snow at Shymbulak, which sits well above the city at around 2,200 m with lifts going higher. The Medeu rink below it is a high-altitude outdoor skating rink and runs through the same season. We are not printing opening dates, lift prices or snow depths here, because those are set each year and a figure we printed would be wrong — check the resort directly when you book.

Which month is wettest, and does it matter?

In Almaty it is April, at about 112 mm on the normals, roughly four times what September gets — and it matters mostly for the mountain roads rather than the city. In Astana the wettest month is July, at about 56 mm, which arrives as short heavy storms rather than days of drizzle, so it changes an afternoon and not a trip. Nowhere in Kazakhstan has a monsoon; even the wettest month here is drier than an average month in Southeast Asia.
