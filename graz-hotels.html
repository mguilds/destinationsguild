Where to Stay in Graz

Where to Stay in Graz — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Graz is the Austrian city Americans have most often never heard of, and the one our readers come back praising. It is the country’s second city, with a red-roofed old town so intact that UNESCO listed it, a wooded hill in the middle crowned by a clock tower, and a student population that keeps the cafes lively and the prices well below Vienna’s. Best of all for a traveller who no longer wants to fight a city: the centre is flat as a table, compact enough to cross in twenty minutes, and served by trams that are free to ride within the old town. The choice of where to sleep is simple — the old town around the Hauptplatz for atmosphere, the Mur riverbank a few minutes west for the grand old houses, or the station quarter for value and an easy arrival. Flat ground, and free trams in the middle

Graz is one of the kindest historic cities in Europe to walk. The old town is level, the squares are broad, and the whole of it — Hauptplatz, the cathedral, the Landhaus courtyard, the Kunsthaus and the island in the river — sits within a twenty-minute stroll. When you have had enough, the trams through the centre are free of charge within the old-town zone, so you can ride a stop or two back to your hotel without so much as buying a ticket. Beyond that zone an ordinary ticket applies, and the machines take cards.

You do not have to climb the Schlossberg

The wooded hill in the middle of the city, with its famous clock tower, looks like a serious climb and need not be one. A funicular railway runs up one side, and a lift built inside the rock rises from the Schlossbergplatz to the terrace at the top, both for the price of a transport ticket. There are stairs for those who want them — some four hundred of them — but nobody is obliged to use them. The view over the red roofs from the top is the best twenty minutes in Graz.

The railway that changed the map

On 14 December 2025 the Koralm railway opened after twenty-seven years of building, and it quietly rearranged southern Austria. Graz to Klagenfurt fell from about three hours to about forty minutes, and Vienna to Klagenfurt to a little over three. For a visitor this means Carinthia and its warm lakes are now a comfortable day trip or an easy onward leg rather than a separate expedition, and it makes Graz a far better base than it was two years ago. Vienna is about two and a half hours away by Railjet.

Lifts, cobbles and where the noise is

The old-town houses are baroque palaces and burgher houses, handsome and old, and their lifts vary: some are modern, some are small later additions, and a few upper floors are reached by a short flight. Ask before booking. The squares are paved in setts that are uneven in places, though far less punishing than Salzburg’s. For quiet, the streets to ask about are the bar lanes around the Mehlplatz and Farbergasse, which are cheerful and loud at weekends; a courtyard room settles it.

The top end — and an honest note on stars

Graz has no five-star hotel. What it has are handsome four-star-superior houses at prices that would buy a small room in Vienna — and we would rather say so than dress it up.

Grand Hôtel Wiesler

$$$ Four-star superiorOn the Mur riverbankArt nouveau landmark of 1909Lift throughoutFive minutes to the Hauptplatz

The grand old house of Graz, an art nouveau landmark on the Grieskai facing the river, with a gold-mosaic facade, high-ceilinged rooms and a famously good breakfast that runs late into the morning. It has been renovated with a light, slightly bohemian hand rather than stripped of its character. Position is excellent and entirely flat: over the bridge and you are in the Hauptplatz in about five minutes, with the Kunsthaus — the blue glass “friendly alien” — right beside you. Proper lifts, and staff used to looking after older guests well.

Quiet & couples — and an honest word on “adults-only”

There is no genuinely adults-only hotel in Graz, and we will not invent one. These two are small, calm and grown-up in feel — children are permitted at both.

Palais-Hotel Erzherzog Johann

$$$ Historic palaisSackstrasse, old townGlass-roofed courtyard loungeLift throughoutNot adults-only

A palace of 1852 on Sackstrasse, two minutes from the Hauptplatz and close to the foot of the Schlossberg funicular, with a glass-roofed courtyard lounge that is the pleasantest place in Graz to sit out a wet afternoon over coffee. The atmosphere is traditional and hushed, the service old-fashioned in the best sense, and the position could hardly be better for anyone who wants everything within a short flat walk. There is a lift. It is not an adults-only hotel; it is simply a quiet, grown-up one.

Graz Hotels — common questions

Where is the best area to stay in Graz?

The old town around the Hauptplatz and Sackstrasse if you want to step out of the door into the picture; the Mur riverbank around the Grieskai for the grand old houses and the Kunsthaus, five flat minutes across the bridge; and the main station quarter for value and the easiest arrival by train. Graz is small and level enough that all three are genuinely walkable to each other. The free tram zone through the centre means you can ride back when your legs have had enough.

Is Graz walkable for older travellers?

It is one of the easiest historic cities in Austria to walk. The centre is flat, the distances are short, the squares are broad and there are benches and cafes everywhere. The paving is old setts in places, uneven but not punishing. And the one real hill, the Schlossberg, is served both by a funicular railway and by a lift built inside the rock, so the famous view costs you a ticket rather than four hundred steps.

How do I get to Graz, and what did the new railway change?

By train from Vienna in about two and a half hours on frequent Railjet services. The change worth knowing is the Koralm railway, which opened on 14 December 2025 and cut Graz to Klagenfurt from roughly three hours to about forty minutes. That puts Carinthia and its warm lakes within an easy day trip, and makes Graz a far more useful base for southern Austria than it was before. Graz has its own small airport, but most visitors arrive by train, and the station is a short tram ride from the centre.

When is the best time to visit Graz?

May and June, or September and October. Graz sits in the warmest corner of Austria, so its summers are hotter than Vienna’s and August can be genuinely sticky; the shoulder months give you warm days, cool evenings and the Styrian countryside at its best — the wine roads south of the city are lovely in early autumn. December brings a cluster of Christmas markets through the squares, among the prettiest in the country. January and February are cold and quiet, and prices fall accordingly.

How many days do you need in Graz?

Two nights covers the old town, the Schlossberg, the Kunsthaus and a proper Styrian dinner without rushing. Three lets you add the armoury — the largest historic arsenal in the world, and more impressive than it sounds — or a half-day out to Schloss Eggenberg with its peacocks. With the new railway, a fourth day now buys you Klagenfurt and the Wörthersee lake and back, which was not practical before.

Is Graz a good alternative to Vienna?

Not a replacement, but an excellent companion — and for some travellers the more restful half of the trip. Vienna has the palaces, the great museums and the music. Graz has an intact old town you can cross in twenty minutes, noticeably lower prices, a warmer climate, better everyday food, and far fewer visitors. Two and a half hours of comfortable railway separates them. If you have a week in Austria, three nights in each is a better trip than six in one.
