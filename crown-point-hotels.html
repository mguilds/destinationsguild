Where to Stay in Crown Point

By the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Crown Point is the south-west tip of Tobago and the practical answer to where to stay on the island. The airport is here, and it is one of the few airports in the world where you can walk from the terminal to a beach in about ten minutes. Store Bay, Pigeon Point and the sheltered water behind Buccoo Reef are all within a couple of miles, the ground is flat, most of Tobago’s rooms and dive shops are in this corner, and you can manage a week without hiring a car if you are content to stay close. It is the busiest part of a quiet island, which still means quiet by most standards.

The areas

Crown Point and Store Bay

The hub: the airport, the Store Bay beach facility with its famous crab and dumpling huts, the glass-bottom boats to the reef, a cluster of small hotels and guest houses, and almost everything within walking distance of everything else. Flat, friendly and a little scruffy in a way most people warm to.

Pigeon Point

A mile north, and the postcard: a long jetty, calm turquoise water and the picture every brochure of Tobago uses. It is a managed beach park with an entry fee, which keeps it tidy. A handful of properties sit nearby, and it is a short taxi or a hot walk from Crown Point.

Bon Accord and Canaan

Inland and east of the airport, residential, with guest houses and apartments at local prices and the island’s best small supermarkets. Sensible if you have a car and want space and a kitchen rather than a pool bar.

Buccoo

Three miles up the leeward coast, a fishing village that gives its name to the reef, holds the goat and crab races at Easter and runs Sunday School — a Sunday-night street party that starts with a steel band and goes on far longer than the name suggests.

The Lowlands

East along the coast towards Scarborough, where the land flattens into mangrove and golf. The largest resort on the island is out here with its own course and a long stretch of beach. Quiet, spread out and a few minutes’ drive from Crown Point.

Mount Irvine and Black Rock

Further up the leeward coast: a surf beach that works in the winter swell, an old golf course, and the road north towards Castara and the fishing villages. Hilly, green and the beginning of the slower Tobago.

What your money is worth at Crown Point
US $1 = about 6.80 TT$
TT$ 100 = about US $14.70
Tobago runs on the TT dollar, near TT$6.80 to the US dollar for years now — a guide, not a fixed rate. Hotels, dive shops and car hire will take US dollars; the beach huts at Store Bay, the maxi-taxis and the supermarkets want local cash, and the ATMs around Crown Point are the easy way to get it. Eating is where Tobago is cheap: a plate of crab and dumpling or curried conch at Store Bay costs a fraction of a hotel dinner and is better. Car hire is worth it for a few days even if you are staying put — the north coast is the reason to have one. Expect Value Added Tax and often a service charge on hotel and restaurant bills, and ask what is included when you book. Rates are highest from January to April and around Christmas, lowest from September to November.

Travelling in your later years

This is the easy end of an easy island. The ground around Crown Point is flat, distances are short, and several places put you on sand within a few minutes of your door. If stairs are a problem, say so when you book: a lot of Tobago accommodation is two-storey with no lift, and the ground-floor rooms go first.

The walk from the airport terminal to Store Bay is about ten minutes on the flat, which is genuinely useful on arrival, though not in the midday sun with luggage. Taxis wait outside and fares are by route — agree the figure before you get in.

Glass-bottom boats to Buccoo Reef and the Nylon Pool mean stepping down into a small boat, usually from the beach or a low jetty, and then standing in waist-deep water on a sandbar. Ask the operator plainly how you get in and out before you pay; several will bring the boat to a jetty rather than the sand if you ask.

Tobago’s hospital is at Scarborough, about fifteen minutes away, and serious cases are flown to Trinidad. Bring prescription medicines in their original boxes with a copy of the prescription, and take insurance that will move you if it has to.

Nearby

Buccoo Reef and the Nylon Pool, by glass-bottom boat from Store Bay or Buccoo — half a morning, and worth choosing an operator who keeps people off the coral.

Scarborough, the island capital, is about twenty minutes east: the Friday market, Fort King George on the hill above the town and the ferry berth. Our Scarborough guide covers staying there.

The leeward road north to Castara, Englishman’s Bay and Parlatuvier is the best drive on the island and takes a full day if you stop properly. Argyle Falls and the Main Ridge Forest Reserve are on the way round to the windward side.

Questions travellers ask

Is Crown Point the best base in Tobago?

For a first visit, yes. It has the airport, most of the rooms, the dive shops, the boats to the reef and two of the island’s best beaches within a short walk or taxi, and the ground is flat. If you have been before, or want the quiet Tobago, the leeward villages north of Black Rock and the far north-east at Speyside are the alternatives — but you will need a car.

Can I really walk from the airport to the beach?

Yes. Store Bay is roughly ten minutes on the flat from the terminal at A N R Robinson International, and several small hotels sit between the two. It is one of the genuinely charming things about arriving in Tobago. In the middle of the day with suitcases, take the taxi anyway.

Do I need to hire a car?

Not to stay at Crown Point, where you can walk or take short taxis to everything. You do need one to see Tobago properly — the leeward coast road north, Argyle Falls, the Main Ridge and Speyside are the island’s best days out and they are awkward without a car. Many people hire one for two or three days of a week. Drive on the left, and the roads are narrow, steep and unlit after dark.

What is the difference between Store Bay and Pigeon Point?

Store Bay is free, public, lively and has the food huts; Pigeon Point is a managed park with an entry fee, the jetty you have seen in photographs and calmer, shallower water. Both are good. Store Bay is where you will eat, Pigeon Point is where you will take the picture.

Is there much nightlife?

Not in the way a big resort island has it. There are beach bars, a few restaurants that run late and live music some nights. The one unmissable fixture is Sunday School at Buccoo, which starts on a Sunday evening with a steel band and turns into a street party. Dress casually and go early for the pan.

When is the water clearest for snorkelling and diving?

The dry season, roughly January to May, with March usually the best of it. In the wet season run-off from the land cuts visibility, particularly after heavy rain. The north-east of the island around Speyside has the bigger diving and the stronger currents all year.
