Marrakech’s Best Places to Stay

Where to Stay in Marrakech — Best Areas & Hotels

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Marrakech is really two cities to choose between: the walled medina, where courtyard riads hide behind blank doors a few steps from Jemaa el-Fnaa and the souks, and the modern quarters — leafy Gueliz and the Palmeraie palm grove — where big hotels bring pools, gardens and easy parking. Luxury

Marrakech does grand hospitality like nowhere else — palace hotels with orange-tree courtyards, hammams and gardens where the city’s glamour has gathered for a century. Book here when you want the hotel to be as memorable as the sightseeing.

La Mamounia

$$$$ LuxuryLegendaryHistoric gardensEdge of the medina

The most famous hotel in Morocco, a 1920s palace set in twenty acres of centuries-old walled gardens just inside the ramparts, beloved by everyone from Churchill to today’s film stars. Sumptuous Moorish-deco interiors, a celebrated spa and several destination restaurants make it a once-in-a-lifetime address. Splurge here when the hotel is the whole point of the trip.

Royal Mansour Marrakech

$$$$ Ultra-luxuryPrivate riadsRooftop spaImpeccable service

Commissioned by the king and built as a miniature medina of private three-storey riads, each with its own plunge pool and rooftop, connected by an unseen network of service tunnels so staff appear as if by magic. The craftsmanship, gardens and spa are extraordinary. The definitive choice for absolute privacy and the finest service in the city.

Boutique Riads

The riad — a traditional house turned inward around a courtyard and fountain — is Marrakech’s signature stay. These are small, characterful and design-led, hidden in the medina lanes with rooftop terraces for breakfast and sundowners.

El Fenn

$$$ BoutiqueMedinaRooftop barArt-filled

A ravishing warren of interlinked riads near the Bahia end of the medina, all bold colour, contemporary art, plunge pools and a much-loved rooftop bar with Atlas views. Each room is different and full of personality, and the whole place feels like a stylish private house. A perennial favourite for design lovers who still want a real medina address.

Budget & Value

Marrakech rewards careful budgets: a simple, pretty riad in the medina costs a fraction of the palace hotels and puts you right in the thick of it. You trade marble lobbies for courtyard charm and a warm welcome — and spend the savings on the souks, the gardens and a desert trip.

Jnane Mogador

$ BudgetMedinaCourtyard riadRooftop & hammam

A long-running, much-loved budget riad a few minutes from Jemaa el-Fnaa, with tiled courtyards, a fountain, a little hammam and a sunny roof terrace for breakfast. Rooms are simple but full of traditional character, and the value and location are hard to beat. A perennial pick for travellers who want authentic riad atmosphere without the price.

Family-friendly

Families do best a little outside the medina crush, where resort-style hotels offer big pools, gardens, kids’ clubs and room to spread out — with the sights a short taxi away. These two are Marrakech classics for a family stay.

It combines Four Seasons service with genuine space for children, a short drive from the medina. An easy, comfortable choice for a multi-generation trip.

Marrakech Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most when choosing where to stay in Marrakech.

What is the best area to stay in Marrakech?

For a first visit, stay in a riad inside the medina, within walking distance of Jemaa el-Fnaa and the souks — it is the most atmospheric choice. If you prefer modern hotels, restaurants and a calmer, greener setting, base yourself in Gueliz, the new town, or in the Palmeraie palm grove for resort-style space a short drive from the sights.

Should I stay in a riad or a hotel in Marrakech?

A riad — a traditional courtyard house — gives you character, quiet and personal service in the heart of the medina, though rooms can be reached only on foot through the lanes and a porter helps with bags. A modern hotel or resort in Gueliz or the Palmeraie offers big pools, spas, parking and easy taxis. Many travellers do a night or two of each.

Is the Marrakech medina walkable, and how far is the airport?

The medina is very walkable but a genuine maze, so note your riad’s location and expect to get pleasantly lost. Cars cannot reach most riads; you walk the final lanes. Marrakech Menara airport (RAK) is only about 15 to 20 minutes by taxi from the medina, one of the closest big-city airports to its old town anywhere.

Do Marrakech hotels have pools?

Many do. The palace hotels and Palmeraie resorts have large outdoor pools and gardens, and even quite modest riads often have a small plunge pool in the courtyard for cooling off. If a pool matters to you, especially in the summer heat, check the size and whether it is heated before you book.
