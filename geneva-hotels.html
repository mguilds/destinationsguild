Where to Stay in Geneva

Where to Stay in Geneva — Best Hotels & Neighbourhoods

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Geneva is French-speaking, sits at the tip of its lake, and is more an international institution than a holiday town — the United Nations, the Red Cross, the watchmakers and the banks. It is also one of the most expensive cities in the world, so it pays to sleep in the right place. The good news for an older traveller is that almost all of the centre is flat: the Rive Gauche around the Rue du Rhône and Place Longemalle is the best all-round base, closest to the Jet d’Eau and the flower clock; the Rive Droite lakefront along the Quai du Mont-Blanc is the grand-hotel strip with the Mont Blanc views. The one genuine hill is the Old Town. Below are the hotels we would send our own family to, by style, each with an honest note and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Good to know before you book

A few plain facts that decide where an older traveller should sleep in Geneva.

CurrencySwiss franc (CHF) — not the euro
AirportGenève Aéroport (GVA), 4 km out
Airport to centreTrain, 7 minutes — and free on arrival
Best monthsMay – June · September – early October
LanguageFrench; English widely spoken in the centre

Your hotel gives you a free transport card — take it

Anyone staying in registered accommodation in Geneva is handed a free Geneva Transport Card at check-in, valid for the whole stay on the buses, the trams, the local trains and the little yellow Mouettes boats that cross the harbour. It is the single most useful thing to ask for at the desk, because it makes the hilly bits entirely optional. And at the airport there is a machine in the baggage hall that dispenses a free ticket into town, valid eighty minutes.

The Old Town is a real hill — and bus 36 solves it

Everything else in the centre is level: the two banks, the harbour, the shopping streets and the Jet d’Eau are all comfortable flat walking. The Vieille Ville is the exception, built on a rise with cobbles, slopes and steps, and it is a proper climb up from the lake. The single most useful piece of information for an older visitor in Geneva is that bus 36 runs right up into it, stopping at Bourg-de-Four, the Cathedral and the Hôtel-de-Ville.

A plain word about Pâquis

Pâquis sits just behind the Rive Droite lakefront and is where a lot of the mid-priced hotels are. It is central, well served and full of good cheap restaurants — and it is also Geneva’s nightlife and red-light quarter, with bars and street noise late into the night around the Rue de Berne and the Rue de Monthoux. It is not dangerous by most standards, but light sleepers should stay on the quay side of the district or choose the Rive Gauche instead.

Take the train, not the plane — even to Paris

Geneva to Paris on the direct high-speed train is about three hours and ten minutes, city centre to city centre, roughly eight services a day. Geneva to Zurich is under three hours at its fastest, with around sixteen direct trains daily. Once you count airport transfers and security, flying wins on neither route. The airport railway station sits directly beneath the terminal, five minutes’ walk from arrivals, and the train reaches the main station in seven minutes.

Luxury

One caution before the picks: three famous Geneva names are not currently reliable. Le Richemond is dormant awaiting renovation, the Hôtel Métropole has been closed for a multi-phase refurbishment, and the Beau-Rivage began roughly two years of works in late 2024. Check the current state of any of those before you book. These two are open and on flat ground.

Four Seasons Hotel des Bergues Geneva

$$$$ LuxuryRive Droite lakefrontSpa with indoor poolFive level minutes to the stationOpened 1834

Geneva’s oldest grand hotel, dating to 1834, standing on the right bank where the Rhône leaves the lake. Everything an older traveller wants here is flat: the Bergues bridge takes you across to the Jardin Anglais, the flower clock and the Jet d’Eau in about ten minutes on level pavement, and Cornavin station is a short easy walk behind. The spa has a daylit indoor pool. One honest note: the Old Town is a genuine uphill climb from here, so take bus 36 rather than walking it.

Budget & Value

Geneva is genuinely expensive, and value here means a small room in a good position rather than a bargain.

ibis Genève Centre Gare

$$ BudgetFive minutes to Cornavin64 roomsRefurbished lobbyModest cooling

The most useful cheap bed in central Geneva: sixty-four small, plain, functional rooms about four hundred metres from Cornavin station, with a recently refurbished lobby. Two honest notes before you book. The rooms are genuinely compact, and the cooling is modest in a July heatwave, which is worth weighing for an August stay. The block itself is workaday rather than pretty. But everything is flat, the station is minutes away, and the free transport card puts the lakefront a few tram stops off.

Quiet & couples (no true adults-only in Geneva)

There is no true adults-only hotel in central Geneva — the concept barely exists in Swiss city hotels, and a search of the main booking sites returns none in the city districts. Rather than pretend otherwise, here are two genuinely quiet, couples-minded houses on the same handsome square, both clearly labelled.

Hôtel de la Cigogne

$$$$ Quiet and couples-mindedRive Gauche, Place Longemalle49 roomsPeriod woodwork and fireplacesLevel walk to the lake

A hushed, antique-filled house of forty-nine rooms on an elegant little square, with panelling, murals and a fireplace in the salon. It feels more like a private club than a hotel, which is precisely why couples take to it. The position is ideal for older visitors: level ground to the lake and the Jet d’Eau in a few minutes, with the Old Town’s hill starting just behind if you want it and easy to avoid if you do not. The courtyard-facing rooms are the quietest.

Geneva Hotels — common questions

Which neighbourhood should I stay in in Geneva?

The Rive Gauche around the Rue du Rhone and Place Longemalle is the best all-round base for an older visitor: flat, elegant, closest to the Jet d'Eau and the flower clock, with the Old Town starting just behind when you want it. The Rive Droite lakefront along the Quai du Mont-Blanc is the grand-hotel strip, level the whole way and calm at night. Paquis behind it is central and cheaper but is the nightlife and red-light quarter, so light sleepers should choose the quay side.

Is Geneva walkable for older travellers?

The centre is small and mostly flat, and the two banks of the lake, the harbour, the shopping streets and the Jet d'Eau are all comfortable level walking. The exception is the Old Town, which is genuinely built on a hill with cobbles, slopes and steps. The answer to that is bus 36, which runs right up into it and stops at Bourg-de-Four, the Cathedral and the Hotel-de-Ville. Trams and buses everywhere else are frequent and free with your hotel's transport card.

How do I get from Geneva airport into the city?

Take the train. The railway station sits directly beneath the terminal, five minutes' walk from arrivals, and the journey to Geneva Cornavin takes seven minutes with up to seven trains an hour. Here is the useful part: you do not need to buy a ticket. A machine in the baggage reclaim hall dispenses a free public-transport ticket valid for eighty minutes, covering the train, buses, trams and the harbour boats. Press the button, take the ticket, and ride into town at no cost.

What is the free transport card everyone mentions?

Anyone staying in registered accommodation in Geneva, whether a hotel, a hostel or a campsite, is given a free Geneva Transport Card at check-in that is valid for the whole stay. It covers the buses, the trams, the local trains within the city zone and the little yellow Mouettes boats that cross the harbour. Ask for it at the desk if it is not handed to you. It is what makes the Old Town hill and the longer lakefront walks entirely optional.

How expensive is Geneva really?

Very. Geneva is consistently among the most expensive cities in the world, and it is fair to expect a coffee, a simple lunch or a taxi to cost roughly double the equivalent at home. Lakefront five-star rooms commonly run from 400 francs a night upwards. Cards are accepted everywhere; euros are sometimes taken but at poor rates, so pay in francs. One relief: service is included in restaurant bills by law and tipping is not expected, so rounding up is plenty.

Is Geneva worth a visit, or should I skip it?

It depends on what you came to Switzerland for. Geneva is businesslike and thinner on the alpine scenery most visitors want, and if your time is short it is the most skippable of the major Swiss cities. It earns its place if you are flying in or out of it, if the United Nations or watchmaking interests you, or if you pair it with Montreux, the Lavaux vineyard terraces and Chillon Castle, which are genuinely lovely and about an hour away.
