Casco Viejo — the old quarter

The restored colonial peninsula, and the most atmospheric place to stay in the country: balconies, plazas, rooftop bars and the skyline across the water. It is also the loudest. Music runs late at weekends, the streets are cobbled and uneven, and the character of a block can change sharply at its edges. Best for a short, lively stay if you do not mind noise and can manage uneven ground.

Bella Vista and the banking district

The towers: international chains, business hotels, shopping and restaurants, and the Cinta Costera waterfront path along the bay. Less charming and far more practical — lifts, air conditioning that works, taxis at the door and easy reach of both the old town and the canal. This is where most first-time visitors are happiest.

El Cangrejo

An older, leafier district behind the towers, long settled and full of small restaurants and cafes. Good value, genuinely walkable, and a short hop from the metro. The pick if you want a neighbourhood rather than a hotel district.

Amador and the causeway

Out on the arm of land that reaches into the Pacific, built from canal spoil. Sea air, cycling, marinas and the cruise terminal, with the city skyline in view across the water. Quiet, spread out and taxi-dependent, which suits some people exactly and frustrates others.

Near Tocumen airport

Forty minutes east of the city and worth it only for a dawn departure or a very late arrival. There is nothing to do out there; the point is the extra hour in bed.

Travelling in your later years

The old quarter is cobbled, uneven and badly lit in places, and it is hot until well after dark. If stairs and loose paving are a problem, the banking district is the kinder base: modern lifts, flat pavements, and a taxi rank or an app car within a minute. The Cinta Costera gives you a long flat waterfront walk with the breeze off the bay, which is the most comfortable exercise in the city.

Taxis are cheap and plentiful; agree the fare first or use an app. The metro is modern and air-conditioned, though some stations mean stairs. Hospital care here is the best in the region, and the larger hotels are used to arranging a doctor.

Questions travellers ask about Panama City

Should I stay in Casco Viejo or the modern city?

If you want atmosphere and you sleep well through noise, Casco Viejo. If you want a lift, a reliable shower, a taxi at the door and a quiet night, the banking district or El Cangrejo. Many people do two nights in each, which is a sensible compromise and takes twenty minutes to move between.

How far is the airport?

Tocumen is about forty minutes east of the city in ordinary traffic, and appreciably longer in the rush. Albrook, the domestic airport, is inside the city and much closer. Do not assume the two are interchangeable when you book a connecting flight.

Can I visit the canal from the city?

Yes. The Miraflores locks are a short drive from the city centre and have a visitor centre with viewing terraces. Ask at your hotel which part of the day ships are expected, because the direction of traffic changes and an empty chamber is a long wait.

Is it safe to walk at night?

Within the busy, lit parts of Casco Viejo, the banking district and El Cangrejo, ordinary city care is enough. Walking between districts after dark is not advised, and the edges of the old quarter change quickly. Take a taxi or an app car; both are inexpensive.
