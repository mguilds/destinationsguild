The Vietnam Veterans Memorial

Before you go

Most people call it simply "the Wall." Nobody forgets their first visit.

The Vietnam Veterans Memorial carries the names of more than 58,000 Americans who died or remain missing from the Vietnam War. They are carved into two long walls of polished black granite that cut into the earth of the National Mall, a short walk from the Lincoln Memorial.

The design was chosen in a national competition and drawn by Maya Lin, then a 21-year-old architecture student. It was dedicated in November 1982. As you walk along it, the Wall rises from ankle height to over ten feet tall at its center and then falls away again. The polished stone reflects your own face back at you among the names.

It is one of the most visited memorials in Washington, and one of the quietest. Veterans come here to find the friends they served with. Families come to find a father, a brother, a son. Give yourself time, and do not be surprised by what you feel.

How to find a name on the Wall

With a panel and a line number, you can walk straight to the name you are looking for.

1. Look the name up. Printed directories at both ends of the Wall list every name alphabetically by last name. There is also an electronic directory at the information kiosk, which you can search by name, branch, rank, home town or date of casualty. Each entry gives a panel number and a line number.

2. Find the panel. Panel numbers are carved at the bottom of each panel. Panels on the east wall end in "E" and panels on the west wall end in "W."

3. Count down the lines. Count the lines from the top of the panel. Each line holds five names, or six where a name was added later.

Ask for help. Park rangers and volunteers are glad to help you find a name, and they can show you how to make a pencil rubbing of it to take home.

Before you travel. The Vietnam Veterans Memorial Fund keeps an online "Wall of Faces" at vvmf.org, where you can look up a name, find its panel and line, and see photographs and remembrances left by families and friends.

Reading the Wall

The names are in the order they fell. They are not alphabetical. They are listed by date of casualty. The first names, from 1959, begin at the top of the center panel of the east wall, marked 1E. They run east to the end of that wall, start again at the far end of the west wall, and finish at the center with the last names, from 1975. The beginning and end of the war meet where the two walls join.

The symbols beside the names. A diamond means the death was confirmed. A cross means the service member was missing at the end of the war and remains unaccounted for. When the remains of a missing service member are recovered and identified, a diamond is carved over the cross.

The two walls point the way. Each wall is about 246 feet long. One points to the Washington Monument and the other to the Lincoln Memorial, joining the Wall to the story of the nation.

What to see at the memorial

The heart of the memorial

The Wall

Two black granite walls carrying more than 58,000 names. Walk the full length. Many visitors touch a name, and that is welcome.

Bronze statue

The Three Servicemen

Frederick Hart's bronze of three young servicemen, added near the Wall in 1984. They look across toward the names of those they served with.

Honoring the women who served

Vietnam Women's Memorial

Dedicated in 1993, honoring the more than 265,000 women who served during the Vietnam era, many of them as nurses. It stands a short walk south of the Wall.

Those who died later

The In Memory Plaque

A plaque near the Three Servicemen honors veterans who came home and later died from causes related to their service.

What visitors leave

Items left at the Wall

Letters, medals, photographs, dog tags and flags are left along the Wall every day. Park staff collect them, and many are kept in a national collection.

Ceremonies

Memorial Day & Veterans Day

The Vietnam Veterans Memorial Fund holds ceremonies at the Wall each Memorial Day and Veterans Day. Expect large, moving crowds.

From the Guild

Our founder, Mark W. Guilds, served in Vietnam in 1967 and 1968. If you served, or if you are visiting for someone who did, take your time here. Nobody at the Wall will hurry you.

How to visit respectfully

Keep it quiet

Speak softly. Many people at the Wall are grieving, and some have waited years to make this visit.

Leave the offerings alone

Do not pick up or move items left at the Wall. They are collected and cared for by the Park Service.

Photography

Photos of the Wall are welcome. Please do not photograph people who are grieving without their permission.

Give people room

If someone is standing at a name, or making a rubbing, wait. Let them have their moment.

Accessibility

The path along the Wall is paved and gently sloping, with no steps, so the full length of the memorial can be visited by wheelchair. The path is lowest at the center, where the two walls meet.

Park rangers and volunteers can help visitors with limited mobility or limited sight find a name and make a rubbing. For other help, call the National Park Service at (202) 426-6841.

Vietnam Veterans Memorial FAQ

Is the Vietnam Veterans Memorial free?

Yes. The Vietnam Veterans Memorial is free to visit and open 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

How do I find a name on the Vietnam Wall?

Look the name up in the printed directories at either end of the Wall, at the electronic directory at the information kiosk, or online in the Wall of Faces at vvmf.org. Each entry gives a panel number and a line number. Find the panel number at the bottom of the panel, then count down the lines from the top.

How many names are on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial?

More than 58,000 names are carved on the Wall. Names are still added from time to time, when the Department of Defense confirms that a death resulted from wounds received in the war.

What do the diamond and cross symbols on the Wall mean?

A diamond means the death was confirmed. A cross means the service member was missing at the end of the war and remains unaccounted for. When remains are recovered and identified, a diamond is carved over the cross.

Can you make a rubbing of a name?

Yes. Park rangers and volunteers can help you make a pencil rubbing of a name to take home.

Why are the names not in alphabetical order?

The names are listed in the order the service members died or went missing, from 1959 to 1975. The first and last names meet at the center, where the two walls join.

What is the nearest Metro station to the Vietnam Veterans Memorial?

Foggy Bottom-GWU, on the Blue, Orange and Silver lines, is the closest station.
