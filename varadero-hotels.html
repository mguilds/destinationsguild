Where to Stay in Varadero

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Twelve miles of pale sand on a sandbar pointing out into the Straits of Florida — and the hardest lodging question in Cuba. One end of the peninsula is a working Cuban town full of private guest houses. The rest is a line of state resorts, many of them closed this year and many of them barred to American travellers. Which end you sleep on changes the whole trip.

Before you book anything

This page carries no booking buttons, and that is deliberate. Our booking partner sells no accommodation in Cuba at all — we checked, city by city, and a search for Varadero returned a beach in Panama. We will not point a button at the wrong country to earn a commission, so this is a guide to where to stay rather than a shop. Book through a casa particular directly, or through an agency that specialises in Cuba.

American readers: U.S. law does not permit tourism to Cuba, and travel must fall within an OFAC licence category. Under the widely used Support for the Cuban People category you are expected to stay in privately owned casas particulares rather than state hotels. The State Department’s Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List separately bars U.S. persons from lodging at, paying for lodging at, or booking a listed property, wherever the booking is made — and on this peninsula it covers many of the best-known hotels. See the full Cuba chapter, and confirm the current position with OFAC and the State Department.

And the particular Varadero warning: this is the stretch of Cuba most changed by the fuel shortage. Hotels here and in the northern cays have been closed to consolidate guests, and the international chains that managed the large resorts wound up their Cuban operations in mid-2026. Whatever you book, get written confirmation that the property is open on your dates.

The two ends of Varadero, and what is between them

Varadero is not a town with a beach attached; it is a sandbar about twelve miles long and in places only a few hundred yards wide, with a single road down the spine. Everything depends on how far along that road you sleep. The numbers below are street numbers — the town is laid out on a grid from Calle 1 at the western tip eastward, and locals give directions by them.

What kind of stay suits you

A casa in the town is the Guild’s answer

It is cheaper, it is open, it puts money straight into a Cuban household, it is the compliant choice for an American traveller under Support for the Cuban People, and it is two blocks from the same sand the resorts are built on. In a year when the big hotels are closing and reopening without warning, it is also the booking least likely to fall through.

If you want the all-inclusive, go in with open eyes

An all-inclusive on the strip can be a genuinely restful week, and for travellers who are not U.S. persons it remains a legitimate choice. But the chains that used to run these properties have gone, the fuel shortage has thinned the operation, and standards vary more than they did. Read recent reviews rather than the brochure, and confirm the property is open.

Think about the walk to dinner

This is the practical difference nobody mentions. From a casa at Calle 30 you can walk to four or five private restaurants. From a hotel eight miles out you cannot walk anywhere at all, and every meal that is not included is a taxi each way. If eating out is part of your holiday, stay in the town.

Varadero as one day, not one week

If you have ten days in Cuba and you want Havana, Viñales and Trinidad as well, Varadero does not need a base. It is two hours from Havana on a good road. Go for the day, swim, eat in the town, look at the Dupont house on the headland and come back.

Getting about, and what is genuinely walkable

On foot

In the town, everything. The grid is flat, the blocks are short, and the beach is never more than a two or three-minute walk from any street in the western end. This is the one place in this chapter where walking is easy and pleasant, and it is a large part of why we send people here.

On the strip, nothing. The hotels sit along a single road with scrub and fencing between them. It is not a promenade and there is no reason to walk it. The beach itself, however, is public along its whole length, and a walk on the sand from the town eastward is one of the best hours in Varadero.

Take water and shade whichever end you are on. There is almost no natural cover on the peninsula and the sun off the sand is fierce.

Buses, taxis and getting here

Getting here. From Havana it is about eighty-five miles on the Vía Blanca, two hours or a shade more by tourist coach or shared colectivo taxi. The airport, Juan Gualberto Gómez (VRA), is about fifteen miles west of the town and takes charters and regional flights; schedules have been unsettled through 2026 by the fuel shortage.

Along the peninsula. An open-top tourist bus runs the length of the strip and stops at the hotels and in the town. It is the cheap and cheerful way to move about and far better value than taxis if you are staying out east. Buy the day ticket.

Taxis. Agree the fare before you get in, every time. Horse carriages and bicycle-taxis work the town end and are fine for short hops. Self-drive is a gamble in Cuba at the moment for fuel reasons, and on this peninsula there is nowhere you need a car to reach.

Things worth doing

Varadero is a beach, and that is an honest answer rather than a thin one. But there is more on and around the peninsula than the resorts let on.

Walk the sand from the town eastward

The beach is public its entire length. Start at the town end in the early morning, walk as far as you like and swim your way back. It costs nothing and it is the single best thing here.

Parque Josone

A public park in the town with a lake, old trees and real shade — the only proper shade on the peninsula. Cubans use it in the evening. A good place to sit when the sun is at its worst.

The Dupont mansion on the headland

Mansión Xanadú, built in the 1920s for the American industrialist Irénée du Pont, stands on the one piece of high ground with a view down the whole beach. It is worth seeing for the architecture and the view. It is state-run and we are not recommending it as a place to stay; U.S. readers must check the prohibited list before spending anything there.

Varahicacos Ecological Reserve

The scrub and cactus at the far point, with marked trails, a cave or two and a very old cactus the guides are fond of. Early morning, hat on, and take your own water.

Cueva de Saturno

A flooded cave inland towards the airport where you can swim in cold fresh water. A welcome change of temperature, and usually busy by mid-morning — go early.

A day in Matanzas

The provincial city half an hour west, with its bridges, its pharmacy museum and its own ordinary life. Almost no visitor from the strip ever goes, which is reason enough.

Practicalities

Power, water and the everyday

Power. Varadero is on the priority grid because it earns foreign currency, and it fares better than most of Cuba — but 2026 has brought repeated national grid collapses and no part of the island is exempt. The larger hotels run generators; casas generally do not. Bring a torch and a power bank.

Water. Bottled or filtered for drinking, always. Most casas keep a tank, so an interruption rarely reaches the guest.

Sun and sea. There is no shade on the sand and the breeze hides the burn. Long sleeves in the middle of the day, and reapply after swimming. The water is usually calm and shallow a long way out, which makes it unusually good for children and weak swimmers — but there are days of wind and current, and lifeguards are not everywhere.

Money, food and the booking question

Money. Cash. Since 6 June 2026 Visa and Mastercard have not been processed anywhere in Cuba, for any cardholder of any nationality. Arrive with what you need for the whole trip, in good condition.

Eating. The private paladares in the town are the good food here, and they are a fraction of a resort meal. Book in the morning for the evening. On the strip you are eating what the hotel serves.

Confirming a hotel. This is the Varadero-specific job. Properties have been closed at short notice to save fuel. Before you pay, get written confirmation that the hotel is open on your dates, and — if you are a U.S. person — check the current Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List, which applies wherever in the world you make the booking.

Varadero — FAQ

Should I stay in Varadero town or out on the resort strip?

Varadero town, at the western end of the peninsula, is where the casas particulares are, where people live, and where you can walk to a shop, a bakery, a private restaurant and the beach without a taxi. The resort strip running east is state-owned hotels behind gates, where the beach is quieter and the town is a drive away. For American travellers the question answers itself: the Support for the Cuban People category expects a privately owned casa, and many of the strip hotels are on the Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List. For everyone else it is a straight choice between a place with a pulse and a place with a pool.

Is the beach at Varadero really as good as people say?

Yes. That part is not marketing. It is roughly twelve miles of fine pale sand on the north side of the Hicacos peninsula, shelving gently into warm, usually calm water, and it runs the whole length of the strip. The sand is public along its entire length in law, so staying in the town end does not give you a lesser beach — it gives you the same beach with people on it.

How many Varadero hotels are actually open in 2026?

Fewer than the brochures suggest, and the number moves. The fuel shortage has led the government to consolidate tourist facilities, closing hotels in Varadero and the northern cays and moving guests elsewhere, and the international chains that used to manage the big resorts wound up their Cuban operations in mid-2026. We are not going to print a count that will be wrong next month. Confirm directly with whoever you are booking through that the property is open on your dates, and get it in writing.

Can Americans stay in Varadero?

Americans can travel to the area under an OFAC licence category, but not as tourists, and the lodging question is the strict part. The State Department’s Cuba Prohibited Accommodations List bars a U.S. person from lodging at, paying for lodging at, or booking a listed property, wherever in the world the booking is made — and it covers many of the best-known hotels on this peninsula. A privately owned casa particular in Varadero town is both the compliant choice under Support for the Cuban People and, in our view, the better stay. Confirm the current lists with OFAC and the State Department before you commit money.

Can you visit Varadero as a day trip from Havana?

Easily, and a lot of people should. It is about eighty-five miles by the Via Blanca, two hours or a little more in a taxi or on a tourist coach, and the road runs along the coast for much of it. A day gives you the beach, lunch in the town and the Dupont mansion on the headland. If the beach is the whole reason you are coming to Cuba, stay; if it is one chapter of a longer trip, a day from Havana does it.

What about the blackouts and the water?

Varadero sits on the priority grid because it earns foreign currency, so it fares better than most of the island — but better is not the same as reliable, and 2026 has seen repeated nationwide grid collapses. Larger hotels run generators. Casas mostly do not, though many keep a water tank and a battery lamp. Bring a torch and a power bank, and take hot water as a bonus rather than a given.
