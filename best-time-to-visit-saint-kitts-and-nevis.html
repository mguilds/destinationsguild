Best Time to Visit Saint Kitts and Nevis

By the Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

These islands are warm every month of the year, so the question is never really about temperature. It is about rain, crowds, cruise ships, price and the two big festivals that take over one island each. This page goes month by month, gives the festival dates their organisers have actually published, says plainly which ones have no published date at all, and names the weeks we would book ourselves.

The short answer

Two windows, and a warning.

Late April and the second half of November are the Guild’s picks. In both the weather is settled, the cruise traffic is lighter than at peak, and the rates sit well below the January-to-March ceiling. February and March have the most reliable sun of the year and you will pay for every hour of it. The first fortnight of December is the value play if you are willing to take a small risk on the weather turning.

The warning is August and September. They are hot, humid, genuinely quiet and the statistical heart of the hurricane season. Some small places close. If that is when you can travel, travel then — but book cancellable rates and buy insurance that covers weather, not just illness.

Month by month

What each month is actually like to travel in. No temperature figures: see the note below, and the question at the foot of the page.

January

The driest, brightest stretch of the year and the start of the busy season. Sugar Mas is still running into the first week and the national carnival officially finishes on the first Tuesday of the month, so the opening days are lively and the following three weeks are the classic dry-season holiday. Rooms are at their highest and the plantation inns on Nevis book out early.

February

Peak season and arguably the best weather of the year — the driest months on these islands run roughly January to April. Cruise calls are frequent, so Basseterre is busy on ship days and quiet otherwise. If you want reliable sun and do not mind paying for it, this is the month.

March

Still firmly in the dry season and still busy. A good month for the fortress, the railway and long days on the South East Peninsula beaches without the afternoon showers that arrive later in the year. Book well ahead; this is when the islands are fullest.

April

The end of the dry season and, to our mind, the sweet spot. The weather is still settled, the cruise season is winding down — the inaugural-call list in the tourism authority’s own 2025-26 cruise booklet ran from late October to mid-April — and rates begin to soften in the second half of the month. The Black san’ Bang-A-Lang festival at Sandy Point falls somewhere in April; the government’s own page gives the month and no date.

May

Quiet, warm and good value, with the rains not yet properly established. The Green Valley Festival at Cayon happens during May, again with no date published on the official page. One of the best months for a first visit if you care more about having the beaches to yourself than about a guarantee of dry afternoons.

June

The start of the hurricane season, which the UK Foreign Office dates from June to November, though June itself is historically among the quietest months of it. Prices are low. The St Kitts Music Festival lands at the end of the month and changes the mood of the whole island for three days.

July

Hot, green and festival-heavy. Culturama on Nevis builds through the second half of the month, and Restaurant Week has run in July in recent years — though no current date is published anywhere we could read. Showers are usually short and heavy rather than all-day. Expect humidity.

August

Culturama’s closing weekend falls around Emancipation Day and August Monday, and it is the biggest thing in the Nevisian year. Outside the festival this is low season: warm sea, green hills, cheap rooms and a real chance of a tropical storm somewhere in the forecast.

September

Statistically the busiest month of the hurricane season and the quietest month for visitors. Some restaurants and small hotels close for a few weeks. If you come, come with flexible bookings and insurance that covers weather, and accept that you are trading certainty for an empty island.

October

Still in the storm season but turning. The cruise season begins again towards the end of the month — the first inaugural call in the 2025-26 booklet was on 23 October. Rates are still low and the islands are at their greenest after the summer rain.

November

The last month of the hurricane season and a genuinely good bet in its second half. The weather is settling, the ships are back, the rates have not yet climbed to Christmas levels and the hills are still green. One of the Guild’s two favourite windows.

December

The dry season returns and so does the high season, steeply, from about the middle of the month. The first half of December is excellent value. Then Sugar Mas — the national carnival, which the government’s own page says runs in December and culminates officially on the first Tuesday in January — takes over St Kitts entirely.

A deliberate absence: there are no temperatures or rainfall millimetres on these cards. We could not source a month-by-month table for Saint Kitts and Nevis from a named meteorological service, and we would rather describe a month honestly than print a neighbouring island’s numbers under this one’s name.

The dry half and the green half

The pattern is the standard eastern Caribbean one. The drier months run from about January into April, when showers are brief and the hills go brown at the edges. The wetter months run from about July through December, when rain arrives in heavy afternoon bursts rather than grey all-day drizzle and the islands turn properly green. May, June, October and November are the hinges, and they are where most of the value sits.

The World Bank’s climate portal, drawing on Climatic Research Unit data for 1991 to 2020, describes the seasons at that level: a wetter stretch from July to December and a drier one from January to April. That is as specific as we are willing to be from a source we can actually read, and it matches what the hotels and the ferry operators behave as though they expect.

One practical consequence for Nevis in particular: the plantation inns sit up the slopes of the volcano, and cloud sits on Nevis Peak more often in the wet months. The island below is often in sunshine while the summit is not. Do not judge the day by the mountain.

Hurricane season

June to November, as stated by the UK Foreign Office. In practice the risk is far from evenly spread across those six months: June and the second half of November are usually settled, while August and September carry most of the activity in this part of the Caribbean.

What we would do, and what we would not. We would travel in the shoulder months of the season without hesitation, booking rates that can be cancelled and insurance that covers weather disruption rather than only medical costs. We would not book a fixed, non-refundable, once-in-a-lifetime fortnight in the first half of September. And we would not believe anyone — including us — who tells you a particular island is reliably missed by storms. Rare is not never.

If a storm is named while you are there, the islands have been through this before and the hotels know the drill. Follow the property’s instructions rather than social media, and keep your airline’s app on your phone: flights usually stop before the weather arrives and resume a day or two after it passes.

Festivals and events

Dates below are only given where an organiser or an official government page publishes them. Where no date has been published we say so rather than guessing from last year.

St Kitts Music Festival — 24 to 26 June 2027

Three nights at Warner Park in Basseterre. The dates are the festival’s own, from its own website; no lineup, prices or ticket on-sale date had been published when we read it.

Nevis Culturama 53 — 22 July to 3 August 2027

Thirteen days in Charlestown, per the Culturama Secretariat’s own site, which also closes registration on 30 November 2026. The site’s footer still says 2026 beside those dates, so confirm before booking flights around it.

Sugar Mas — December into January, no dates published

The government’s own festivals page says the National Carnival runs in December and culminates officially on the first Tuesday in January. We found no published schedule for the coming edition. Check locally.

Restaurant Week — no current date published

It has run in July in recent years. The official tourism page for it would not load for us, and the Nevis events listing shows only a 2025 entry. Nobody has published a current date that we could read.

Nevis Triathlon — no date published

The Nevis Tourism Authority’s own page for the event gives no date, no distances and no start location, and calls it by two different names. Its events index lists only November 2025. Treat as unscheduled until the organiser says otherwise.

Black san’ Bang-A-Lang and Green Valley

Two village festivals the government’s own page places in April at Sandy Point and in May at Cayon, with no dates attached to either. Worth asking about locally if you are there in those months.

A caution for anyone planning around a festival: the Nevis Tourism Authority’s own annual-events page was, when we read it, entirely populated with 2025 dates — swim, mango festival, marathon, triathlon, restaurant week, all of them. It is not a current calendar and should not be used as one.

What it costs when

We do not print room rates, because they move and a stale price is worse than none. What is stable is the shape of the year, and it is pronounced on these islands because the hotel stock is small. Mid-December to mid-April is high season and the plantation inns on Nevis in particular can be full months ahead. Mid-April to the end of June, and again through November, is shoulder. July to early November is low, with August and September lowest of all.

Two things distort that curve. The first is Sugar Mas, which fills St Kitts over Christmas and New Year at the highest rates of the year. The second is the two festivals — the Music Festival in late June and Culturama in late July — each of which creates a short spike on its own island while the other island stays cheap. If the festival is not why you are coming, that is a reason to cross the channel for those few nights rather than to stay away.

Flights follow the same curve and lead it: the long-haul fares into Robert L. Bradshaw move first and move hardest. If your dates are flexible, price the flight before you fall in love with a hotel.

Questions travellers ask about when to go

What is the best month to visit Saint Kitts and Nevis?

If you want the most reliable weather, February or March. If you want the best balance of weather, price and space, late April or the second half of November. Those two shoulder windows are the Guild’s usual answer for these islands: the dry season has either just ended or is about to start, the cruise traffic is thinner, and the rates have not reached their peak. The first two weeks of December are a third, slightly riskier option that is often very good value.

When is the hurricane season, and should it stop me?

The UK Foreign Office gives the season as June to November. It should not stop you, but it should change how you book. The risk is heavily concentrated in August and September; June and the back half of November are usually settled. Book rates you can cancel, buy travel insurance that covers weather disruption rather than just medical cover, and do not plan a once-in-a-lifetime trip around a fixed non-refundable week in September.

When is the St Kitts Music Festival?

The festival’s own website gives 24 to 26 June 2027 at Warner Park in Basseterre, and says to add all three nights to the calendar. No lineup, ticket prices or on-sale date had been published when we read the site. It is a three-night event and rooms on St Kitts go quickly around it, so if you are coming for the music book the room before the tickets.

When is Culturama on Nevis?

The Culturama Secretariat’s own site gives Culturama 53 as 22 July to 3 August 2027, thirteen days in Charlestown, with registration closing on 30 November 2026. The same site records Culturama 52 as 23 July to 4 August 2026. One caveat we will not hide: the site’s own footer still reads 2026 alongside those 2027 dates, so confirm with the Secretariat before you book flights around it.

When is Carnival on St Kitts?

The government’s own festivals page says the National Carnival — Sugar Mas — runs in December and culminates officially on the first Tuesday in January. That is the only official framing we could confirm. We found no published schedule for the next edition, and a government release naming it gave no start or end dates at all. So: expect it to build through December and finish in the first week of January, and check locally for the specific days.

Why does this page not have a temperature chart?

Because we could not find a month-by-month temperature or rainfall table for these islands from a meteorological source we could name and read. There is no national met service website we could find for Saint Kitts and Nevis, and the World Bank’s climate portal renders its monthly figures only inside interactive charts. We could have borrowed numbers from Antigua or Saint Kitts’ neighbours and nobody would have noticed. We would have noticed. The month cards describe what each month is like to travel in instead, which is the part that changes a booking.

Is there a cruise season?

Not one that anybody publishes as a date range. The closest thing we found is the tourism authority’s own 2025-26 cruise booklet, whose calendars run October through September and whose list of inaugural calls ran from 23 October to 15 April. So the heavy months are roughly late October to mid-April, with the Minister’s foreword projecting 454 calls and more than a million passengers for that season — projections he himself described as subject to change.
