Burgas

Where to Stay in Burgas

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Burgas is the working city of the southern Black Sea, and its value to a visitor is almost entirely about position. It sits at the back of a wide bay with salt lakes behind it, has an airport at the edge of town that takes charter traffic from across Europe, and puts three of the best places on the Bulgarian coast within half an hour: Nessebar, the walled old town on its rock peninsula and on the UNESCO list; Sozopol, the quieter Greek-founded old town on a headland to the south; and Sunny Beach, the biggest and loudest resort in the country. The city itself has been quietly made over — a long pedestrianised centre, a Sea Garden along the front with a restored pier, and a decent archaeological museum — and the lakes on its landward side are among the best bird sites in Europe. It is cheaper than Varna, less handsome, and better placed.

Where to stay in Burgas, area by area

Where to stay in and around Burgas, and the honest answer that it is often a base rather than a destination.

The Sea Garden and the pedestrian centre

Most centralOpen all yearWalk to the beach

The grid between the railway station and the park: Aleksandrovska and Bogoridi, the two pedestrian streets, with cafes, the opera and the archaeological museum, running down to the Sea Garden and the restored pier at the bottom. Most of the city’s year-round hotels are here. It is flat, the beach is at the end of the street, the bus station for Nessebar and Sozopol is beside the railway station, and everything stays open in winter. This is the right choice for almost everyone, and particularly if you intend to day-trip along the coast.

Troikata and the streets behind the centre

Good valueLocalShort walk in

The ordinary residential blocks immediately inland of the pedestrian streets, where most of the city actually lives: local restaurants and bakeries, small apartment hotels, and prices below the front. It is a five to ten-minute walk to the Sea Garden and rather less to the bus station. Nothing to look at, good value, and convenient in the way that a base should be.

Sarafovo and the airport side

Near the airportQuiet beachNeeds transport

A former fishing village a few kilometres north-east of the centre, now a low-rise suburb beside the airport with its own beach, a small harbour and a run of apartments and family hotels. It is markedly quieter than the centre and useful for an early flight, since the terminal is minutes away. The trade-off is the obvious one: aircraft overhead in the season, and a bus or taxi whenever you want the city or the bus station.

Nessebar, half an hour north

UNESCO old townAtmosphericBusy by day

Not Burgas at all, but the choice many visitors should make: an old town of churches and wooden houses on a rock peninsula joined to the mainland by a narrow neck, on the UNESCO World Heritage list, about half an hour up the coast. By day in summer it fills with coaches from Sunny Beach next door; in the evening and early morning it is yours. Staying inside the walls means cobbles, no car and small old rooms, which is the point. The new town on the mainland side is ordinary and cheaper.

Sozopol and the southern coast

Quieter old townTwo beachesSeasonal

Half an hour south: a Greek foundation on a small headland, with an old town of stone and timber houses, a church or two worth the detour, and a beach on either side of the neck. It is the calmer of the two old towns and the better one to sleep in if you want a seaside town rather than a monument. Beyond it the coast runs south past Primorsko towards the Strandzha and the Turkish border, which is the emptiest stretch in the country. Much of it closes out of season.

Getting around Burgas

On foot

Central Burgas is flat and small, and the two pedestrian streets plus the Sea Garden are the whole of it for most visitors. The walk from the railway station to the pier takes about fifteen minutes at an easy pace, and the ground is level the whole way.

Buses along the coast

This is the thing Burgas is for. The bus station beside the railway station runs frequent services north to Nessebar and Sunny Beach and south to Sozopol, taking roughly half an hour each way, through the day and late into the evening in season. Out of season the frequency drops but the services continue. Buy a ticket at the window or on board; fares are very low.

Taxis

Cheap and plentiful, with the usual Bulgarian caution about checking the per-kilometre rate on the rear side window or booking through an app. A taxi to Nessebar or Sozopol is affordable split between a few people and much quicker than the bus at the end of the evening, when the last services thin out.

To and from the airport

Burgas Airport is about ten kilometres north-east of the centre at Sarafovo and takes heavy charter traffic from across Europe through the summer, with a thin winter schedule. A city bus runs into town, taxis wait outside, and most package visitors are met by transfer coaches that go straight to the resorts.

The lakes, and the birds

The three lakes behind the city — Vaya, Atanasovsko and Mandra — sit on the Via Pontica migration route and are among the best bird sites in Europe, with pelicans, storks and flamingos in numbers at the right time of year. Atanasovsko is also a working salt pan. There are hides and a visitor centre, it is twenty minutes from the centre, and in spring and autumn migration it is the most interesting thing within reach of the city.

When to come

Burgas follows the coast’s calendar with a city’s exceptions. Mid-June to the end of September is the beach season, with August the hottest and busiest and the first fortnight of September the best value of the year. May, June and late September are the right months for the old towns, when Nessebar and Sozopol are walkable rather than packed. Spring and autumn are the bird months on the lakes. Winter is quiet and cheap, and the city keeps working — museum, opera, restaurants, the Sea Garden in the wind — while the resorts shut.

The practical warning is the same as for the whole coast: a great deal of Sunny Beach and much of Sozopol’s accommodation closes from October to May. If you are coming out of season, stay in Burgas itself, where things are open, and treat the old towns as day trips.

Getting in: what applies, and what does not

Bulgaria is in the European Union and, since 1 January 2025, a full member of the Schengen area — air and sea borders opened in March 2024 and the land borders followed on New Year’s Day 2025. Arriving from another Schengen country is now an internal journey with no routine passport check, which is a real change from older guidebooks. Bulgaria is also in the euro area: it adopted the euro on 1 January 2026 at a rate fixed at 1.95583 lev to the euro, and the lev stopped being legal tender on 1 February 2026, so prices here are in euro and there is no currency to change.

Europe’s Entry/Exit System is now in force. Since 10 April 2026 it has operated at every Schengen external border crossing and passport stamps have been discontinued. On your first crossing you will have your fingerprints taken and your photograph made at the border, which adds a few minutes; after that the check is quicker. There is nothing to apply for and nothing to pay.

ETIAS - the travel authorisation you may have read about, expected to cost around EUR 20 and last three years - is not in force yet. No applications are being taken, and the European Union has not announced the exact start date; it has said it will give several months’ notice before the system goes live. It has been postponed repeatedly and no start date is confirmed, so treat it as coming rather than cancelled, and ignore any website offering to sell you one today - nobody can issue one yet. When it does begin there will be six months in which you can travel without one, then six months in which only first-time arrivals are exempt.

What this means for your trip right now: you do not need ETIAS. You do need a passport issued within the last ten years and valid for at least three months beyond the date you plan to leave. Ninety days in any one hundred and eighty is the visa-free tourist allowance for American, Canadian, British and Australian visitors, and it is counted across the whole Schengen area rather than against Bulgaria alone.

Burgas — questions

Is Burgas worth staying in, or just a gateway?

Honestly, mostly a gateway — and a very good one. The city has a pleasant pedestrian centre, a long seafront park with a restored pier, a decent archaeological museum and the remarkable lakes behind it, but nobody comes to Bulgaria for Burgas. What it gives you is an airport, a bus station, year-round hotels and Nessebar, Sozopol and Sunny Beach all within half an hour, at prices below Varna. If you want a beautiful seaside town to sleep in, sleep in Sozopol or Nessebar.

Should I stay in Burgas, Nessebar or Sunny Beach?

Burgas for a base with transport and everything open all year. Nessebar for atmosphere — the walled old town is the most remarkable place on this coast, and staying there gives you the early morning before the coaches. Sozopol for a quieter version of the same idea with better beaches. Sunny Beach if a big, loud, cheap resort with every water sport is what you actually want, which for some trips it is.

How do I get to Nessebar and Sozopol?

Buses from the station beside Burgas railway station, roughly half an hour to each, frequent through the day and into the evening in season, and very cheap. Taxis are affordable split between a few people. Nessebar is north, past the airport; Sozopol is south. Both are walkable once you arrive and both old towns are cobbled, so wear flat shoes.

What is there to do in Burgas itself?

Walk the two pedestrian streets and the Sea Garden out to the pier; the archaeological museum, which is small and good; the opera; and the lakes behind the city, which are a serious bird site on the Via Pontica route, with hides and a visitor centre and flamingos and pelicans at the right times of year. Atanasovsko is a working salt pan as well. That is an easy day and a half.

Is the beach in Burgas any good?

It is adequate rather than a reason to come: a strip of darker sand below the Sea Garden, convenient and busy in season, with the city immediately behind it. The good sand on this stretch of coast is north at Sunny Beach and south around Sozopol and Primorsko, and all of it is within half an hour of a Burgas hotel, which is the argument for staying here.

Do I need cash in Burgas?

Very little. Bulgaria has used the euro since 1 January 2026 and cards are taken almost everywhere a visitor goes. Keep some cash for the coast buses, a beach bar or a market stall. Prices quoted in lev online are from before the changeover.
