When to Go to Argentina

The Best Time to Visit Argentina

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Short answer: Argentina is in the southern hemisphere, so the seasons run backwards from ours — December to February is high summer and June to August is winter. For a trip taking in both Buenos Aires and Patagonia, the sweet spots are October to early December and March to April. Patagonia has a real season and largely closes outside November to March. Buenos Aires and Iguazú work all year. Below is the full picture, season by season and month by month.

Argentina runs roughly 2,300 miles from the subtropical rainforest of Iguazú to the sub-Antarctic islands off Tierra del Fuego, so there is no single national answer to “when should I go?” What there is, is one rule that governs everything: the far south has a season and the rest of the country does not. Get the Patagonian window right and the rest of your itinerary will fall into place around it.

The Seasons at a Glance

Remember that these are southern-hemisphere seasons — the reverse of North America’s.

Spring — September to November

$$ ShoulderThe best all-rounderJacarandas in Buenos AiresPatagonia opens

If we could send you at one time of year, this would be it. Buenos Aires is mild and, in November, hazed purple with jacaranda blossom. Mendoza’s vines are coming into leaf. Patagonia reopens through October and November — windy, but with long days, wildflowers and far fewer people than in January. Prices sit below the summer peak.

Summer — December to February

$$$$ PeakThe only real Patagonia seasonHot in the northBook far ahead

High summer, and the peak in every sense. This is when Patagonia is fully open, when the Antarctic ships sail from Ushuaia, and when Argentine families take their own holidays — so El Calafate, Bariloche and Ushuaia are busy and expensive, and must be booked months ahead. Buenos Aires, by contrast, is hot, humid and oddly empty in January, as the city decamps to the coast. Iguazú is steamy.

Autumn — March to May

$$ ShoulderMendoza harvestAutumn colour in the southOur quiet favourite

The connoisseur’s season. March brings the vendimia, the Mendoza wine harvest, with its festival at the start of the month. Buenos Aires settles into warm, clear autumn weather. The Patagonian beech forests turn red and gold in late March and April, which is one of the great sights of the south — but services begin winding down from late April, so do not push it too far.

Winter — June to August

$ Low seasonPatagonia largely closesSki season in BarilocheBest for the northwest

Buenos Aires is cool but perfectly pleasant, and cheap; the theatres and tango halls are in full swing. Bariloche turns into a ski town and fills up accordingly. But El Calafate and Ushuaia are a different matter: the days are very short, many hotels and excursions close, and weather can shut roads. This is, however, the ideal dry season for Salta and the red canyons of the northwest, and a fine time for Iguazú.

Month by Month

The concise version — weather, crowds, and what each month does best.

January

$$$$ PeakPatagonia at its busiestBuenos Aires hot & empty

Peak summer. Patagonia is in full swing and priced accordingly — book well ahead. Buenos Aires is hot and humid at around 30°C and many Portenos are away at the coast, so some restaurants close. Iguazú is hot and wet, with the falls at their most powerful.

February

$$$$ PeakStill peak in the southCarnival

Much like January in the south, but Argentine holidays begin tailing off in the second half of the month, which takes a little pressure off. Carnival falls in February or early March. Buenos Aires stays hot; Patagonia is at its warmest and its windiest.

March

$$$ ExcellentMendoza harvestOur pick of the year

One of the two best months to be in Argentina. The Mendoza vendimia festival opens the harvest; Buenos Aires cools to something lovely; Patagonia is still fully open and beginning to turn colour. Crowds thin from mid-month.

April

$$ ShoulderAutumn colour in PatagoniaGood value

Beautiful and quiet. The southern beech forests around Bariloche and El Chaltén turn red and gold, and prices drop. Watch the calendar: from the second half of April some Patagonian hotels and excursions start closing for the winter.

May

$ LowPatagonia winding downFine in the city and the north

Cool, clear and cheap in Buenos Aires. Patagonia is largely shutting; El Calafate is quiet and Ushuaia is heading into its dark months. Salta and the northwest, however, are entering their best dry season.

June

$ LowWinter beginsSki season opens

Buenos Aires is chilly but perfectly comfortable, with the shortest days around the solstice. Bariloche’s ski season opens and the town fills. El Calafate and Ushuaia are at their quietest, with only a handful of hours of daylight.

July

$$ Ski peakArgentine winter holidaysBook Bariloche early

The Argentine school winter holidays fall in July, which makes Bariloche expensive and busy and lifts prices generally. Buenos Aires is at its coldest, though rarely below freezing. A good month for Iguazú and for the northwest.

August

$ LowStill winterWhales at Puerto Madryn

Quiet and cold in the south, mild in the north. Bariloche is still skiing. Southern right whales are in the bay at Puerto Madryn, on the Atlantic coast, from roughly June through December — August and September are prime.

September

$$ ShoulderSpring beginsPatagonia reopening

Spring arrives. Buenos Aires warms up and the parks come to life. Patagonian hotels and excursions begin reopening through the month, though the weather is unsettled and the wind can be fierce. Prices are still low.

October

$$ ShoulderExcellent all roundLong days returning

A very good month almost everywhere: mild in Buenos Aires, warming in Mendoza, and Patagonia open with lengthening days and spring flowers but without the summer crowds or prices. The wind is the price you pay.

November

$$$ High shoulderJacaranda seasonAntarctic season opens

Buenos Aires turns purple with jacaranda, which is worth timing a trip around on its own. Patagonia is fully open and lovely; the first Antarctic voyages leave Ushuaia. Prices begin climbing towards the summer peak.

December

$$$$ PeakSummer startsChristmas & New Year busy

Summer proper. Long days, warm weather and the start of the Argentine holidays in the second half of the month. Everything in Patagonia is open and everything is filling up; Christmas and New Year need booking months ahead.

The Cheapest Time to Go

Where the savings actually are — and where a bargain is really a closed season.

May, June and September are the value months

$ Low seasonBuenos Aires at its cheapestCity & north

Airfares and hotel rates are lowest in the shoulders either side of winter. Buenos Aires is excellent value in these months and loses none of its appeal — the theatres, the tango halls and the steakhouses are all indoors anyway. Salta and the northwest are in their dry season, and Iguazú is comfortable.

Two catches worth knowing

$$ Watch outJuly in BarilocheClosed Patagonia

First, July is not cheap in Bariloche — it is the Argentine school winter holiday and the peak of the ski season. Second, and more important, low prices in El Calafate and Ushuaia between June and September reflect the fact that much of the town is shut. That is not a bargain; it is a different, quieter and much colder trip, and you should choose it deliberately.

The genuine sweet spot

$$ ShoulderOctober & MarchOpen but not peak

If you want Patagonia open, prices below the peak and room to move, aim at October and early November or March. You accept some wind and some unsettled weather; you save a great deal and share the boardwalks with far fewer people.

Crowds & Weather

What to actually expect, and how to plan around it.

The Argentine holiday calendar matters

Plan around itJanuaryJulyEaster

Argentines holiday in January and in July, and the effect is dramatic: Patagonia and the coast fill in January while Buenos Aires empties, and Bariloche fills in July. Easter week is busy everywhere. If your dates are flexible, sidestep all three.

Wind is the Patagonian weather story

Pack for itSpring is windiestLayers

It is not the cold that surprises visitors to Patagonia, it is the wind — strongest in spring and early summer, and capable of making a mild day feel bitter. A proper windproof shell matters more than a thick coat. Weather in Ushuaia and El Calafate can turn from sunshine to sleet inside an hour in any month, so carry layers on every excursion.

Heat and humidity in the north

Take it gentlyBuenos Aires in JanuaryIguazú

Buenos Aires in January and February is hot and humid, and Iguazú more so. If you are travelling in those months and the heat is hard on you, sightsee in the morning, keep the afternoon for a cool museum or a rest, and follow the local rhythm of a late dinner.

Best Time by Region

Argentina is too big for one answer — here is the timing region by region.

Buenos Aires

Best: Sep–Nov, Mar–MayYear-round cityAvoid: peak January

The city works in any month. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable, with mild days and clear light; November’s jacaranda bloom is the loveliest single sight. January is hot, humid and half-deserted; winter is cool, cheap and culturally rich. There is no bad time, only better ones.

Mendoza & the wine country

Best: Mar for harvest, Oct–Apr generallyDry climate

Mendoza is dry and sunny most of the year. March is the month, for the harvest and its festival. October to April is warm and reliable for vineyard lunches outdoors. Winter is cold and the vines are bare, but the light on the Andes is extraordinary and prices are low.

Bariloche & the Lake District

Best: Dec–Mar and Jul–AugTwo seasons

Bariloche is one of the few Argentine destinations with two distinct high seasons. December to March is for the lakes, walking and the Circuito Chico. July and August are the ski season, when the town is busiest and dearest. April, May, October and November are quiet, moody and good value.

El Calafate & the glaciers

Best: Nov–MarWinter: much closes

A genuinely seasonal destination. November to March is the window, with long days and everything running. The Perito Moreno Glacier is open all year and is arguably more atmospheric in the snow — but from June to September the days are short, many hotels and excursions close and roads can be affected. Book December and January months in advance.

Ushuaia & Tierra del Fuego

Best: Nov–MarAntarctic seasonSki in winter

The Antarctic cruise season runs from about November to March, and that is when Ushuaia is alive: Beagle Channel boats sail daily, the national park is open and the light lasts until late. Winter brings skiing at Cerro Castor and only a few hours of daylight. Pack layers and a windproof coat whenever you come — the weather here changes within the hour.

Iguazú Falls & the north

Best: Mar–May, Aug–OctYear-round

Subtropical, so hot and humid rather than seasonal. The falls are impressive all year. March to May and August to October give the most comfortable temperatures; December to February is hot and sticky, though the water is at its most powerful after summer rain. The northwest around Salta is best in the dry winter, from May to September.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best time to visit Argentina?

For a first trip covering both Buenos Aires and Patagonia, October to early December or March to April. Both give you mild weather in the city, an open and far less crowded Patagonia, and prices below the December-to-February peak. If you can only travel in the northern summer, June to August, plan around Buenos Aires, Iguazú, Salta and skiing at Bariloche rather than the far south.

Are the seasons in Argentina really the opposite of ours?

Yes, completely. Argentina is in the southern hemisphere, so December, January and February are high summer and June, July and August are winter. Christmas is a hot weather holiday; July is the ski season. This catches out more North American visitors than any other single fact about the country, so build your plans around it from the start.

When is the cheapest time to visit Argentina?

Broadly May, June and September — the shoulders either side of winter. Buenos Aires is at its cheapest then and remains thoroughly enjoyable. Be aware of two exceptions: July is expensive in Bariloche because of the Argentine school winter holidays, and Patagonia in the low season is cheap largely because much of it is shut.

Can I visit Patagonia in winter?

You can, but go in with your eyes open. From June to September the days in El Calafate and Ushuaia are very short, a good many hotels and excursions close for the season, and weather can close roads. The Perito Moreno Glacier stays open and is magnificent in snow. Bariloche is the exception — it is a full ski resort in winter and thoroughly alive.

When can I see whales in Argentina?

Southern right whales gather in the bays around Puerto Madryn and the Península Valdés on the Atlantic coast from roughly June to December, with August through October the prime months. It pairs awkwardly with the Patagonian summer season, so it is usually a trip of its own rather than an add-on.

How far ahead should I book?

For December and January in Patagonia, and for the Christmas and New Year period anywhere, book four to six months ahead; the good hotels in El Calafate and Ushuaia are genuinely finite. For the shoulder months, six to eight weeks is usually comfortable. Buenos Aires can be arranged at short notice in almost any month.

Weather patterns and opening seasons are described from long-run averages and current published practice, and Patagonia in particular varies year to year.
