Where to Stay in Beijing — Best Hotels by Style

Are you after a palace-grade landmark near the Summer Palace, a courtyard boutique tucked into a hutong lane, a family base with a pool the children won’t leave, or simply honest value steps from the subway? Luxury

Boutique

Budget & Value

Beijing rewards a careful budget: a tidy courtyard room down a hutong costs a fraction of the towers and puts you closer to real neighborhood life. You trade marble lobbies for character and location — and spend the savings on the Wall, the temples and long lunches.

Beijing hotels — quick questions

What is the best area to stay in Beijing?

For most first-time visitors, Dongcheng around Wangfujing and the Forbidden City is the best base: it is central, walkable to the major sights and sits right on the subway. For dining and nightlife, choose Sanlitun in Chaoyang; for old-Beijing character and hutong lanes, stay near the Drum and Bell Towers in the Gulou district.

Is Beijing walkable?

Individual neighborhoods, parks and sights are very pleasant on foot, but Beijing is enormous and the major attractions are spread far apart, so you will not walk between them. The subway is the answer: it is cheap, clean, extensive and signed in English, so pair walking within a district with the metro to cross the city.

When is the best time to visit Beijing?

Autumn, roughly September and October, is the loveliest season, with crisp air and clear blue skies. Spring, April and May, is also mild and pleasant. Summers are hot, humid and busy, while winters are cold and dry but bring the smallest crowds and occasional snow on the Great Wall.
