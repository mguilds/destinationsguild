Where to Stay in Bariloche — Best Hotels on Lake Nahuel Huapi

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated August 2026

Bariloche gives you a straight choice. The town centre, around the stone-and-timber Civic Centre, puts restaurants, chocolate shops and the lakefront within a few flat blocks — no car, no taxi, no planning. Avenida Bustillo runs west along the shore of Lake Nahuel Huapi for twenty-five kilometres, and the further out you go the better the view and the more dependent you become on a car or a taxi for every meal. At the far end sits the Llao Llao, one of South America’s famous hotels. Below are our picks by style, each with an honest note about how far out it is, and a Book Me link straight to Trip.com.

Judge a Bariloche hotel by its distance and its slope, not its star rating. The town is built on a hillside above the lake, so “ten minutes from the centre” often means ten minutes down and a stiff climb back. We have flagged, for every hotel below, whether Trip.com lists a lift and how far it really is from dinner. Where we say no lift is listed, that is what Trip.com shows — it is not proof there is none, so ask the hotel directly if it matters.

Luxury

Lake views, and the grandest hotel in Argentine Patagonia.

Llao Llao Resort, Golf-Spa

$$$$ Luxury25 km out on BustilloIndoor & outdoor poolsStep-free entrance

The famous one — a great timber-and-stone lodge at the far end of the Circuito Chico, from about US$232. Trip.com lists a lift, a stair-free main entrance and accessible rooms, along with indoor and outdoor pools, a full spa, a private beach and a golf course. Be clear-eyed about the distance: it is twenty-five kilometres from town, so a car or taxi is essential and dinner in Bariloche is a thirty-five to forty-minute run each way. The grounds themselves are large and sloping. Pets are not accepted.

Bariloche Hotels — Frequently Asked Questions

Which part of Bariloche should I stay in?

The town centre, if you want to walk to dinner — and most of our readers do. The chocolate shops, restaurants and the lakefront are within a few flat blocks of the Civic Centre, and the Sheraton, Tirol, Nahuel Huapi, NH Edelweiss, Hampton and Cacique Inacayal are all in it. Along Avenida Bustillo you trade that convenience for the view, kilometre by kilometre: Alma del Lago at 1.2 km is still an easy walk, Design Suites at 2.5 km is a short taxi, and the Llao Llao at 25 km is a world of its own.

Which Bariloche hotels have a lift?

Trip.com confirms lifts at the Llao Llao, Design Suites, Alma del Lago, Sheraton Bariloche, Huinid Bustillo, Hotel Tirol, Hotel Nahuel Huapi, NH Edelweiss, Hampton by Hilton and Cacique Inacayal. It lists no lift for Aldebaran or the Rochester. The strongest accessibility we found anywhere in Bariloche is at the Sheraton, which lists a stair-free entrance, accessible rooms, handrails, a pool ramp and wheelchairs available; the Hotel Tirol also lists a stair-free entrance.

Are there adults-only hotels in Bariloche?

Not that we can verify. Every property we checked on Trip.com either welcomes children of all ages or accepts them in some rooms, so we have not listed an adults-only section for Bariloche — we would rather tell you the category is empty than fill it with something that does not fit.

Do I need a car in Bariloche?

Only if you stay out on the Bustillo road. In the town centre you will not use one. For the Circuito Chico — the classic lake and mountain loop — a half-day tour or a hired driver is easier than self-driving, and lets you look at the view instead of the road.
