St George’s & Grand Anse

Where to Stay in St George’s & Grand Anse

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Almost everybody who comes to Grenada sleeps within fifteen minutes of St George’s, and almost nobody sleeps in it. The capital is a working port wrapped round a horseshoe harbour, steep, handsome and short of hotel rooms; the beds are on the coast immediately south of it, along the long crescent of Grand Anse and the smaller bay at Morne Rouge. That strip is the practical centre of a Grenada holiday: the main beach, the shops, most of the restaurants a visitor will use, a ten-minute taxi into town and a fifteen-minute one to the airport.

The areas, and which one suits you

Where the beds actually are, and the honest word on steps, hills, road noise and how far it really is to the sand.

The Carenage and downtown

The inner harbour, the market square, the tunnel under the hill and Fort George above it. Busy by day, closed up by evening, and with very few places for a visitor to stay. Come here for the market on a Saturday morning and for an hour on foot — not for a bed.

Grand Anse

The main event: a long, sheltered, gently shelving crescent of pale sand about ten minutes south of town, with the hotels set back behind the trees rather than built out over the beach. The widest choice of rooms on the island, the easiest swimming, and a line of shops and restaurants on the road behind. Flat, which matters more than people expect.

Morne Rouge

The next small bay south, round the headland from Grand Anse — quieter, more sheltered still, and popular with families for the calm shallow water. A handful of places to stay, a short walk or a very short taxi from the Grand Anse strip.

Point Salines

The headland at the south-western tip, where the airport is. Convenient to the point of being unromantic, with a couple of beaches of its own. Worth considering for a single night either side of a flight, rather than for a week.

What this coast is not

It is not a resort strip in the Mexican or Jamaican sense. The hotels are small, there is no fleet of jet skis, and the island closes gently in the evening. If you want a mega-resort with nine restaurants and a water park, Grenada is the wrong island, and that is most of its appeal.

Getting about

Minibuses run from the terminal in town along the Grand Anse road all day for a few EC dollars; the Grenada Ports Authority says buses leave the Melville Street terminal on weekdays from 7am to 7pm. Taxi fares are set by zone — agree the figure before you set off. Water taxis cross between the Carenage and Grand Anse, which is the pleasantest way to arrive in town.

What your money is worth in St George’s
US $1 = 2.70 EC$
EC$ 10 = about US $3.70
Grenada uses the Eastern Caribbean dollar, pegged at EC$2.70 to the US dollar, so a price of EC$27 is about ten US dollars. US dollars are accepted in most hotels, restaurants and taxis, and change often comes back in local money. Cards work in hotels, supermarkets and the larger restaurants; carry cash for minibuses, market stalls and small bars. A government tax and a service charge are normally added to hotel and restaurant bills — ask what is included when you book. St George’s market and the small cook-shops near the waterfront feed the town rather than the ships and are priced accordingly, so lunch in town costs a fraction of lunch on the beach road. The supermarkets on the Grand Anse strip are well stocked and useful if your room has a kitchen.

The accommodation is described as suites, a number of them with private plunge pools and open-air garden terraces, and the site names its spa as Janissa Spa and describes a private beachfront pavilion used for yoga. Coyaba Beach Resort

Grand Anse Beach80 roomsMeal plans, not all-inclusive by default

Coyaba gives its address as P.O. Box 336, St George, Grenada, West Indies, and says on its own homepage that Grand Anse Beach is only a few steps from your room. Its accommodation page states the figure plainly: eighty guest rooms, in eight blocks of two floors each, scattered through five and a half acres of garden. There are two restaurants, Arawakabana for breakfast, lunch and dinner and the open-air Carbet by the pool, along with the Cacique bar and lounge, a beach bar called The Cove and a swim-up pool bar. Note that the site lists several meal plans — bed and breakfast, half board, full board and all-inclusive — so all-inclusive here is a choice you make rather than the default, and it is worth checking which one your rate includes. Two-storey garden blocks mean some rooms are up a flight of stairs; ask for ground floor if that matters.

Travelling in your later years

This is the easiest base in Grenada for anyone who would rather not spend a holiday on steps. Grand Anse is flat, the sand shelves gently, the water is calm and the beach road has supermarkets, pharmacies and restaurants along it, so a day can be done on foot. The airport is about fifteen minutes away, which spares you the hour-and-a-half transfer that tires people out on bigger islands. Two cautions. Coyaba’s garden blocks are two storeys, so ask for a ground-floor room if stairs are a problem, and ask any Grand Anse hotel how far the room is from the sand — the gardens here are generous and the walk can be longer than the photographs suggest. St George’s itself is genuinely steep: take a taxi up to the fort and walk slowly down, and do the market early before the heat.

Questions about staying in St George’s & Grand Anse

Should I stay in St George's itself?

Probably not, and not because there is anything wrong with the town. There are simply very few rooms in it: the downtown is a working port that shuts in the evening, and the hotel coast starts about ten minutes south at Grand Anse. Stay on the beach and treat the capital as the place you walk round in the morning and take a water taxi into for dinner. If being able to walk into town matters to you more than the beach does, look at the hillside above the Carenage and expect steps.

How far is Grand Anse from the airport and from town?

Both are close, which is one of Grenada's quiet advantages over bigger islands. Maurice Bishop International is on the Point Salines headland at the south-western tip, roughly fifteen minutes from the Grand Anse hotels, and St George's is about ten minutes the other way. Nothing on the main island is more than about an hour from the terminal, so you are not losing half a day to a transfer at either end.

Is Grand Anse a good beach?

It is the reason most people come. A long sheltered crescent of pale sand with a gentle entry into calm water, hotels set back behind the trees rather than built over the sand, and public access along its length. It is swimmable every day of a week without getting dull, and it is flat, which matters if steps and slopes are a consideration. Morne Rouge, round the next headland, is smaller and calmer still.

Can I walk into town from the hotels?

In theory, and not in practice for most people. The road from Grand Anse into St George's climbs over a headland and is hot, narrow and not built for walkers. Use a minibus from the main road, a taxi, or best of all a water taxi across the harbour from the Carenage, which takes minutes and is the pleasantest arrival in the Caribbean. Within the downtown itself everything a visitor wants is a few blocks from the cruise terminal.

Is it safe to walk around St George's?

The centre is busy and ordinary by day and the usual town sense applies: keep valuables out of sight, carry small local notes, and take a taxi after dark rather than walking unlit streets. The streets round the harbour empty out in the evening once the ships have gone, and most visitors eat on the Grand Anse strip or at their hotel rather than downtown at night.
