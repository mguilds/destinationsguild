Where to Stay in Phoenix — Downtown, Camelback & the Resorts

Curated by Our Guild Explorers · Updated November 2026

Phoenix is a big, spread-out desert city, and the hotel you pick decides how much of your trip is spent in a car. Its visitors bureau gives it almost 300 days of sunshine a year, which is why winter and spring are the busy seasons, and why so many of its hotels are really resorts, with pools, golf and spas on their own grounds. Sky Harbor International Airport sits in the middle of the Valley, less than ten minutes from downtown by the bureau’s reckoning. The real choice is between a walkable downtown, the smart Camelback and Biltmore corridor, or a resort where you can stay put, so this page sorts the city into those choices and names the hotels we could confirm today.

Choosing Your Area in Phoenix

Four ways to stay in Phoenix, each suited to a different trip.

Downtown, for ball games, concerts and the light rail

Downtown is the business and entertainment centre: the convention centre, Chase Field for baseball, the arena, theatres, and the Roosevelt Row arts district a short walk north. The Valley Metro light rail runs through it, connecting to the airport by the PHX Sky Train and on to Tempe and Mesa, so it is one of the few parts of Phoenix where you can manage without a car.

The trade-off is that downtown is busy on game nights and quiet on some evenings, and it has hotels rather than resorts. Choose it for a short city break, a convention or a game.

The Camelback Corridor and the Biltmore, for shopping and dining

Camelback Road east of Central Avenue runs through the Biltmore area, with Biltmore Fashion Park, restaurants, and some of the city’s best-known hotels, then on towards Arcadia at the foot of Camelback Mountain. It sits between downtown and Scottsdale, which makes it a good middle point for a trip that takes in both.

You will drive most places, but distances are short, and this is where you will find a polished city hotel or a classic resort within the city.

North Phoenix, for resorts beside the desert preserves

North of the centre, the city backs onto the Phoenix Mountains Preserve and, further out, the Desert Ridge area. The resorts here are self-contained, with several pools, golf and spas, and trails close by.

They suit families and couples who want to settle in and not move the car every day. Expect a longer drive to downtown and the airport.

South Phoenix by South Mountain, for a family resort near the airport

In the south of the city, below South Mountain, sits one of the city’s big family resorts, with its own water park. It is a practical base for a family that wants pools first and sightseeing second.

It is a drive to most restaurants and sights, so it works best for a stay built around the resort itself.

Getting There & When to Go

The handful of Phoenix details that change how a trip goes.

Getting to Phoenix

Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport is the main airport for the Valley. The visitors bureau says it serves more than 120 domestic and international destinations, sits less than ten minutes from downtown, and is within 30 minutes of most hotels and resorts. The PHX Sky Train links the terminals to the Valley Metro light rail.

The light rail line runs about 30 miles, connecting Phoenix with Tempe and Mesa, and the bureau says it operates 18 to 22 hours a day, seven days a week. Outside downtown, most visitors rent a car.

When to go

Winter and spring are the comfortable seasons. The visitors bureau says winter skies stay blue with highs in the 60s and 70s and nights that rarely drop below freezing, and that spring days hover around 80 degrees, with cactus and wildflowers in bloom. October brings warm days and cool nights.

Summer is hot: the bureau’s own table puts average highs above 100°F in June and July. The monsoon season runs from June to September, with short, fierce storms and dust storms. If you come in summer, plan your outdoor time for the early morning.

Phoenix, Scottsdale or both?

Phoenix and Scottsdale run into each other. If your trip is mostly about Old Town Scottsdale’s restaurants and galleries, our Where to Stay in Scottsdale guide covers that side; the Camelback Corridor and Arcadia hotels on this page sit between the two.

Phoenix Hotels FAQ

The questions travellers ask most before booking a room here.

Where is the best place to stay in Phoenix?

Downtown if you want to walk to games, concerts and the light rail. The Camelback Corridor and Biltmore area for shopping, dining and an easy drive to Scottsdale. A north Phoenix resort beside the desert preserves, or the South Mountain resort with its water park, if you would rather stay put with the family.

How far is Phoenix Sky Harbor Airport from downtown?

Less than ten minutes, according to the Phoenix visitors bureau, which also says most hotels and resorts are within 30 minutes of the airport. The PHX Sky Train connects the terminals to the Valley Metro light rail into downtown.

Can I get around Phoenix without a car?

Downtown, yes: the Valley Metro light rail runs about 30 miles through Phoenix, Tempe and Mesa and links to the airport. For the resorts, the desert trails and day trips, most visitors rent a car.

When is the best time to visit Phoenix?

Winter and spring. The visitors bureau describes winter highs in the 60s and 70s and spring days around 80 degrees. Summer average highs are above 100°F in June and July, and the monsoon storm season runs from June to September.

Which Phoenix resorts have a water park?

Arizona Grand Resort & Spa describes its seven-acre Oasis water park, JW Marriott Phoenix Desert Ridge describes its AquaRidge WaterPark with a lazy river and slides, and Hilton Phoenix Tapatio Cliffs describes a 138-foot waterslide, each on its own website. Check opening times for your dates, as pools and slides can be seasonal.
